Abstract
Regional synthetic aperture radar (SAR) reconnaissance depends on more than geometric visibility alone. An executable obser-
vation must simultaneously satisfy geometric feasibility, image quality constraints, and timely data delivery within the mission
horizon. This paper develops an effective window framework for regional SAR window generation, per window signal level qual-
ity screening, and hybrid direct-relay closed loop scheduling. Through coarse angular bandpass screening, a planar characteristic
curve containment test, and one dimensional boundary bisection, the framework forms geometry feasible candidate observation
windows with millisecond-level accuracy for their entry and exit times. Each candidate window is then assessed in stripmap mode
with a companion point target under a unified echo generation and Back Projection (BP) imaging workflow; only windows whose
range and azimuth impulse response width (IRW), peak sidelobe ratio (PSLR), and integrated sidelobe ratio (ISLR) all satisfy the
preset thresholds are retained. The retained observation, relay, and downlink windows feed a quality constrained hybrid direct-
relay closed-loop mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) formulation for joint scheduling of observation and ground return.
Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework unifies STK consistent millisecond level window accuracy and
explicit per window quality constraints within one hybrid closed loop scheduling model, and that this model improves closure
performance and ground returned data volume relative to a relay only baseline.

1. Introduction
Time critical reconnaissance, disaster response, maritime surveillance, and persistent monitoring continue to in-
crease the demands placed on synthetic aperture radar (SAR) acquisition and data return. As mission assessment shifts
from the simple question of whether a target is visible to the broader question of whether useful information can be
formed and delivered in time, the value of an imaging activity increasingly depends on target illuminability, focused
image quality, and ground delivery within the admissible mission horizon. From this perspective, a geometrically
visible interval is only a potential opportunity rather than an executable observation window. For side looking SAR,
incidence, squint, and dwell constraints further compress the geometrically visible arcs, and geometric accessibility
alone does not guarantee interpretable imagery. At the same time, the mission is not complete until the acquired data

governed jointly by visibility, image quality, and data return. These coupled requirements call for a unified framework
that can both generate candidate SAR observation windows efficiently and coordinate observation, relay, and down-

Satellite observation scheduling has evolved from single satellite activity selection to closed-loop integrated for-
mulations. Early work treated the problem as constraint-driven or priority-driven task selection [1, 2]. It was later
extended to agile satellites by Lemaˆıtre et al. [3] and to multi satellite, multi orbit management by Bianchessi et
al. [4]. Subsequent studies refined the formulation through time dependent transition times [5], onboard MILP re-
planning [6], rigorous continuous time MILP for constellations [7], attitude coupled scheduling [8], exact algorithms
for time dependent profits [9], and branch cut and price exact methods [10]. The survey of Wang et al. [11] docu-
ments the growing emphasis on integrated mission metrics. Recent developments also include learning augmented
heuristics [12], distributed constellation decision making [13], and decentralized optimization [14]. On the down-
link side, Rojanasoonthon and Bard [15] showed that relay resources often cap throughput. This result motivated
standalone downlink formulations [16, 17], integrated imaging and downloading through branch and price or genetic
algorithms [18, 19, 20], and relay network scheduling with time windows [21]. Closer to the present mixed direct and
relay setting, Chen et al. [22] considered a hybrid direct GEO relay topology, He and Dong [23] coupled observation,
relay, and downloading in a single model, and Kim et al. [24, 25] proposed the observation relay downlink MILP
backbone extended in the present study.
Fast visibility prediction has a long lineage in astrodynamics. Early studies established cumulative coverage and
rapid in view computation for points, ground stations, and simple fields of view [26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31]. Later work
accelerated rise and set prediction and multi restriction prediction through analytical or adaptive techniques [32, 33],
and extended these methods to agile field of regard and oblate Earth models [34, 35]. A geometric abstraction layer
then emerged with envelope curve and calculation plane constructions: Zuo et al. [36] cast coverage as an envelope
curve problem on a sensor fixed plane, while Han et al. [37] generalized visibility computation to arbitrary sensor fields
through a calculation plane point in region test. The present paper specializes the latter construction to side looking
SAR. Recent regional target studies refined this reduction through minimum observation sampling [38], arbitrary
attitude maneuver paths [39], semi analytical area target visibility [40], elevation view elements [41], triple time
interval hybridization [42], and the SAR-oriented VTWSAR method of Shi et al. [43]. The main unresolved point
is explicit enforcement of side looking SAR admissibility, rather than generic geometric accessibility, throughout the
reduction process.
Per window image quality evaluation has received far less attention in the scheduling literature. Standard signal
processing references establish impulse response width (IRW), peak sidelobe ratio (PSLR), and integrated sidelobe
ratio (ISLR) as canonical point target figures of merit for spaceborne SAR [44, 45]. Recent raw data simulators
reproduce the geometry dependent range history required for realistic quality assessment at high resolution [46, 47].
The connection between these signal level metrics and window based scheduling remains open. Most scheduling
models treat all geometry feasible windows as interchangeable tasks with a single profit coefficient, and they do not
test whether the focused image meets a prescribed quality standard. A per window quality screening layer anchored
in IRW, PSLR, and ISLR is still missing from the closed-loop scheduling pipeline.
To address these gaps, this paper develops an effective window framework for regional SAR scheduling. The
framework couples a hybrid direct-relay closed-loop optimization model with a three stage window construction chain
and per window signal level quality screening. Window generation follows angular bandpass screening, a planar
characteristic curve containment test, and one dimensional boundary bisection. Side looking SAR admissibility is
enforced throughout the preprocessing chain. Each geometry feasible window is then evaluated by a companion point
target stripmap simulation under a unified echo generation and Back Projection (BP) workflow, and only windows
satisfying the prescribed range and azimuth IRW, PSLR, and ISLR criteria are retained for optimization.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• A hybrid direct-relay closed-loop MILP model for regional SAR reconnaissance. Direct downlink and relay
assisted downlink are optimized jointly under shared observation, communication, and latency constraints, and
mission value is attached to completed request closure.

2. Hybrid Direct and Relay Satellite Downlink Model
This section formulates the exact scheduling model for the reconnaissance mission. The implementation pipeline
runs from available window generation to per window signal level quality evaluation and then to exact scheduling, but
the hybrid closed-loop MILP is presented first because it defines the closure relations that the preprocessing pipeline
must satisfy. Its inputs are four families of candidate windows, and its outputs are the exact start times and execution
durations of the selected windows. Window generation and imaging quality assessment are introduced in Section 3.
2.1. Mission and Task Description
The mission considered in this paper is not adequately described as a simple collection of visible imaging op-
portunities. As illustrated in Fig. 1, mission completion depends on three tightly coupled components: a target set
defining the observation demand, an observation resource layer determining effective observability under SAR imag-
ing constraints, and a communication resource layer determining whether the acquired data reach the ground within
the admissible latency. Accordingly, this paper is limited to regional SAR imaging of stationary targets under hy-
brid ground return and does not address the additional dynamics and coupling relations introduced by mission mixes
involving movers or heterogeneous payloads.
2.1.1. Observation and Communication Resources
The observation resource layer is the set S of SAR observation satellites in low Earth orbit. A geometrically
visible interval does not directly imply an observation opportunity usable for the mission, and SAR specific physical
admissibility conditions must be evaluated for each candidate window. The communication resource layer comprises
the set R of relay satellites and the set G of ground stations. Within the planning horizon [0, H], two return modes
are admitted: a direct downlink from an observation satellite to a ground station, and a relay assisted downlink routed
through a relay satellite. Regional targets are indexed by j ∈J and represented by boundary polygons G j for each
regional area of interest (AOI) on the Earth reference surface; Section 3 specifies how these boundaries enter window
generation. Three task types jointly form the closure loop:
• Observation task: an observation satellite s ∈S images target j ∈J in an effective observation window.
• Relay task: an observation satellite s ∈S transfers acquired data to a relay satellite r ∈R.
• Downlink task: a ground return is performed either by an observation satellite s ∈S through direct downlink
or by a relay satellite r ∈R through relay downlink to a ground station g ∈G.
The scheduler determines which targets are closed, which effective observation opportunities are selected, and
how the resulting data are routed to the ground. This task decomposition induces four window families in the MILP:

3. Effective Window Closed-Loop Scheduling for Regional SAR Reconnaissance
This section presents the effective window construction method for regional SAR tasks. The procedure consists
of two stages: rapid discretization of continuous time visibility into candidate observation windows, and geometry
dependent quality evaluation together with threshold based filtering for effective window formation.
3.1. Rapid discretization of continuous time visibility into candidate windows
Under a SAR field of view description aligned with the Systems Tool Kit (STK) side looking payload model,
continuous time regional visibility is sampled and screened into a sparse set of candidate observation windows. The
procedure combines a coarse geometric filter that retains orbit arcs with potential target intersection and a projection
plane refinement that determines the entry and exit times of each candidate window.

4. Experiments
This section presents experimental validation of the proposed framework. The experiments followed the method
chain of the framework. Experiment I validated the hybrid mixed integer formulation on a controlled benchmark
with prescribed feasible windows and focused on closed-loop scheduling performance under relay and direct downlink
competition. Experiment II evaluated the first preprocessing stage by comparing the candidate window discretization
procedure with an independent reference in terms of geometry feasible candidate window generation. Experiment III
evaluated the second preprocessing stage by examining geometry dependent imaging quality assessment and the
resulting effective window screening behavior. Experiment IV integrated the full pipeline, from effective window
construction to hybrid MILP scheduling, in an operationally realistic scenario. All experiments were executed on a
64 bit Windows laptop with an Intel 12th Gen Core i9-12900H CPU (base frequency 2.5 GHz, 14 physical cores, 20
logical processors). The mixed integer linear programming layer of the scheduling formulation was implemented in
C++ using Microsoft Visual Studio 2022, and the induced programs were solved with Gurobi 12.0. Satellite state
propagation employed the SGP4 model. Rapid candidate window generation and geometry dependent per window
quality analysis were implemented in Python 3.11.

4.3. Experiment III: Signal Level Imaging Quality Validation Across Three Observation Windows
The third experiment validated the imaging quality differentiation method described in Section 3.2 by executing
a complete echo generation and focusing cycle on all three geometry feasible windows produced by Experiment II
for the Beijing AOI. Different observation geometries, although all satisfying the sensor visibility envelope, produced
measurably different imaging quality outcomes.
4.3.1. Time Varying Observation Geometry
For each of the three geometry feasible windows produced by Experiment II, four governing geometric descriptors
introduced in Section 3.2.1 were sampled at 60 uniformly spaced instants within the window. These descriptors were
the incidence angle θinc, the squint angle θsq, the slant range R from the satellite to the regional centroid, and the
ground range resolution δg inferred from Eq. (73). The satellite state at each sampling instant was obtained from the
SGP4 plus GMST pipeline used throughout Section 3, and the descriptors were evaluated between that state and the
ECEF position of the regional centroid. This yielded four time series per window that together characterised how
the observation geometry evolved during each candidate dwell, and provided the basis for a compact midpoint based
summary in Table 12.
The resulting time series were plotted in Figs. 14a–14d. Over the 20 to 34 s window durations, the incidence angle
and slant range varied by less than 0.4◦and 5 km, respectively, so both quantities stayed effectively constant within
a window. The ground range resolution also remained nearly invariant within each window, and Table 12 reported
midpoint values for inter window comparison. The inter window contrast remained substantial: W1 at a moderate
incidence of 40.8◦yielded 4.06 m, whereas W0 and W2 at approximately 59◦yielded roughly 3.1 m. The squint
angle swept approximately linearly from +4◦to −4◦, crossing zero Doppler near the window center and reflecting the
azimuth synthesis geometry intrinsic to stripmap SAR integration.
The window descriptors summarized later in Table 12 retained the midpoint quantities most relevant to inter
window comparison. Windows W0 and W2 were left looking passes at large incidence angles of 59.1 and 58.2 degrees,
while W1 was a right looking pass at a moderate incidence of 40.8 degrees.

4.4.2. Results and analysis
Table 17 summarized the scheduling outcomes. All 87 quality screened observation windows were selected, 31

capability for the full observation constellation.
Fig. 18 showed the full 24 hour schedule. Observation tasks were distributed throughout the planning horizon in

by the availability of relay and ground contact resources. The schedule reached full mission closure for all selected
satellites at this constellation scale.

5. Conclusion
This paper presents a unified effective window framework for regional SAR scheduling. The framework couples
access analysis, image quality evaluation, and mission closure optimization in one pipeline. The method combines a
hybrid direct-relay closed-loop MILP with regional window generation based on coarse angular screening, a planar
characteristic curve containment test, and boundary bisection, together with per window signal level quality evaluation
using a companion point target and six IRW, PSLR, and ISLR indicators. The experiments showed accurate recovery
of reference windows, rejection of geometrically feasible but imaging inferior opportunities, and closed-loop schedul-
ing at constellation scale under practical computational budgets. Regional SAR scheduling requires joint treatment of
visibility, image quality, and data return.
Future work will extend the present SAR framework to more general heterogeneous observation scenarios. One
direction is a unified effective window model for multiple payload types, especially optical sensors and radar sensors.
The extension will need to incorporate illumination conditions, cloud sensitivity, viewing geometry, spatial resolution,
and radar imaging modes. Another direction is dynamic target tracking. That problem requires joint treatment of target
motion, state uncertainty, revisit demand, closed-loop replanning, payload assignment, and relay or downlink resource
allocation.
