Zero Cancellation and Equation Structure in
Kiselman’s Semigroup
Luka Andrenšek
Abstract
We investigate equations in Kiselman’s semigroup Kn, generated by a1, . . . , an.
Let f denote the zero element of Kn. We prove that if y ∈Kn lies in the
subsemigroup generated by a2, . . . , an, then xy = f implies x = f. In contrast,
the equation xa1 = f admits non-trivial solutions. We describe the solution set
of this equation, show that its cardinality is 1 + |Kn−1|, and study its algebraic
structure. Moreover, we show that |K2n+1| is even, whereas |K2n| is odd.
Keywords: Kiselman’s semigroup, canonical words, zero element, cancellation,
equation, parity

4 Zero Cancellation Theorem
In this section, we prove the Zero Cancellation Theorem, which we now state.
Theorem 4.1 (Zero Cancellation Theorem). Let x, y ∈Kn satisfy xy = f.
(i) If c(y) ⊆{2, 3, . . ., n}, then x = f.
(ii) If c(x) ⊆{1, 2, . . . , n −1}, then y = f.
The proof proceeds as follows. We ﬁrst recall the notion of a quasi-subword. We
then establish several technical results concerning canonical forms, exploiting the key
role of the letter a1, and use these to prove the Zero Cancellation Theorem.
Deﬁnition 4.2. Let w ∈W(A) be a word. Write w = ai1ai2 . . . aik. A word of the
form v = aij1aij2 . . . aijl, where 1 ≤j1 < j2 < · · · < jl ≤k, is called a quasi-subword
of w. In this case, we write v ≤w.
It follows immediately from the deﬁnition that if u ≤v and v ≤w, then u ≤w.
Moreover, we have w ≤w for every word w ∈W(A).
Lemma 4.3. For every w ∈W(A), we have can(w) ≤w.
Proof If w = can(w), the statement follows from w ≤w. Otherwise, there exist k ≥1
and words v1, . . . , vk−1 ∈W (A) such that w →v1 →· · · →vk−1 →can(w). Since ≤is
transitive, it suﬃces to show that for any u, u′ ∈W (A), if u →u′, then u′ ≤u. This follows
immediately from the deﬁnition of →.
□
Remark 4.4. Let w ∈W(A). If the letter ai occurs in can(w), then ai ≤can(w) ≤w,
which implies that ai ≤w. Therefore, ai occurs in w.
