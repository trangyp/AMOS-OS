Abstract
We propose a unified probabilistic framework for sparse count tensors with excess
zeros, motivated by single-cell Hi-C data. The observed data are naturally represented
as a three-way tensor indexed by genomic loci pairs and cells, exhibiting pronounced
sparsity, zero inflation, and cell-to-cell heterogeneity.
We introduce a zero-inflated
Poisson tensor model that integrates low-rank CP structure, cluster-specific latent
embeddings, and smoothness along ordered genomic loci, thereby jointly capturing
multiway dependence, heterogeneity, and structured variation.
We develop a Bayes-optimal procedure for distinguishing structural from techni-
cal zeros, enabling principled inference and uncertainty quantification. We establish
identifiability of the model parameters and derive consistency rates for the proposed
estimators in a high-dimensional regime. Simulation studies and analyses of single-cell
Hi-C data demonstrate improved performance in zero detection, latent structure recov-
ery, and downstream tasks such as clustering and 3D chromatin organization inference.
The proposed framework provides a flexible approach for multiway count data with
excess zeros and structured dependencies, and suggests several directions for future
work, including mixture-based modeling of cell populations and scalable computation
for large-scale applications.
Keywords: Counting Tensors, Zero-inflation, False Zero Detection, Imputation

Introduction
High-dimensional count data with excess zeros arise in many modern applications, including
single-cell genomics (Nagano et al., 2017; Pierson and Yau, 2015), ecology (Martin et al.,
2005; Zuur et al., 2009), and recommender systems with implicit feedback (Hu et al., 2008).
Understanding the mechanisms generating excess zeros is essential for recovering the under-
lying signal, since observed zeros may arise from multiple sources, including true structural
absence, technical limitations, detection failure, and measurement noise (Hicks et al., 2018).
A substantial literature has addressed zero inflation in lower-dimensional settings through
hurdle and zero-inflated models that explicitly separate structural and sampling zeros (Mul-
lahy, 1986; Lambert, 1992; Cameron and Trivedi, 2013). These approaches are well estab-
lished for univariate and low-dimensional count data. In parallel, tensor methodology has
emerged as a flexible framework for modeling multiway data via low-rank representations
(Kolda and Bader, 2009; De Lathauwer et al., 2000). However, existing tensor decomposition
methods typically do not accommodate excess zeros, while classical zero-inflated models do
not naturally extend to tensor-valued observations. Bridging these two literatures is therefore
essential for analyzing modern multiway count data.
In this paper, we study inference for sparse count tensors under zero inflation as a pri-
mary modeling challenge. We propose a zero-inflated Poisson tensor model in which both
the intensity and masking probability tensors admit low-rank structure. Beyond zero infla-
tion, the proposed framework accommodates additional structures commonly encountered
in multiway data. In particular, different modes may exhibit distinct forms of organization:
one mode may index heterogeneous units with latent group structure, while another may
correspond to an ordered domain along which the underlying signal varies smoothly. These
components are incorporated through structured latent representations that jointly capture
heterogeneity across units and smooth variation along ordered modes, yielding a flexible

model for complex tensor-valued count data.
A key challenge is that existing approaches address these components in isolation. Zero-
inflated regression models accommodate excess zeros but assume independent observations
and do not extend to tensor-valued data (Mullahy, 1986; Lambert, 1992). Tensor decom-
position methods capture low-rank multiway dependence but typically rely on standard
likelihood models that do not account for zero inflation (Kolda and Bader, 2009). At the
same time, probabilistic tensor models generally impose a shared distributional form across
slices or modes, limiting their ability to capture heterogeneity across subpopulations (Hoff,
2011). Smoothing methods for ordered or spatial domains are well developed in classical
regression settings (Wood, 2017), but are largely disconnected from tensor factorization and
zero-inflated frameworks. Consequently, a unified approach that simultaneously accommo-
dates zero inflation, low-rank multiway dependence, heterogeneity, and smooth structure
remains lacking.
Motivating application
A key application motivating this work is single-cell chromatin
contact data obtained via Hi-C technology (Lieberman-Aiden et al., 2009; Rao et al., 2014).
Hi-C is a genomic assay that measures the frequency of spatial proximity between pairs of
genomic loci and is widely used to reconstruct three-dimensional (3D) chromatin organization
(Duan et al. (2010); Hu et al. (2013) and, more recently, Tuzhilina et al. (2022, 2024)),
with broad applications to gene regulation, chromosome architecture, and disease studies.
The output of Hi-C is a symmetric, integer-valued contact matrix whose entries record the
observed interaction frequencies between pairs of genomic loci, with higher values typically
interpreted as indicating closer spatial proximity.
Early work focused on bulk Hi-C data, which aggregate signals across many cells into
a single contact matrix whose entries represent interaction frequencies at the population
level. More recently, single-cell Hi-C technologies enable measurements at the level of in-
3


## Page 4


dividual cells, yielding a collection of contact matrices that capture cell-to-cell variability
in chromatin organization (Nagano et al., 2017; Stevens et al., 2017; Ramani et al., 2017).
These data naturally form a three-way tensor (genomic loci × genomic loci × cell). While
enabling characterization of cell-to-cell heterogeneity in chromatin organization, single-cell
measurements also introduce substantial statistical challenges.
1. Extreme sparsity: Single-cell contact matrices are highly sparse due to both biolog-
ical absence of contacts and technical limitations such as low sequencing depth and
incomplete capture efficiency. Disentangling structural zeros (true absence of contact)
from technical zeros (dropout events) is essential for downstream tasks such as 3D
reconstruction. Failure to distinguish these sources can bias inference, while treating
all zeros as missing discards informative structural signal.
2. Cell-to-cell heterogeneity: Chromatin organization exhibits variability across cells.
Statistical methods must therefore accommodate population heterogeneity while bor-
rowing strength across cells through shared structure.
3. Smoothness along the genome: Genomic loci are ordered along chromosomes, and
neighboring loci tend to exhibit similar interaction patterns due to the continuous
nature of chromatin. Leveraging this smoothness can improve signal recovery.
Existing methods address these challenges in isolation. HiCImpute (Xie et al., 2022)
distinguishes structural zeros from technical dropouts via a Bayesian hierarchical model.
scHiCSRS (Xu et al., 2022) combines self-representation smoothing with mixture modeling
for zero identification. Higashi (Zhang et al., 2022) employs hypergraph neural networks
to learn cell embeddings and impute contact maps by leveraging correlations across cells.
However, these approaches lack a unified probabilistic framework that exploits the inherent
tensor structure of the data and jointly models zero inflation, cell-to-cell heterogeneity, and
smoothness along the genomic loci.

Proposed framework and main contributions.
We propose Zero-Inflated Tensor model
with Smoothing (ZITS), a likelihood-based framework for sparse count tensors integrating
three components: (i) zero-inflated Poisson model with entry-specific masking probabilities
to explicitly separate technical and structural zeros; (ii) low-rank CP tensor decomposi-
tion with heterogeneous structure across one mode via latent grouping; and (iii) functional
smoothing along ordered modes via basis expansion.
1. Theoretical contributions: We establish identifiability conditions for the model pa-
rameters and derive consistency rates for the maximum likelihood estimator. Notably,
despite O(K) parameter growth with sample size, consistent estimation is achievable
through the block structure constraint, providing theoretical justification for the model
design.
2. Methodological contributions: We develop a likelihood-based estimation algorithm
and propose a Bayes-optimal classifier for distinguishing structural from technical zeros,
enabling principled zero-inflation detection with posterior uncertainty quantification.
The unified probabilistic framework allows joint inference over all model components.
3. Empirical validation: We demonstrate effectiveness through simulation studies and
real single-cell Hi-C data, showing improved signal recovery, enhanced zero detection,
and more accurate latent representations compared to existing approaches.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We begin in Section 2 by introduc-
ing the proposed model, followed by Section 3, which develops the estimation and inference
procedures. The theoretical properties are studied in Section 4, where we establish identi-
fiability and consistency and introduce the zero classification and imputation method. We
then assess the performance of the approach through simulation studies in Section 5 and
illustrate its practical utility on real single-cell Hi-C data in Section 6. Section 7 discusses
limitations and extensions.

Let C = (Ci,j,k) ∈RN×N×K be a Hi-C contact tensor on N genomic loci among K cells.
Precisely, Ci,j,k records the contact counts between genomic loci i and j in cell k, for any
i, j ∈[N], and k ∈[K]. In practice, due to insufficient sequencing depth and measurement
errors, many contact counts will be inevitably and randomly masked by zeros, leading to
a great among of false zero contact counts and hence a sparse pattern of the observational
Hi-C tensor (Xie et al., 2022). Moreover, heterogeneity is a ubiquitous phenomenon for Hi-C
data. For example, the chromatin conformations could be substantially different from cells
in one tissue. These two unique phenomena render the challenges of intensity estimation
and downstream analysis for the Hi-C data. To circumvent these difficulties, we propose a
data-driven zero-inflated tensor model with smoothing (ZITS) for the Hi-C tensor in several
steps.

potential false zero observations. Specifically, we assume that for any i < j ∈[N], and

Real Data Analysis
We analyze human’s single-cell Hi-C data from Ramani et al. (2017) at 10 Mb resolution,
focusing on chromosomes 1 to 4, each containing more than 20 loci at this resolution. The 10
Mb bin size is chosen to mitigate extreme sparsity, which can exceed 95% at finer resolutions.
The original dataset consists of four cell types: GM12878 , HAP1 , HeLa, and K562. To
alleviate severe class imbalance, we exclude GM12878 due to its small sample size and
randomly subsample 48 cells from each of HAP1 and HeLa. Together with the 48 K562
cells, this yields a balanced dataset of K = 144 cells across R = 3 cell types. This sampling
procedure and all the following experiments would be repeated 10 times to avoid potential
sampling bias. For each contact matrix, the largest two diagonals are set to zero to reduce
diagonal dominance, which is a common treatment in analyzing human’s single-cell Hi-C
data. In our analysis, we choose cubic splines to model the smoothness of the chromatin and
the spline basis dimension is fixed at Q = 5 throughout.
We evaluate whether imputation improves cell type separability, a standard benchmark
in single-cell Hi-C analysis. Specifically, we compute the adjusted Rand index (ARI) ob-
tained from k-means clustering with R = 3 clusters. Clustering is performed on the first 20
principal components of the upper-triangular entries of either the original contact tensor C
or its imputed counterpart. We additionally assess clustering based on the estimated cell
embeddings ˆβ and ˆξ. All results are averaged over 10 independent random subsamples from
HAP1 and HeLa cells.

the mean ARI across 10 repeated experiments with different subsamples. As shown in the
left panel of Figure 4, clustering on the raw tensor consistently yields lower ARI values than
clustering on the imputed tensor, demonstrating improved separability after imputation. We
also note that embeddings produced by ZITS based on the Poisson intensity factor ˆβ, exhibit

Conclusion
In this paper, we developed a unified probabilistic framework for sparse count tensors with
excess zeros. The proposed approach integrates a zero-inflated Poisson observation model,
low-rank CP tensor decomposition, cluster-specific latent embeddings to capture hetero-
geneity, and functional smoothing along ordered modes. This integration provides a flexible
framework for addressing key challenges in modern multiway count data.
We develop a Bayes-optimal classifier for distinguishing structural from technical zeros,
enabling principled inference on zero inflation and uncertainty quantification. We estab-
lish identifiability of the model parameters and derive consistency rates for the proposed
estimators. Simulation studies and analyses of single-cell Hi-C data demonstrate improved
performance in signal recovery, zero detection, and latent structure inference compared to
existing methods.
Although motivated by chromatin contact data, the proposed framework extends natu-
rally to other multiway count data with zero inflation, heterogeneity, and smooth structure,
such as ecological data indexed by species, location, time, and environmental conditions.
Several directions remain for future work. First, while heterogeneity is currently captured
via latent embeddings and post hoc clustering, a more principled approach would model
cluster membership directly through mixture models for tensor-valued data, enabling explicit
probabilistic characterization of subpopulations. Second, scaling the method to ultra-high-
dimensional settings such as high-resolution Hi-C data may require distributed optimization
and parallel computation.
Finally, extending the framework to higher-order tensors and
developing data-driven procedures for selecting model complexity, such as the number of
latent factors, are important directions for future investigation.
