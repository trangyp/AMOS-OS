Wideband Illumination with Liquid Crystal
Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces: Modeling,
Design, and Experimental Tests
Mohamadreza Delbari , Robin Neuder , Alejandro Jiménez-Sáez , Qikai Zhou , and Vahid Jamali
Abstract—Despite their advantages in scalability and energy
efficiency for realizing extremely large reconfigurable intelligent
surfaces (RISs), liquid crystal (LC) elements exhibit frequency-
dependent phase-shift characteristics. Ignoring this inherent
property can severely impact the overall system performance,
particularly in wideband scenarios. This challenge is particularly
problematic for physical layer security, as even slight frequency-
induced phase deviations can cause significant unintended signal
leakage. This paper addresses these frequency-induced variations
by developing a physics-based model for an LC unit cell across
varying frequencies and proposing a novel phase-shift design
framework that maximizes secure communication across all
subcarriers. Furthermore, given the large number of elements in
millimeter wave (mmWave) LC-RISs, acquiring full channel state
information (CSI) is often impractical. Therefore, we optimize
the phase shifts based solely on the locations of the legitimate
mobile users (MUs) and potential eavesdroppers. Additionally,
we optimize the RIS to illuminate a specific spatial zone instead
of focusing on a precise target point. This area illumination
strategy improves robustness against inaccurate positioning
data for both the MUs and eavesdroppers, and reduces the
overhead associated with frequent re-acquisition of positioning
data. To solve the formulated problem, we introduce both a
semi-definite programming (SDP)-based solution and a low-
complexity heuristic method. While the SDP-based approach yields
superior performance, it incurs higher computational complexity.
Conversely, the scalable method exhibits a much slower scaling
of complexity with respect to the number of RIS elements, which
makes it highly suitable for extremely large RISs. Our simulation
results demonstrate that both proposed algorithms improve the
secrecy rate compared to baseline methods that neglect frequency-
dependent effects. In our considered setup operating at 60 GHz,
the proposed SDP-based and low-complexity frameworks achieve

I. INTRODUCTION
Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) have emerged as
a promising technology for next-generation wireless networks
by enabling highly programmable propagation environments
[2]–[5]. Recently, liquid crystal (LC) technology has gained
attraction as a scalable and energy-efficient solution for deploy-
ing large-scale RISs, particularly at millimeter wave (mmWave)
frequencies [6]–[8]. The literature has extensively investigated
LC-based RISs from both theoretical and practical perspectives.
For example, [6] demonstrated a compact hardware prototype,
while [7] evaluated its potential in visible light communications.
System-level metrics, including cost and energy efficiency,
were benchmarked against competing technologies in [9].
Furthermore, recent works have modeled the temporal transition
behaviors of LCs in time-division multiple-access setups [8],
[10] and analyzed their thermal dependencies [11], [12]. In
addition, [13], [14] discussed and analyzed the loss model of
LC-RIS. Together, these studies highlight a growing momentum
toward refining the practical viability of LC-driven RISs.
The phase-shift response of LC-based RISs is fundamentally
frequency-dependent [6], which can cause severe performance
bottlenecks in wideband deployments. Existing literature ad-
dressing wideband challenges includes near-field beamforming
[15], [16], advanced signal processing [17], and novel system
architectures [18]–[20] to mitigate beam splitting, which all rely
predominantly on idealized RIS models. These studies typically
assume that phase shifts are either frequency-independent [16]–
[20] or perfectly adjustable per subcarrier [15]. Furthermore,
even approaches utilizing more realistic hardware models [21]
generally depend on the restrictive assumption of perfect user
location data for beamforming.

Physical layer secrecy in RIS-assisted systems has been ex-

studies have investigated the maximization of weighted secrecy
sum-rates in generic multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)
wiretap settings using fractional programming and alternating
optimization [23], as well as leveraging artificial noise alongside
RIS phase shifts to further degrade eavesdropper reception
[24]. Hardware-constrained scenarios have also been addressed,
such as mmWave systems employing low-resolution digital-to-
analog converters, where algorithms based on successive convex
approximation and block coordinate descent are used to mitigate
hardware losses and optimize phase shifts [25]. Furthermore,
robust designs have been developed to tackle the practical
challenges of imperfect channel state information (CSI) in
multi-user and multi-eavesdropper settings, utilizing methods
like semi-definite programming (SDP) combined with iterative
hybrid optimization algorithms to maintain high confidentiality
rates despite channel estimation errors [26].
In contrast, this paper tackles the phase-shift design problem
under the practical hardware constraints of LC-RISs, oper-
ating without precise knowledge of legitimate mobile users
(MUs) and eavesdropper locations. Specifically, we propose a
secure illumination strategy that maximizes signal strength
within a target zone occupied by legitimate MUs, while
actively suppressing power in surrounding areas vulnerable
to eavesdropping. By explicitly incorporating the frequency-
dependent response of individual LC-RIS elements into a novel
optimization framework, we bridge the gap between idealized
wideband beamforming theory and practical, secure spatial
coverage1. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the
first study to investigate the frequency-dependent characteristics
of LC-RISs within a wideband context. Our main contributions
are summarized as follows:
• Physics-based Modeling of LC-RIS Phase-shift over
Different Frequencies: First, we develop a rigorous
physical model characterizing how frequency variations
dictate the phase-shift response of LC-RIS elements. This
model characterizes the phase shift induced at a given

• Problem Formulation: Next, we introduce a realistic
physical layer security framework including multiple

the exact locations and instantaneous CSI of all parties

data. This formulation reflects the practical challenges
of securing RIS-aided communications in real-world
environments where users are movable and the location
estimation errors are unavoidable. Then, we formulate a
non-convex optimization problem to jointly design the
LC-RIS phase shifts while accounting for their hardware-
specific frequency dependencies.

C. Frequency Response of an LC Cell
This subsection details the physical mechanism by which LC
molecules impart a phase shift onto incident electromagnetic
waves, as well as the frequency-dependent nature of this re-
sponse. LC-based RISs control signal reflections by leveraging
the tunable electromagnetic characteristics of LC molecules,
whose spatial orientation can be dynamically altered via an
external biasing voltage [9]. This voltage-driven reorientation
directly changes the effective permittivity of the LC medium,
which in turn dictates the phase shift generated by each
individual RIS element.

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS
In this paper, we studied the effect of frequency variations
on the phase-shift response of LC-RIS elements. To address
the inherent frequency dependency in wideband systems, we
proposed two novel algorithms that explicitly account for
this behavior in the phase-shift design, with the objective of
maximizing the secrecy rate within a targeted spatial area. The
first algorithm leverages SDP, while the second employs a
surrogate function approach, which successfully reduces the
computational complexity compared to the SDP-based method.

Extensive simulation results confirmed the critical importance
of incorporating the frequency-dependent characteristics of
LC-RIS elements, demonstrating significant performance gains
over baseline approaches that neglect this factor. Specifically,
the proposed SDP-based and scalable methods achieved secrecy
rates of approximately 2 and 1 bits/symbol, respectively, over an
8 GHz bandwidth at a center frequency of 60 GHz. Furthermore,
our experimental validations clearly demonstrated that the
proposed framework effectively enhances the received power in
practical scenarios compared to existing benchmark schemes.
