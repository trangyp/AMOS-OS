WEIGHTED MIXED-NORM ESTIMATES FOR CIRCULAR AVERAGES
AND EXCEPTIONAL SET ESTIMATES FOR THE WAVE EQUATION
YIXUAN PANG AND CHENJIAN WANG
Abstract. We prove mixed-norm estimates for circular averages with respect to α-dimensional
fractal measures on R2, using circle tangency bounds when α P p0, 1s and a δ-discretized slicing
lemma for fractals when α P p1, 2s. The former estimate is sharp, while the latter improves previous
results for α P p 3
2, 2s.
These estimates can be viewed as X-ray-type extensions of Wolff’s and
Bourgain’s circular maximal functions. As applications, we obtain new exceptional set estimates
for the radial integrability of functions in Lebesgue spaces, as well as for the Hölder regularity in
time of solutions to the linear wave equation on R2. The latter results are the first of their kind.

6.4. Historical remarks. In this final subsection, we will provide additional historical remarks
and discuss possible further directions regarding the regularity of the wave equations in time t, or
the closely related An in radius r for general n ě 2.
We have mentioned that our Theorem 1.6 on Hölder regularity, although only for n “ 2, is the
first of its kind in the literature. Possible reasons are that classical Fourier analytic techniques do
not work very well in the weighted mixed-norm setting, and if n ě 3, then even the well-posedness
of the problem might fail. For example, when n “ 3, by the well-known Kirchhoff’s formula, the
solution of the wave equation is represented exactly via A3 due to the Huygens property, and so
Abel-type regularity-improving results like Proposition 6.1 are no longer available. One cannot
expect upx, tq to be jointly continuous in both x and t if we only have h P LppR3q, and so it is
subtle to even formulate the problem of bounding exceptional sets for Hölder regularity in t.
Historically, similar issues also happens in the study of divergence sets of dispersive equations
mentioned in Section 1.1. A typical way [1] of remedying this is to fix one multiplier representation
of u, and interpret pointwise convergence to the initial data as up to a null set. In Theorem 1.6,
we do not need to worry about choosing a specific interpretation of weak solutions due to the
continuity in Section 6.1, which is kind of lucky as our main results are in the admissible regime
p ą 2. In other words, everything is unique and deterministic at least in R2, which is physically
satisfactory to see.

mensions if we allow h to lie in Sobolev spaces Lp
