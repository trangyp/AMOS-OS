OF 2-NILPOTENT GROUPS
ALESSANDRO DIOGUARDI BURGIO
, MANUEL MANCINI
,
AND TIM VAN DER LINDEN
Abstract. In this article, we investigate the representability of actions of
the category Nil2pGrpq of 2-nilpotent groups. We first provide an algebraic
characterisation of derived actions in Nil2pGrpq by determining a universal strict
general actor of an object X, which turns out to be the group AutcpXq of central
automorphisms of X. We also characterise the morphisms B Ñ AutcpXq that
define an action of B on X in Nil2pGrpq.
We then show that Nil2pGrpq is not action representable, and that the
existence of a weak representation is related to the amalgamation property.
Using the construction of an amalgam of a suitable family of abelian subgroups
of AutcpXq, we prove that the category Nil2pGrpq is weakly action representable,
and that a weak representing object can be chosen to be an abelian group.
Finally, we show that Nil2pGrpq is not locally algebraically cartesian closed.
Introduction
Internal actions were defined in [2] by F. Borceux, G. Janelidze and G. M. Kelly
in order to extend the correspondence between actions and split extensions from
the category of groups to arbitrary semi-abelian categories [23]. In some of those
categories, internal actions are exceptionally well behaved, in the sense that the
actions on each object X are representable. More explicitly, this means that there
exists an object rXs such that the functor
Actp´, Xq – SplExtp´, Xq,
sending an object B to the set of actions of B on X (equivalently, to the set of
isomorphism classes of split extensions of B by X), is naturally isomorphic to the
functor Homp´, rXsq.
The notion of action representability in a semi-abelian category was further
investigated in [3], where it is shown there that the category of commutative
associative algebras over a field is not action representable. Subsequently, it was
proved in [15] that, over an infinite field of characteristic different from 2, the only
action representable varieties of non-associative algebras are the category Lie of Lie
algebras and the category AbAlg of abelian algebras. The strength of this notion
naturally motivated the introduction of weaker related concepts.
In [6], D. Bourn and G. Janelidze introduced the notion of an action accessible
category so as to encompass relevant examples that do not fit into the frame of
action representable categories, such as rings, associative algebras, and Leibniz
algebras [24]. It was later proved by A. Montoli in [26] that every Orzech category
of interest [28] is action accessible. Moreover, in [8], the authors showed that every
Orzech category of interest admits a weaker form of representing object, called the
universal strict general actor (USGA).

More recently, G. Janelidze introduced in [21] the notion of a weakly action
representable category. Rather than requiring that, for every object X of a semi-
abelian category C , there exist an object rXs and a natural isomorphism
Actp´, Xq – HomC p´, rXsq,
one only asks for the existence of an object T “ TpXq of C together with a natural
monomorphism of functors
Actp´, Xq ↣HomC p´, Tq.
Such an object T is called a weak representing object of X. Examples of weakly
action representable categories include the category of associative algebras [21] and
the category of Leibniz algebras [10].
The notion of weak action representability was further investigated in [7] in the
setting of operadic varieties of non-associative algebras over a field. In that work, the
authors exhibited new examples of weakly action representable categories, including
the variety of commutative associative algebras and the varieties of 2-nilpotent
(commutative and anti-commutative) algebras.
Another important observation made by G. Janelidze in [21] is that every weakly
action representable category is action accessible. The converse, however, does not
hold. Indeed, J. R. A. Gray observed in [20, Proposition 2.2] that weak action
representability is closely related to the existence of a so-called amalgam [22], a notion
that had already appeared in [3] in connection with action representability, and he
proved that the varieties of n-solvable groups are not weakly action representable,
for any n ě 3. This result was later extended in [12] to the varieties of 2-solvable
groups and of k-nilpotent groups, for any k ě 3. There, the authors also proved
that the varieties of n-solvable Lie algebras (n ě 2) and k-nilpotent Lie algebras
(k ě 3) fail to be weakly action representable. These are the first examples of
action accessible varieties of non-associative algebras which are not weakly action
representable.
The main goal of this article is to investigate the representability of actions in the
category Nil2pGrpq of 2-nilpotent groups. After recalling the necessary background
in Section 1, we give an algebraic characterisation of (external) actions in Nil2pGrpq.
More precisely, we characterise the actions of a 2-nilpotent group B on another
2-nilpotent group X such that the corresponding semidirect product B ˙ X is still
2-nilpotent.
As a consequence, we obtain an explicit description of the universal strict general
actor of X, which turns out to be the group AutcpXq of central automorphisms
of X. We also provide a complete characterisation of the acting morphism, that is,
of those group homomorphisms B Ñ AutcpXq that define an action of B on X in
the category Nil2pGrpq.
In Section 3, we investigate weak action representability in the category Nil2pGrpq.
We first prove in Proposition 3.1 that Nil2pGrpq is not action representable. Then,
similarly to what was done in [7] for the variety of commutative associative algebras,
we show that, for a 2-nilpotent group X, the existence of a weak representation for
the actions on X is closely related to the so-called amalgamation property (AP) [22].
Finally, by constructing the amalgam of a suitable family of abelian subgroups of
AutcpXq, we prove that Nil2pGrpq is weakly action representable, and that as a weak
representing object of X we may choose an abelian group. This result is analogous
to the case of 2-nilpotent Lie algebras. Indeed, it was shown in [7, Theorem 2.21]
that the variety of 2-nilpotent Lie algebras over a field F with charpFq ‰ 2 is weakly
action representable, with a weak representing object of an object X being an
abelian algebra.

Definition 1.2. [3] A semi-abelian category C is said to be action representable if
for any object X in C , the functor SplExtp´, Xq is representable. This means that
there exists an object rXs of C and a natural isomorphism of functors
SplExtp´, Xq – HomC p´, rXsq.
The prototype examples of action representable categories are the category Grp
of groups and the category Lie of Lie algebras over a commutative unital ring. In
the first case, every action of B on X is represented by a group homomorphism
B Ñ AutpXq, where AutpXq is the group of automorphisms of X. In the second case,
any action of B on X is represented by a Lie algebra homomorphism B Ñ DerpXq,
where DerpXq is the Lie algebra of derivations of X.
However, the notion of an action representable category has proved to be quite
restrictive: for instance, the authors of [15] showed that, over an infinite field F with
charpFq ‰ 2, the only action representable varieties of non-associative algebras [30]
are the variety of abelian algebras and the variety of Lie algebras.
Remark 1.3. We recall that a semi-abelian category C is action representable if and
only if, for every object X of C , the category SplExtpXq of split extensions in C
with kernel X admits a terminal object of the form

This condition can be relaxed by requiring only that every object of SplExtpXq is
accessible, i.e., it admits a morphism into a subterminal (or faithful) object. Here,
we recall that an object is faithful if there exists at most one morphism into it. This
gives rise to the following notion, introduced by D. Bourn and G. Janelidze in [6] in
order to compute centralisers of normal subobjects and equivalence relations.
Definition 1.4. [6] A semi-abelian category C is action accessible if, for any object
X of C , every object in SplExtpXq is accessible.
The concept of action accessibility makes it possible to include a broader class of
categories that fail to be action representable, such as the category of rings and the
variety of associative algebras.
We observe that, since the existence of a terminal object in SplExtpXq is stronger
than every object being accessible, it follows from Remark 1.3 that
action representability ñ action accessibility.
A different way to weaken action representability is to relax the requirements
on the representing object rXs, rather than the existence of a terminal object in
SplExtpXq. This is the approach adopted in [8], where it was shown that every
Orzech category of interest [28] admits a so-called universal strict general actor
(USGA). It was then proved by A. Montoli in [26] that every Orzech category of
interest is action accessible. Moreover, it was shown in [10] that, for every object X
of an Orzech category of interest C , there exists a natural monomorphism of functors
τ : SplExtp´, Xq ↣HomC 1pUp´q, USGApXqq,
where C 1 is a category containing C as a full subcategory, and U : C Ñ C 1 is the

Another important observation made by G. Janelidze in [21] is that every weakly
action representable category is action accessible. We thus have that
action representability ñ weak action representability ñ action accessibility.
The author ended with an open question: whether reasonably mild conditions may
be found on a semi-abelian category under which the second implication may be
reversed.
This question was addressed by J. R. A. Gray, who showed in [20] that the
notion of weak action representability is related to the so-called amalgamation
property (AP) [22]. Using (AP), he established sufficient conditions under which a
Birkhoff subcategory of an action representable category fails to be weakly action
representable. In particular, he proved that the varieties of n-solvable groups are
not weakly action representable for any n ě 3. This result was later adapted in [12]
to prove that the varieties of k-nilpotent groups (k ě 3), as well as the variety of
2-solvable groups, are not weakly action representable. In the same paper, it was
also shown that the varieties of k-nilpotent Lie algebras (k ě 3) and the varieties of
n-solvable Lie algebras (n ě 2) fail to be weakly action representable. Note, however,
that this is no longer true for k “ 2: indeed, it was proved in [7, Theorem 2.21] that
the variety of 2-nilpotent anti-commutative algebras over a field F with charpFq ‰ 2
is weakly action representable, with a weak representing object on an object X
being an abelian algebra.
In this article, we show that an analogue of Theorem 2.21 of [7] holds for the
variety Nil2pGrpq of 2-nilpotent groups. More precisely, in the next section we provide
an algebraic characterisation of actions in the variety of 2-nilpotent groups, and
we give an explicit description of the universal strict general actor of a 2-nilpotent
group X. We then use this description in Section 3 to prove that Nil2pGrpq is weakly
action representable, and that a weak representing object of X is an abelian group.

3. Weak action representability of 2-nilpotent groups
The result of Theorem 2.8 may be viewed as a particular case of [10, Corollary 4.2].
Indeed, AutcpXq is a universal strict general actor of X in the Orzech category of
interest Nil2pGrpq. However, since AutcpXq is not, in general, 2-nilpotent, it does
not provide a weak representing object for the actions on X in the variety Nil2pGrpq.

4. A note on local algebraic cartesian closedness
We conclude the article with a proof that the variety Nil2pGrpq is not locally
algebraically cartesian closed (LACC). As explained in the introduction, this con-
dition was introduced by J. R. A. Gray in [18] in connection with the concept of
algebraic exponentiation, and it is closely related to the study of internal actions in
semi-abelian categories.
Recall that a semi-abelian category C is said to be locally algebraically cartesian
closed when the change-of-base functors in the fibration of points are left adjoints.
This is equivalent to the condition that for every object B of C , the functor
B5p´q: C Ñ C ,
where for any object X the object B5X is defined as the kernel of the canonical
morphism
ridB, 0s: B ` X Ñ B,
is a left adjoint. As explained in [19], when C is a variety of algebras, this is further
equivalent to the conditions
(1) B5p´q preserves all colimits;
(2) B5p´q preserves binary sums.
Hence, C is (LACC) if and only if, for every pair of objects X and Y of C , the
canonical comparison morphism
rB5 idX, B5 idY s: pB5Xq ` pB5Y q Ñ B5pX ` Y q
(4.1)
is an isomorphism.
Examples of semi-abelian categories satisfying the condition (LACC) include the
category of groups, the variety of Lie algebras over a unital commutative ring [17],
and the category of cocommutative Hopf algebras over a field of characteristic zero,
see [9, 16]. It was then proved in [14] that, among varieties of non-associative
algebras over an infinite field of characteristic different from 2, the only non-abelian
(LACC) variety is the category of Lie algebras. Thus, for varieties of non-associative
algebras, action representability is equivalent to the condition (LACC). On the
other hand, for arbitrary semi-abelian categories this is known to fail in general.
Indeed, the category of Boolean rings is action representable [3] but does not satisfy
(LACC), as explained in [19, Proposition 6.4]. It remains an open problem whether
local algebraic cartesian closedness implies action representability, or whether a
counterexample exists. This makes us wonder what happens in the case of 2-nilpotent
groups. We prove the following result:
Theorem 4.1. The variety Nil2pGrpq is not locally algebraically cartesian closed.
