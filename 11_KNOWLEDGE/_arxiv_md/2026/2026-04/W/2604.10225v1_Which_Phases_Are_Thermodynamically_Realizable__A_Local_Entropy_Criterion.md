WHICH PHASES ARE THERMODYNAMICALLY REALIZABLE? A
LOCAL ENTROPY CRITERION
C. EVANS HEDGES
Abstract. In the variational approach to statistical mechanics, equilibrium states are
the rigorous analogues of thermodynamic phases; the question of which invariant mea-
sures can arise as equilibrium states is therefore the question of which phases are thermo-
dynamically realizable. We prove that for continuous actions of locally compact amenable
groups on compact metrizable spaces with finite topological entropy, an ergodic measure
µ is an equilibrium state for some continuous potential if and only if the entropy map
h is upper semicontinuous at µ; equivalently, the unrealizable phases are exactly those
hidden behind the convex envelope of the free energy.
More generally, the same criterion applies whenever (X, T) has bounded entropy and
embeds as an invariant subsystem of a compact metrizable system.
As a canonical
case, one-point compactification yields a C0-potential realization theorem for locally
compact σ-compact systems, with applications to countable-state Markov shifts. We also
show that the equilibrium-face realization stated by Jenkinson (2006) omits a necessary
continuity hypothesis, exhibiting a counterexample on the full shift, and give the sharp
corrected statement: a weak-∗closed set E of ergodic measures determines an equilibrium
face if and only if h|E is continuous and h is upper semicontinuous at each point of E.
1. Introduction
In the variational formulation of thermodynamic formalism, equilibrium states play the
role of thermodynamic phases: they are the invariant measures that maximize the pressure

A basic question is which phases are thermodynamically realizable, i.e., which invariant

This paper gives a

σ-compact systems, with applications to countable-state Markov shifts.

4.3. Application to Countable State Markov Shifts. We apply Theorem 5 to two-
sided countable-state Markov shifts, where the Z-action by the shift map σ acts by home-
omorphisms. Recall that a two-sided countable-state Markov shift X ⊂NZ is locally com-
pact if and only if its transition matrix is both row-finite and column-finite (each symbol
has finitely many successors and finitely many predecessors). Under this condition, each
cylinder [a]0 = {x ∈X : x0 = a} is compact: the set of allowable symbols at each coordi-
nate k, given x0 = a, is finite (by induction on |k| using the row/column-finiteness), so [a]0
is a closed subset of a product of finite discrete sets, hence compact by Tychonoff. Since
X = S
a∈N[a]0 is a countable union of compact sets, X is σ-compact.
The thermodynamic formalism for countable-state Markov shifts was developed by Fiebig,
Fiebig, and Yuri [4].
Iommi, Todd, and Velozo [6] proved that for any countable-state
Markov shift with finite entropy, h is upper semicontinuous at every ergodic measure. Com-
bining with Theorem 5 we arrive at:
Corollary 6. Let X be a locally compact two-sided countable-state Markov shift with supµ h(µ) <
∞. For every ergodic µ ∈Mσ(X), there exists f ∈C0(X) such that µ is the unique equilib-
rium state for f.
We note here that in independent work, Iommi and Velozo [7] prove that for a transitive
countable Markov shift, every ergodic measure with finite one-cylinder entropy Hµ(B) is
the unique equilibrium measure of a uniformly continuous function; they also show that
measures which are not equilibrium measures for any bounded-above continuous function
are dense. Their argument adapts the global upper semicontinuity framework of Phelps [15,
Chapter 12]. This does not subsume Corollary 6, which requires only local compactness
and produces a C0 potential, but the two results address closely related questions in the
countable-state setting.
Corollary 6 requires local compactness (equivalently, that the transition matrix is both
row-finite and column-finite).
Theorem 4 removes this restriction: any countable-state
Markov shift X ⊂NZ embeds as a shift-invariant subset of (N ∪{∞})Z, which is com-
pact metrizable under the product topology. Therefore, if supµ h(µ) < ∞and h is upper
semicontinuous at an ergodic µ ∈Mσ(X), then µ is the unique equilibrium state for the
restriction of some ϕ ∈C((N ∪{∞})Z) to X. By [6], the upper semicontinuity hypothesis
is automatic when htop(X) < ∞, so the conclusion holds for every ergodic measure on any
countable-state Markov shift with finite entropy, without any local compactness assumption.
