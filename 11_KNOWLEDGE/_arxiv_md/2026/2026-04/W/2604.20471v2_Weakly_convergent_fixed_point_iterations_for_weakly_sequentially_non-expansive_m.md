WEAKLY CONVERGENT FIXED POINT ITERATIONS
FOR WEAKLY SEQUENTIALLY NON-EXPANSIVE MAPPINGS
THOMAS P. WIHLER
Abstract. Fixed point iterations are a fundamental tool in numerical analysis and sci-
entific computing for the approximation of solutions to nonlinear problems. Their con-
vergence is often established via the Banach fixed point theorem, provided that a suit-
able contraction property can be verified. However, such conditions are typically too
restrictive for more complex nonlinear equations that lack key structural features such as
monotonicity or convexity. In this paper, we develop a general framework for the weak
convergence of fixed point iterations based on asymptotic bounds. In particular, we in-
troduce and exploit a weak sequential non-expansiveness property, which is significantly
weaker than the global Lipschitz assumptions commonly employed in this context. This
approach permits us to extend classical convergence results to a broader class of mappings
in general (reflexive) Opial spaces, without relying on additional geometric assumptions
such as uniform convexity.
1. Fixed point iterations in Opial spaces
In practical applications, solutions of nonlinear problems (such as algebraic or differ-
ential equations) are often approximated with the aid of suitable fixed point iteration
schemes, viz.
xn+1 = f(xn),
n ⩾0,
(1)
where f : M →X is a mapping on a (non-empty) subset M of a (real) Banach space X,
equipped with a norm ∥· ∥, and x0 ∈M is a starting value so that xn ∈M for all
n ⩾0 (i.e., f can be iterated for x0). The focus of this contribution is to study the weak
convergence of the above iteration to a fixed point of f, i.e., to the set Fix(f) = {x ∈
M : f(x) = x} of all fixed points of f in M, under asymptotic conditions. To simplify
notation, for a given initial value x0 ∈M, we denote by
Σ(x0) = {xn}n⩾0 = {f n(x0)}n⩾0
(2)
the resulting sequence generated from (1), which, as indicated before, is assumed to be-
long entirely to the set M.
From a theoretical perspective, a convergence analysis for (1) that yields practically
useful results involves three key aspects. These will be outlined in the sequel in suffi-
cient detail to guide interested non-experts into the subject and to briefly indicate a few
historical landmarks.

I. Existence of weakly convergent subsequences. The sequence Σ(x0) ⊂M from
(2) needs to contain a weakly convergent subsequence, with a weak limit x∞∈M, i.e.,
xnk ⇀x∞as k →∞. Evidently, this is guaranteed if the set M is weakly sequentially
compact. In the present paper, by referring to the Eberlein-ˇSmulian theorem, this property
is established by assuming that X is a reflexive Banach space. Indeed, under this condition,
any bounded sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence; moreover, if M is weakly
sequentially closed then x∞∈M. For practical reasons, we consider the following
property:
(M) The set M is non-empty, bounded, (norm) closed, and convex.
This is a useful assumption since, by Mazur’s lemma (which implies that any closed and
convex set is weakly sequentially closed), we observe the following fact:
(⋆) Any set M of type (M) in a reflexive Banach space is weakly sequentially compact.
II. Full convergence. A mechanism that yields the weak convergence of the full se-
quence Σ(x0) to a limit x∞, i.e. xn ⇀x∞as n →∞, is required. In order to address this
point, in the present article, we assume that X is a so-called Opial space; by definition, in
such spaces, any weakly convergent sequence xn ⇀x∞satisfies the Opial condition:
lim inf
n→∞∥xn −x∞∥< lim inf
n→∞∥xn −z∥
∀z ̸= x∞.
(3)
This property, which is an important tool in proving weak convergence of (entire) se-
quences, was discovered by Opial in his seminal paper [Opi67]; we will expand on this
approach in §3.1 of the current work. In particular, Opial proved that (3) holds true in
any Hilbert space, cf. [Opi67, Lem. 1], as well as in uniformly convex Banach spaces with
a weakly continuous duality mapping; this justifies the relevance of (3) in many prac-
tical applications. We also mentioned that, for any separable Banach space, there is an
equivalent norm such that (3) holds true, see [vD82, Thm. 1]. For a characterization of
Opial spaces in the context of certain classes of Banach spaces, we further refer to, e.g.,
in [Sim85, Thm. 2] or [ST16, §3].
III. Convergence of fixed point iterations. Finally, the question of whether the (weak)
limit of the sequence Σ(x0) is a fixed point of f needs to be answered affirmatively.
This usually requires an appropriate continuity assumption on the mapping f, which is
typically expressed in terms of global Lipschitz-type bounds in the literature. For instance,
in the framework of the celebrated Banach fixed point theorem, a uniform contraction
bound
∥f(x) −f(y)∥⩽κ∥x −y∥
∀x, y ∈M,
with a constant κ ∈(0, 1), yields the strong convergence (i.e. in norm) of the iteration (1)
to the unique fixed point of f in M. Various generalizations are available in the litera-
ture, including, e.g., the Boyd-Wong [BW69] or Meir-Keeler [MK69] contraction criteria.
Moreover, under suitable compactness assumptions (on either the set M or the map-
ping f), Edelstein’s theorem [Ede62, Thm. 1] states that the contraction framework can
be extended to so-called weak contractions, viz.
∥f(x) −f(y)∥< ∥x −y∥
∀x, y ∈M,
x ̸= y,
where strong convergence to the (unique) fixed point of f can be retained. We refer to
the monograph [Pat10] for further details on the subject.

the convergence of (1), occurs when f is merely uniformly 1-Lipschitz:

longer be a singleton (as can be seen, e.g., if f is the identity map), and strong conver-
gence of the fixed point iteration is generally unattainable. Yet, under the condition (4),
