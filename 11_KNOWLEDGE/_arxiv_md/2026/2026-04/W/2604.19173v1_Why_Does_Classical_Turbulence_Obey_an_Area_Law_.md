Abstract
In incompressible flow the viscous force is solenoidal, whereas the Madelung trans-
form of a spinless Schr¨odinger equation produces only gradient forces. The two are
orthogonal, so viscosity cannot arise from Hamiltonian quantum mechanics alone; an
open quantum treatment is required. Reducing the N-body density matrix to its one-
body component and closing the dynamics via Born-Markov yields Lindblad jump op-
erators with k2 scattering rates, which we unravel via quantum state diffusion (QSD)
into a norm-preserving stochastic nonlinear Schr¨odinger equation.
Dissipation and
stochastic forcing are not separate ingredients: both come from the same Lindblad
operators, and their amplitudes are locked by the QSD structure.
The Madelung
transform of this equation, under incompressibility, gives a stochastic Navier-Stokes
equation whose viscosity is set by the mean free path and whose noise correlator satis-
fies the fluctuation-dissipation relation by construction, in agreement with the Landau-
Lifshitz framework. The recovery is conditional: the viscous identification holds at the
ensemble level via the vortex decomposition of the velocity field; the single-trajectory
identification remains open. The zeros of the wavefunction carry quantised circulation;
their codimension-2 topology yields the Migdal area law for circulation statistics [1] un-
der a Poisson assumption, here through a different mechanism than the loop-functional
saddle point and verified numerically even in the quantum regime where the de Broglie
length exceeds the Kolmogorov scale.

Introduction
The hydrodynamic interpretation of quantum mechanics [2] recasts the Schr¨odinger equation
as a continuity equation for the probability density and a compressible Euler-type momentum
equation augmented by the quantum potential. It has long served as a conceptual bridge
between quantum and classical mechanics [3, 4, 5], but its implications for classical fluid
dynamics, and turbulence in particular, remain underexplored. We show that introducing
dissipation into the Madelung framework via the Born-Markov approximation recovers the
Navier-Stokes equations with a well-defined kinematic viscosity, under incompressibility and
at the ensemble level via the vortex decomposition of the velocity field. The codimension-2
topology of the wavefunction zeros then yields the Migdal area law for circulation statistics [1,
6, 7, 8] via a topological mechanism distinct from the loop-functional saddle point on which
the law was originally derived.
The Madelung transform ψ = √ρ eiS/ℏidentifies ρ with density and ∇S/m with velocity,
yielding an inviscid, irrotational flow [2, 9, 10, 11]. Previous attempts to add viscosity span
stochastic mechanics [12, 13, 14] (placed on variational foundations [15, 16], with quantisation
subtleties [17]), non-Hermitian Schr¨odinger equations [18], spinor formulations [19, 20], and
quantum computational methods [21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28]. Within the single-component
(spinless) setting, every deterministic route fails: complex kinetic coefficients [18] introduce
spurious mass diffusion and break norm conservation; logarithmic nonlinearities [29] give
uniform friction rather than scale-selective dissipation; and spinor models [19, 20] step outside
the single-particle Lindblad framework. The reason is structural (§2.2): for incompressible
flow ν∇2u is solenoidal, while Madelung forces are gradients, and the two are orthogonal
under the Helmholtz decomposition. Related work has examined the quantum potential
as a Korteweg capillary stress [30], its connection to Fisher information and barotropic
pressure [31], geometric reformulations of the Madelung transform [32], and vortex dynamics
in the perfect-liquid Madelung equations [33]. The present work resolves the obstruction
within the spinless, position-basis framework by unravelling the Lindblad master equation
via QSD, so that dissipation and stochastic forcing emerge jointly from the same operators.
The construction proceeds from quantum to classical. Starting from the N-body Schr¨odinger
equation, we trace out N −1 particles to obtain the one-body density matrix, derive Lind-
blad jump operators with k2 rates via Born-Markov closure and Fermi’s golden rule, unravel
via QSD into a stochastic NLS whose trajectories ψ(x, t) are scalar fields on R3, and apply
the Madelung transform to conditionally recover the Navier-Stokes equations. To the extent
that this recovery holds, standard turbulence results (the K´arm´an-Howarth equation, the
four-fifths law, the k−5/3 spectrum [34, 35, 36]) follow as corollaries of Navier-Stokes, not as
independent contributions of this work.
The defining feature of the construction is that dissipation enters through stochastic
forcing: the Lindblad operators that generate viscous damping also generate the noise, and
the two cannot be tuned independently. The fluctuation-dissipation relation is therefore
a structural consequence of the QSD unravelling rather than an imposed condition, and
the wavefunction representation is preserved throughout.
The construction may inform
future closure modelling, although no closure model is developed here [37, 38]. The need
for stochastic forcing in reduced turbulence models has been argued independently [39]:
deterministic closures fail to capture uncertainty growth in shell models at high Reynolds

2.1
Overview
2.1.1
Derivation Strategy
Vorticity in incompressible flows is carried by codimension-2 objects: point vortices in 2D
and vortex filaments in 3D. As shown in §2.2, viscosity cannot arise from any Hamiltonian
Schr¨odinger equation via the Madelung transform, which forces an open-system description
through the Lindblad master equation.
We derive the k2 scaling of the Lindblad jump operators from microscopic N-body scat-
tering theory (§2.3). Of the available unravellings into stochastic pure-state evolutions, we
adopt quantum state diffusion (QSD): it yields a diffusive stochastic NLS with continuous
trajectories that preserves the norm (§2.4), recovers the Lindblad master equation upon en-
semble averaging (§2.5), and reproduces the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations under

stochastic Navier-Stokes equation whose noise correlator satisfies the fluctuation-dissipation
relation (§2.7).
The remaining sections derive the area law for circulation statistics (§3.2) from the
codimension-2 topology of the wavefunction zeros and verify it numerically, including in
the quantum regime Knq ≳1.
Two physical ideas underlie the construction.
First, viscosity emerges from decoher-
ence: a Born-Markov trace over unresolved degrees of freedom in the N-body system pro-
duces dissipative dynamics, and a dimensional argument links the kinematic viscosity to
the entanglement entropy production rate (§2.3). Second, the zeros of the wavefunction are
codimension-2 vortex cores, providing the topological framework for circulation quantisation
and the area law.

2.1.3
Dimensional Structure of the Correspondence
The vortex-quantum correspondence hinges on the codimension of the vorticity-carrying
structures (Table 1). In 2D (point vortices) and 3D (vortex filaments), the carriers have
codimension 2 and admit a geometric potential—the winding angle θ or the solid angle
Ω—with an associated topological invariant; the framework is complete in both cases. For
codimension-3 point particles in 3D, the geometric bridge fails: no linking number, no area
rule, and the Hamiltonian structure requires spinor wavefunctions. The analytical framework
therefore applies to both 2D and 3D.
The constructive encoding of [28] demonstrates that a sufficiently regular divergence-free
velocity field with quantised circulations [56] can be represented as the Madelung velocity
of a complex scalar field ψ, with vortex filaments as the zero set Z = {x : ψ(x) = 0}; the
method reformulates the encoding as a Hermitian eigenvalue problem solved via a variational
quantum eigensolver. The geometric connection between the wavefunction zeros and the
classical vortex potentials (the winding angle θ in 2D and the solid angle Ωin 3D) is developed
in §3.2.

2.3.2
Reduced One-Body Dynamics: From N Bodies to One
The goal of this subsection is to derive a closed equation for the single-particle reduced density
matrix ˆρ1(t) from the N-body dynamics, by tracing out the other N −1 particles. The re-
duction is performed at the density-matrix level, not the wavefunction level, for two reasons.
First, the N-body wavefunction Ψ(x1, . . . , xN, t) lives on the 3N-dimensional configuration
space and is not a scalar field on R3; a single-argument scalar field ψ(x, t) suitable for the
Madelung transform cannot be obtained by restricting Ψ to one particle. Second, the bath
of N −1 particles enters as a mixed (thermal) state, which has no pure-state representative
in the N-body Hilbert space; only the reduced density matrix admits a clean projection.

part of the pure-state Feshbach construction [65] that is traditionally used to derive Fermi
golden rule rates for single-particle scattering; the two routes yield the same rates, but the
density-matrix version is the correct level of description here.

Discussion
4.1
Robustness and Sensitivity
The spinless, position-basis representation ψ(x, t) = ⟨x|ψ(t)⟩captures the translational de-
grees of freedom that determine shear viscosity in the Chapman-Enskog expansion [59].
Internal molecular modes (rotational, vibrational) enter only through the bulk viscosity ζ
and thermal relaxation, both of which vanish for incompressible isothermal flow; extending
to compressible or non-isothermal conditions would require a multi-component wavefunc-
tion, paralleling the monatomic-to-polyatomic Boltzmann extension [59].
The Doebner-
Goldin classification [29] implies that no deterministic, norm-preserving, gauge-covariant

single-component NLS can produce ν∇2v, so a stochastic resolution is forced within this
framework. When discretised on a grid of N = 2n points, ψ becomes a vector in CN that
coincides formally with an n-qubit statevector, each basis state |j⟩encoding a spatial grid
point.
Two structural ingredients are robust beyond the dilute-gas model used to derive them:
the k2 form of Γ(k) (universal under detailed balance, isotropy, and analyticity, with only
the prefactor c = 2ν dependent on the microscopic potential; §2.3), and the codimension-2
topology of the zero set on which the area law rests (generic for any complex scalar field).
The two model-dependent ingredients — incompressibility (Remark 2.1) and the rank-one
recycling closure (§2.3.6) — leave the ensemble-level predictions (Navier-Stokes recovery,
FDR) invariant but affect the single-trajectory dynamics and hence the vortex statistics.

4.3
Backscatter and Fluctuation-Dissipation Balance
On a single QSD trajectory, the stochastic noise can inject energy into a given mode faster
than the dissipation removes it, transiently amplifying that mode.
This amounts to an
instantaneous negative effective viscosity for that mode at that instant: the mode grows
rather than decays. However, the FDR Sk/γk = 2 guarantees that on average each mode is
damped at rate νk2, so the mean viscosity is always positive.
25


## Page 26


In turbulence, this phenomenon is known as backscatter: transient energy transfer from
small scales to large scales, reversing the forward cascade. Stochastic backscatter models for
large-eddy simulation [99, 100] explicitly incorporate this effect to avoid the excessive dissi-
pation of purely forward-cascade subgrid models. The QSD framework produces backscatter
naturally through the noise term, with the amplitude fixed by the FDR rather than fitted
to data.
In stochastic thermodynamics, the same phenomenon appears as transient violations of
the second law: the fluctuation theorems [101, 102] quantify the probability and duration
of trajectories along which entropy production is instantaneously negative. These are not
pathological; they are required by the underlying time-reversal symmetry of the microscopic
dynamics. The QSD noise-dissipation balance is a specific instance of this structure: the
Lindblad construction guarantees Sk/γk = 2, which is the detailed-balance condition en-
suring that negative-viscosity fluctuations occur at exactly the rate needed to maintain the
noise-dissipation equilibrium.
4.4
Path Forward
The physical water viscosity (ν∗= 283; Appendix A.7) places the system deep in the classical
regime at any Reλ, but the grid cost N ∼283 Reλ is steep. A 2D simulation at Reλ = 10
(N = 3,000, i.e. 9 × 106 grid points) is computationally feasible; 3D simulations at the same
parameters (N 3 ≈2.7 × 1010) are not. The present simulations at ν∗∼O(1) serve as a
validation of the numerical infrastructure; production runs at physical viscosity, targeting
Reλ ≳9 [103] (where structure-function exponents have been argued to saturate at high
Reynolds number [75, 104]) and yielding Knq ≪1, are the subject of ongoing work.
4.5
Open Problems
Single-trajectory viscous identification.
The most significant gap. The viscous term
ν∇2v is identified at the ensemble level by symmetry and matching (Remark 2.1); a deriva-

smooth-region Madelung map produces only longitudinal velocity noise (Prop. 2.4), and the
transverse amplitude required by the Landau-Lifshitz form is identified via structural FDR
rather than derived from the QSD dynamics at vortex zeros.
Bulk vortex statistics.
QSD noise nucleates vortex-antivortex pairs by transversality, but
the bulk nucleation rate is not derived. Whether the rank-one projector closure produces
Poisson-like statistics consistent with the vortex-gas model [72, 73] and the DNS circulation
PDFs [74, 93] is unknown and likely tied to the operator choice; alternative recycling op-
erators sharing the same Γ(k) = 2νk2 would change the single-trajectory predictions while
preserving the master equation.
Quantum regime.
The Born-Markov approximation is well-controlled for all physically
realised classical flows (§2.3.6); the regime Knq ∼1 requires separate analysis. Below the

5
Conclusion
The area law for turbulent circulation statistics was proposed by Migdal in 1994 [1] as an
exact scaling solution of the loop-functional equation, and has since been derived from a dual
one-dimensional fermion chain via complex-trajectory instantons [6, 7, 8] and confirmed
in DNS [74, 93] and in GPE quantum-turbulence simulations [97, 98]. The contribution
of this work is a different microscopic origin story for the same area law: a derivation
chain that begins with the many-body Schr¨odinger equation, traces out N −1 particles
to obtain a one-body density matrix, applies Born-Markov closure to a Lindblad master
equation with Lk =
√
2νk2 ˆΠk, unravels via QSD to a stochastic nonlinear Schr¨odinger
equation, and applies the Madelung transform under incompressibility to recover the Navier-
Stokes equations. Within this chain, three structural features are new relative to the loop-
functional program: viscous damping and stochastic forcing arise jointly from the same
Lindblad operators, so the fluctuation-dissipation relation is automatic rather than imposed;
the wavefunction ψ(x, t) is preserved throughout as the fundamental object; and the area
law follows from the codimension-2 topology of the zero set of ψ via the preimage theorem
and a Poisson assumption on vortex positions, rather than from a saddle point on a loop
functional.
The recovery of Navier-Stokes is conditional on incompressibility and on an
ensemble-level identification of ν∇2v (§4); closing the single-trajectory gap—showing that
QSD vortex dynamics produces νk2 damping of each velocity mode trajectory by trajectory,
not only on average—would lift this to an unconditional result.
