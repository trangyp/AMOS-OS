WICKSTEAD’S CONJECTURE ON POSITIVE PROJECTIONS
AND NON-REPRESENTABLE BANACH LATTICE ALGEBRAS
DAVID MU˜NOZ-LAHOZ
Abstract. Let X be a Dedekind complete Banach lattice, and let P : X →X
be a positive projection for which the largest central operator below P is α idX,
for some α ≥0. Wickstead conjectured that α must either be 0 or 1/n, for some
n ∈N, and proved it for finite-dimensional X. In this paper, we show that
the conjecture holds in general. As a consequence, we settle the representation
problem for Banach lattice algebras: we show that there exist Banach lattice
algebras of dimension 2 that do not admit a faithful representation as regular
operators on any Dedekind complete Banach lattice.
1. Introduction
In [11], A. W. Wickstead formulated the following question. Let X be a Dedekind
complete Banach lattice, and let P : X →X be a positive projection for which there
exists an α ≥0 satisfying:
(i) α idX ≤P, and
(ii) whenever T is a positive operator such that both T ≤λ idX, for some λ ≥0,
and T ≤P, then also T ≤α idX.
Which values can α take? Wickstead conjectured that either α = 0 or α = 1/n, for
some n ∈N. The first main goal of this paper is to prove that this is indeed the
case (Corollary 3.1).
When X is finite-dimensional, conditions (i) and (ii) imply that the diagonal
elements in the matrix representing P are all equal to α. Thus we will refer to
these two conditions by saying that P has constant diagonal α. When X = C(K),
for some compact Hausdorff space K, or X = L1(µ), for some finite measure µ,
that P has constant diagonal α means that the biggest multiplication operator (by
an element of C(K) or of L∞(µ), respectively) below P is α idX.
The motivation behind this question lies in the representation of Banach lattice
algebras.
A Banach lattice algebra is a Banach lattice together with a Banach
algebra structure for which the product of positive elements is positive. One of the
most prominent questions regarding Banach lattice algebras is whether every such
object can be faithfully represented (both as a lattice and as an algebra) in Lr(X),
the space of regular operators on some Dedekind complete Banach lattice X [9].

are α = 0 or α = 1/n, where n is a divisor of the dimension of X. It was left
open whether α can take any other values or not. In Corollary 3.1 we show that it
cannot. The unital two-dimensional examples announced above are then the cones
in Wickstead’s family corresponding to the forbidden values of α.
The result on positive projections will follow from a version of it for positive
projections with constant diagonal on general C(K) spaces (Theorem 2.1). As a
consequence, we will derive a condition guaranteeing that a Banach lattice algebra
does not admit any faithful representation—unital or not—on a space of regular
operators on a Dedekind complete Banach lattice (Proposition 4.3). We will also
generalize Wickstead’s complete description of contractive positive projections with
constant diagonal on finite-dimensional Banach lattices to ℓp(A), for an arbitrary
set A and 1 ≤p < ∞(Proposition 3.2).
1.1. Preliminaries and notation. For a compact Hausdorff space K, we denote
the space of continuous functions on K by C(K) and its dual, which we identify
with the space of all finite signed regular Borel measures on K, by M(K). For
every f ∈C(K) and µ ∈M(K), we denote the dual pairing by ⟨µ, f⟩, whilst the
value of µ at a Borel set B will be denoted by µ(B). The point mass measure at
t ∈K will be denoted by δt. Every f ∈C(K) has an associated multiplication
operator Mf : C(K) →C(K) defined by Mf(g) = fg, for all g ∈C(K).
For the basic theory of vector and Banach lattices, we refer the reader to the
monographs [7, 8]. For the theory of positive operators and related topics, see [1,
2]; in particular, a detailed treatment of positive projections can be found in [1,
Chapter 5].
Now we want to introduce a couple of definitions that are convenient for this
paper and, in order to do so, we have to recall first some basic facts of positive and
regular operators. Let X be a vector lattice. An operator T : X →X is central
if there exists λ > 0 such that ±T ≤λ idX, where idX denotes the identity of X.
The center of X is the set formed by all central operators, and it is denoted by
Z(X). The central operators on C(K) are precisely the multiplication operators.
When X is Dedekind complete, the space of regular operators Lr(X) becomes a

The projection onto this band D: Lr(X) →Lr(X) is usually called the diagonal

Definition 1.1. Let X be a Dedekind complete vector lattice, and let T : X →X
be a regular operator. We say that T has constant diagonal α if D(T) = α idX.
Notice that T having constant diagonal α is the same as α idX being the supre-
mum of all the central operators that lie below T. This is the characterization we
used at the beginning of the introduction.
A lattice-ordered algebra is a vector lattice together with a real algebra struc-
ture for which the product of positive elements is positive.
The monograph [3,
Chapter XVII] and the surveys [4, 6] provide an overview of lattice-ordered rings
and algebras. A Banach lattice algebra is a lattice-ordered algebra together with a
complete lattice norm that is submultiplicative. An introduction to the theory of
Banach lattice algebras can be found in [9].
Definition 1.2. A representation of a lattice-ordered algebra A is a lattice and
algebra homomorphism Φ: A →Lr(X), for some Dedekind complete vector lattice
X. We say that Φ is faithful if it is injective. In the case that A has an algebraic
identity e and Φ(e) = idX, we say that Φ is unital.

4. On the representation of Banach lattice algebras
In this section we use Corollary 3.1 to settle the representation problem for
Banach lattice algebras in the negative. We begin with two preparatory lemmas:
the first records that the range of a positive projection is itself a Dedekind complete
vector lattice, and the second shows that disjointness from a positive idempotent in
Lr(X) becomes disjointness from the identity on its range. These lemmas will let
us apply Corollary 3.1 on the range of an arbitrary positive idempotent of Lr(X).
In the following lemmas, when X is a vector lattice, Y is a sublattice, and S ⊆Y
is a set, we denote by supX S the supremum of S in X (whenever it exists) and by
supY S the supremum of S in Y (whenever it exists).
