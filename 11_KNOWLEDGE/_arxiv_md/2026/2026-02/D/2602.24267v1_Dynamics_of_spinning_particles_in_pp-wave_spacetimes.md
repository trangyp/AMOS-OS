Dynamics of spinning particles in pp-wave spacetimes
K. Andrzejewski∗
Abstract
In this work, we study the dynamics of a spinning particle in pp-waves spacetimes;
in particular, plane gravitational waves and impulsive shockwaves. W pay special at-
tention to analytical considerations; this is possible due to an appropriate choice of
the spin supplementary condition, various Hamiltonian formalisms (including a non-
minimal one) and constants of motion associated with conformal fields. Based on these
results, we establish a relation between the motions of a spinning particle in pp-waves
and electromagnetic fields suggested by a gauge-gravity duality.

Introduction
The motion of the relativistic spinning bodies in external fields has been the subject of
intense studies throughout the years. Recently, such investigations have gained an additional
motivation due to direct observation of gravitational waves, as well as various spin-gravity
phenomena, see e.g. [1]-[20]. In the pole-dipole approximation (the field changes little through
the body) such a motion can be reduced to the reference worldline xα(τ) together with four-
momentum P α and spin tensor Sαβ = −Sβα. These quantities are governed by the Mathisson-
Papapetrou-Dixon-Souriau (MPDS) equations [21]-[25]1

The main problem which appears in the analysis of the motion of the spinning body is

The choice of V is a longstanding problem and various conditions were proposed, see [6]
for a wider discussion and references. This problem is, for example, reflected in the notion
of mass: M 2 ≡−PαP α or m ≡−Pαxα′, which are constant only for some special choices
of the SSC. The oldest and widely studied conditions are: V α = xα′ suggested by Frenkel-
Mathisson-Pirani (FMP) [21, 26, 27], and the Tulczyjew-Dixon (TD) condition defined by
V α = P α/M [23, 24, 28]. Although these conditions have their own physical justifications,
they make the MPDS equations very difficult to solve (especially in an analytical way). On
the other hand, recently Ohashi, Kyrian and Semer´ak (OKS) [29, 30] have proposed a new
condition.

Conformal fields and spinning particles

helpful in the analysis of dynamics. Remarkably, this observation was extended to spinning
particles [38]. A nice example of such a situation is given in Ref. [39] where, for de Sitter
space and an arbitrary spin condition, momentum and spin are explicitly expressed in terms

are the simplest example of such a situation; however, there are spaces where this occurs for

the spinning body. Such a reasoning was applied, e.g., in Ref. [32, 33, 34] where a non-minimal
extension of the Hamiltonian H0 has been proposed. Namely, motivated by the gravitational
counterpart of the Stern-Gerlach force, the spin-spin interaction via space-time curvature has

8
Conclusion and outlook
Let us summarize. In this work we have investigated the dynamics of spinning bodies in
pp-waves spacetimes. Namely, using the OKS condition (alternatively, a suitable Hamiltonian
formalism), we showed that the solution of the equations of motion can be formulated in step-
by-steep algorithm. Moreover, we showed that the vector field defining the OKS condition can
be used to find spin components. These results are particularly efficient for plane gravitational
waves; they provide analytically solutions and enable to find the change of spin after the
passage of the wave. Moreover, in this case, we saw a notable role of integrals of motion
associated with the conformal Killing fields (e.g. homothetic field in the integrability of the
v-coordinate). We also applied our reasoning to the impulsive gravitational shockwaves (in
particular, for the impulsive plane waves this was done by a special choice of the profile
and limiting procedure). For such spacetimes, we found a jump in the velocities and spin
17


## Page 18


dynamics. In the next step, we considered a non-minimal Hamiltonian (extended by the spin-
curvature-spin interaction). In particular, we showed that in the case of plane gravitational
waves, equations can be integrated and the spin components are only shifted by a suitable
change in parameters (if they are properly selected, they significantly simplify equations).
Finally, we showed that the motions of the spinning particle in pp-wave and electromagnetic
field (following from the double copy conjecture) are very similar and the main difference is
in one spin component; this, in turn, is related to a different evolution of the vector field
defining the corresponding spin conditions.
Turning to further developments, it is worth analysing the role of conformal fields for other
examples of pp-waves (see e.g. [41, 49]) and non-minimal Hamiltonians. We can also try to
extend the results obtained to spacetime with non-zero torsion [58] or deflection and lensing
problems [59]. On the other hand, we have seen that the OKS condition is compatible with
a relevant spin condition for charged spinning particle placed in a electromagnetic field. In
view of this it would be interesting to see what happens when we apply the OKS condition
for the Kaluza-Klein metric (in analogously to the FMP case studied in Ref. [60]). Finally,
the analysis of the non-minimal Hamiltonians deserves for further attention, especially the
role of special coupling values discussed here and in Ref. [33].
