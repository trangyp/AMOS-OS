Abstract
Recent multi-agent frameworks have broadened
the ability to tackle oncology decision support
tasks that require reasoning over dynamic, hetero-
geneous patient data. We propose Contribution-
Aware Medical Multi-Agents (CoMMa), a decen-
tralized LLM-agent framework in which special-
ists operate on partitioned evidence and coordi-
nate through a game-theoretic objective for robust
decision-making. In contrast to most agent archi-
tectures relying on stochastic narrative-based rea-
soning, CoMMa utilizes deterministic embedding
projections to approximate contribution-aware
credit assignment. This yields explicit evidence
attribution by estimating each agent’s marginal
utility, producing interpretable and mathemati-
cally grounded decision pathways with improved
stability. Evaluated on diverse oncology bench-
marks, including a real-world multidisciplinary
tumor board dataset, CoMMa achieves higher ac-
curacy and more stable performance than data-
centralized and role-based multi-agents baselines.
Source code will be released upon publication.

Despite their promise, most medical multi-agent frame-
works implement collaboration through role-play, assign-
ing clinician-like personas such as oncologists or radiolo-
gists (Chen et al., 2025b; Kim et al., 2024; Zhang et al.,
2025). This setup often relies on long-form dialogue as the
coordination channel, which increases interaction overhead

centralized, exposing all agents to the same full patient
context. In this case, role specialization is largely seman-
tic rather than structural, and it becomes difficult to isolate
which evidence drives a decision or to assign responsibility
when errors occur. As a result, current frameworks offer lim-

cost, as shown in Figure 1(a) and (b).

Aware Medical Multi-Agents (CoMMa), a framework that
replaces role-based interaction with data-decentralized spe-
cialization. As illustrated in Figure 1(c), CoMMa partitions
the clinical context into distinct information streams and as-
signs each to a dedicated agent, enforcing specialization by
construction rather than by persona simulation. Agent com-
munication is conducted through deterministic embedding
projections instead of natural language dialogue, and the fi-
nal clinical decision is formed by aggregating agent outputs
with learnable contribution weights. This design improves
scalability, reduces inference cost, and preserves clinical
data privacy compared to data-centralized and role-based

influence of specific data modalities.
• We demonstrate that CoMMa outperforms existing frame-
works in predictive accuracy and interpretability across
complex, multidisciplinary oncology scenarios, establish-
ing a new state-of-the-art for agentic clinical decision
support.

2.2. Game Theory in Clinical Decision-Making
Game theory provides mathematical frameworks for model-
ing strategic interactions among rational agents and has been
widely applied in general-domain decision-making, includ-
ing economics, management, defense, and computer science
(Von Neumann & Morgenstern, 2007; Dutang et al., 2013;
Ho et al., 2022; Parsons & Wooldridge, 2002; Lanctot et al.,
2017). Despite its strong theoretical foundations, its adop-
tion in clinical settings remains limited (Mendonc¸a et al.,
2020; Lau et al., 2023), particularly in oncology. Oncology
decision-making relies on heterogeneous and interdepen-
dent evidence sources, where quantifying the contribution of
each component is essential for interpretability and clinical
reliability. Among cooperative game-theoretic approaches,
the Shapley value (Shapley, 1953; Beechey et al., 2023)
provides a principled and axiomatized solution for fair al-
location of a total payoff among participating agents. In
a clinical AI setting, the payoff corresponds to the final
clinical decision or predictive outcome, while each agent
represents a distinct modality or specialized source of clini-
cal information. The Shapley value of an agent is defined
as its expected marginal contribution, averaged over all pos-
sible coalitions, satisfying desirable payoff. To the best of
our knowledge, this work is the first to incorporate Shapley
value–based contribution modeling as a regularization mech-
anism in a multi-agent oncological tumor board framework.

4.4. Baseline Methods and Evaluation Methods
We evaluate our method against three categories of base-
line approaches. For locally trainable baselines optimized
for categorical and structured data, we employ XGBoost
(Chen, 2016), LGBMClassifier (Ke et al., 2017), and Cat-
Boost (Prokhorenkova et al., 2018). These locally trainable
models operate exclusively on structured inputs, therefore,
all clinical information is formatted as tabular features for
these baselines, whereas our model consumes purely textual
input. For non-trainable online in-context learning base-
lines, we use GPT-4.1, which also serves as the backbone
for all non-trainable online multi-agent baselines. From
MDAgents (Kim et al., 2024), we adopt the Basic setting,
where a single agent performs in-context learning without
explicit multi-agent interaction. For in-context learning, the
entire training dataset with paired ground-truth annotations
is provided as context. For non-trainable online multi-agent
baselines, we include MDAgents (Kim et al., 2024) and
MAC (Chen et al., 2025b), which simulates structured tu-
mor board discussions. In MDAgents, the Intermediate
setting induces discussions among five domain-specific spe-
cialists, while the Advanced setting organizes specialists
into multiple tumor board teams and facilitates inter-team
deliberation. In MAC, a single supervisor agent coordinates
discussions among four doctor agents. We follow the for-
matted text prompts provided in the baseline models, with
slight modifications to incorporate task-specific instructions.
For all multi-agent baselines, GPT-4.1 is consistently used
as the underlying agent model to ensure a fair comparison.

On the HCC Tumorboard treatment recommendation task,
CoMMA achieves the strongest overall performance among
all compared methods. As shown in Table 2, CoMMa at-

Compared to traditional machine learning models such

neous clinical signals. Moreover, CoMMA also outperforms
GPT-4.1 in-context learning and prior online multi-agent
baselines, indicating that narrative discussion or retrieval-
augmented prompting alone does not provide sufficiently
reliable coordination for treatment recommendation. In con-
trast, CoMMa optimizes trainable multi-agent collaboration
with a deterministic, contribution-aware credit assignment
objective, explicitly rewarding agents based on marginal
utility rather than stochastic dialogue heuristics. This yields
more stable aggregation of evidence across decentralized
partitions and translates into consistent improvements in
recommendation quality, while providing agent-level attri-

5. Conclusion, Limitations and Future Work
In this study, we proposed CoMMa, a contribution-aware
medical multi-agent framework that that replaces stochastic
dialogue with deterministic specialization and structured
credit assignment. Evaluations on oncology benchmarks
demonstrate that CoMMa significantly improves predic-
tive stability over centralized and narrative-based baselines.
Moreover, CoMMa demonstrates that locally deployed ar-
chitectures can achieve superior performance without rely-
ing on costly cloud-based inference or external data transfer.
This marks a important step toward high-performance clini-
cal AI in resource-constrained, privacy-sensitive settings.
Despite these strengths, our evaluation is currently con-
strained to specific oncology cohorts, reflecting the intrinsic
scarcity and curation challenges of diverse real-world tu-
mor board data. Furthermore, achieving granular reasoning
remains challenging when processing highly clustered in-
formation across modalities. Future research will prioritize
increasing data scale and disentangling data representations
to enhance the resolution and minimize information entan-
glement. In addition, we will continue to address the limita-
tions of the role-based multi-agents by further strengthening
credit assignment principles to better capture the complex
dynamics of oncology decision-making. Finally, the current

A.2.1. SHAPLEY VALUE APPROXIMATION STRATEGY
Exact computation of the Shapley value is computationally expensive, especially as the number of agents N increases.
To achieve a tractable yet accurate approximation, we employ Monte Carlo sampling over M = 2N/2 randomly sampled
marginal contributions, where N denotes the total number of agents. This strategy substantially reduces computational
complexity while preserving the statistical fidelity of the Shapley estimation. Furthermore, although the classical Shapley
value admits both positive and negative contributions, we apply a rectified linear unit (ReLU) operator [·]+ to enforce
non-negativity. This modification ensures that the resulting Shapley estimates form a valid target distribution for the KL
divergence regularization in Equation (10), and that agents are credited exclusively for reducing the task loss.
A.2.2. COMPARISON TO ADVANTAGE VECTOR
Unlike the advantage vector Ai, as introduced in Section 3.3.1, which only considers the marginal impact of removing
agent i from the full coaliations N, Shapley value defined in Equation (12) evaluates the contribution of agent i across a
diverse set of sub-coalitions S ⊆N \ {i}. of varying cardinalities. Consequently, the resulting Shapley matrix Φ captures
an expected marginal contribution that explicitly accounts for inter-agent interactions and dependencies, rather than being
dominated by sample-specific or coalition-dependent fluctuations.
