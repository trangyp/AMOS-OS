Non-Trivial Consensus on Directed
Matrix-Weighted Networks
with Cooperative and Antagonistic Interactions
Tianmu Niu, Bing Mao, Xiaoqun Wu, and Tingwen Huang, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—This paper investigates the non-trivial consensus
problem on directed signed matrix-weighted networks—a novel
convergence state that has remained largely unexplored despite
prior studies on bipartite consensus and trivial consensus. No-
tably, we first prove that for directed signed matrix-weighted
networks, every eigenvalue of the grounded Laplacians has
positive real part under certain conditions. This key finding
ensures the global asymptotic convergence of systems states to
the null spaces of signed matrix-weighted Laplacians, providing
a foundational tool for analyzing dynamics on rooted signed
matrix-weighted networks. To achieve non-trivial consensus, we
propose a systematic approach involving the strategic selection of
informed agents, careful design of external signals, and precise
determination of coupling terms. Crucially, we derive the lower
bounds of the coupling coefficients. Our consensus algorithm
operates under milder connectivity conditions, and does not im-
pose restrictions on whether the network is structurally balanced
or unbalanced. Moreover, the non-trivial consensus state can be
preset arbitrarily as needed. We also carry out the above analysis
for undirected networks, with more relaxed conditions on the
coupling coefficients comparing to the directed case. This paper
further studies non-trivial consensus with switching topologies,
and propose the necessary condition for the convergence of
switching networks. The work in this paper demonstrates that
groups with both cooperative and antagonistic multi-dimensional
interactions can achieve consensus, which was previously deemed
exclusive to fully cooperative groups.
Index Terms—Non-trivial consensus, matrix-weighted net-
works, directed graphs, grounded Laplacian, null space, signed
graphs, fixed/switching togologies, positive-negative path.

However, the above researches are confined to scalar-

including coupled oscillators and multiple-link pendulums

agents, can be seen as matrix weights without restriction of
being positive or negative (semi-)definite [15].
Regarding consensus algorithm on matrix-weighted net-
works, [16] identified a geometric condition called “positive
spanning tree” for reaching consensus on undirected unsigned
matrix-weighted networks. For undirected signed networks,
[17] introduced “balancing set” to reveal relationship between
the network structure and the bipartite consensus solution.
Subsequent works on directed and undirected signed networks
established algebraic and geometric conditions for bipartite
and trivial consensus [18] [19] [20]. Unlike scalar-weighted
networks, structural balance condition alone is not sufficient
for bipartite consensus here due to semi-definite edge weights;
equivalence holds only when all edge weights are definite
matrices [21] [22]. Meanwhile, the algebraic conditions are
concentrated on the null space and spectral properties of
matrix-weighted Laplacian [19] [20] [21] [22]. Recent exten-
sions include privacy-preserving average consensus algorithm
via matrix-weighted coupling [23], randomized gossip-type
matrix-weighted consensus algorithms [24] and the event-
triggered average consensus on matrix-weighted networks
[25], constituting an enrichment to this field.
Non-trivial consensus, introduced in [26], defines a largely
unexplored convergence behavior on signed networks where
agents achieve consensus in both value and sign despite
cooperative-antagonistic interactions. This paradigm carries
significant theoretical value and practical potential—for ex-
ample, in formation control of UAV network [27] and opinion
manipulation within social networks [28] [29] [30]. Trust

and mistrust relationships coexist in social networks, which
commonly leads to opinion separation among countries, po-

manipulation strategy, through which a group of agents that
contains mistrust relationships can be driven to follow one

Existing studies on non-trivial consensus remain limited.
[26] established non-trivial consensus feasibility, albeit only
for (essentially) cooperative networks. [31] derives neces-
sary and sufficient conditions for undirected signed networks,
which requires connectivity and structural balance and offers
no explicit control design. The study in [32] achieves non-
trivial consensus on directed signed networks, covering both
structurally balanced and unbalanced cases. Nevertheless, all
aforementioned results are confined to scalar-weighted frame-
works.
Moreover, network model with fixed topology does not
always catch the trend. Switching network model [2] has been
adopted to capture environmental variability. While uniform
convergence under directed switching topologies has been
analyzed for scalar-weighted signed networks [33], research on
matrix-weighted cases leaves a significant gap—existing works
primarily address undirected switching topologies, covering
consensus [34], cluster consensus [35], distributed optimiza-
tion [36], and privacy-preserving consensus [23], and there is
few conclusion about dynamic behaviors on directed matrix-
weighted networks with switching topologies.
From the above discussion, we first generalize the concept
of non-trivial consensus from the conventional signed scalar-
weighted networks to the matrix-weighted case, given the
latter’s broader modeling generality. Specifically, when a group
of multi-dimensional agents coupled by a signed matrix-
weighted network reach consensus, both on magnitude and on
sign of each component of their state vectors, respectively, then
we say non-trivial consensus on the signed matrix-weighted
network is achieved.
The essential target of this paper is to realize non-trivial
consensus on directed signed matrix-weighted networks, both
for fixed and switching topologies cases. To achieve this,
external input will be exerted, which leads to the emergence
of grounded Laplacians in the framework of signed matrix-
weighted networks. The spectral properties of grounded Lapla-
cians play a key role in ensuring stability and measuring
the convergence speed of consensus algorithms [6] [13] [37]
[38]. In [6], it is proved that when a signed digraph has a
spanning tree, then all the eigenvalues of grounded Laplacian
have positive real parts. Remarkably, in this paper, for directed
signed matrix-weighted networks, the specific conditions un-
der which every eigenvalue of the grounded Laplacians has
positive real part are derived, which is an essentially basic
work for analysing the dynamics of signed matrix-weighted
networks with rooted vertices.
Formally, the main contributions of this paper, along with
comparisons with some existing related works, are summa-
rized as follows.
1) Firstly, for directed signed matrix-weighted networks,
the specific conditions under which every eigenvalue
of the grounded Laplacian has positive real part are
established. Crucially, the lower bounds of the coupling
coefficients between external signal and agents are de-
rived. This serves as a foundational contribution: positive
definiteness of the grounded Laplacian guarantees global
asymptotic convergence of system states to the Laplacian
null space. Our result generalizes the scalar-weighted
case in [6] and is enabled by a novel analytical frame-
work combining network decomposition via positive-
negative paths and the concept of “in-degree-dominated
vertex”—a generalization of the classical “balanced
node” [2]. The above analysis is also carried out for
undirected signed matrix-weighted networks, with more
relaxed conditions on the coupling coefficients.
2) Secondly, non-trivial consensus algorithm on directed
signed matrix-weighted networks with fixed topologies
is developed. This result demonstrates that a group with
both cooperative and antagonistic multi-dimensional in-
teractions can achieve consensus—a capability previ-
ously attributed only to fully cooperative groups. Specifi-
cally, given a desired non-trivial consensus state θ ̸= 0d,
appropriate informed agents are selected, effective cou-
pling coefficients, coupling matrix weights and external
signal are designed to guarantee the convergence of the
agents on a signed matrix-weighted network toward the
shared non-zero state θ. “Non-trivial consensus space”
is introduced to facilitate our analysis, and this concept
makes an equivalent characterization of reaching non-
trivial consensus in the system. Non-trivial consensus
for undirected signed matrix-weighted networks is also
studied. The connectivity conditions in our work are
more relaxed, compared with most of the existing results
on consensus algorithm of matrix-weighted networks,
which often demands positive spanning tree [16] [20] or
positive-negative spanning tree conditions [17] [21] [22].
Notably, our non-trivial consensus algorithm imposes no
restriction on network property of structural balance or
unbalance, thereby enhancing its applicability.
3) Thirdly, non-trivial consensus on directed signed matrix-
weighted networks with switching topologies is studied.
To begin with, a necessary condition for convergence
is derived, emphasizing the significance of null spaces
of those temporary Laplacian matrices appearing for
infinite times. Comparing with the corresponding result
on undirected networks [35], ours is more general,
applying to directed networks without stricter assump-
tions. Utilizing the properties of logarithmic norm, it
is proved that non-trivial consensus remains achievable
under switching topologies. Unlike prior approaches
[34] [35] that rely on finding specific time subsequences,
our protocol dynamically adjusts algorithm parameters
in response to topology switches, offering an explicit
strategy to steer the network toward the desired non-
trivial consensus state.

A signed matrix-weighted communication graph with N
vertices is represented by a triple G = (V, E, A), in which
V = {v1, ..., vN}, E ⊆V×V are the vertices set and the edges
set, respectively. For directed G, (vj, vi) ∈E if and only if

undirected, (vj, vi) ∈E and (vi, vj) ∈E holds simultaneously.
A = [Aij] ∈RNd×Nd is the block adjacency matrix, in

(semi-) definite edge, and sgn(Aij) = −1 if and only if
(vj, vi) ∈E is a negative (semi-) definite edge. When G
is undirected, Aji = Aij for any i, j ∈N, i ̸= j. For

VI. CONCLUSION
In this paper, non-trivial consensus algorithm of directed
signed matrix-weighted networks is established, which facili-
tates the convergence of agents on a signed matrix-weighted
network to a shared non-zero consensus state, and this con-
sensus state can be arbitrarily preset as needed. Remarkably, it
is first proved that under certain conditions, every eigenvalue
of the grounded matrix-weighted Laplacians has positive real
part, and crucially, the lower bounds of the coupling coeffi-
cients between external signal and agents are derived. This
result guarantees that the states of directed signed matrix-
weighted networks with rooted vertices converge to Laplacian
null spaces, and thus serves as one foundational work. The
connectivity conditions in our work is milder than most of
the existing results on matrix-weighted networks’ consensus
algorithm, and no restriction is imposed on network property
of structural balance or unbalance. Both fixed and switching
topologies situations are studied. In the latter scenario, an
explicit strategy featuring dynamic adjustment of algorithm
parameters in response to topology switches is offered. The
necessary condition for the convergence of switching networks
is derived, which highlights the significance of null spaces of
