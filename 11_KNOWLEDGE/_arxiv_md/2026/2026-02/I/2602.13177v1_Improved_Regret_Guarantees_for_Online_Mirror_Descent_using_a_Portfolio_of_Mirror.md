Abstract
Online Mirror Descent (OMD) and its variants give a flexible framework for Online Con-
vex Optimization (OCO) where the performance depends crucially on the choice of the mirror
map. While the geometries underlying Online Projected Gradient Descent (OPGD) and Online
Exponentiated Gradient (OEG), both special cases of OMD, are well understood, it remains a
challenging open question on how to construct an optimal mirror map for any given constrained
set and a general family of loss functions, e.g., sparse losses. Motivated by parameterizing a
near-optimal set of mirror maps, we consider a simpler question: is it even possible to obtain
polynomial gains in regret by using mirror maps for geometries that interpolate between L1 and
L2, which may not be possible by restricting to only OEG (L1) or OPGD (L2).
Our main result answers this question positively. We show that mirror maps based on block
norms adapt better to the sparsity of loss functions, compared to previous Lp (for p ∈[1, 2])
interpolations. In particular, we construct a family of online convex optimization instances in
Rd, where block norm-based mirror maps achieve a provable polynomial (in d) improvement in
regret over OEG and OPGD for sparse loss functions. We then turn to the setting in which the
sparsity level of the loss functions is unknown. In this case, the choice of geometry itself becomes
an online decision problem. We first show that naively switching between OEG and OPGD can
incur linear regret, highlighting the intrinsic difficulty of geometry selection. To overcome this
issue, we propose a meta-algorithm based on multiplicative weights that dynamically selects
among a family of uniform block norms. We show that this approach effectively tunes OMD to
the sparsity of the losses, yielding adaptive regret guarantees. Overall, our results demonstrate
that online mirror-map selection can significantly enhance the ability of OMD to exploit sparsity
in online convex optimization.

The first question (a) is not new, and there is some evidence that this is true. [11] considered
families of sparse losses and gave instances where OMD with Lp norm interpolations for p ∈(1, 2)
provides factor Ω(log d) improvement in regret over OEG, and other instances where it provides
factor Ω(log d) improvement over OPGD. However, it remains open if there are instances where
such bounds hold simultaneously.
In this work, we show that in fact this can be pushed even more, by showing instances where a
poly(d) improvement is possible using mirror maps corresponding to block norms. A block norm
partitions a vector into various blocks, and takes the L1 norm over the L2 norms of each block,
thereby interpolating cleanly between L1 norm (with n = d blocks), and L2 norm with n = 1.
We show that we can choose mirror maps corresponding to these block norms to get the desired
improvements in regret for sparse loss functions.
The first half of the second question (b) is answered easily due to the structure of block norms. In
particular, we show that the regret due to any block norm for a random sparsity pattern of the loss
family L is approximated up to a factor O(ln d) by the regret using a certain ‘uniform’ block norm.
This helps us show that a small portfolio of O(ln d) block norms is sufficient to consider.
Finally, contrary to intuition, we construct an example where using the standard step size in the
OEG and OPGD setups, switching the mirror maps in each iteration can in fact cause the algorithm
to converge to a suboptimal point in the domain, resulting in a linear regret. This is surprising as
any online mirror descent setup must decrease a certain potential function across each iteration.
However, alternatively changing the mirror maps breaks this monotonicity due to the interaction
of divergences. To then design an adaptive algorithm that can tune to the best block norm in the

1.1.2
Learning the Best Mirror Map Online
When sparsity S is known a priori, Theorem 1 provides a general recipe to find a good mirror
map for a given OCO instance: minimize (over the number of blocks n) the corresponding regret
upper bound, and choose the corresponding mirror map. However, sparsity S may not be known in
advance, as is the case in many practical settings (e.g., [1, 12]). In such cases, we choose from among
the portfolio of 1 + log2 d mirror maps, corresponding to n ∈{20, . . . , 2log2 d}. More generally, given
any portfolio (i.e., set) M of mirror maps for OMD, we discuss strategies to learn the optimum
mirror map from M online for any instance.
First, we rule out the naive strategy of alternating between mirror maps across time steps: even
though each mirror map enjoys convergence via a decrease in a tailored potential function that
combines the distance from the optimum and the drop in the function value (e.g., see [2]), mixing
two mirror maps arbitrarily does not even lead to sublinear regret. In particular, we construct
examples of instances and iteration-specific sparse losses where alternating between OPGD and
OEG can lead to Ω(T) regret. At a high level, while both algorithms individually decrease some
potential function customized to their respective divergences, changing the mirror maps breaks this
monotonicity due to the interaction of divergences.

Parameter-free algorithms for online convex optimization, such as AdaHedge [7], MetaGrad [18],
and AdaFTRL [14] use multiplicative-weights updates to aggregate over learning rates while keeping
the underlying mirror map fixed, typically quadratic or entropic. In contrast, our method performs
an explicit MW aggregation across multiple distinct mirror maps, enabling adaptation not only over
step sizes but also over geometries. Other approaches [5] dynamically update the mirror map as
more data is revealed.
Outline.
We present some preliminaries on OMD and block norms in Section 2. Section 3 discusses
improvements using block norms for sparse losses. In particular, we characterize regret for block
norms for loss functions with sparse gradients (Theorem 1) in Section 3.1, and prove Theorem 2
in Section 3.2 and Section 3.3 respectively. Learning the optimal mirror map online and tuning to
the sparsity of loss functions is discussed in Section 4, where we prove Theorem 3, Theorem 4 and
Corollary 1. We conclude in Section 5. Some technical proofs are deferred to the appendices.

5
Conclusion
The choice of the mirror map in OMD captures the geometry of the OCO problem, and can be
exploited to show regret improvements in specific regimes of loss functions. As we showed, spe-
cific block norm mirror maps can yield polynomial-in-dimension improvements in regret in certain
regimes over standard algorithms like OPGD and OEG.
However, very little may be known about the loss functions a priori, adhering to a fixed mirror map
may lead to suboptimal regret. In this work, we show that a portfolio of block norm mirror maps
can provide significant improved performance. We show that, using the multiplicative algorithm,
we can switch between various block norms at runtime, achieving the regret rate of the best block
norm, up to an O(
√
ln ln d) factor.
A natural direction for future work is to explore other families of mirror maps that interpolate
between Euclidean and entropic geometries and to investigate whether a small, universal portfolio
of geometries can achieve near-optimal regret across all OCO instances. In particular, this work
focuses on block norms with equal-size blocks. Incorporating unequal blocks (that sometimes better
adapt to the loss functions with non-uniform sparsity patterns) may require new techniques since
the number of such partitions is much larger (even if we allow a O(ln d)-approximation).
More broadly, our results suggest viewing geometry itself as a learnable component of online opti-
mization.
