Abstract
In the Cover Small Cuts problem, we are given a capacitated (undirected) graph G = (V, E, u) and a
threshold value λ, as well as a set of links L with end-nodes in V and a non-negative cost for each link ℓ∈L;
the goal is to find a minimum-cost set of links such that each non-trivial cut of capacity less than λ is covered
by a link.
Bansal et al. [3] showed that the WGMV primal-dual algorithm, due to Williamson et al. [17], achieves
approximation ratio 16 for the Cover Small Cuts problem; their analysis uses the notion of a pliable family
of sets that satisfies a combinatorial property. Later, Bansal [1, 2] and then Nutov [13, 14] proved that the
same algorithm achieves approximation ratio 6. We show that the same algorithm achieves approximation
ratio 5, by using a stronger notion, namely, a pliable family of sets that satisfies symmetry and structural
submodularity.
1
Introduction
Williamson et al. (WGMV) [17] defined a family of sets F to be uncrossable if the following holds: for any pair
of sets A, B ∈F, both A ∩B, A ∪B are in F, or both A −B, B −A are in F. They used this notion to design
and analyse a primal-dual approximation algorithm for the problem of covering an uncrossable family of sets,
and they proved an approximation ratio of two for their algorithm. (The algorithmic problem is as follows:
The input consists of a family of sets F of a ground-set V , a set of links L, where each link is an unordered
pair of nodes of V , and a non-negative cost for each link in L; the goal is to find a minimum-cost set of links
L′ such that each set S ∈F is covered by a link of L′, i.e., δL′(S) ̸= ∅, ∀S ∈F.) Recently, Bansal et al. [3]
defined a family of sets F to be pliable if the following holds: for any pair of sets A, B ∈F, at least two of
the (four) sets A ∩B, A ∪B, A −B, B −A are in F. Bansal et al. [3] showed that the WGMV primal-dual
algorithm [17] achieves an approximation ratio of 16 for the problem of covering a pliable family of sets that
satisfies property (γ). (We discuss property (γ) in the following section; it is a combinatorial property, and the
analysis of [3] relies on it.)
In the Cover Small Cuts problem, we are given a capacitated (undirected) graph G = (V, E, u) and a thresh-
old value λ, as well as a set of links L with end-nodes in V and a non-negative cost for each link ℓ∈L; the goal
is to find a minimum-cost set of links such that each non-trivial cut of capacity less than λ is covered by a link.

The Cover Small Cuts problem is a special case of the problem of covering a pliable family of sets that satisfies
property (γ), hence, the WGMV primal-dual algorithm [17] achieves approximation ratio 16 for the former
problem. Later, Bansal [1, 2], and then Nutov [13, 14] proved approximation ratio 6 for the same algorithm
for a generalization of the Cover Small Cuts problem, namely, the problem of covering a pliable family of sets
that satisfies both property (γ) and the (so-called) sparse crossing property (see section 2). Moreover, Nutov
[13] shows that the 6-approximation analysis is tight for the generalization. There are other results by Nutov
on related topics, see [10, 11, 12] (we mention that these papers are not directly relevant for the results in our
paper).
Diestel et al. [4, 5, 6, 7, 8] introduced the notion of abstract separation systems that satisfy a submodularity
property, and they call this structural submodularity. One of their motivations was to identify the few structural
assumptions one has to make of a set of objects called ‘separations’ in order to capture the essence of tangles
in graphs, and thereby make them applicable in wider contexts.
A pliable family of sets F satisfies structural submodularity if the following holds: for any pair of crossing
sets A, B ∈F, at least one of the sets A ∩B, A ∪B is in F, and at least one of the sets A −B, B −A is in F.
A family of sets F of a ground-set V such that S ∈F iff V −S ∈F is said to satisfy symmetry.
We present an improved analysis of the WGMV primal-dual algorithm for the Cover Small Cuts problem that
achieves approximation ratio 5; our analysis uses the notion of a pliable family of sets that satisfies symmetry
and structural submodularity.
We apply a result of Bansal [1, 2], to improve the approximation ratio from 6 to 5. Bansal’s result states the
following: The approximation ratio for the WGMV primal-dual algorithm applied to the problem of covering
a pliable family of sets F is (3 + ρ(F)), where ρ(F) denotes the so-called crossing density of F. Section 2
discusses this result in detail. We show that ρ(F) ≤2 for a pliable family of sets F that satisfies symmetry and
structural submodularity, see section 3.
Recently, Nutov [15] constructed an example of the Cover Small Cuts problem such that the WGMV primal-
dual algorithm finds a solution of cost (5 −ϵ)opt, where opt denotes the cost of an optimal (integral) solution
and ϵ > 0 is an arbitrarily small number. This shows that our (upper bound) analysis is tight, and concludes the
quest for the tight approximation ratio for the WGMV primal-dual algorithm applied to the Cover Small Cuts
problem.
Simmons, in his thesis, [16], uses the notion of a strongly pliable family of sets. This notion is the same
as the notion of structural submodularity of Diestel et al. [6], and, in this paper, we use the term structural
submodularity (rather than strongly pliable). The results in this paper are based on a sub-chapter of the first
author’s thesis, see [16, Chapter 2.3.3].

Preliminaries
For a positive integer k, we use [k] to denote the set {1, 2, . . . , k}. A pair of subsets A, B of V (the ground-set)
is said to cross if each of the four sets A ∩B, V −(A ∪B), A −B, B −A is non-empty.
Assumption. When discussing a family of sets F, we assume that the empty set and the ground-set are not
in F. (Otherwise, the problem of covering F would be infeasible.)

A family of sets F satisfies the sparse crossing property if for any set S ∈F, the number of inclusion minimal
sets of F that cross S is zero or one.
Lemma 1. Let F be a pliable family that satisfies symmetry and structural submodularity. Then the inclu-
sion minimal sets of F are (pairwise) disjoint.
Proof. Let C1, C2 ∈F be inclusion minimal sets. If C1, C2 are not disjoint, then F would contain a proper
subset of C1 or C2, and this would contradict inclusion minimality. (In particular, if C1, C2 cross, then one of
C1 −C2 or C2 −C1 would be in F.)
Lemma 2. Let F be a pliable family that satisfies symmetry and structural submodularity. Then F satisfies
the sparse crossing property.
Proof. We use a contradiction argument. Suppose that S ∈F crosses two inclusion minimal sets C1, C2 ∈F;
by the previous lemma, C1, C2 are disjoint. Then, since proper subsets of C1 or C2 cannot be in F, both S ∪C1
and S ∪C2 would be in F. Note that S ∪C1 and S ∪C2 cross (S ∪C1 ∪C2 = V is not possible, by symmetry).
Then (S ∪C1) −(S ∪C2) = C1 −S or (S ∪C2) −(S ∪C1) = C2 −S is in F; this gives a contradiction.

submodularity. We prove a stronger statement in section 3, see Proposition 6.
