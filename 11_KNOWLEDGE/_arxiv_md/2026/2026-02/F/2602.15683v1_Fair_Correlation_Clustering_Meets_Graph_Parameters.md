Abstract. We study the generalization of Correlation Clustering
which incorporates fairness constraints via the notion of fairlets. The cor-
responding Fair Correlation Clustering problem has been studied
from several perspectives to date, but has so far lacked a detailed analysis
from the parameterized complexity paradigm. We close this gap by pro-
viding tractability results for the problem under a variety of structural
graph parameterizations, including treewidth, treedepth and the vertex
cover number; our results lie at the very edge of tractability given the
known NP-hardness of the problem on severely restricted inputs.

Introduction
Clustering is a fundamental task that arises in a wide range of applications,
where the goal is to partition a set of objects based on pairwise similarity or
dissimilarity information. On graphs, the task is typically formalized via the
Correlation Clustering problem [6,9,12]: given a graph G and an integer k,
can we modify (i.e., add or remove) at most k edges of G to obtain collection
of pairwise independent cliques? This problem is sometimes also referred to
as Cluster Editing [17,26,27], and is known to be NP-hard even if we are
promised that the optimal solution contains two clusters [35]. The number of
modifications is often called the cost of a correlation clustering.
While Correlation Clustering can be seen as a well-suited model for
finding an optimal clustering of unlabeled elements (i.e., vertices), in many sce-
narios one needs to ensure that the clustering respects additional constraints
arising from the specific context it is applied in. Fairness considerations are a
prime example of this: on labeled data, a solution to the base Correlation
Clustering problem can yield clusters where the presence of the labels is dis-
proportional when compared to the label distribution on the input. In view of
these considerations, Chierichetti, Kumar, Lattanzi and Vassilvitskii initiated
the study of fair clustering problems via the lens of fairlets [11]. When view-
ing the individual vertex labels as colors, a fairlet is a minimum set of vertices

Fair Correlation Clustering (FCC)
Input:
An undirected n-vertex graph G, a budget B ∈N and a κ-coloring
χ : V (G) →{1, . . . , κ} for an integer κ ≤n.
Question: Is there a fair correlation clustering of cost at most B?
As a generalization of Correlation Clustering, FCC is NP-hard [2].
However, unlike the former problem, FCC was shown to remain NP-hard even
when restricted to very simple input graphs, such as graphs of diameter 2 and 2-
colored trees of diameter 4 [10]. Most foundational research on FCC to date has
focused on overcoming its inherent intractability via approximation. Ahmadian,
Epasto, Kumar, and Mahdian [2] reduced the problem to the “fairlet decomposi-
tion” problem with a standard median cost function to achieve a constant-factor
approximation, while Ahmadi, Galhotra, Saha, and Roy [1] focused on the num-
ber of colors and the relative amount of vertices in the most common color and
obtained an approximation, which is quadratic in both parameters.
In this work, we take a different approach and ask whether it is possible to
exploit the structural properties of the input graph G to yield exact algorithms
for FCC. The parameterized complexity paradigm (see Section 2) offers the per-
fect tools for answering this question, and we obtain a detailed overview of how
the problem’s complexity depends on the structure of G.

Contributions. The aforementioned NP-hardness of FCC on 2-colored trees
rules out fixed-parameter and even XP-algorithms under the vast majority of
graph parameterizations, including not only the classical treewidth [34] but also
the closely related pathwidth [33], treedepth [31] and even the feedback edge num-
ber [3,21]. Indeed, all of these measure how “structurally similar” the input graph
is to a tree (or a special class thereof), and all attain small constant values on
trees of diameter 4. In spite of these lower bounds, as our first contribution we
show that one can still achieve fixed-parameter tractability for FCC by using a
suitable structural parameter:
Theorem 1. FCC is in FPT w.r.t. the vertex cover number of G.
Here, the vertex cover number measures the size of a minimum vertex cover
of G and is a baseline parameter that has found a variety of applications on
problems where decomposition-based parameters such as treewidth do not yield
tractability [4,14,18,25]. While the structure of graphs with a small vertex cover
number might seem simple, establishing Theorem 1 is non-trivial and relies on
a combination of structural insights (among others establishing a bound on the
cluster sizes), branching techniques and a matching subroutine.
For our next contributions, we revisit the setting of tree-like graphs and in
particular the parameterization by treewidth, which can be seen as the most
classical and fundamental graph parameter in the field. The aforementioned NP-
hardness reduction of Casel, Friedrich, Schirneck and Wietheger [10] rules out
XP-tractability w.r.t. treewidth even when combined with the number of colors;
however, the construction relies on having large fairlets. On the other hand, in
many natural scenarios one would expect the fairlet sizes to be small—a fairlet
size of 1 yields the classical Correlation Clustering, while a fairlet size of
2 captures a balanced clustering with two colors. Hence, it is natural to ask
whether one can achieve parameterized tractability for FCC when combining
treewidth with restrictions on the fairlet size. We answer this affirmatively by
establishing:
Theorem 2. FCC is in XP w.r.t. the treewidth of G plus the fairlet size.
Theorem 3. FCC is in FPT w.r.t. the treewidth of G when restricted to in-
stances with fairlet size at most 2.
We remark that in spite of the similarity of these two results, they are in fact
incomparable and neither implies the other. Theorem 3 is interesting already for
the case of fairlet size 1, as the fixed-parameter tractability of Correlation
Clustering (i.e., Cluster Editing) w.r.t. treewidth was not known and pre-
viously posed as an open question [10]. That being said, the main technical
challenges arise when dealing with fairlet size 2. To prove the theorem, we first
establish that there exists a solution such that each cluster of size greater than 2
forms a connected subgraph in G. This structural insight then enables the use
of treewidth-based dynamic programming, where connectivity is used to handle

For Theorem 2, the above approach unfortunately cannot be replicated—it is

careful dynamic programming procedure which tracks information about sets
of “incomplete” clusters, and the associated dynamic programming tables then
require XP-space w.r.t. both the fairlet size and treewidth.
Theorems 2 and 3 substantially expand the frontiers of tractability for FCC
and the former generalizes the known XP-algorithm for FCC w.r.t. the fairlet
size on trees [10]. However, they also give rise to a natural follow-up question:
is the problem fixed-parameter tractable w.r.t. treewidth plus the fairlet size?
None of the known hardness results rules out such an outcome, but—as discussed
above—the insights used to establish the former do not generalize to the setting
with fairlet size 3. While we leave this as a prominent open question for future
work, as our final contribution we provide an affirmative answer when treewidth
is replaced with treedepth:
Theorem 4. FCC is in FPT w.r.t. the treedepth of G plus the fairlet size.
Treedepth is a parameter which measures distance to trees of bounded height;
it has fundamental connections to the theory of graph sparsity [31] and has found
a variety of algorithmic applications as well [16,30,8]. While Theorem 3 can be
seen as a step towards settling the fixed-parameter tractability of FCC w.r.t.
treewidth plus fairlet size, the technique underlying its proof is entirely differ-
ent from the dynamic programming based approach used in Theorems 2 and 3.
In particular, we design a reduction rule which operates on a treedepth-based
decomposition of the graph and whose exhaustive application results in a graph
where each connected component has size bounded by the two parameters.
For “typical” problems, such a reduction rule would immediately imply fixed-
parameter tractability via kernelization [13]—however, for FCC one cannot treat
each connected component separately. Hence, after applying the aforementioned
preprocessing the algorithm uses the bound on the size of connected compo-
nents to solve the problem via an encoding as an Integer Linear Program where
the number of variables and constraints is upper-bounded by a function of our
parameters; at this point, one can invoke known techniques for ILP solving to
obtain the desired result.

Preliminaries
We assume familiarity with standard graph terminology. For n ∈N, set [n] =
{1, . . . , n}. For a vertex v, let δ(v) denote the degree of v in G and let δS(v) be
the number of neighbors of v in a vertex set S ⊆V (G).
Consider a vertex coloring χ : V (G) →[κ] for a set [κ] of colors, with κ ∈[n].
Recalling the discussion in Section 1, a fairlet F is a minimum multiset of colors
from [κ] such that the multiset {χ(v) | v ∈V (G)} of colors occurring in G can

an instance of FCC in polynomial time, and we will denote the fairlet size |F|
as ˜c; we will sometimes represent fairlets as a vector (c1, . . . , cκ) encoding the
number of times each of the respective colors occurs in F. A vertex subset W
is fair if {χ(v) | v ∈W} can be partitioned into copies of F. A clustering is a
partition of V (G) where each part of the clustering is called a cluster. The cost
of a clustering is the total number of edges in E(G) with endpoints in different
clusters plus the total number of non-edges inside each cluster, and a clustering
is fair if each of its clusters is fair. In view of the definition of FCC, we call a
clustering a solution if it is fair and achieves a minimum cost. We remark that
each of the algorithms presented in this paper can also output a solution as a
witness for a YES-instance.
Parameterized Complexity. In parameterized complexity [15,13], the com-
plexity of a problem is studied not only with respect to the input size, but also
with respect to some problem parameter(s). The core idea behind parameterized
complexity is that the combinatorial explosion resulting from the NP-hardness
of a problem can sometimes be confined to certain structural parameters that
are small in practical settings.
Formally, a parameterized problem Q is a subset of Ω∗×N, where Ωis a fixed
alphabet. Each instance of Q is a pair (I, k), where k ∈N is called the parameter.
The class FPT denotes the class of all fixed-parameter tractable parameterized
problems, that is, problems which can be solved in time f(k) · |I|O(1) for some
computable function f. A weaker tractability class is XP, which contains all
parameterized problems that can be solved in time |I|f(k) for some computable
function f.
Parameters of Interest. The vertex cover number of a graph G (vcn(G)) is
the size of its minimum vertex cover. Recall that a vertex cover is a subset X of
vertices such that for each edge e in the graph, at least one endpoint of e lies in
X. We note that the vertex cover number—along with a minimum-size vertex
cover as a witness—can be computed in time (1.25284vcn(G)) · |V (G)|O(1) [22].
A nice tree decomposition of an undirected graph G is a pair (T, B), where
T is a tree (whose vertices are called nodes) rooted at a node r and B is a set
{Bt | t ∈T} where ∀t, Bt ⊆V (G) such that the following hold:
– For every {u, v} ∈E(G), there is a node t such that u, v ∈Bt.
– For every vertex v ∈V (G), the set of nodes t satisfying v ∈Bt forms a
subtree of T.
– |Bℓ| = 1 for every leaf ℓof T and |Br| = 0.
– There are only three kinds of non-leaf nodes in T:
• introduce: a node t with exactly one child t′ such that Bt = Bt′ ∪{v} for
some v /∈Bt′.
• forget: a node t with exactly one child t′ such that Bt = Bt′ \ {v} for
some v ∈Bt′.
• join: a node t with exactly two children p, q such that Bt = Bp = Bq.
We call each set Bt a bag. The width of a nice tree decomposition (T, B) is
the size of the largest bag Bt minus 1, and the treewidth of G (tw(G)) is the

Tractability via Treewidth for Fairlets of Size at Most 2
While the question of whether Fair Correlation Clustering in general is fixed-
parameter tractable w.r.t. the treewidth remains open, for the special case of
fairlet size ˜c ≤2 we can answer it affirmatively.
In particular, the algorithm underlying Theorem 2 stores a large amount of
information about clusters in the past graph. However, for ˜c ≤2, we show that,
without loss of generality, clusters intersecting the past and future vertices but
not the current bag Bt are trivial and easy to keep track of. Towards this, we
establish the following lemma:
Lemma 2. Every YES-instance of Fair Correlation Clustering with fair-
let size ˜c = 2 on a graph G admits a solution P with the following property: each
cluster in P of size greater than 2 induces a connected subgraph of G.
Proof. Consider an arbitrary solution P′ to the instance and suppose it contains
a cluster Z not satisfying the claimed property. Let A ⊆Z be maximal such that
A induces a connected component in G and let r and b be the numbers of vertices
of the first color (red) and the second color (blue) in A, respectively. Without loss
of generality, assume r ≥b. Consider an arbitrary set R ⊂A of r −b red vertices
from A and let P be the fair clustering obtained from P′ by replacing Z by
Z1 := A \ R and Z2 := Z \ Z1. Now Z1, satisfies the lemma’s requirement, while
Z2 may still not satisfy it. We will show that the cost of P is at most the cost of
P′. Assuming that this holds, we can exhaustively apply the above modification
to obtain a solution satisfying the lemma’s requirement, where termination is
guaranteed as each modification strictly increases the number of clusters.
Thus, it remains to show cost(P) ≤cost(P ′), where cost(·) denotes the cost
of the respective clustering. Just like in the proof of Lemma 1, the former can
be obtained from the latter as follows:
cost(P) = cost(P′) + |{{v, w} | v ∈Z1, w ∈Z2, {v, w} ∈E(G)}|
−|{{v, w} | v ∈Z1, w ∈Z2, {v, w} ̸∈E(G)}|.
By construction, the set in the first line has size at most |Z1| · |R| and the set
in the second line has size at least |Z1| · |Z \ A|. As Z is fair, the excess of red
vertices in A equals the excess of blue vertices in Z \ A. Thus, there are at least
r −b = |R| blue vertices in Z \ A and thereby cost(P) ≤cost(P′).

restrict our focus on identifying whether an instance admits any nice solution.

To construct (G′, B′, χ), consider a rooted forest F over vertices V (G) which
witnesses the treedepth td of G (i.e., the height of each tree in F is at most td

in F). For every vertex v ∈V (G), let its type be recursively defined from leaves

G, and the multiset of types among its children. Perform the following procedure
for every tree T in F: Start at the bottom layer (the leaves). From this layer,
select γ vertices of each type (or all vertices with that type if it contains fewer
than γ vertices). For each vertex v that is not selected, remove all edges between
v and its ancestors in T (in both G and F) and update all types accordingly.

until reaching the root layer. If at any point the budget B drops below 0, reject
the instance. This is correct as we remove an edge if we know that we cannot
keep all neighbors of some vertex v in the same cluster as v, making at least this
number of cuts necessary. If the instance has not been rejected after all trees in
the forest are processed, let G′ be the updated graph G and B′ be the updated

Concluding Remarks
Our results identify two routes to tractability for Fair Correlation Cluster-
ing: Either we may use Theorem 1 to solve instances with large fairlets but a
simple graph structure, or Theorems 3-4 for instances with a richer graph struc-
ture but small fairlets. While the fixed-parameter tractability of the problem
w.r.t. treewidth on instances with fairlets of size 2 (Theorem 3) also settles an
analogous question for Correlation Clustering, it simultaneously leads to
the main open question arising from our work: is Fair Correlation Clus-
tering fixed-parameter tractable w.r.t. treewidth plus the fairlet size?
