Abstract
Inference-time algorithms are an emerging paradigm in which pre-trained models are used
as subroutines to solve downstream tasks.
Such algorithms have been proposed for tasks
ranging from inverse problems and guided image generation to reasoning. However, the methods
currently deployed in practice are heuristics with a variety of failure modes—and we have very
little understanding of when these heuristics can be efficiently improved.
In this paper, we consider the task of sampling from a reward-tilted diffusion model—that
is, sampling from p⋆(x) ∝p(x) exp(r(x))—given a reward function r and pre-trained diffusion
oracle for p. We provide a fine-grained analysis of the computational tractability of this task for
quadratic rewards r(x) = x⊤Ax + b⊤x. We show that linear-reward tilts are always efficiently
sampleable—a simple result that seems to have gone unnoticed in the literature. We use this as a
building block, along with a conceptually new ingredient—the Hubbard-Stratonovich transform—
to provide an efficient algorithm for sampling from low-rank positive-definite quadratic tilts,
i.e. r(x) = x⊤Ax where A is positive-definite and of rank O(1). For negative-definite tilts, i.e.
r(x) = −x⊤Ax where A is positive-definite, we prove that the problem is intractable even if A
is of rank 1 (albeit with exponentially-large entries).

Introduction
It is increasingly common to use pre-trained generative models as components within algorithms
for more complex downstream tasks. Broadly, such algorithms are termed as inference-time or
meta-generation algorithms [WBF+24]. Examples of tasks that can be framed in this paradigm
include inverse problems in the sciences [BH24], guidance to perform conditional sampling in image
generation [DN21], and tilting a distribution by a (trained or pre-specified) reward in reasoning
tasks [KPB22, GMAM25] and even protein design [LGT+25, HWMO25].
In this paper, we focus on the algorithmic task of steering a pre-trained diffusion model according
to a given reward function. Specifically, given a diffusion model for a base distribution p(x) (which
provides access to the scores of convolutions of p with Gaussian noise), and a reward function r(x),
our task is to sample from the tilted distribution
p⋆(x) ∝p(x) exp(r(x)).
For any reward function r, p⋆has a natural variational interpretation as the solution to the KL-
regularized optimization problem arg maxq Eq[r] −DKL(q∥p) [KPB22]. Moreover, with appropriate

From a theoretical perspective, steering with quadratic rewards is computationally intractable
with no further assumptions [GJP+24, BH24]. Indeed, if the base distribution p(x) is uniform over
the discrete hypercube, then p⋆(x) ∝exp(x⊤Ax + b⊤x) is precisely a classical Ising model [Isi25],
and a seminal line of work has shown that exact and approximate sampling from an Ising model can
be intractable [JS93, SS12, GŠV16]. However, there is also a rich literature on efficient algorithms
for sampling from Ising models with special structure [JS93, EKZ22, KLR22, CE22]. Can these
sorts of structural assumptions help explain when steering a general base measure p accessible via a
diffusion model is indeed tractable?
1.1
Contributions
In this paper, we provide a fine-grained understanding of the computational landscape of steering
diffusion models with quadratic rewards r(x) := x⊤Ax + b⊤x. Through the lens of the rank of the
quadratic form (i.e. rank(A))—a fundamental quantity in the special case of sampling from Ising
models [KLR22]—we delineate regimes in which the task is computationally tractable, and regimes
in which it is not. Precisely, we show the following:
Linear rewards admit an efficient sampler (Section 3).
If the reward is a linear function
r(x) = b⊤x for some vector b ∈Rd (in other words, rank(A) = 0), we can efficiently sample from the
tilt p⋆. This relatively simple result was seemingly missed in prior literature, and is a consequence of
the fact that the scores of p⋆have a simple closed-form expression in terms of the reward function
and scores of the base distribution p.
Negative-definite quadratic rewards induce intractability, even for rank-1 matrices
(Section 4).
If r(x) = x⊤Ax, where A is a rank-1 negative semi-definite matrix, then the task of
sampling from p⋆is computationally intractable, assuming NP ⊈BPP.
Positive-definite, low-rank quadratic rewards admit an efficient sampler (Section 5).
If
r(x) = x⊤Ax, where A is a rank-O(1) positive semi-definite matrix, then there is an algorithm that
samples from p∗(approximately, in Wasserstein distance) in polynomial time.1 For completeness, we

1.2
Related work
Several prior works have studied the problem of provably steering diffusion models with a quadratic
reward function r(x) = −1
2σ2 ∥Ax −b∥2 [GJP+24, BH24, XGP25, PRSS25], motivated by the task
of linear inverse problems, i.e. posterior inference with noisy linear measurements. [GJP+24]
show that steering with general (i.e. potentially high-rank) negative-definite quadratic rewards is
cryptographically hard, and [BH24] show that the same problem is intractable via reduction from
the problem of sampling Ising models. Our result in Section 4 strengthens these by showing that
intractability holds even when the quadratic form is rank-1.
[BH24] show that the hardness can be circumvented when σ is sufficiently small. In the context
of inverse problems, A corresponds to a measurement operator, and σ to a signal-to-noise ratio;
the small-σ regime is easier since it makes p⋆more log-concave. The main tool they use is the
Polchinsky flow [BBD24]. [XGP25] develop an efficient algorithm for the same problem, without
assumptions on A and σ, but require that the base distribution p satisfies a condition called “local
log-concavity”. [PRSS25] develop an efficient algorithm with no assumptions, but it (necessarily)
has no guarantee of closeness in total variation or Wasserstein distance.
Zooming out, [CGC+24] analyze a popular heuristic for guidance—i.e. sampling from a class-
conditioned diffusion model—and show some natural examples in which it has the intended behavior,
and some examples of failure modes. [KSC25] provide a wrapper for existing heuristics, which admits
a theoretical guarantee that is weaker than approximate sampling. [RSS+25] consider sampling
from tilted distributions when p is the law of an autoregressive language model, and reward access
is augmented by a “process reward” that estimates the quality of partial generations.
Finally, at a technical level, our application of Hubbard-Stratonovich in the PSD setting is
inspired by that of [KLR22] for sampling from (approximately) low-rank Ising models, given access
to their unnormalized density. Their algorithm uses the transform in conjunction with techniques
from Markov Chains and variational inference, whereas ours requires building on our algorithm for
linear rewards.

6
Conclusion
In this paper, we considered the task of sampling from a diffusion model, tilted by a quadratic
reward. We provide a fine-grained analysis of the computational tractability of this task through the
lens of the rank of the quadratic form. In particular, this task is computationally intractable even for
rank-1 negative-definite tilts. For low-rank positive-definite tilts, we give an efficient algorithm based
on two conceptually new algorithmic ingredients: sampling from linearly tilted diffusion models,
and the Hubbard-Stratonovich transform.
There are many natural directions for further work. Our results, like many other results in the
literature, assume that the score oracle is exact—whereas in practice, the oracle we have access to
is trained, and thus would have errors. Handling errors is non-trivial because they will be small
on average under the base distribution—but algorithms which sample from the tilted distribution
may deviate substantially from the trajectories that would arise when sampling from the base
distribution.
Towards handling more complex rewards, it would also be interesting to understand what
interaction between the structure of the reward and the structure of the base distribution allow for
efficient algorithms.
