Abstract
We show that the spanning tree congestion problem is NP-complete even for proper interval
graphs of linear clique-width at most 4.
Keywords:
spanning tree congestion, proper interval graph, clique-width.
1. Introduction
Let G be a connected graph and T be a spanning tree of G. The congestion of an edge
e ∈E(T) is the size of the fundamental cut of e with respect to T, and the congestion of T is
the maximum congestion of an edge of T. The spanning tree congestion of G, denoted stc(G),
is the minimum congestion over all spanning trees of G. The problem studied in this paper,
Spanning Tree Congestion, is formalized as follows.
Problem. Spanning Tree Congestion (STC)
Input. A connected graph G and an integer k.
Question. stc(G) ≤k?
Our goal in this paper is to prove the following theorem.
Theorem 1. Spanning Tree Congestion is NP-complete on proper interval graphs of linear
clique-width at most 4.
1.1. Related results
After Ostrovskii [19] formally introduced the concept of spanning tree congestion, it has
been studied extensively from both graph-theoretic and algorithmic perspectives. Here we only
list known results on the complexity of STC on restricted graph classes. For more information
about spanning tree congestion, see the survey by Otachi [20].
In general, STC is known to be NP-complete [16, Section 5.6], and its NP-completeness holds
even on planar graphs [3], split graphs [18], chain graphs (2K2-free bipartite graphs) [18], and
graphs of maximum degree 3 [1]. Recently, we noticed that a proof in a recent paper by Lampis
et al. [12] can be easily modified to prove the NP-completeness of STC on interval graphs of
linear clique-width at most 3 (see its updated full version [11, Theorem 9]). The main result of
this paper (Theorem 1) strengthens this hardness to proper interval graphs.
On the other hand, it is known that STC is polynomial-time solvable for trivially per-
fect graphs (chordal cographs) [18], cochain graphs (cobipartite chordal graphs) [9, 10], and
outerplanar graphs [4]. Furthermore, the XP-time algorithm parameterized by treewidth [12,

1.2. On a recently announced algorithm for interval graphs
Recently, Lin and Lin [14] have announced a polynomial-time algorithm for STC on interval
graphs. However, after their announcement, we noticed that a known hardness proof can be
easily modified to prove the NP-completeness of STC on interval graphs as mentioned above.
Unless P = NP, these results contradict each other. We had a close look at their paper, but
the descriptions there contain gaps that make the algorithm difficult to verify in its current
form. A subsequent private communication with the authors of [14] indicated that, in light of
the NP-hardness result, they think that their algorithm is incorrect [13].
2. Preliminaries
For integers m and n, let [m, n] be the set of integers from m to n, and let [n] be the set of
positive integers less than or equal to n; that is, [m, n] = {d ∈Z | m ≤d ≤n} and [n] = [1, n].
Let G = (V, E) be a graph.
We sometimes refer to the vertex set and the edge set of
G as V (G) and E(G), respectively. For S ⊆V , let G[S] denote the subgraph of G induced
by S.
If G[S] has all possible edges, then S is a clique.
For v ∈V , we denote by NG(v)
and NG[v] the (open) neighborhood and the closed neighborhood of v in G, respectively; i.e.,
NG(v) = {u | {u, v} ∈E} and NG[v] = NG(v) ∪{v}. The degree of v in G, i.e., |NG(v)|, is
denoted by degG(v). We denote the minimum degree of G by δ(G) and the maximum degree
by ∆(G). For two disjoint vertex subsets A, B ⊆V , let E(A, B) be the set of edges between A
and B; that is, E(A, B) = {{a, b} ∈E | a ∈A, b ∈B}.
Now let G = (V, E) be a connected graph and T be a spanning tree of G. For e ∈E(T),
we define its congestion, denoted cngG,T (e), as the number of edges between the two connected
components T1 and T2 of T −e; i.e., cngG,T (e) = |E(V (T1), V (T2))|. We define the congestion

of T as cngG(T) = maxe∈E(T) cngG,T (e). The spanning tree congestion of G, denoted stc(G), is

Let T be a tree. A vertex v ∈V (T) is a leaf of T if degT (v) ≤1. If degT (v) ≥3, then we
call v a branching vertex. A tree is a spider if it has at most one branching vertex. A tree is
a star if there is a vertex c, called the center, adjacent to all other vertices; i.e., a star is the
complete bipartite graph K1,s for some s ≥0.
An interval representation of a graph G = (V, E) is a set of closed intervals {Iv | v ∈V } on
a line such that {u, v} ∈E if and only if Iu ∩Iv ̸= ∅. An interval representation is proper if no
two vertices u, v satisfy Iu ⊊Iv. A graph is an interval graph if it has an interval representation,
and it is a proper interval graph if it has a proper interval representation. It is known that a
graph is a proper interval graph if and only if it is a claw-free (K1,3-free) interval graph [21].
Although we use the concept of (linear) clique-width, we omit the definition since we do not
need it in the proof. See [5] for its definition.
3. An auxiliary lemma
In this section, we present an auxiliary technical lemma, which is a key tool in our proof
and a generalization of the fact that, for complete graphs, the minimum congestion spanning
trees are stars. Intuitively, the lemma says that a spanning tree T of low congestion cannot
“non-trivially split” a highly connected vertex subset S of large size, and thus, T has a star-like
(or spider-like) structure over S. This implies that T gives us an assignment of the vertices
not in S to elements of S; i.e., we assign v /∈S to s ∈S if v and s belong to the same leg in
the star-like structure of T. In Section 4, we use this lemma (with ρ1 = 1 and ρ2 = 1/2) in a
reduction from 3-Partition. We hope the lemma will be useful for other reductions or even
for some algorithms on dense graphs as well.
Let T be a spanning tree of G. We say that an edge e ∈E(T) splits S ⊆V into S1 and S2
if the two connected components T1 and T2 of T −e satisfy {V (T1) ∩S, V (T2) ∩S} = {S1, S2}.
We say that e non-trivially splits S if e splits S into S1 and S2 with min{|S1|, |S2|} ≥2.

clique-width at most 4.

First consider the case u = xi ∈X and w = yj ∈Y . Since {xi, yj} ∈E, xi is adjacent to all
vertices yj′ with j′ < j. Also, every vertex xi′ with i < i′ is adjacent to yj since ai ≤ai′. Since
w ∈{xi′ | i′ > i} ∪{yj′ | j′ < j}, we have {u, v}, {v, w} ∈E. (Recall that X and Y are cliques.)

the previous case. Since {yi, zj} ∈E, yi is adjacent to all vertices zj′ with j′ < j. Also, every
vertex yi′ with i < i′ is adjacent to zj since NG(yi) ∩Z ⊆NG(yi′) ∩Z by Observation 3. Since
w ∈{yi′ | i′ > i} ∪{zj′ | j′ < j}, we have {u, v}, {v, w} ∈E. (Recall that Y and Z are cliques.)

5. Concluding remarks
In this paper, we settled an open case of Spanning Tree Congestion by showing that
the problem is NP-complete even on proper interval graphs of linear clique-width at most 4.
Although our result considerably deepened the understanding of STC, there are still several
important unsettled questions, as mentioned below.
Graph classes. From the viewpoint of graph classes, one of the most relevant ones would be
cographs, i.e., graphs of clique-width at most 2 (see Fig. 1). Note that STC is known to be
NP-complete on graphs of clique-width at most 3 [18]. Note also that a connected cograph has
diameter at most 2 and the complexity of STC on graphs of diameter at most 2 is not known.
Fixed k. Let k be the target congestion in STC. If k ≤3, then STC is polynomial-time solv-
able [3]. On the other hand, for every fixed k ≥5, STC is NP-complete [17]. The case of k = 4
remains unsettled.
Approximation. Recently, Kolman [8] has given an approximation algorithm of a factor O(∆·
log3/2 n), where ∆is the maximum degree. On the other hand, the NP-hardness of STC with
k = 5 implies that an approximation factor strictly better than 1.2 is impossible unless P = NP.
It would be an interesting challenge to narrow the gap between these upper and lower bounds.
Structural parameterizations. In a recent paper, Lampis et al. [12] resolved the complexity of
STC for many cases, while some cases still remain unsettled. For example, the cases param-
eterized by neighborhood diversity and by treewidth plus maximum degree are mentioned as
unsettled cases.
