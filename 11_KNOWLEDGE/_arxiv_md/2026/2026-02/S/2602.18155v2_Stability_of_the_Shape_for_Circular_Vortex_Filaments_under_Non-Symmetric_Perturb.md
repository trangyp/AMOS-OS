Stability of the Shape for Circular Vortex Filaments
under Non-Symmetric Perturbations
Masashi Aiki *
Mitsuo Higaki †
Abstract
We establish the nonlinear orbital stability of circular vortex filaments governed
by the Localized Induction Equation (LIE) under non-symmetric perturbations, within
the framework of Tani-Nishiyama [9]. This result extends the first author’s recent
work [1] by removing symmetry assumptions on perturbations. While the circular
filaments are known to be Lyapunov unstable due to linear growth of the translational
mode, we prove that their shape remains globally stable modulo spatial translations
and rotations about the symmetry axis. The crucial ingredient is a geometric stability
lemma derived from the conservation of vector fluid impulse, which constrains the
low-frequency modulations that are not covered by the relative energy. Finally, we
relate the fluid impulse to an isoperimetric inequality, yielding a geometric constraint
for closed filaments.

Remark 1.2
(i) It is important to distinguish between Lyapunov stability and orbital sta-
bility. As discussed in [1], the circular filament xR is Lyapunov unstable due to the
linear growth of the zero mode. Theorem 1.1 shows orbital stability by factoring out
this translation and z-axis rotation via dist(x, Σ). Consequently, we prove the stabil-
ity of the filament’s shape without assuming the symmetry conditions in [1].
(ii) The quantity P [x], often referred to as the fluid impulse, is analyzed by Arms-Hama
[2] and Fukumoto [6]. The fluid impulse is conserved in time for closed curve so-
lutions of (LIE). The assumption (1.3) ensures that the initial impulse P [x0] of the
solution x is sufficiently close to the reference value P [xR
0 ] = πR2e3. In fact, this as-
sumption is connected with an isoperimetric inequality; see Appendix A. We empha-
size that, even if the inequality in Appendix A holds, Theorem 1.1 remains nontrivial:
it is a nonlinear stability of solutions to (LIE) and requires a dynamical control of the
low-frequency modulation parameters along the evolution.

(iii) The regularity assumption x0 ∈H4(T)3 is imposed to ensure the uniqueness of the
global solution, as established in Tani-Nishiyama [9]. While the existence of weak
solutions in H2(T)3 is known by Nishiyama-Tani [8, Remark 4.1], to the best of our
knowledge, the uniqueness of solutions in this class remains an open problem.
(iv) The linear stability of various closed vortex filaments, including circular filaments,
with respect to closure-preserving periodic perturbations has been investigated via the
Hasimoto map; see Calini-Keith-Lafortune [3, Section 6]. However, proving the non-
linear orbital stability of a vortex filament against more general perturbations requires
a different approach. Extending the analysis in [1], our current result establishes this
stability by using the conservation of fluid impulse.
(v) In the geometric framework of Langer-Perline [7], elastic curves arise as critical points
of the bending energy with a length-type constraint and yield rigid motion solutions
of the LIE. In the closed filament setting, the circular vortex filament is an example of
such an elastic curve; see [7, Remarks] for the observation that one could equally con-
sider closed curves. In this setting, the circle (hence the circular filament) is the sim-
plest example. However, their analysis does not explicitly involve the vector-valued
fluid impulse, which is crucial for controlling the ±1 modes. Our proof utilizes this
conserved quantity to show that the circular filament is not merely a critical point, but
a (constrained) local minimum.
