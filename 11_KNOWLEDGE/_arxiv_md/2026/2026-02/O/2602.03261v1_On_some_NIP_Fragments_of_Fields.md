ON SOME NIP FRAGMENTS OF FIELDS
PAULO ANDRÉS SOTO MORENO
Abstract. In this note we study sets of NIP formulas in some theories of fields and valued fields, with a special
focus on the sets of quantifier-free and existential formulas. First, we give a new proof of the fact that Separably
Closed Valued Fields of any characteristic and any imperfection degree are NIP, and use this result to fill some
gaps of a proof of the so-called NIP Transfer Theorem for henselian valued fields of equal characteristic. Second,
we prove a variant of a theorem of Johnson: every positive characteristic valued field whose existential formulas
are NIP is henselian. Finally, we set the ground for the finer question of transfer of NIP formulas of valued
fields with bounded quantifier rank. Namely, we prove that for any henselian equicharacteristic valued field,
any formula of quantifier rank at most n ≥1 is NIP if and only if the same is true for the residue field and the
value group, provided that the valued field is separably defectless Kaplansky and conditional on a multi-variable
generalization of a well known statement about indiscernible sequences of singletons in ac-valued fields.

Introduction
In this document we study the interplay between the independence property and the quantifier complexity
of definable sets in some theories of valued fields of equal characteristic. These subjects have played central
roles in the model theory of valued fields for the last years, with outstanding results covering, on the one hand,
a classification of henselian valued fields without the independence property, cf. [JS20] and [AJ24], and on
the other hand, a thorough analysis of quantifier complexity of definable henselian valuation rings, cf. [FJ17]
for a survey. The goal of this document is to present refined versions of a number of well known results of
the theory of NIP henselian valued fields of equal characteristic, improving on the quantifier rank of formulas
without the independence property, thus setting the ground for the study of the aforementioned interplay.
Let L be a language and let T be an L -theory.
If φ(x, y) is an L -formula, we say that φ(x, y) has the
independence property (with respect to T) or that φ(x, y) has IP if there is some model M of T and some
sequences (ai : i < ω), (bS : S ⊆ω) of |x|- and |y|-tuples of M respectively such that M |= φ(ai, bS) if and only
if i ∈S. Equivalently, φ(x, y) has IP if there is some model M of T, and indiscernible sequence (ai : i < ω)
of |x|-tuples of M and a |y|-tuple b of parameters from M such that M |= φ(ai, b) if and only if i is even,
cf. [Sim15, Lemma 2.7]. A formula is NIP if it does not have IP, and an L -theory is called NIP if all L -
formulas φ(x, y) are NIP with respect to T. Also, [Sim15, Lemma 2.5] states that a formula φ(x, y) is NIP if and
only if φop(y, x) = φ(x, y) is NIP, which allows to interchange the position of the indiscernible sequence and the
parameters. For more on NIP theories, see e.g. [Sim15]. If (K, v) is a valued field, we will usually denote by vK
and Kv its value group and residue field, O = Ov will denote its valuation ring and M = Mv its maximal ideal.
Finally, since henselian NIP valued fields of equal characteristic are separable-algebraically maximal Kaplansky,
we set this class of valued fields as our underlying object of study. Our main theorems are the following.
Theorem 1.1 (Theorem 3.8, cf. [Hon13, Corollary 5.2.13] for e ∈N and Delon for e = ∞). Quantifier-
free formulas of the language of valued fields Ldiv expanded by the parameterized λ-functions are NIP. As a
consequence, the theory SCVFe of Separably Closed Valued Fields of Ershov degree e ∈N ∪{∞} is NIP.
Theorem 1.1 is well stated in the literature for e ∈N, see e.g. [Hon13, Corollary 5.2.13], and the case e = ∞
is attributed to Delon, who argued using Poizat’s coheir counting argument.
However, to the best of our
knowledge, her result remains unpublished.
Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 5.16, cf. [Joh21, Theorem 2.8]). Let (K, O) be a valued field of positive characteristic
whose existential formulas are NIP. Then O is henselian.
For a given language L and a natural number n ≥1, denote by ∃n the set of formulas in prenex normal
form, of quantifier rank at most n, whose block of quantifiers starts with an existential quantifier, cf. Definition
2.3. Below, Corollary 6.7 states a resplendent version of the next theorem.
Theorem 1.3 (Theorem 6.8). Let L3s be the usual three-sorted language of valued fields, let n ≥1 and let M
be the L3s-structure generated by a henselian equi-characteristic valued field (M, v). If M satisfies that all of
its formulas from ∃n are NIP, then (M, v) is separably-defectless Kaplansky and all Lring-formulas from ∃n are
NIP with respect to the theory of Mv. The converse implication holds if Condition 6.5 is satisfied.

2.1
Languages and theories of SAMKe fields
A valued field (K, v) is called separable-algebraically maximal if it does not admit any proper separable
immediate algebraic extension, and it is called Kaplansky if char(Kv) = 0 or char(K) = p > 0, vK is p-divisible
and Kv does not admit any finite extension of degree divisible by p. We will use the notations introduced in
Section 2 of [Mor25], which gathers a more detailed introduction to separable-algebraically maximal Kaplansky
fields. Throughout this document, this class will be denoted by SAMK. Unless stated otherwise, we will work
with three sorted languages for valued fields, with sorts K for the home field, k for the residue field and G for the
value group. If L is any such language, we write L (K) (resp. L (k), L (G)) to mean the language associated
to the sort K (resp. k and G), and we always assume that any such L contains a function symbol v : K →G to
be interpreted as a valuation, a function symbol res : K →k to be interpreted as the associated residue map,
and we also assume that L (K) and L (k) contain the language of rings Lring and L (G) contains the language
of ordered groups Log together with a symbol ∞for a point at infinity. In order to stress the dependence on
the auxiliary languages L (k) and L (G), we will even write L = L (Lk, LG) to mean that L (k) = Lk and
L (G) = LG for a given couple of languages Lk and LG containing Lring and Log ∪{∞} respectively. Let
L3s = L3s(Lk, LG) be the three sorted language for valued fields, possibly expanded in L3s(G) and L3s(k),
i.e. where Lk and LG are allowed to have more symbols than Lring and Log ∪{∞} respectively. Define Lac as

2.2
Fragments and NIP Formulas
Definition 2.3. Let L be a language.
• An L -fragment is a set of L -formulas closed under ∧and ∨, containing ⊤and ⊥. If ∆0 is a set of
L -formulas, the L -fragment generated by ∆0 is the intersection of all the L -fragments that contain ∆0.
• If ∆0 is a set of L -formulas, we denote by ±∆0 the L -fragment generated by ∆0 ∪{¬φ : φ ∈∆0}.
• Given a language L and some n ≥1, we denote by ∃n (resp. ∀n) the set of L -formulas in prenex normal
form, of quantifier rank n, whose outermost quantifier is ∃(resp. ∀). Note that the sets ∃n and ∀n are, up to
logical equivalence, closed under conjunctions and disjunctions, i.e. they form L -fragments for any language
L . We call these sets the classical fragments.
Though a fragment is not formally a set of equivalence classes of formulas, for most of our purposes we do not
make the distinction between a formula φ actually belonging to a fragment ∆and the formula φ being logically
equivalent to a formula from ∆. Note that if ∆is an L -fragment closed under negations, then ±∆= ∆. In
Item 3 of the last definition, we adopted Cherlin’s notation for the set of ∃n-formulas, cf. [Che76, Definition 28].
This notation is not standard in the literature, see e.g. Anscombe and Fehm’s [AF24, Definition 3.1] where the
same notation represents a different fragment.

In this section we revisit the so-called NIP transfer principle for valued fields of equal characteristic, [AJ24,
Proposition 4.1], building on Sections 2 and 3 of [JS20].
As mentioned in the introduction, although the
statement of the transfer principle is correct, we found that the proofs given in [JS20, Lemma 3.2] and [AJ24,
Proposition 4.1] are not accurate as stated, and that a small but consequential modification of the argument
is required. We study this transfer principle in a language expanded by the parameterized λ-functions, and
restate the conditions SE and Im accordingly in Definition 4.5. We record an updated transfer principle in
Corollary 4.14, and to give a corrected and self-contained proof of [JS20, Lemma 3.2] and [AJ24, Proposition

Remark 4.1. The issue in the proof of [JS20, Lemma 3.2] is the following. Let (M, v) be an equi-characteristic
separable-algebraically maximal Kaplansky field of characteristic exponent p and finite imperfection degree e.
Let (K, v) ⪯(M, v) and let b ∈M be some element that is transcendental over K and such that (K(b)|K, v)
is immediate. The authors proceed to find a separable extension eK|K containing b which is also separably
algebraically maximal Kaplansky and which is uniquely determined up to L -isomorphism over K(b). This
extension does not necessarily embed elementarily over K(b) into M, so tp e
K(b/K) and tpM(b/K) may differ:
for instance, as it is constructed, eK contains all pn-th roots of b, while this may not be the case in M. Even if
we considered L = eK ∩M ∩K(b)sep instead of eK alone, the extension M|L is not guaranteed to be separable:
since L|K(b) is algebraic, then M|L is separable if and only if M|K(b) is separable, which in turn is equivalent
to b ̸∈Kp(B) for any p-basis B of K, see e.g. [KP16, Lemma 2.9]. This is the same reason why, in the proof of
[AJ24, Proposition 4.1] for e = ∞, the extension M|L is not necessarily separable. In this case, L is chosen to
be M ∩K(b)sep ∩F, where F is a maximal immediate algebraic extension of K(b).5
In Lemma 4.13 we fix this issue by studying the lambda closure ⟨K, b⟩of K(b) in M instead of just K(b), in
