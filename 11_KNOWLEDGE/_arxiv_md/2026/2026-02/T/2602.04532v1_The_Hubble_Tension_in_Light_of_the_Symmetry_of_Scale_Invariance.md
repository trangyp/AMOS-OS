Abstract: When the expansion rate of the Universe at recombination is used to infer the present
expansion rate H0, the value derived in the ΛCDM model, H0 = 67.4 km s−1 Mpc−1, is about in
6 σ tension with the value measured locally, H0 = 74 km s−1 Mpc−1. In this work, we consider
instead the expansion history in the context of the symmetry of scale-invariant vacuum (SIV model).
We ﬁrst perform two major cosmological tests: the Hubble diagram for type-Ia supernovae and the
fundamental relation between H0, the age of the Universe, and the total density of matter, Ωm. This
allows us to constrain Ωm in SIV, with both tests giving the best agreement for Ωm = 0.20. We then
study the physical connections of the dynamical and thermal states of the Universe at recombina-
tion with the present Hubble constant, H0, and the present temperature, T, in the ΛCDM and SIV
contexts. We ﬁnd that, in SIV, the properties at recombination may be conveyed to the present ones
(T = 2.726 and H0 at z = 0) without any tension, indicating H0 = 74 km s−1 Mpc−1 in spite of
the anchoring on the CMB. This is due to the slightly different expansion and temperature histories
of the two cosmological models. Importantly, this happens to occur for Ωm = 0.20, as constrained
in SIV with supernovae and cosmic age. This suggests that the Hubble tension currently found be-
tween H0 values in the early and late Universe may simply be the result of ΛCDM ignoring the
small but still measurable effects of scale invariance.
Keywords: cosmology; Hubble tension; supernovae; cosmic ages

2.1. Short Basics
The scale-invariant theory assumes general covariance like general relativity; in addi-
tion, it also assumes scale covariance to a form
ds′ = λ ds .
(1)
ds′ is the line element in the space-time of GR, while ds is the scale-transformed line ele-

of λ. The notion of cotensor was introduced by Dirac [17]: mathematical bodies possibly
subject to general covariance and to scale covariance of the form Y′ = λnY. For n = 0 one
speaks of scale invariance and of intensor, invector or inscalar. A cotensoral expression of
the scale-invariant ﬁeld equation has been developed by Dirac [17] and Canuto et al. [24],
it was further conﬁrmed by an action principle [15]. In this context, it is worth to empha-
size some remarks by Einstein [13] in his Autobiographical Notes of 1949: “the existence
of rigid standard rulers is an assumption suggested by our approximate experience, assumption
which is arbitrary in its principle”.
A gauging condition is necessary to constrain the scale factor. Dirac [17] and Canuto
et al. [24] adopted the so-called large number hypothesis. It was preferred to adopt as a
gauge condition that “the macroscopic empty space must be scale invariant” [14], which
implies a gauge factor λ ∼1/t. Moreover, this gauging condition is also demanded by the
above mentioned action principle [15].

3. Confronting SIV with Observations
We now confront the expansion history of the Universe predicted in the SIV theory
to two key tests: 1- The Hubble diagram of type Ia supernovae, 2- The relation between
H0, the age of the Universe τ0 and Ωm. Further tests of the cosmological expansion history
are being performed and will be the subject of future works to comply with our second re-
quirement in the introduction “The new model should not do worse than standard ΛCDM
in describing all other cosmological observations”.
3.1. Testing SIV with the Hubble Diagram of SN Ia
Type Ia supernovae are standard candles used in the distance ladder method. They
allowed us to measure the accelerated expansion rate of the Universe [32,33]. Because type
Ia supernovae are part of a ladder, the method is subject to potential errors propagating in
the consecutive steps of the ladder. For this reason, they do not stand alone as a measure-
ment method for H0. However, they constrain the density of matter Ωmwell. This is what
is performed in the SIV paradigm without trying to measure H0.
The data set considered is the one compiled by Pierel et al. [34] that includes Pantheon
+ SH0ES [35] and two lensed high-redshift supernovae extending the redshift range to
z = 2.9. Using Equation (15), we perform a Bayesian ﬁt to the distance modulus model
for SIV, where the free parameters are the matter density, Ωm, and an arbitrary constant,
K, in the distance modulus. The results are shown in Figure 2, leading to Ωm= 0.18 ± 0.03.

4. The Past Thermal and Dynamical Evolutions in ΛCDM and SIV Models and the
Possible Origin of the Hubble Tension
As mentioned above, the Hubble tension is the discrepancy between the Hubble pa-
rameter H0 = 74 ± 2 km s−1 Mpc−1 determined from supernovae of type Ia at redshifts z
generally below 2.5, and the value H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km s−1 Mpc−1 obtained from the analy-
sis of CMB ﬂuctuations. The tension appears as a highly signiﬁcant cosmological problem
[4,5,12], which could possibly ﬁnd its origin in the physics of the early Universe and its
relation to later phases.
We now consider the problem of the Hubble tension within the SIV theory. To do this,
we closely compare the past thermal and dynamical cosmological evolutions of ΛCDM
and of SIV models, in particular we examine the expansion rates H(z) and temperatures
T in the two models. For SIV theory, we recall the rather simple analytical relations which
govern the evolution properties above. For the ΛCDM model, we apply the analytical
relations developed in Appendix A.

ΛCDM and SIV models, suggests that the origin of the Hubble tension resides in the way
the physical conditions at recombination are connected by the ΛCDM model to the present
ones in terms of H(z) and temperature evolutions. The account of an additional symme-
try, scale invariance, introduces rather minor but measurable effects and appears as an
interesting possible solution to the tension.
5. Conclusions
We conclude that if the thermal and dynamical properties of the CMB at T = 3000 K
are used to infer the present expansion rate, H0, the value derived within SIV is
H0 = 74 km s−1 Mpc−1 for a speciﬁc SIV matter density of Ωm= 0.2. This last value is
supported by the interpretation of the Hubble diagram of type Ia supernovae in the SIV
context and independently by the diagram that compares H0, Ωm and the age of the Uni-
verse. For that value of Ωm, the expansion rates of the Universe at T = 3000 K in SIV and
ΛCDM coincide almost exactly, implying a similar thermal and dynamical state. Thus,
connecting this state to the present time using the ΛCDM model leads to H0=67.4 km s−1
Mpc−1, while the connection using the SIV model instead gives H0=74 km s−1 Mpc−1.
About the major question raised in the introduction whether some scale-invariant ef-
fects are present in our low density Universe, the present results suggest a positive answer.
For the above mean density, some, in fact very limited, effects remain of what they would
be in the empty space. As a consequence, the Hubble tension currently found between
H0 values in the early and late Universe may simply be the result of ΛCDM ignoring the
small but still measurable effects of scale invariance.
