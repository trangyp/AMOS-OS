Abstract
We study approximation algorithms for Maximum Constraint Satisfaction Problems (Max-
CSPs) under differential privacy (DP) where the constraints are considered sensitive data.
Information-theoretically, we aim to classify the best approximation ratios possible for a given
privacy budget ε. In the high-privacy regime (ε ≪1), we show that any ε-DP algorithm can-
not beat a random assignment by more than O(ε) in the approximation ratio. We devise a
polynomial-time algorithm which matches this barrier under the assumptions that the instances
are bounded-degree and triangle-free. Finally, we show that one or both of these assumptions
can be removed for specific CSPs–such as Max-Cut or Max k-XOR–albeit at the cost of compu-
tational efficiency.

Introduction
Maximum Constraint Satisfaction Problems (Max-CSPs) play a fundamental role in modeling and
solving a wide range of complex real-world challenges, from scheduling and planning to configu-
ration and optimization [CS97, BPS99, SGB08, Ghe13]. Given n Boolean variables x1, x2, · · · , xn ∈
{−1, +1}, an instance of constraint satisfaction problem is a set of m constraints, where each con-
straint is a predicate applied to a constant size subset of these variables. The task is to assign each
variable a Boolean value that maximizes the number of constraints satisfied. We say that a random-
ized algorithm achieves ρ-approximation if it outputs an assignment whose expected number of
satisfied constraints is at least ρ times that of the optimum. Max-CSPs are one of the most well stud-
ied problems in approximation algorithm literature (e.g. [GW94, KZ97, Hås01, KKMO07, ST09,
Rag08, RS09, Cha13]). An example of Max-CSP is the Max-kXOR problem where each constraint
is of the form xi1 ⊕· · · ⊕xik = y. A special case of Max-kXOR is Max-Cut: Given an undirected
graph, find a set of vertices S that “cuts” as many edges as possible, where an edge is considered to
be cut if exactly one of its endpoint belongs to S. Max-Cut can be viewed as a special case of Max
2-XOR where the right hand side of the constraint y is always -1.

Given its importance and wide-ranging applications, ensuring the privacy of sensitive data
involved in Max-CSP is of particular significance, particularly in domains such as personal data
processing. For example, some notable examples of CSPs are equivalent to graph problems–such
as Max-Cut, and graph data including social networks can usually be very sensitive and thus de-
signing private problems on these has already drawn growing attention in recent years. One of the
main privacy notions that has been proposed and used extensively in such graph analysis is differ-
ential privacy (DP) [DMNS06], which has been successfully applied across numerous graph prob-
lems. Key applications include generating private approximations of graph cuts [GRU12, EKKL20,
DMN23, LUZ24, CDFZ24], spectral properties [BBDS12, AU19, UUA21], correlation and hierarchi-
cal clustering [BEK21, CFL+22, IEM+23, CAEL+22, DGU+25], as well as in the release of various
numerical graph statistics [KNRS13, US19, DSZ+21, IMC22, SPHH25].
DP is a rigorous mathematical framework designed to provide privacy guarantees for personal
data. Formally, for a privacy budget parameter ε ≥0, a randomized algorithm A is ε-differentially
private1 (ε-DP) if, for every possible output o and any two “neighboring” inputs Φ and Φ′, we have
Pr [A(Φ) = o] ≤eε · Pr

The definition of “neighboring” datasets is critical to this framework, as it determines what
information is considered sensitive and must be protected. To align with practical applications and
the standard notion of edge-level privacy in graphs [HR12, GRU12, BBDS12, DR14, AU19, EKKL20,
UUA21, LUZ24, ACD+25], we consider the constraints themselves to be the “private information”
in this paper, while the number of variables is public. That is, two CSP instances are neighboring
if one can be obtained from the other by adding or removing an arbitrary constraint. Despite its
critical importance, there is a striking lack of literature on deploying differential privacy for CSPs
under such privacy notion, or even for simple special cases like Max-Cut2.
Nevertheless, a trivial approximation algorithm for any CSP is to output a uniformly random
assignment. We use µ to denote the fraction of the constraints satisfied by such a random assign-
ment in expectation. For example, in the Max-kXOR problem (including Max-Cut), a random
assignment satisfies an expected fraction of µ = 1
2 of the constraints. This µ-approximation algo-
rithm is already perfect in privacy (i.e., satisfies DP with privacy budget ε = 0) as it does not require
any information about the constraints. Setting computational complexity aside, the other extreme
occurs when ε tends to infinity, allowing us to achieve arbitrarily close to 1-approximation. A nat-
urally interesting but previously unexplored problem is to classify the best approximation ratio for
CSP in terms of the privacy budget ε beyond these two extremes of no privacy (ε →∞) and perfect
privacy (ε = 0). This is the main question our work aims to address:
What is the best approximation ratio for Max-CSPs achievable by ε-DP algorithms?
Before we continue, we stress that the above question is interesting even without any compu-
tational complexity consideration. Indeed, our impossibility results (i.e. upper bounds on the
approximation ratios) will be all information theoretic (via privacy requirement). Nevertheless,
as highlighted below, some of our algorithms actually run in polynomial time.

1.1
Our Contributions
We make some initial progresses towards answering the aforementioned questions. Specifically,
we identify two regimes of ε which results in different approximation ratios:
• High-Privacy Regime (ε ≪1): In this regime, we show that the approximation ratio can be
at most µ + O(ε). In other words, the “advantage” we can get over the random assignment
is at most O(ε). We devise a polynomial-time algorithm matching this bound for bounded-
degree and triangle-free instances. Finally, we show that these assumptions can sometimes
be removed for Max-Cut and Max-kXOR, although the algorithms either become inefficient
or achieve worse advantage over the random assignment. Our results for this regime are
summarized in Table 1.
• Low-Privacy Regime (ε ≫1): We show here that we can nearly achieve the optimum, with a
“deficit” of Θ(1/ε). Furthermore, the best approximation ratio is no better than 1 −Θ(1/eε).
We next explain these results in more detail, starting with the simplest setting: Low-privacy.
1.1.1
Low-Privacy Regime
On the algorithmic (lower bound on the approximation ratio) side, perhaps the most direct ap-
proach is to use the well known Exponential Mechanism (EM) [MT07] that samples an assignment
of n Boolean variables according to a specific distribution decided by the constraints. Standard
analysis yields an additive approximation error of O(n/ε) compared to the optimal number of sat-
isfiable constraints. This does not yet translate to any approximation ratio, i.e. when the number
of constraints is o(n). Nevertheless, we observe that, if there is any isolated variable (with no con-
straints), then the resulting distribution from EM (on the remaining variables) remains the same.
As such, we may assume w.l.o.g. that no variable is isolated, implying that there are Ω(n) con-
straints. This, together with the aforementioned additive error bound, gives the following:
Corollary 1. For any ε > 0, there is an ε-DP (1 −O(1/ε))-approximation algorithm for Max-CSPs.
Complementing this, we also present the optimal “deficit” we can hope for:
Theorem 2 (Informal version of Theorem 36, Section 6.1). For any ε > 0 and 0 ≤δ ≤1, there is no
(ε, δ)-DP algorithm that achieves (1 + δ −Ω(1/eε))-approximation for Max-CSPs.
1.1.2
High-Privacy Regime: General Max-CSPs
We now move on to the more interesting yet more challenging small-ε regime. Recall again that
we let µ to be the (expected) fraction of constraints satisfied by a random assignment, which is
perfectly private. In this regime, we show that one can only get an “advantage” of at most O(ε)
over the random assignment:
Theorem 3 (Informal version of Theorem 36, Section 6.1). For any ε > 0 and 0 ≤δ ≤1, there is no
(ε, δ)-DP algorithm that achieves (µ + δ + O(ε))-approximation for Max-CSPs.

Max-CSP instances. Nevertheless, we can obtain such a bound for a special type of instances. To
do this, we turn to the approximation algorithm literature, where there is a wealth of techniques
to beat random assignments [She92, HV02, KN07, BMO+15]. Specifically, Barak et al. [BMO+15]

Technical Overview
Here, we introduce the technical ingredients that underpin our results on private CSP approxima-
tion with multiplicative error, and outline the key ideas behind our privacy and utility analysis.
To improve readability, we first illustrate our approach in the graph setting, where the goal is to
approximate the Max-Cut with an approximation ratio better than 1/2. This special case already
captures the core of our technical ideas. We then discuss how the ideas extend to general CSPs.
We present the technical elements by first considering the simplest yet most restrictive setting,
assuming bounded degree and triangle-freeness. We then describe how both assumptions can be
relaxed and outline the mechanism for general graphs, which achieves nearly optimal utility.

Preliminaries
3.1
Constraint Satisfaction Problem
An instance of constraint satisfaction problem Φ consists of n Boolean variables and m constraints.
We denote the n variables by x1, · · · , xn and each xi takes values in {±1}. Each constraints is
denoted by (Pi, Si) where each Pi : {±1}k →{0, 1}, is a predicate of arity at most k and Si its scope
i.e. the ordered list of indices from [n]. Throughout, we assume k to be a constant. A constraint is
said to be satisfied if Pi(xSi) = 1, where xSi are variables restricted to indices in Si. The Max CSP is
an optimization problem where the goal is to assign values to variables to maximize the number
or total weight of satisfied constraints. For the special case of Max-kXOR, all constraints are XORs
of k variables, i.e. for Si = {1, · · · , k}, Pi({x1, · · · , xk}) = x1 ⊕x2 ⊕· · · ⊕xk. The degree of variable
xi, denoted by deg(xi), is the number of constraints xi is part of and we assume deg(xi) ≤D for
all i.

6.1
Multiplicative Upper Bounds

the constraints of Max-Cut do not have negations of variables. For completeness, we will therefore
prove a separate hardness result specifically for Max-Cut.

Conclusion and Open Questions
In this work, we instantiate the general study of approximation algorithms for Max-CSPs under
differential privacy where the privacy protection is at the constraint level. While we have made
some initial progresses towards understanding this question, our study also leaves several impor-
tant open questions, which we highlight below.
• Closing Gaps for All CSPs: In the (more interesting) high-privacy regime (ε ≤1), we have
established an upper bound (impossibility result) of µ + O(ε) on the approximation ratio.
However, we only present algorithms that (nearly) match this bound only in certain cases
(such as Max-Cut and Max-kXOR for odd k). The obvious open question here is to devise an
algorithm that works for all Max-CSPs and matches the µ + O(ε) upper bound.
38


## Page 39


• Computational Efficiency:
As discussed in the introduction, it is in general impossible to
hope for a polynomial-time algorithm for Max-CSPs with an approximation ratio better than
random (i.e. µ) since this is NP-hard for certain CSPs (e.g. [Cha13]). Nevertheless, some
other CSPs are known to admit better-than-µ approximation in polynomial time, including
the celebrated Goemans-Williamson algorithm for Max-Cut [GW94]. For such CSPs, it would
be interesting to achieve an efficient better-than-µ approximation with DP as well.
• Low-Privacy Regime: Although the low-privacy regime (ε > 1) is generally less studied in
the literature, we nevertheless point out that there is still an exponential gap on the “deficit”
in our upper bound of 1 −O(1/eε) and lower bound of 1 −O(1/ε). It remains an interesting
question to close this gap.
