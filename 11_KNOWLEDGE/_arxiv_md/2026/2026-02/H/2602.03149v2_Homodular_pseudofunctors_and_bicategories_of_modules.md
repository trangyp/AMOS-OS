Introduction
The universal property for the Bénabou bicategory of distributors (although we
call them “modules”) presented here is somewhat implicitly spread over the series
of papers [7, 23, 3, 13, 14, 28, 30, 31, 4, 9] and yet, to my knowledge, does not
appear in print. The inclusion of a bicategory W into the bicategory W -Mod of
W -enriched categories and modules between them does have a completion property
with respect to freely adjoining lax colimits (collages); see [31, 8]. What we have
in mind is an objective version of the notion of homological functor used by André
Joyal in [18].
Here we are interested in the universal property of the construction of W -Mod
from W -Cat.
As we pointed out in [30], cofibrations appear naturally in this
construction, so it is not surprising that they play a role here. In the first section,
we revue cofibrations in bicategories and related concepts. In the second section we
interpret all that for enriched categories. We will restrict to the case where the base
W has but one object and indeed reduces to a monoidal category V so that we can
refer to Kelly’s book [21] for background. The usual inclusion V -Cat →V -Mod
taking each V -functor to a left adjoint V -module has certain properties which
make it a homodular pseudofunctor in a sense defined in the third section; indeed,
it is the universal such with domain V -Cat.
In Section 4 we obtain the just mentioned universality as a special case of
universality for a slightly modified version of pro-arrow equipment K →M in the
sense of Richard Wood [33]; we use the term “module equipment”. For example, the
inclusion M∗→M of the subbicategory of left adjoint morphisms into a finitary
cosmos M (in the sense of [9]) is universal homodular with domain M∗.

Universal homodularity and equipment
Definition 4.1. A homodular pseudofunctor S : K →M is universal when, for
each homodular pseudofunctor T : K →X , there is a pseudofunctor ¯T : M →X
which is unique up to equivalence with the property ¯T ◦S ≃T.
A pseudofunctor E : K →M is essentially surjective on objects when, for
each object X ∈M , there exists an object K ∈K with EK ≃X.
Proposition 4.2. Universal homodular pseudofunctors are essentially surjective
on objects.
Proof. Suppose S : K →D is a universal homodular pseudofunctor. Factor it
as S ≃F ◦E where E : K →M is the identity on objects and F : M →D is
fully faithful. By Example 3.2, E is homodular. Since S is universal, there exists
a pseudofunctor R : D →M with R ◦S ≃E. Then F ◦R ◦S ≃1D ◦S and
the uniqueness clause for universality imply F ◦R ≃1D. By uniqueness of the
factorization, R ◦F ◦E ≃E and F ◦R ◦F ≃F imply R ◦F ≃1M . So F is a
biequivalence with the result that S ≃F ◦E is essentially bijective on objects.
Recall from Section 6 of [9] that an finitary cosmos is a bicategory M possessing
(a) finite bicategorical coproducts;
(b) a Kleisli object for every monad;
(c) finite colimits in the hom categories preserved by composition on both sides.
As mentioned atop page 266 of [9], the construction proving Proposition 1 of [31]
shows that any finite cosmos admits finite collages.
The following definition connects with the notion of proarrow equipment due
to Wood [32, 33]. Indeed, as pointed out by Nathaneal Akor, the axioms of [27]
imply that M is a finitary cosmos.
