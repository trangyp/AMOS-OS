Abstract
Preordering is a generalization of clustering and partial ordering with applications in
bioinformatics and social network analysis. Given a finite set V and a value cab ∈R for
every ordered pair ab of elements of V , the preordering problem asks for a preorder ≲
on V that maximizes the sum of the values of those pairs ab for which a ≲b. Building
on the state of the art in solving this NP-hard problem partially, we contribute new
partial optimality conditions and efficient algorithms for deciding these conditions. In
experiments with real and synthetic data, these new conditions increase, in particular,
the fraction of pairs ab for which it is decided efficiently that a ̸≲b in an optimal
preorder.

Introduction
A preorder is a binary relation on a set that is reflexive and transitive. Given a finite set V and a
value cab ∈R for every ordered pair ab ∈V 2 such that a ̸= b, the preordering problem asks for
a preorder ≲on V that maximizes the total value P
a≲b,a̸=b cab. See Fig. 1 for an example. The
preordering problem has applications in bioinformatics (Jacob et al., 2008; B¨ocker et al., 2009)
and social network analysis (Irmai et al., 2026). It is closely related to clustering and ordering:
In clustering, the feasible solutions can be identified with equivalence relaxations, i.e. symmetric
preorders. In partial ordering, the feasible solutions are anti-symmetric preorders. A preorder
need neither be symmetric nor anti-symmetric. Yet, the symmetric subset of a preorder defines
a clustering, and the anti-symmetric subset well-defines a partial order on the clusters (Fig. 1).
Thus, preordering is both a hybrid and a joint relaxation of clustering and partial ordering (Irmai
et al., 2026). At the same time, preordering remains NP-hard, even for values in {−1, 1} (Weller
et al., 2012).
We attempt to solve the preordering problem partially by deciding efficiently for some pairs
ab ∈V 2 with a ̸= b whether a ≲b in an optimal preorder. Using a technique introduced by
Shekhovtsov (2013, 2014) and applied, e.g., by Shekhovtsov et al. (2015), we prove new partial
optimality conditions for the preordering problem that can be verified by establishing that certain
maps from the feasible set to itself are improving (as made rigorous in Def. 4 below). For these
decision problems, we propose efficient algorithms. To examine the effectiveness and efficiency
of these algorithms and thus the effectiveness of the underlying partial optimality conditions, we
conduct experiments with real and synthetic data. Compared to the state of the art in solving the
preordering problem partially, the new conditions increase, in particular, the fraction of pairs ab for
which it is decided efficiently that a ̸≲b in an optimal preorder. All proofs are deferred to Sec. A.

Related Work
The preordering problem in the form of Def. 1 is stated by Wakabayashi (1998) who establishes
NP-hardness and discusses specializations with additional constraints. The state of the art in
solving this problem partially is by the conditions, bounds and algorithms of B¨ocker et al. (2009).
Here, we prove their partial optimality conditions via improving maps (Shekhovtsov, 2013, 2014)
and establish a more general condition that subsumes these.
More specific is the transitivity editing problem that asks for the minimum number of edges to
insert into or remove from a given digraph in order to make it transitive. This problem is isomorphic
to the preordering problem with values in {−1, 1} and remains NP-hard (Weller et al., 2012).
Partial optimality conditions for the transitivity editing problem are established and analyzed also
by Weller et al. (2012), including conditions transferred from B¨ocker et al. (2009) and additional
conditions specific to this problem.
More restrictive than the preordering problem are the clique partition problem (Gr¨otschel et al.,
1984) and the correlation clustering problem (Bansal et al., 2004) whose feasible solutions are
equivalence relations. Partial optimality conditions for these problems established and studied
by Alush and Goldberger (2012); Lange et al. (2018, 2019); Stein et al. (2023). Some partial
optimality conditions we define relate to theirs, and we point out these relations below. To describe
clusterings constrained by partial optimality, Horˇn´akov´a et al. (2017) introduce the notion of
maximally specific partial characterizations. We adapt their definition to preorders.
Also more restrictive than the preordering problem is the partial ordering problem (M¨uller,
1996) for which partial optimality has not been discussed specifically, to the best of our knowledge.
More constrained still is the linear ordering problem (Mart´ı and Reinelt, 2011) for which partial
optimality conditions are established by Stein and Andres (2024). The conditions we define do not
relate directly to theirs because the totality constraint changes the problem fundamentally.
A branch-and-cut algorithm for the preordering problem in the framework of Gurobi Optimiza-
tion, LLC (2023) is defined by Irmai et al. (2026). Local search algorithms for the preordering
problem are defined by B¨ocker et al. (2009); Irmai et al. (2026). The geometry of the preorder
polytope is studied by Gurgel (1992); Gurgel and Wakabayashi (1997).

Experiments
We examine the algorithms defined in Sec. 6 for deciding the partial optimality conditions established
in Sec. 5 empirically, on three collections of instances of the preordering problem. We report

7.1
Synthetic Instances
For a systematic study, we synthesize instances wrt. a preorder x′ and a design parameter α ∈[0, 1].
Values cpq are drawn from two Gaussian distributions with means +1 −α and −1 + α, the first in

Fig. 5 shows the percentage of variables fixed and the runtime, both as a function of α, for
the cut conditions Thm. 1, Cor. 2 and Cor. 5 (b = 0), and the join conditions Thm. 2, Thm. 3
(together with Prop. 4) and Cor. 5 (b = 1) separately. All join conditions are applied on top of
partial optimality from the cut conditions. It can be seen that Thm. 1 fixes more variables to
zero than Cor. 2 consistently but requires more than two orders of magnitude longer to apply.
Cor. 5 (b = 0) and Thm. 1 are similarly effective in fixing variables to zero and take similar time to

variables are fixed for instances with sufficiently large α. It can be seen from Fig. 7 that the time
for applying the conditions jointly is polynomial in |V |.
7.2
Social Networks
Toward real applications, we examine partial optimality for instances of the preordering problem
defined wrt. published Twitter and Google+ ego networks (Leskovec and Mcauley, 2012). We
define these instances such that cij = 1 if i follows j, and cij = −1, otherwise. We analyze all
Twitter instances and those Google+ instances with |V | ≤250. The results for Google+ are similar
to those for Twitter and are thus deferred to Sec. D, in particular Fig. D.9.
For Twitter, Fig. 8 shows the percentage of variables fixed and the runtime, both as a function
of |V |, for applications of cut conditions, Thm. 1, Cor. 2 and Cor. 5 (b = 0), and join conditions,
Thm. 2, Thm. 3 (together with Prop. 4) and Cor. 5 (b = 1), separately and jointly. Also here, all
join conditions are applied on top of partial optimality from the cut conditions. It can be seen that
Thm. 1 fixes more variables to zero (median: 29.6%) than Cor. 2 (median: 16.7%) consistently, but
takes two orders of magnitude more time to apply. Both Thm. 1 and Cor. 2 are generally more
effective in fixing variables to zero than Cor. 5 (b = 0). Fewer variables are fixed to one, but each
join condition fixes some variables.
For a direct comparison of Cor. 5 to all conditions applied jointly, see Sec. E.
