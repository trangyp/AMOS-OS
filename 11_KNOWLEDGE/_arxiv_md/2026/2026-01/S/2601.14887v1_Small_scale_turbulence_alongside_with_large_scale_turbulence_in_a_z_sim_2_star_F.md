FORMING GALAXY WITH OUTFLOWING WIND, REVEALED BY MULTI-POINT STRUCTURE

Abstract
Recently, Goldman (2024) used observations by Vasan G. C . K., et al. (2024) and obtained evidence
for a large scale compressible, Burgers turbulence in the ism of CSWA13, a gravitationally lensed,
star-forming galaxy at z = 1.87, with an outflowing wind,
Goldman (2024) analyzed the residual line of sight velocity field of the nebular gas, derived from
by the C|||] emission line, and the residual outflowing wind velocity field obtained by observations of
the Si∗
|| florescent emission line. The two velocity fields are functions of the position along the major
galactic axis,
The turbulent timescale on the largest spatial scale has been found to be ∼500Myr. This together
with the large spatial scale of ∼6.4kpc suggest a large scale generating mechanism (such as tidal
interaction or merger) that lasted for a time ∼500Myr. On the other hand, the outflowing wind is
much younger and is probably the result of the intense star formation. Therefore, could it be that the
star formation drives also turbulence on small scales?
In the present work we utilize multi-point second order structure functions (Cho 2019; Seta &
Federrath 2024) to find whether there exists also a small scale turbulence in this galaxy, and if so, try
to identify its drivers.
We obtained evidence for small scale turbulence whose largest spatial scale is ls ∼240pc for the
nebular gas velocity field and ls ∼290pc for the outflowing wind velocity field. These values suggest
that stellar sub clumps or giant star clusters with an high concentration of young massive stars could
be responsible for both the outflow and for the small scale turbulence.
Subject headings: galaxy evolution, interstellar turbulence, galactic outflows
1. INTRODUCTION
High redshift galaxies are characterized by high star
formation rates as well as outflowing winds, generated by
the young stars or by AGNs, e.g. (Bournaud et al. 2009;
Hoffmann et al. 2022; Rizzo et al. 2021; Sanders et al.
2023; Shah et al. 2022). The high rate of star formation
is attributed to the assembly process of the galaxy, The
gas supply can be in the form of inflow from the circum
galactic medium (CGM) and also by more violent events
such as mergers and tidal interactions.
Observations of high redshift galaxies display velocity
dispersions that are usually interpreted as manifestation
of turbulence e.g. Burkert et al. (2010). It has been ar-
gued that accretion onto disk galaxies can generate large
scale turbulence, in particular at the disk outskirts, e.g.
(Forbes et al. 2023; Goldman & Fleck 2023). Turbulence
can be generated also by mergers and tidal interactions.
To establish the existence of turbulence and moreover,
to understand its nature, a power spectrum or structure
function of the velocity field are needed. This in turn, de-
mands observations with high enough spatial resolution
which, for galaxies at high redshifts, are challenging.
Gravitational lensing can help in this regard. A recent
paper (Vasan G. C . K., et al. 2024) presented a study
of a wind outflow in CSWA13, which is a gravitation-
ally lensed star-forming galaxy at z = 1.87. The gravita-
tional lensing allowed for a substantially improved spatial
and kinematic resolution. The authors obtained, among
other results, two line of sight velocity fields along the
major axis of the galaxy: the nebular gas velocity traced
by the C|||] emission line, that represents also the velocity
of the young stars embedded in the nebular gas (as was
validated by Rhoades et al. (2025), and the outflowing
wind velocity traced by the Si∗
|| florescent emission line.
Each of these velocity fields , exhibits a large scale shear.
In Goldman (2024) we used the residual velocity fields
(after subtracting the large scale shear and the remain-
ing mean) and computed power spectra and 2 point sec-
ond order structure functions. The latter. indicated the
existence of a large scale (∼6.4kpc) compressible, Burg-
ers turbulence. The timescale of the turbulence on the
largest spatial scale turned out to be ∼455 Myr for the
nebular gas velocity field and ∼523 Myr for the out-
flowing wind velocity field. The large spatial scale of the
turbulence as well as the large timescales suggest that the
generating mechanism of the turbulence is a large scale
one: a merger or tidal interaction with another galaxy.

scale.

a plateau, the case for the existence of small scale turbu-

at the plateau equals twice the standard deviation of the
small scale turbulence velocity.

Lee et al. (2025) applied multi-point structure func-

probably generated by a tidal interaction with the Large

2000).

5. DISCUSSION
We utilized multi-point structure functions to study
small scale turbulence in this galaxy, As a first step we
derived continues fits to each of the discrete velocity fields
of Goldman (2024): the residual nebular gas velocity and
the residual outflowing wind velocity.. Then, the multi-
point structure functions for each were obtained, using
integrations of the fitted velocity fields to evaluate the
averages appearing in the definitions of the multi point
structure functions.
This study led to the conclusion that in addition to the
large scale turbulence studied by Goldman (2024) there
exists also a small scale turbulence.
The nebular gas
velocity field has a small scale turbulence with largest
scale ls = 240 pc and a turbulent velocity on this scale
of 1.86km/s.
The turbulent velocity of the large scale turbulence at
this spatial scale is evaluated using the power spectrum
obtained in Goldman (2024). In the range of dimension-
less wavernumbers 1 ≤q ≤1.5 the power spectrum scales
as q−2 and the turbulent velocity scales as q−0.5. For
q > 1, 5 the power spectrum scales as q−3 and the tur-
bulent velocity scales as q−1, The turbulent velocity on
the largest scale (that corresponds to q = 1) is 13.9km/s.
Thus, the turbulent velocity of the large scale turbulence
at ls = 240pc is
v = 13.9km/s1.5−0.5 (ls/(6.43kpc/1.5)) = 0, .64km/s

The small scale turbulence velocity at the spatial scale
ls is a factor of ]sim2.9 larger than that of the large scale

The timescale of the large scale turbulence at ls is thus
∼2.9 times larger than that of the small scale turbulence.
So, the rate of energy cascaded by at ls by the small scale
turbulence is ∼2.93 ∼24 times larger than that of the
large scale turbulence on that spatial scale.
So the small scale turbulence dominates on the small
spatial scale over the large scale turbulence.

6. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
Goldman (2024) found evidence for a large scale (6.43
kpc) compressible turbulence in the ism of the galaxy
CSWA13 t a (redshift z = 1.87). That turbulence was
found in both the velocity field of the nebular gas and in
the velocity field of the outflowing wind. The timescale
of the turbulence on the largest scale is 455Myr for the
velocity field of the nebular gas and 523 Myr for the
velocity field of the outflowing wind.
In the present work we obtained evidence for a small
scale turbulence (largest spatial scale ls = 240 pc for
the nebular gas velocity field and ls = 290 pc for the
outflowing wind velocity field). For each of the velocity
