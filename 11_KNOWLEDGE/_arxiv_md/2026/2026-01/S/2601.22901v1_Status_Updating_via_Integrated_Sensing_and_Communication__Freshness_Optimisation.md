Status Updating via Integrated Sensing and
Communication: Freshness Optimisation
Touraj Soleymani, Mohamad Assaad, and John S. Baras
Abstract—This paper studies strategic design in an integrated
sensing and communication (ISAC) architecture for status up-
dating of remotely navigating agents. We consider an ISAC-
enabled base station that can sense the state of a remote source
and communicate this information back to the source. Both
sensing and communication succeed with given probabilities
and incur distinct costs. The objective is to optimise a long-
term cost that captures information freshness, measured by the
age of information (AoI), at the source together with sensing
and communication overheads. The resulting sequential decision
problem is formulated as a discounted inﬁnite-horizon Markov
decision process with a two-dimensional AoI state, representing
information freshness at the source and at the base station.
We prove that the optimal stationary policy admits a monotone
threshold structure characterised by a nondecreasing switching
curve in the AoI state space. Our numerical analysis illustrates
the structures of the value function and the optimal decision
map. These results demonstrate that freshness-based objectives
can be naturally integrated into ISAC design, while yielding
interpretable and implementable strategies.
Index Terms—Age of information, integrated sensing and
communication, semantic metrics, Markov decision processes,
remote navigation, optimal strategies.
I. INTRODUCTION
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) has recently
emerged as a foundational paradigm for next-generation wire-
less systems, where sensing and communication functionalities
are jointly designed to share spectral, hardware, and energy
resources. Unlike conventional architectures that treat environ-
mental sensing and data transmission in separate operational
planes, ISAC systems co-design these two functions to achieve
more efﬁcient use of resources. This integration is particu-
larly critical for applications built upon remote navigation—a
concept in which an agent makes motion decisions based on
externally acquired situational information, rather than solely
on its onboard perception. Examples include unmanned ground
vehicles requiring external support for situational awareness
and collision avoidance. In such systems, accurate navigation
hinges on the timely delivery of relevant situational informa-
tion, which must be both sensed and communicated under
stringent reliability and bandwidth constraints.
In remote navigation systems, performance is fundamentally
constrained not only by communication latency, but also by the
freshness of the information available at the decision-making

A. Related Work
Research on the semantics of information and on ISAC has
largely progressed along separate tracks. We brieﬂy review
both areas to position our contributions.
1) Semantics of Information: Kaul et al. [1] introduced the
AoI metric to quantify the timeliness of status updates in real-
time systems. The AoI and its variants have since been studied
extensively under a variety of queueing models and wireless

into scheduling, sampling, and resource allocation for time-
sensitive systems. Beyond the AoI, the value of information
(VoI) has also been introduced as a decision-theoretic notion
of how much a new observation improves closed-loop per-
formance [14]. In particular, Soleymani et al. developed a
theoretical framework for quantifying the VoI in networked
control systems and established optimality conditions for VoI-
driven communication scheduling [15]–[20]. Although the
VoI offers a principled link between sensing, communication,
and control, it can be computationally demanding in high-
dimensional settings.
2) Integrated Sensing and Communication: ISAC has been
proposed as a new paradigm for next-generation wireless sys-
tems. Liu et al. [21] provided a comprehensive survey of joint
radar-communication systems, reviewing applications, system
architectures, and design methodologies. Luong et al. [22] pre-
sented a comprehensive survey on resource management issues
for ISAC systems. Several works have addressed signalling
and waveform design for dual-function radar-communication
systems [23], [24]. In general, ISAC is increasingly viewed
as a key enabler for a range of application domains, including
intelligent transportation, vehicular networks, and IoT systems,
where the joint design of sensing and communication enables
the simultaneous acquisition and exchange of safety-critical
information to improve efﬁciency and reliability [25]–[28].
Despite this progress, much of the ISAC literature remains
centred on physical-layer metrics (e.g., estimation error, de-
tection probability, and throughput) that quantify signal-level
performance but do not directly reﬂect the time-sensitivity of
downstream decisions.
B. Contributions and Outline
In this paper, we adopt the AoI as a freshness metric and
study how sensing and communication in an ISAC system
should be coordinated to maintain timely situational awareness
for a remote source under resource constraints. We formulate
the resulting sequential decision problem as a discounted
inﬁnite-horizon Markov decision process (MDP) with a two-
dimensional AoI state, representing information freshness at
the source and at the base station. Our main contribution is
a structural characterisation of the optimal policy. We prove
that the optimal stationary policy admits a monotone threshold
structure characterised by a nondecreasing switching curve in
the AoI state space. Our numerical analysis also illustrates
the structures of the value function and the optimal decision
map. These results demonstrate that freshness-based objectives
can be naturally integrated into ISAC design, while yielding
interpretable and implementable strategies.
The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Sec-
tion II presents the system model and formulates the problem
of interest. Section III presents the main results of the paper.
Section IV provides numerical results. Lastly, Section V
concludes the paper.

II. PROBLEM FORMULATION
We consider a discrete-time remote navigation setup sup-
ported by an ISAC system. A remote source evolves over time,
but cannot directly observe its state due to limited onboard
sensing. An ISAC-enabled base station can sense the state
of the source and communicate this information back to the
source. The ISAC system operates at each time in either
sensing or communication mode. The goal is to minimise
the long-term expected AoI at the remote source along with
sensing and communication costs.

Theorem 1 establishes that the optimal policy admits a
monotone switching structure. For each αb, the threshold

optimal. The nondecreasing nature of τ(·) reﬂects the fact
that, as the base-station’s information becomes staler, the
system becomes increasingly inclined to sensing rather than
communication. This threshold-based structure reveals that
sensing and communication decisions of the ISAC system
under a freshness-based objective admit an interpretable and
implementable policy geometry.

V. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we studied joint sensing-communication
scheduling in an ISAC architecture for status updating of
remote navigating agents using the AoI as a freshness metric.
We formulated the problem as a discounted inﬁnite-horizon
MDP. We analytically established that the optimal stationary
policy admits a monotone threshold structure in the two-
dimensional AoI state space. This structure follows from
monotonicity and submodularity properties of the value func-
tion, and leads to an interpretable and implementable policy
