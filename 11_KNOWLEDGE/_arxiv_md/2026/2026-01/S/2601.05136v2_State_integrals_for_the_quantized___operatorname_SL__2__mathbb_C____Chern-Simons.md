Abstract
Previous work of the author and N. Reshetikhin deﬁnes an invariant
Zψ
N(K, ρ, µ) of a knot K, a representation ρ: π1(S3 \ K) →SL2(C),
and a logarithm µ of a meridian eigenvalue of ρ. It can be interpreted as a
geometric twist of the Kashaev invariant or as a quantization of the SL2(C)
Chern-Simons invariant and is deﬁned via a discrete state-sum involving
quantum dilogarithms. In this paper we show how to express Zψ
N(K, ρ, µ)
as a sum over contour integrals in a space parametrizing hyperbolic struc-
tures on the knot complement. Such state integral presentations are an
important step in determining the asymptotics of quantum invariants as
predicted by the Volume Conjecture. We discuss this perspective and the
remaining obstacles to establishing exponential growth of Zψ
N.

1. Introduction
1.1. Background and motivation
Kashaev’s Volume Conjecture [Kas97] proposes a striking connection between the geometry of
hyperbolic knots and their quantum invariants. This conjecture appears to be related to quantum
SL2(C) Chern-Simons theory, a TQFT arising as a complexiﬁcation [Guk05] or analytic continu-
ation [Wit10] of the more familiar quantum SU(2) Chern-Simons theory [Wit89]. A number of
authors [BB04; BWY21; KR05; McP25a] have constructed geometric quantum invariants that can
be interpreted as candidates for a mathematical realization of SL2(C) Chern-Simons theory.
We discuss some typical features of a candidate mathematical realization.1 For concreteness
let K be a hyperbolic knot in S3, although some versions are additionally or instead deﬁned for
closed 3-manifolds or related objects. A realization is a family of functions

1.3. Cyclic and principal type invariants
There appear to be two inequivalent (but perhaps both natural) branches of complex Chern-Simons
theory. Above we described cyclic type invariants [BB04; BWY21; KR05; McP25a] that
• are deﬁned as a sum over a ﬁnite state space,
• are conjectured to exponentially grow as N →∞, and
• are well-deﬁned for any (or any geometrically non-degenerate) hyperbolic structure.
In contrast, principal type invariants [AK14; AK13; Liu+25]
• are deﬁned using integrals over a noncompact, continuous state space,
• are conjectured to decay as N →∞, but
• are only deﬁned when the hyperbolic structure satisﬁes a positivity condition so that the
integrals converge.
These two branches are related to diﬀerent quantizations of the moduli space of SL2(C) local
systems. Principal type invariants are related to the representations of quantum cluster varieties
deﬁned by Kashaev [Kas99] and Fock and Goncharov [FG09]. These are inﬁnite-dimensional
representations analogous to the principal series representations of SL2. The cyclic quantizations
studied in detail by Ishibashi [Ish26] are ﬁnite-dimensional and analogous to highest-weight mod-
ules. Cyclic type invariants are more directly related to the Kashaev invariant and the original
Volume Conjecture. It is typically easier to prove volume conjectures for principal type invari-
ants: they are deﬁned in terms of integrals, so Step 1 holds automatically, subject to the positivity
condition.
Asymptotic growth of cyclic type invariants has been established only for knots with up to 7
crossings [Oht16; Oht17; OY18] and for twist knots [CZ23]. For principal type invariants much
more is known: the invariant of Liu, Ming, Sun, Wu, and Yang [Liu+25] satisﬁes the conjecture for
every hyperbolic manifold with totally geodesic boundary, and the Andersen-Kashaev Teichmüller
TQFT satisﬁes it for twist knots [AGP23] and many other manifolds [AW24; AGW25; Won25]. We
hope that our result leads to a proof of asymptotic growth of Zψ
N using similar techniques.
In addition, our work may lead to a better understanding of the relationship between cyclic
and principal invariants. Andersen and Kashaev [AK13] showed that their principal type TQFT can
be deﬁned using integrals over compact state spaces. This form of the partition function formally
resembles the sum of state integrals (1.3) after a Wick rotation and changing the overall sign of the
action. Their reformulation is accomplished via a Weil-Gel’fand-Zak transform taking values in a
space of quasi-periodic functions. Similar quasi-periodic functions are used in the deﬁnition of
Zψ
N [McP25a] and this may lead to a more precise relationship between the two constructions.
