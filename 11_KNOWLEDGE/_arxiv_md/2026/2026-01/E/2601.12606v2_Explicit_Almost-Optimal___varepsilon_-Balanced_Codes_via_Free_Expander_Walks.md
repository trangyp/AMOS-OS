Abstract
We study the problem of constructing explicit binary codes whose rate and distance match
the Gilbert–Varshamov bound in the low-rate, high-distance regime. In 2017, Ta-Shma [TS17]
gave an explicit family of codes where every pair of codewords has relative distance 1−ε
2 , with
rate Ω(ε2+o(1)), matching the Gilbert–Varshamov bound up to a factor of εo(1). Ta-Shma’s
construction was based on starting with a good code and amplifying its bias with walks arising
from the s-wide-replacement product.
In this work, we give a simpler almost-optimal construction, based on what we call free
expander walks—ordinary expander walks where each step is taken on a distinct expander from
a carefully chosen sequence. This sequence of expanders is derived from the construction of
near-X-Ramanujan graphs due to O’Donnell and Wu [OW20]. We additionally discuss some
additional applications of near-X-Ramanujan graphs to “on average” lossless expansion and
rotating expanders.

Introduction
A longstanding and fundamental open problem in coding theory is to construct binary codes with
the best possible tradeoffs between rate and distance. In this work, we seek to understand the
best possible rate achievable by a code family whose relative distance approaches 1
2. The classical
Gilbert–Varshamov (GV) bound guarantees the existence of a binary code family with relative
distance ⩾1
2 −ε and rate Ω(ε2) — indeed, a random linear code satisfies such a property; this
is tight up to a log 1
ε factor due to the lower bound of [MRRW77] (see also [Alo09]). However,
constructing explicit codes meeting this rate-distance tradeoff is not yet known and represents an
important open problem.
Following [TS17], we seek to construct what are known as ε-balanced codes, where the relative
distance between any pair of codewords is in the interval
 1−ε
2 , 1+ε

. Notice that the property of
being ε-balanced is more restrictive than having relative distance ⩾1
2 −ε, which simply requires
that the relative distance between every pair of codewords is bounded below by 1
2 −ε.
In the context of error-correction, explicit constructions of ε-balanced codes often have struc-
ture that plays well with designing fast decoding algorithms [AJQ+20, JQST20, JST21], a feature
that random linear codes do not enjoy. In pseudorandomness, constructions of ε-balanced codes
immediately give constructions of almost k-wise uniform distribution [NN90], which is an impor-
tant primitive in a myriad of applications: derandomization [NN90], probabilistically checkable
proofs [BSGH+04], constructing PRGs for restricted classes of functions [GMR+12], expander
constructions [MOP20, OW20], and constructing explicit quantum codes [JMO+22] to name a few.
Obtaining explicit constructions of ε-balanced codes achieving the GV bound is an active
thread in coding and pseudorandomness. One popular approach for constructing good codes
is to concatenate a large alphabet code with a small binary code [Jus72]. In the 1
2 −ε relative
distance regime, explicit codes obtained via concatenation achieve rate Ω(ε3), a result traditionally
attributed to Zyablov [Zya71]; see [GRS12, Exercise 10.3] for an English presentation. A second
approach for obtaining good ε-balanced codes, pioneered by Rozenman and Wigderson and
exposited in the lecture notes of Bogdanov [Bog12], is to amplify bias via expander walks. In the
original presentation, such expander walk-based codes obtained only a rate of Ω(ε4). In 2017,
the breakthrough work of Ta-Shma [TS17] showed how to sparsify these expander walks using
a wide replacement product, thereby obtaining an explicit family of ε-balanced codes with rate

factor was recently improved in [CC25].

optimal ε-balanced code with rate Ω(ε2+o(1)).

One of the sources of suboptimality in our construction as well as previous work [TS17, CC25] is
the factor of 2 per step on a Ramanujan graph. If one were to obtain an explicit construction of
ε-balanced codes achieving the GV bound without an εo(1) overhead in the rate based on expander
walks, it would be necessary to circumvent the fact that the operator norm of the adjacency matrix
of any Ramanujan graph has spectral radius ≈
