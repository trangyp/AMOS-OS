Abstract
This paper introduces an efficient perturbed difference-of-convex algorithm (pDCA) for com-
puting d-stationary points of an important class of structured nonsmooth difference-of-convex
problems. Compared to the principal algorithms introduced in [J.-S. Pang, M. Razaviyayn, and
A. Alvarado, Math. Oper. Res. 42(1):95–118 (2017)], which may require solving several sub-
problems for a one-step update, pDCA only requires solving a single subproblem. Therefore, the
computational cost of pDCA for one-step update is comparable to the widely used difference-of-
convex algorithm (DCA) introduced in [D. T. Pham and H. A. Le Thi, Acta Math. Vietnam.
22(1):289–355 (1997)] for computing a critical point. Importantly, under practical assumptions,
we prove that every accumulation point of the sequence generated by pDCA is a d-stationary
point almost surely. Numerical experiment results on several important examples of nonsmooth
DC programs demonstrate the efficiency of pDCA for computing d-stationary points.
Keywords: difference-of-convex programming, directional stationary point, nonconvex optimiza-
tion, nonsmooth optimization.

While DCA and its variants are popular and demonstrate good practical performance in many
applications, they only guarantee to converge to some critical points in general. To address this
issue, Pang et al. [18] advocate for the stronger concept of directional-stationary (d-stationary)
points, where the directional derivative is nonnegative in every feasible direction, and is arguably
the sharpest kind among the various stationary solutions for the DC programs. One can refer
to [3, 18] and the references therein for a more detailed discussion. Moreover, they proposed a
novel revised DCA [18] shown in Algorithm 2 to compute d-stationary points of the nonsmooth
DC programs (1) by developing the ϵ-active index set. In particular, at each iteration k, instead of
solving a single subproblem in DCA, the revised algorithm will solve all strongly convex linearized
subproblems with proximal term (4) identified by an ϵ-active index set (3), and update xk+1 by
selecting the best one with smallest “proximal objective value”. Such an ϵ-active set can identify all
active pieces at the cluster point, which can contribute to guaranteeing a subsequential convergence
to a d-stationary point of the nonsmooth DC programming problem (1). However, the computa-
tional cost for a one-step update could be expensive or even prohibitive for large-scale problems.
On the one hand, identifying the ϵ-active set is not easy in general for large-scale problems. On the
other hand, when the ϵ-active set Mϵ(xk) is large, it requires solving multiple large-scale subprob-
lems. To mitigate the computational challenge, Pang et al. [18] also proposed a randomized variant
(in [18, Sec. 5.2]) that selects a single index from Mϵ(xk) per iteration and solves the correspond-
ing subproblem. Moreover, a safeguard strategy of sufficient decrease condition is included in their
randomized algorithm to ensure the almost sure convergence to a d-stationary point of (1). The
existence of the safeguard strategy may reject many candidate updates due to their violation of
the sufficient decrease condition, potentially largely increasing the total number of subproblems to
be solved. While numerous enhanced versions of Algorithm 2 have been proposed and successfully
computed d-stationary points across various scenarios (e.g., [4,16,19,27,34]), they indeed depend
on the ϵ-active index set technique, which remains computationally expensive for solving large-scale
problems. Therefore, designing a new algorithm that can balance the tradeoff between the theo-
retical sharpness and the computational efficiency for solving large-scale nonsmooth DC problem
(1) is highly desirable.
In this paper, we address this challenge by proposing a (randomly) perturbed difference-of-
convex algorithm (pDCA), which can guarantee a subsequential convergence to a d-stationary
point of the nonsmooth DC program (1) almost surely. Our key innovation is, at each iteration
k, instead of linearizing the concave component ψ(·) in (1) at xk, we linearize ψ(·) at a (tiny)
randomly perturbed point ˆxk, where the set of active gradients reduces to a singleton almost
surely due to Rademacher’s theorem [22, 25]. This allows pDCA to only solve a single strongly
convex subproblem at each iteration. Importantly, under practical assumptions, we prove that the
subsequential convergence of the sequence generated by pDCA to a d-stationary point of (1) almost
surely.
The main contributions of this work are:
• We design a simple yet effective algorithm pDCA, for solving the nonsmooth DC program
(1).
• The proposed pDCA balances the theoretical sharpness and computational efficiency for solv-
ing (1). In particular, the pDCA only solves a single strongly convex subproblem for a one-step
update, and it guarantees a subsequential convergence to a d-stationary point almost surely.

Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed a simple yet effective algorithm pDCA for solving a class of important
nonsmooth DC programs in the form of (1). Benefiting from the tiny random perturbations at each
iteration, the active gradient of the concave component is a singleton almost surely. This allows
pDCA to solve a single strongly convex subproblem for updating the current solution in each
iteration with comparable per-iteration computational cost to DCA. Importantly, under practical
assumptions, we prove that every accumulation point of the sequence generated by pDCA is a
d-stationary point to the nonsmooth DC program (1) almost surely. The efficiency of pDCA for
computing d-stationary points has been demonstrated numerically. Currently, it is not clear to us
whether we can get almost surely sequential convergence of the sequence generated by pDCA to a
d-stationary point of (1) under practical assumptions. We leave this as our future research topic.
