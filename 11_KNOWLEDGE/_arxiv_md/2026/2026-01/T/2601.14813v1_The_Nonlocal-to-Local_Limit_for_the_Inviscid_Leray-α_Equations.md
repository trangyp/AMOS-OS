THE NONLOCAL-TO-LOCAL LIMIT FOR THE INVISCID
LERAY-α EQUATIONS
JULE SCHINDLER AND EMIL WIEDEMANN
Abstract. We consider the inviscid Leray-α equations – an inviscid nonlocal
regularisation of the Euler equations. In the first part, we prove the conver-
gence of strong solutions of the Leray-α equations to strong solutions of the
Euler equations in Hs(Rd) for s > d/2 + 1, d ∈{2, 3}, for a large class of regu-
larising kernels. In the second part, we consider weak solutions on a bounded
domain with a local scaling property far away from the boundary. The scaling
relates to second-order structure functions from turbulence theory and does
not imply regularity. Nonetheless, under these assumptions, the weak solutions
converge to (possibly wild) weak solutions of Euler in L2 for almost every t.

It is not surprising – due to the vorticity formulation – that the Euler-α model
has gained more interest in the literature than the inviscid Leray-α equations.
Apart from [34], there is ample literature about the α-convergence of the Euler-
α equations: e.g. [1] on the two-dimensional torus, [13, 36] on a two-dimensional
bounded domain with Dirichlet boundary conditions, [3] considering vortex sheet
initial data, [12] on the half-plane with no-slip boundary conditions – all of them
making use of the vorticity structure of the two-dimensional system. In particular
in [36], the difference between the viscosity limit of Navier-Stokes and the α-limit
of Euler-α regarding the boundary layer is highlighted. The authors did not end
up with a Kato-like criterion as expected but overcame the boundary layer under
appropriate regularity assumptions.
To our knowledge, there has been no contribution in the literature about the α-
limit of the inviscid Leray-α equations. In [10], Onsager’s conjecture for the inviscid
Leray-α model and other inviscid α-models is studied. For comparisons with other
α-turbulence models see also [27, 28]. In [18], the authors investigate the dimension
of the global attractor and the energy spectrum of the viscous Leray-α model. The
convergence of the Leray-α model to the Navier-Stokes equations is investigated
in [16] and [17].
In the context of sub-grid scale models, the Leray-α model is
treated in [24, 25, 42] and is considered as successful large eddy simulation model
of turbulence for channel and pipe flows [18].

In the second part, we assume the initial data only in L2, thus arguably allowing
for turbulent behaviour. To conclude convergence of weak solutions of the Leray-
α equations, we assume a local scaling property. This idea was used in [19] and
then in [20] to show convergence of a vanishing viscosity sequence of Navier-Stokes
solutions to weak solutions of the Euler equations. The condition is considered far
away from boundaries and problems with expected boundary layers are avoided.
The scaling property, which will be defined in (12), relates to second-order struc-
ture functions from Kolmogorov’s theory of turbulence from 1941 (see [23]). The
theory suggests that in three-dimensional turbulent flow, energy is (on average)
transferred from larger to smaller scales until it is dissipated through viscous ef-
fects.
The range of scales where eddies disaggregate and where the dynamics,
described by the Navier-Stokes equations, is dominated by the nonlinear term is
called inertial range. The scaling of the second-order structure function was orig-
inally inferred from the self-similarity hypothesis of turbulent flow in the inertial
range and is supported by experimental results. Due to the fact that the scaling
property in (12) is only assumed up to η > 0 (which would correspond to the Kol-
mogorov dissipation scale in the case of viscosity), our assumption does not imply
any regularity.
A second important statement of turbulence theory (Kolmogorov’s ‘Zero-th Law’)
is that turbulent flow is dissipative, i.e. it does not conserve energy as strong solu-
tions of Euler or of Leray-α do. Note that Theorem 2 allows for solutions of Euler
that display such anomalous dissipation of energy.

3. Convergence of strong solutions in Hs
In this section, we show Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, i.e. the convergence of
strong solutions of the Leray-α equations to strong solutions of the Euler equations
on the whole space. The proof is based on Masmoudi’s work in [40] where the

authors in [34] utilise the vorticity structure of the Euler-α model to handle the
additional term in the equation compared with the Leray-α model, here, we do not
need to make use of the vorticity.
