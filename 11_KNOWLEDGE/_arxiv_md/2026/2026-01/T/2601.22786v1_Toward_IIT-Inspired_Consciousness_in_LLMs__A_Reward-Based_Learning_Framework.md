Abstract
The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence
(AGI) is a central goal in language model devel-
opment, in which consciousness-like processing
could serve as a key facilitator. While current lan-
guage models are not conscious, they exhibit be-
haviors analogous to certain aspects of conscious-
ness. This paper investigates the implementation
of a leading theory of consciousness—Integrated
Information Theory (IIT)—within language mod-
els via a reward-based learning paradigm. IIT pro-
vides a formal, axiom-based mathematical frame-
work for quantifying consciousness. Drawing
inspiration from its core principles, we formulate
a novel reward function that quantifies a text’s
causality, coherence and integration, characteris-
tics associated with conscious processing. Em-
pirically, it is found that optimizing for this IIT-
inspired reward leads to more concise text gen-
eration. On out of domain tasks, careful tuning
achieves up to a 31% reduction in output length
while preserving accuracy levels comparable to
the base model. In addition to primary task perfor-
mance, the broader effects of this training method-
ology on the model’s confidence calibration and
test-time computational scaling is analyzed. The
proposed framework offers significant practical
advantages: it is conceptually simple, computa-
tionally efficient, requires no external data or aux-
iliary models, and leverages a general, capability-
driven signal rather than task-specific heuristics.
Code available at Github.

in LLMs using IIT, leveraging reinforcement learning (RL)
with a tailored reward function for training. We employ
a reward function—namely, the Φ Reward Function—to
quantify properties associated with a conscious system and
evaluate it on LLMs. According to IIT, the Φ value repre-
sents the degree of a system’s consciousness with respect
to a given state. This study demonstrates that LLMs exhibit
limited performance when trained directly using a Φ-based
reward function.

involved in computing Φ and introduce an novel reward
function aligned with the cause–effect power property, re-
ferred to as the “Intrinsic Information (II) Reward Function”,
which is derived from the information postulate. Experimen-
tal results demonstrate that LLMs trained with this reward
function achieve a substantial reduction in response length
(up to 31%), while maintaining accuracy close to the origi-
nal performance with appropriate hyperparameter selection.

• We propose a principled framework for investigating

• We propose a novel reward function that reduces re-

that relies on a general capability-driven signal without
requiring external data or auxiliary models.

and efficiency on complex reasoning tasks, comparing
against multiple baselines across in-domain settings,
out-of-domain generalization, test-time scaling, and
confidence calibration.

2.4. Length Control in LLMs.
Although reasoning models often achieve strong perfor-
mance by producing extended chains of thought, this can
substantially increase inference latency (Sui et al., 2025;
Yeo et al., 2025). Prior work on length control mitigates this
trade-off through techniques such as adjusting positional
encodings to enforce a target output length (Butcher et al.,
2025), incorporating length constraints into the training
objective (Jie et al., 2023; Singhal et al., 2023), and train-
ing on curated datasets annotated with desired reasoning
length (Yuan et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2025; Biju et al., 2025).
In the context of training objectives, several RL-based post-
training methods have been proposed. Some of these ap-
proaches control generation length based on a user-selected
value (Aggarwal & Welleck, 2025) or dynamically adjust
the number of tokens according to a predefined budget (Han
et al., 2025; Fang et al., 2025). On the other hand, some
methods define a reward function to encourage a general re-
duction in the number of generated tokens(Arora & Zanette,
2025; Luo et al., 2025; Lee & Tong, 2025).
Our proposed framework operates without reliance on cu-
rated datasets and eliminates the need for manually specified
computational budgets, explicit sequence-length constraints,
or predefined task complexity assumptions. Importantly, the
observed reduction in output length emerges as a struc-
tural consequence of increased intrinsic information
and system-level consciousness, rather than being imposed
through external constraints. In contrast, most existing ap-
proaches achieve length reduction via direct enforcement or
heuristic-based length control mechanisms.

3. Method
3.1. Integrated Information Theory
IIT aims to answer such questions—that is, to offer a sci-
entific account of consciousness, of what it feels like to be
you right now. The IIT method can be summarized as “con-
sciousness first”. It starts from the fact that one’s experience
exists, right now, and it uses introspection to characterize
the properties of experience. The theory then asks how we
might account for these properties of experience in physical
terms—which means in terms of something we can observe
and manipulate “out there.” The starting point of IIT is the
existence of experience. This truth is not the result of an
inference; it is rather immediate and confirmed through in-
trospection. The five axioms of IIT express the essential
properties of experience, discovered through introspection
and reasoning. The five axioms are essential properties of
experience because they characterize what experience is—or
the nature of experience—and because they are irrefutably
true of every conceivable experience, as confirmed through
introspection and reflection alone. The axioms can be most
succinctly stated as follows :
Existence: Experience exists; there is something.
Intrinsicality: Experience is intrinsic; it exists for itself.
Information: Experience is specific; it is this particular
experience.
Integration: Experience is unitary; it is irreducible to sepa-
rate experiences.
Exclusion: Experience is definite; it is this whole experi-
ence.
Composition: Experience is structured; it consists of dis-
tinctions and relations forming a phenomenal structure.
According to IIT, A system is any subset of units that can be
considered as a candidate substrate of consciousness. A unit
is a constituent of a substrate, operationally defined by the
grain at which it is observed and manipulated to evaluate
its cause–effect power. A substrate of consciousness (also
called a complex) is a set of units in a state that satisfies
all postulates of IIT. A substrate graph is a way to visually
represent the substrate under investigation as a network. It
typically consists of the (labeled) units and their causal con-
nections, as nodes and edges of a network respectively. The
state of a unit in IIT is binary. A Transition Probability
Matrix (TPM) is a mathematical object that completely
describes the dynamics of a substrate of units. More pre-
cisely, it is a matrix of numbers that can be used to find the
probability that a substrate state will transition from one to
another.
A central issue concerns how the level of consciousness of
a given substrate can be determined. The procedure can be
understood as six sequential steps, each checking whether a
candidate substrate satisfies each of the postulates (0th plus
five)(Albantakis et al., 2023). A conscious system generates
integrated information, measured as ϕ.
1. Existence: Define a substrate model.
2. Intrinsicality: Select a candidate complex.
3. Information: Compute intrinsic information.
4. Integration: Compute integrated information (ϕs).
5. Exclusion: Identify the main complex.
6. Composition: Unfold the Φ-structure of the main com-
plex.

According to IIT, a system is composed of interconnected
units with binary states. The transition probabilities between
these states are defined by a TPM. A key question is how a
LLM can be utilized to construct such a system—including
its units and TPM—from generated texts. For this purpose,
the token embeddings of the LLM serve as system states.
Initially, the output representation is adjusted according to
the input representation via an attention mechanism. Due
to the high-dimensional nature of LLM embeddings, the
resulting state space becomes prohibitively large. Moreover,

the TPM. By normalizing the frequency of these transitions,
the resulting matrix is converted into a probabilistic form.
3.3. Reward Function
The reward function in this study is derived from two
methods—Φ and II. The phi-based method incorporates
all postulates of IIT, while the II approach employs only the
information postulate.
Phi (Φ)
As stated at the end of 3.1, the computation of a state-level
consciousness measure is performed via a six-step proce-
dure. In the proposed framework, the scalar quantity Φ is
defined as a token-level metric that quantifies the degree of
consciousness attributed to each token. A high Φ indicates
that the system’s elements interact in a way that cannot
be reduced to their individual contributions, implying a
unified conscious state. For computational details, please
refer to (Albantakis, 2026). The (Mayner, 2026) library was
used to compute Φ.
Intrinsic Information
According to the information postulate,
a system’s
cause–effect state is determined by computing intrinsic
information (II) and selecting the cause and effect that
maximize its value.
II quantifies the system’s specific
cause–effect power in its current state. Systems with high
II are both deterministic and reversible, exhibiting robust
causal properties whereby each state uniquely arises from
its preceding state and deterministically transitions to its
subsequent state (The consequences of these properties can
be observed in the example provided in Figure 1).
3.4. Why Intrinsic Inforamtion Reward?
This study aims to investigate which aspects of conscious-
ness in a language model can be meaningfully quantified
under IIT. Based on these quantifiable aspects, the model
is trained to enhance its consciousness-related features. Im-
portantly, the presence of such features does not imply that
the language model itself is conscious.According to IIT,
a conscious system is characterized by two fundamental
features:
1. It has intrinsic cause–effect power.
2. The experience is irreducible to a set of separate or
independent experiences.
A high Φ value in a LLM indicates that it exhibits the two
aforementioned features. One of the main challenges associ-
ated with computing Φ is its high computational cost, which
increases exponentially with the number of units, thereby
rendering its calculation difficult and impractical for large-
scale systems. The fundamental formulation proposed by
IIT is computationally intractable and, in certain cases, lacks
a well-defined specification(Barrett, 2019; Moon, 2019)
To address this challenge, the present study proposes a new
metric, termed intrinsic information, which has lower com-
putational complexity and focuses specifically on the first
feature. The II is computed for each output token of the
LLM according to the information postulate. In each com-
putation, the target token is considered as the current state
of the system. Consequently, by evaluating the II for each
token, a quantitative measure of token-level consciousness
is obtained. Subsequently, the corresponding cause state
and effect state are identified by selecting those states that
maximize the II associated with the current state. Formally,
conditioned on the current state, two II measures are com-
puted: iic, corresponding to the cause state, and iie, cor-
responding to the effect state. The II of the current state
is then defined as the sum ii = iic + iie. Although, in
accordance with the Principle of Maximal Existence in
IIT, one may alternatively define the II of the current state
as max(iic, iie), this formulation was also evaluated exper-
imentally. Nevertheless, empirical findings demonstrate that
the additive formulation yields more favorable performance
during the model training process. The computation process
of II is illustrated in Figure 2(b).

4. Experiments
This section presents preliminary experiments, the experi-
mental setup, training analysis, results, and ablation studies.
4.1. Preliminary Experiments
To study the behavior of different reward designs, we first
conduct a comprehensive set of exploratory experiments
using the Qwen-3B-Instruct model (Team et al., 2024) on
the GSM8K dataset within a post-training framework, em-
ploying the GRPO algorithm. Training is performed us-
ing Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), updating approximately
7% of the model parameters. Both accuracy-based and
consciousness-based rewards are considered: the accuracy
reward is binary, assigning a value of 1 for correct answers
and 0 otherwise, while the consciousness-based reward (Φ
or II) is defined as a continuous value in the range [0, 1] via
an exponential mapping.
These experiments evaluate model behavior under a vari-
ety of training settings and hyperparameter configurations
(details provided in Appendix A). Model performance is
assessed using accuracy, average response length, and re-
sponse entropy. The insights obtained from this systematic
analysis are then used to inform the design of our main
experimental setup, which is presented and discussed in the
following section.
4.2. Experimental Setup
Training data, models, and methods: DeepSeek-R1-
Distill-Qwen-7B (Guo et al., 2025) is adopted as the base
model, with OpenThoughts-114k-Math used as the training
dataset to construct a stronger reasoning-oriented language
model. Based on preliminary experimental results and the
characteristics of the selected model and dataset, the TPM
construction policy is configured in prompt mode, with re-
duced dimensionality evaluated at values of 4 and 5. To im-
prove training efficiency, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning
(PEFT) strategy is employed, in which only 14% of the
model parameters are updated during training. Configura-
tions with a reduced number of trainable parameters are
further evaluated; however, these settings do not achieve the
expected performance..
Evaluation: We evaluate the trained model on a diverse
suite of mathematical and reasoning benchmarks, includ-
ing Math-500, Countdown, GPQA-Diamond, covering a
wide range of problem difficulties and reasoning require-
ments (Cao et al., 2025). To evaluate the generalization
behavior of the trained models with respect to the dataset
used during language model training, MATH-500 is consid-
ered as the in-domain dataset, while Countdown and GPQA-
Diamond are treated as out-of-domain datasets. Meanwhile
The trained models are evaluated against the baselines in
terms of Accuracy and Average Response Length.
Baselines: To rigorously evaluate our approach, we com-
pare it against three baseline methods, which are detailed
below. All models are evaluated using identical inference
settings to ensure a fair comparison.
• Base reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-
7B): This model is a distillation of the main R1 reason-
ing model into Qwen-2.5-7B, released by DeepSeek
(Baseline 0).
• Base reasoning model with Accuracy Reward:
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B was trained on the
OpenThoughts-114k-Math dataset using the GRPO
algorithm with an accuracy reward, thereby isolating
the effect of the proposed reward formulation (Baseline
1).
• Base reasoning model with Entropy Minimization:
As reported in (Agarwal et al., 2025), LLMs can be
trained using an entropy-based reward. Their results
show that minimizing response entropy improves ac-
curacy. Since their approach, like ours, also reduces
response entropy, a comparison of the two methods is
well justified (Baseline 2).

Conclusion
We presented a framework to enhance feature-consciousness
in LLMs via reinforcement learning with an intrinsic in-
formation reward based on integrated information theory
information postulate. Benchmark evaluations show that
models with improved cause-effect power produce more
concise responses. While validated on both in-domain and
out-of-domain data, extending this approach to more com-
plex tasks—such as very hard mathematical reasoning and
code generation—remains a valuable direction for future
work.
Additionally, consciousness was quantified through pre- and
post-state; given its abstract nature, alternative definitions
could provide further insights.
Given the inherent trade-off between improving predictive
accuracy and reducing output length, a systematic anal-
ysis of the interaction between these two objectives is re-
quired. This analysis should be grounded in training-process
variables, such as the in intrinsic information, which can
significantly influence optimization dynamics. From an
information-theoretic perspective, examining how reward
shaping affects this trade-off may yield insights into the
emergence of compact yet high-accuracy representations
during model training.
Hyperparameter selection poses challenges due to dynamic
model behavior during training, such as varying response
lengths affecting the data required for transition probability
matrix construction. Exploring adaptive or hyperparameter-
free strategies represents a promising avenue for further
enhancing this framework.

A.1. Hyperparameters
In the process of determining the number of units and constructing the TPM, the following two hyperparameters are
considered:
Reduced dimensionality: This hyperparameter defines the size of the discretized state space used to represent the system.
The dimensionality must be compatible with the batch size of the training data to ensure reliable estimation of transition
probabilities. Notably, when computing rewards based on the Φ measure, there is an inherent limitation on the number of
dimensions due to combinatorial explosion; given our available computational resources, Φ-based rewards cannot be reliably
computed for dimensionalities greater than three. In contrast, rewards derived from II do not impose this dimensionality
restriction.
Dataset size / TPM construction policy: Different strategies can be adopted for TPM construction, reflecting the way
trajectories or responses are aggregated:
• Batch mode: All responses generated by the LLM within a single batch are aggregated to construct a single TPM,
representing the collective state transitions of that batch.
• Prompt mode: Since multiple responses may be generated for a single prompt during training, only responses
corresponding to the same prompt are used to construct the TPM for that specific prompt. Consequently, one TPM is
generated per prompt, allowing reward computation that is specific to individual prompts.
• Trajectory mode: A separate TPM is constructed for each individual response, capturing the transition dynamics at the
granularity of single trajectories.
State size: As discussed in Section 4, the reward assigned to a response is computed as the sum of the rewards for each
individual token in the response. In this approach, each state is initially defined as a single token. However, the state size can
be extended beyond a single token to include a fixed-width sequence of multiple tokens. For instance, if the state size is set
to four tokens, an output sequence of 200 tokens would correspond to 50 states. Increasing the state size allows the model to
capture higher-level cause–effect relationships across token sequences, aligning with the principles of transition dynamics in
both RL and IIT frameworks. Therefore, the state size is considered in two forms: token-based and chunk-based. In the
chunk-based setting, the state size is treated as a hyperparameter.
