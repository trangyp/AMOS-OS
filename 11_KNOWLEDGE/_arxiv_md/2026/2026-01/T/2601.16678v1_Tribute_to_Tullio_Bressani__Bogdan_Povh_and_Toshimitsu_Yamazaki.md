Tribute to Tullio Bressani, Bogdan Povh and
Toshimitsu Yamazaki
Avraham Gal
Racah Institute of Physics, The Hebrew University, Jerusalem 91904, ISRAEL
(Received January 19, 2026)
In this HYP25 talk I pay tribute to Tullio Bressani (1940-2024), Bogdan Povh (1932-2024)
and Toshimitsu Yamazaki (1934-2025), all of whom made lasting contributions to shaping
up Strangeness Nuclear Physics. Yoshinori Akaishi’s (1941-2025) record is also noted.
KEYWORDS: strangeness nuclear physics
1.
Introduction
The modern period in hypernuclear physics started in the early 1970s using the in-flight
(K−, π−) production reaction at CERN by Bressani’s and Povh’s groups and later on by
Chrien’s group at BNL. It was followed in 1985 by the (π+, K+) production reaction at BNL
and applied since 1995 at KEK, led by Yamazaki. The resulting Λ-hypernuclear g.s. binding
energies form large fraction of the world data shown in Fig. 1. Below I summarize major
physics contributions made by Bressani, Povh and Yamazaki, noting also Akaishi’s record.

The early years
Bressani and Povh, separately, started (K−, π−) counter experiments at the CERN-PS
as documented for K−in-flight in the following publications.
• Bressani’s group: G.C. Bonazzola, et al. (pK−=390 MeV/c), Production of 12
Λ C by K−
in flight, PLB 53 (1974) 297; Observation of 16
Λ O and 27
Λ Al, PRL 34 (1975) 683. However,
their energy resolution of about 6 MeV was insufficient to draw strong conclusions.
• Povh’s group: W. Br¨uckner, et al. (pK−=900 MeV/c), Hypercharge exchange reac-
tion on nuclei, PLB 55 (1975) 107; Strangeness exchange reaction on nuclei, PLB 62
(1976) 481; followed by the Heidelberg-Saclay-Strasbourg collaborative work at pK−=715
MeV/c, reaching 2 MeV energy resolution: Spin-orbit interaction of Λ particles in nuclei,
PLB 79 (1978) 157; and many more Bertini, et al., papers in the 1980s [1].
Both Povh at CERN and Yamazaki at the Tokyo-INS and in KEK were looking for Σ
hypernuclei in (K−/K−
stop, π−) reactions during the 1980s, but the statistical significance
of the signals observed was rather poor. It took some time to realize that the Σ-nuclear
interaction is largely repulsive so Σ hypernuclei generally do not exist [2] except for 4
ΣHe
established by Hayano, et al. [3], in a 4He(K−
stop, π−) reaction at KEK and soon explained in
a work led by Akaishi [4]. Povh subsequently left hypernuclear physics in favor of other topics,
notably the EMC effect, whereas Yamazaki became fully involved in hypernuclear physics
around 1985. As for Bressani, he moved on to other topics at CERN, notably antineutrons,
returning to hypernuclear physics around 2000 during which he was leading FINUDA at
DAΦNE, Frascati, using their high-quality ϕ-factory K−
stop source.

Toshimitsu Yamazaki (1934-2025)

Toshimitsu Yamazaki (1934-2025)
Toshimitsu Yamazaki was leading Strangeness Nuclear Physics worldwide, Japan in par-
ticular, for several decades. It is reflected today in the rich & imaginative experimental
program in J-PARC, where many of his students have become leaders on their own right.
Yamazaki’s leading contributions to Hadronic Physics, in particular to Strangeness Nu-
clear Physics, are classified in the Japan Academy website by five Recurring Themes.
• I.
Meson effects in nuclear magnetic moments.
• II. Muon spin rotation (µSR) relaxation.
• III. Discovery of metastable antiprotonic helium.
• IV. Discovery of deeply bound pionic-atom states.
• V. Search for kaonic nuclei; Kaonic Proton Matter (KPM).
Here I focus on the last two Recurring Themes, developed and matured throughout the last
40 years since I first met him at TRIUMF in 1985 on my sabbatical stay there.

5.1
Deeply bound pionic atoms
Deeply bound pionic atoms refer to 1s and 2p states in heavy pionic atoms which cannot
be reached in X-ray cascade because upper levels such as 3d are already broadened by Strong
Interactions rendering the radiative yield exceedingly small. In 1985, Friedman and Soff [5]
noted that deeply bound 1s pionic states are sufficiently narrow to make them well defined,
see Fig. 5 (left) where the calculated 1s widths remain small up to the top end of the periodic
table. The relative narrowness of the 1s states follows from the repulsive real part of the s-wave
pion-nucleus potential that keeps the absorptive imaginary part off the nuclear volume.
Three years later in 1988 Toki and Yamazaki [6], unaware of the Friedman-Soff paper,
realized that the width of 1s pionic states in heavy nuclei is considerably smaller than the
1s→2p excitation energy, see Fig. 5 (right), so it made sense to look for strong-interaction
reactions to populate such states. It took another eight years to apply a good ‘recoil-less’
208Pb(d,3He) reaction at beam energy about 600 MeV in GSI to create a deeply bound pion
as close to rest as possible in 207Pb (Yamazaki, Hayano, et al. [7]). This was followed by
experiments on 206Pb and on Sn isotopes, as reviewed by Kienle and Yamazaki [8], Friedman
and Gal [9] and Yamazaki, Hirenzaki, Hayano and Toki [10]. More recently, both 1s and 2p
deeply-bound pionic states in 121Sn were identified and studied at RIKEN [11].

Right: π−energy levels in 90Zr with & without Strong Interactions (Figure adapted from Toki &

5.2
Kaonic Proton Matter
Interpreting the Λ(1405) as a K−p quasibound state suggests that K−mesons are likely
to bind strongly into nuclear clusters, the simplest of which is K−pp. That a Jπ = 0−, I = 1
2
K−pp state might be bound by ∼10 MeV was suggested by Nogami already in 1963 [16].
Yamazaki and Akaishi [17, 18], using a complex energy-independent ¯KN potential within
a single-channel K−pp calculation, obtained binding energy BK−pp ∼50 MeV and width
ΓK−pp ∼60 MeV. Subsequent ¯KNN–πΣN coupled-channel Faddeev calculations [19, 20]
confirmed this order of magnitude for B but gave larger width values (∼100 MeV). Using
a chirally motivated ¯KN interaction in such Faddeev calculations lowers BK−pp to about
32 MeV and ΓK−pp to about 50 MeV [21], while few-body calculations using a single-channel
effective chirally motivated ¯KN interaction find even smaller values of binding energies and
widths [22, 23]. Experimentally, following several dubious claims by several experiments, J-
PARC E15 reported a statistically reliable K−pp signal [24] with BK−pp = 42 ± 3+3
−4 MeV,
ΓK−pp = 100 ± 7+19
−9 MeV. Part of this large width comes from K−NN absorption processes
that are not accounted for in most calculations.
Calculations of ¯K nuclear clusters up to six nucleons [25] demonstrate that replacing one
nucleon by a ¯K meson increases substantially the overall binding energy. This suggests to
look for a maximal increase by considering aggregates of bound Λ∗hyperons, so-called Kaonic
Proton Matter (KPM) by Akaishi and Yamazaki [26] who argued that it would also provide
an absolutely stable form of matter for less than ten Λ∗hyperons. This exciting proposal was
questioned by the Jerusalem-Prague collaboration [27–29] within a Relativistic Mean Field
(RMF) calculational scheme, as follows.

was found then, as shown on the left-hand side of Fig. 6, that the binding energy per Λ∗
saturates at values well below 100 MeV for mass number A ≥120, implying that Λ∗matter
is highly unstable against strong-interaction decay to Λ and Σ aggregates. Recall that about
300 MeV is required to reduce the Λ∗(1405) mass in the medium below that of the lightest
hyperon Λ(1116). It is worth noting that RMF calculations for multi ¯K-N-Λ hadronic systems
reported earlier by Gazda et al. [31–33] reached similar conclusions, although in the context
of ruling out kaon condensation.
The saturation of B/A observed on the left-hand side of Fig. 6 follows from the decrease
of the scalar density ρs associated with the attractive σ field with respect to the conserved
vector density ρv associated with the repulsive ω field: ρs = (M∗/E∗)ρv < ρv, where M∗=
M−gσB¯σ is the baryon-B effective mass. Thus, Lorentz invariance implies that the scalar-field
attraction is reduced as density increases. The upper curve on the right-hand side of Fig. 6
demonstrates then how a fixed ratio (ρs/ρv) = 0.97, corresponding to 16O calculation, leads
to non-saturating B/A values in contrast to the saturated values obtained in the properly
calculated RMF lower curve. It is worth noting that the central density of Λ∗matter is found
to saturate as well, at roughly twice nuclear-matter density.
Appendix: Yoshinori Akaishi (1941-2025)
Yoshinori Akaishi passed away a short while before HYP25 was convened. I was asked
by the Organizers to include him too in my Obituary talk, which I respectfully accepted to
do. Akaishi-san made valuable contributions to Nuclear and Hadronic Physics in Japan and
elsewhere. A brief CV follows.
• 1969-1991: Hokkaido Univ, Sapporo (Ph.D. 1971 Kyoto).
• 1992: Prof. INS, Univ Tokyo; 1997: INS →KEK.
• 1999: retired; Nihon Univ; visitor at RIKEN.
• 156 publications 1966-2024, mostly in Few-Body Physics.
• 1981: 1st paper on Λ hypernuclei (5
ΛHe).
• 1988: 1st paper on Σ hypernuclei (9
ΣBe).
• 1997-2024: many papers with Yamazaki, mostly ¯K nuclei.
