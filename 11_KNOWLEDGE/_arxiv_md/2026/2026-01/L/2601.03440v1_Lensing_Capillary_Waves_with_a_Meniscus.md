Abstract
The propagation of water waves is altered when interacting with curved surfaces.
Here, we
consider the problem of capillary waves interacting with a 3D meniscus.
We show that when
capillary waves scatter oz an object surrounded by a meniscus, the resulting wave}eld can be
drastically altered and lensing phenomena is observed.
Our results are not only an important
step in surface tension dominated wave interactions, but may have implications in the biological
communication of surface dwelling animals.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The interactions between capillary waves and menisci are fundamentally important to
understanding the dynamics of small objects near the capillary length scale (2.71 mm).
In nature, surface dwelling organisms may use capillary waves to communicate [1] while
abiological ~oating particles can be propelled and interact through waves in the laboratory
[2]. Studies into a meniscus’s in~uence on the scattering of these waves can provide then
new insights on the underlying dynamics at play in these systems.
Despite their ubiquity and intimate relationship through surface curvature, there are
only a handful of studies that directly investigate the scattering interactions between a
meniscus and capillary waves. In 2016, experimental studies investigated how the re~ection
coe{cients depend on the height of pinned menisci at a ~at wall [3]. In 2025, complementary
experimental and computational studies expanded to considerations of both re~ection and
transmission coe{cients for menisci at shallow, 2D boundaries [4, 5]. These studies were
limited to 2D ezects, and thus, full 3D scattering pattern incurred by the ezect of a meniscus
remains to be seen.
The archetypal 3D scattering problem for water waves is that of plane waves incident
upon a hard circular cylinder (see Supplemental Material for analytical solution [6]). This
problem is generalizable and has been studied in many other wave systems including water
surface gravity waves, electromagnetic waves, and acoustic waves, among others. One may
alter the scattering with changes in the properties of the cylinder or additional (ezective)
coatings. For example, with an appropriate depth pro}le, one may cloak objects from ocean
waves [7] and tsunamis are naturally focused by the sloping beaches around islands [8]. For
waves at the capillary-gravity scale, surfactant regimes azect the scattering pro}les [9] and

B.
Interpretations
To begin identifying the possible mechanisms behind the phenomena observed, we }rst
limit our consideration to waves in the ray limit con}ned to propagate along a surface S. If
the surface appears su{ciently planar such that the dispersion relation remains unchanged
across the surface, the ray trajectories must be the geodesics of the surface.
While the
absolute speed of the wave is unchanging, its projected speed in the xy-plane appears to
be slowed (what would be observed in our shadowgraphs).
This in itself can result in
focusing phenomena, known in the context of gravitational lensing [15] and optical devices
[16]. Lensing ezects are additionally observed on menisci, strongly refracting glancing and
impinging rays as seen in Fig. 3.
These ezects are not dependent on the sign of the surface curvature, and thus will be
the same for complimentary positive and negative menisci. As such, the asymmetry in our
results precludes signi}cant attribution to geodesic ezects in the mild menisci. Furthermore,
in practice, pure geodesic trajectories will not be observed in our system due to refractive
and dizractive ezects. However, the waves are still con}ned to surface and so these ezects
may be of more importance near the contact line of extreme menisci, albeit obscured.

V.
OUTLOOK
In this study, we performed experiments to investigate capillary waves scattering oz a
meniscus in a 3D system. Our experimental results show that the meniscus’s presence acts
as an ezective coating to objects, leading to phenomena such as lensing of the waves behind
an obstacle. Across positive and negative menisci, we observe asymmetric behavior but a
general trend of a decrease in wave speed. From this, we conclude that inertial ezects play a
large role in azecting the scattering for positive mild menisci while geodesic, surface tension,
and inertial ezects likely play a combined role for more extreme menisci.
Beyond cylindrical geometries, one may design novel meniscus pro}les formed at mem-
branes [19] and objects of varying shapes [20] which may allow for increase control over
capillary wave propagation. Considerations of menisci could also allow for greater insight
into studies of interacting, wave-propelled ~oating particles [2]. Lastly, our results are of rel-
evance to biological communication. Small water surface dwelling animals such as whirligig
beetles, water striders, and }shing spiders are known to be highly sensitive to capillary-
gravity waves [1]. Our results suggest that the meniscus around these animals may play the
role of an abiological lens, allowing for increased perception.

In dizerent parameter regimes or geometries, other physical mechanisms beyond those
considered may play more prominent roles in altering the scattering behavior.
Gravitational Ezects: At lower frequencies, gravity’s role as a restoring force grows rela-

~at surface, there is the added complexity of the restoring force no longer necessarily being
normal to the curved surface. As the waves we investigated are of su{ciently high frequency
as to be considered pure capillary waves, gravity should only play a role in the formation of
the meniscus.
