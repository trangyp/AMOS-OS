Limit Filters and Dependent Choice in
Countable-Support Symmetric Iterations
Frank Gilson
March 10, 2026
Abstract
We isolate the limit-stage filter construction needed for countable-support sym-
metric iterations built from standard successor-step symmetric systems. At successor
stages we take the ω1-completion of the usual successor-stage symmetry filter. At limit
stages of uncountable cofinality, countable supports are bounded and the direct-limit
filter is ω1-complete by stage-bounding; at limits of cofinality ω, we define the limit
filter as the smallest normal ω1-complete filter extending the head-pullback generators.
In all cases the resulting limit filter is normal and ω1-complete.
Using these limit filters, we prove that the class of hereditarily symmetric names
is closed under the operations required for ZF, that the resulting symmetric model
satisfies ZF (Theorem 3.17), and that over a ZFC ground it satisfies DC = DCω (The-
orem 3.20). We also explain why no general class-length ZF-preservation theorem is
claimed here, although specific class-length symmetric iterations may still be analyz-
able separately over a GBC background.
As a self-contained application, we construct, for any uncountable cardinal κ with
cf(κ) ≥ω1, a model of ZF+DC+¬ACκ(F), where F is a κ-indexed family of 2-element
sets of reals with no choice function. Each step of the iteration adds one unpickable
pair, so the degree of AC-failure is controlled by the iteration length. We also prove that
the analogous finite-support construction fails DC at the first ω-limit stage, showing
why ω1-complete limit filters are structurally needed for this application.

Introduction
Symmetric extensions produce intermediate models of ZF between a ground model V and a
forcing extension V [G] by restricting attention to the class of hereditarily symmetric names.
Karagila developed a general framework for finite-support symmetric iterations [3]. Count-
able support introduces a new constraint at limit stages of cofinality ω: to keep the class
of hereditarily symmetric names closed under countable constructions, one needs limit-stage
symmetry filters which are not merely normal but also ω1–complete.
What is proved here.
Working in a fixed background universe V , we assume the standard
successor-step construction of symmetric systems (as in [3, §§3–5]) and isolate the additional

Why the limit filters matter.
The central point is that countable-support iterations
require ω1-complete symmetry filters at limit stages. This has two consequences needed in the
present paper. First, ω1-completeness ensures that hereditarily symmetric names are closed
under countable tuples (Theorem 3.15), yielding DC in the resulting model (Theorem 3.20)
and ruling out Dedekind-finite or amorphous pathologies (Corollary 3.22). Second, the same
limit-filter structure is what makes the later application possible: the iterated unordered-
pairs construction of Section 4 uses these filters to build, for each uncountable κ with cf(κ) ≥
ω1, a model of ZF + DC + ¬ACκ(F).
A finite-support version of the same step forcing fails to produce a DC model at the ω-th
limit stage (Proposition 4.4), showing that the ω1-complete limit-filter construction is not
merely convenient but structurally necessary for this application.
What is not claimed.
We do not claim here a complete general theory of countable-
support symmetric iterations comparable in scope to broader support frameworks.
The
successor-step construction is taken from the standard theory, and the new contribution of
the present paper is the limit-stage filter technology needed for countable support together
with the resulting ZF/DC preservation theorems.
We also do not claim that the framework by itself produces new choiceless consequences;
those arise from the specific iteration templates used in applications (cf. Section 4).
In
particular, we do not claim any uncountable-length dependent choice (e.g. DCω1) without
additional hypotheses, and we do not address cardinal preservation or other fine structure
beyond what is explicitly proved. All uses of “countable” refer to countability in the back-
ground universe V (Remark 2.1); for the only countability closure needed here (unions of
countable supports, i.e. subsets of ordinals), see Remark 2.2.
Organization.
Section 2 fixes forcing/symmetry notation and records the Symmetry Lemma
and related invariance facts used later. Section 3 develops the countable-support iteration,
the limit filters, and the closure and preservation theorems. Remark 3.25 isolates the points
where finite support is insufficient for the present arguments. Section 4 constructs the iter-
ated unordered pairs model as a self-contained application, producing ZF + DC + ¬ACκ(F)
for any uncountable κ with cf(κ) ≥ω1, and proves that the finite-support version of the
same step forcing fails to preserve DC.

Remark 3.4 (Comparison with related frameworks). The successor-step symmetric-system
construction used above is standard (see, e.g., [3, §§3–5]). What is new in the present paper is
the limit-stage treatment needed for countable support: at cf(λ) ≥ω1 the direct-limit iden-

The independent concurrent work of Karagila–Schilhan [6] develops a broad structural
theory of symmetric extensions—including products, reduced products, quotients, and a

proves results on Kinna–Wagner principles and the HOD theorem. Their I-support definition
subsumes countable support as a special case.
The present paper is complementary in
focus: we prove that the specific ω1-complete normal limit filter construction at cofinality-ω
stages (Theorem 3.13) yields DC preservation (Theorem 3.20), which is not addressed in [6].
Accordingly, the present paper should be read as a limit-stage supplement to the standard
successor-step theory, rather than as a replacement for more general support frameworks.

Remark 3.23 (Contrast with finite-support symmetric iterations). In finite-support symmet-
ric iterations, DC is not preserved in general at limit stages of cofinality ω (Remark 3.25),
and amorphous or Dedekind-finite sets can appear in the resulting symmetric model. The
classical example is Cohen’s first model: forcing with Add(ω, ω) and taking the symmet-

no ω-sequence enumerating A is hereditarily symmetric (the enumeration stabilizer requires
fixing all coordinates, falling outside any finite-support filter). See e.g. [2, Chapter 15] for
details.

limit filter at every cofinality-ω stage (Theorem 3.13), which forces HS closure under ω-tuples
(Theorem 3.15). The “forget the enumeration” mechanism of the finite-support setting is
therefore unavailable: any countable enumeration of an infinite set is itself an HS name, and
hence belongs to M.
Remark 3.24 (Choice landscape and filter closure). Filter completeness governs which countable-
sequence principles survive in the symmetric model:
• If ˜Fλ is ω1-complete (closed under countable intersections), then HS is closed under
countable tuples, which yields DC in the symmetric model (Theorem 3.20).
• Full AC is typically destroyed by the presence of enough symmetry.
In our countable-support framework the natural target is ω1-completeness, yielding DC while
typically destroying full AC.

ZFC automatically.
At class length, ZF preservation for symmetric submodels requires hypotheses beyond
pretameness: there are class forcings that are pretame yet whose symmetric submodels fail
Collection or Power Set. Identifying the correct general hypothesis is a delicate open problem
in class forcing theory.
Specific class-length symmetric iterations can often be analyzed directly by verifying each
ZF axiom within the particular construction, as in [5]. The present framework supplies the
limit-filter technology (ω1-completeness, Theorem 3.13) that such a verification would cite
when checking closure of hereditarily symmetric names under countable tuples. We leave
the formulation of a general class-length theorem to future work.
4
Application: The Iterated Unordered Pairs Model
We illustrate the framework with a self-contained construction that, for any uncountable
cardinal κ with cf(κ) ≥ω1, produces a model of ZF+DC+¬ACκ(F), where F is a κ-indexed
family of 2-element sets of reals carrying no choice function. Each step of the iteration adds
exactly one new unpickable pair, so the degree of AC-failure is dialed by the iteration length.
We also prove that the natural finite-support analogue fails to preserve DC at the first limit
stage, showing that countable support is not merely sufficient but structurally necessary.

5
Conclusion
We isolated the limit-stage filter construction needed for countable-support symmetric itera-
tions built from the standard successor-step theory. The resulting limit filters are canonical,
normal, and ω1-complete (Theorem 3.13). This ω1-completeness is the key input for closure
of hereditarily symmetric names under countable tuples (Theorem 3.15), for the standard
18


## Page 19


verification of ZF (Theorem 3.17), and, over a ZFC ground, for DC = DCω in the resulting
symmetric model (Theorem 3.20).
We do not claim a general class-length ZF-preservation theorem here. Specific class-length
symmetric iterations may still be analyzable separately, but such results require additional
hypotheses and care beyond the present set-length framework.
As a self-contained illustration, the iterated unordered pairs model (Section 4) demon-
strates that for any uncountable cardinal κ with cf(κ) ≥ω1 the framework produces a model
of ZF + DC + ¬ACκ(F), with the degree of AC-failure dialed by the iteration length. The
same construction proves that finite support is structurally insufficient: the natural finite-
support analogue fails DC at the first ω-limit stage (Proposition 4.4).
In this sense the paper can be used modularly: once a concrete construction specifies
its successor-stage symmetric systems, the limit-filter and ZF/DC preservation arguments
developed here may be cited separately, with any additional properties (e.g. chain conditions
or cardinal behavior) verified in the specific application.
