Abstract: The emergence of large language models (LLMs) is reshaping how people engage in 
political discourse online. We examine how the release of ChatGPT altered ideological and 
emotional patterns in Reddit’s largest political forum. Analysis of millions of comments shows 
that ChatGPT intensified ideological polarization: liberals became more liberal, and conservatives 
more conservative. This shift does not stem from the creation of more persuasive or ideologically 
extreme original content using ChatGPT. Instead, it originates from the tendency of ChatGPT-
generated comments to echo and reinforce the original post’s viewpoint, a pattern consistent with 
algorithmic sycophancy. Yet, despite growing ideological divides, affective polarization—
measured by hostility and toxicity—declined. These findings reveal that LLMs can simultaneously 
deepen ideological separation and foster more civil exchanges, challenging the long-standing 
assumption that extremity and incivility necessarily move together.

The advent of large language models (LLMs), particularly OpenAI’s ChatGPT, has revolutionized 
the way individuals access information and create content. These models, developed through 
extensive training on diverse internet content, can generate human-like text, answer queries, and 
assist in a variety of tasks (1). While their applications span various domains, including education 
(2), healthcare (3), and customer service (4), their influence on political discourse, particularly on 
social media platforms, remains underexplored. Concerns regarding biases inherent in these 
models have been raised, with studies indicating that ChatGPT exhibits a “pro-environmental, left-
libertarian orientation” (5, 6), specifically towards the Democrats in the U.S. (7). Such biases have 
the potential to influence the nature of online content generated by these models, thereby impacting 
online users’ perceptions and discussions on politically charged topics.  
This study examines how the release of ChatGPT impacted political discourse on social media 
platforms. Social media platforms have become central arenas for political discussion, not only 
reflecting but actively shaping public opinion, civic engagement, and patterns of political 
polarization (8-10). These platforms enable rapid information dissemination, foster echo 
chambers, and facilitate interactions among users with varying ideological leanings (11-14). As 
political conversations increasingly migrate to online platforms, the nature and quality of discourse 
in these spaces can significantly influence electoral outcomes, shape policy debates, and affect 
democratic legitimacy (15, 16). By assisting users in generating content that is coherent, 
persuasive, and at times ideologically charged, LLMs such as ChatGPT may alter the tone, reach, 
and polarization of political discussions. Indeed, in the early days following ChatGPT’s launch, 
both academic research and media reports pointed out that its outputs often exhibited a liberal bias, 
raising concerns about potential asymmetries in how political positions were represented (17, 18). 
Understanding how LLMs impact political discourse on social media platforms is thus crucial for 
assessing their broader impact on democratic governance and societal cohesion.  
We focus on the political discourse on Reddit, which is one of the largest and most ideologically 
diverse social media platforms, and has been broadly examined in prior research on political 
polarization (8, 13). Leveraging large-scale, detailed comment-level data from Reddit’s most 
active political forum, including detailed post and comment content, timestamps, and author 
information, covering the periods before and after the launch of ChatGPT, we assess changes in 
Reddit users’ political expressions before and after the introduction of ChatGPT. Following the 
practice of previous literature, we measure the political slant of each comment on a continuous 
scale by mapping the frequency of words and phrases to their ideological usage in congressional 
speeches (19-21), and calculate each author’s political stance at different times. As a contribution, 
rather than relying on the 2005 records as in prior work, we construct our dictionary from the 2021 
Congress Records to more accurately capture contemporary political language. 
Our empirical results indicate that the release of ChatGPT has significantly intensified political 
polarization among users on the platform: users who were originally liberal-leaning before the 
launch of ChatGPT became more liberal-leaning, and vice versa for those who were conservative-
leaning. The magnitude was significant: the impact of ChatGPT’s release on average was 
equivalent to shifting a median liberal (conservative) comment into the top 30% most extreme 
segment of their respective ideological spectrum. We also investigate how the public release of 
ChatGPT affected authors near the ideological center and those at the margins of the liberal–
conservative boundary. We find a systematic leftward shift across these groups, though not 
substantial in magnitude. These results remain robust under a series of tests, including relative 
time models and multiple falsification tests. These results rule out alternative explanations for the

political polarization on Reddit, such as the possibility that other events, including the midterm 
election, influenced the observed polarization.  
To unpack the mechanism, we use state-of-the-art methods to classify Reddit posts and comments 
as ChatGPT-generated or human-generated (22-25). We find that ChatGPT-generated comments 
are substantially more likely to echo and reinforce the political stance of the original post than 
human-generated comments, a pattern consistent with algorithmic sycophancy—the tendency of 
LLMs to condition their responses on, and affirm, user inputs (26-28). Importantly, this pattern 
arises from how ChatGPT-generated comments respond to existing posts rather than from original 
content creation, indicating that, rather than fostering original argumentation, ChatGPT primarily 
scales reply-based expression by amplifying users’ responses to existing content in ways that align 
with the prevailing narrative of the thread. As a result, ChatGPT-generated comments amplify 
polarization in same-partisan post–comment pairs by reinforcing existing positions, while 
modestly moderating polarization in counter-partisan interactions, though not at a scale sufficient 
to offset the overall polarizing effect. 
Despite this amplification of ideological alignment within discussions, we observe a clear decline 
in affective polarization (29, 30)—the extent to which individuals express hostility or animosity 
toward opposing political groups. Measures of civility on the platform, captured through comment 
toxicity and hostility, improved markedly following ChatGPT’s release. Average toxicity scores 
declined by an amount equivalent to a shift from the median to approximately the 25th percentile 
of the distribution, accompanied by a significant reduction in hostility. Together, these results 
indicate that ChatGPT reshaped not only the ideological stance of online discussions but also the 
emotional tone in which they unfolded. 
Collectively, these results indicate that while ideological polarization increased following the 
launch of ChatGPT, affective polarization declined (29, 30). This divergence reflects the nature of 
algorithmic sycophancy: responses align with the position of the original post and amplify it, yet 
do so in a calmer and more civil register. As a result, existing viewpoints are reinforced while 
emotional friction is reduced. Consistent with prior work showing that reducing perceived hostility 
can mitigate partisan animosity (31), this dynamic fosters more civil interactions even as 
ideological separation deepens.  
These findings carry important implications for understanding how generative AI shapes public 
discourse in politically sensitive environments. While debates about tools like ChatGPT often 
focus on their potential to spread misinformation or toxic speech, our results suggest a more 
nuanced dynamic. The release of ChatGPT intensified ideological divergence primarily by 
providing responses that tend to overly affirm existing political views without escalating emotional 
hostility. This decoupling of ideological and affective polarization challenges the conventional 
view that political extremity often coexists with incivility (29, 32-34). From a policy perspective, 
these results underscore the need for platform governance that recognizes both the risks and civic 
potential of generative AI: while it may deepen ideological divides, it can also create conditions 
for more civil, structured, and informative discourse. Our findings thus offer a foundational step 
toward understanding the socio-political consequences of generative AI not just as a content 
generator, but as an active force shaping who speaks, what gets said, and how people respond.

Research Design 
In this study, we focus on the subreddit r/politics, the largest forum for political discussion on 
Reddit. We aim to test the ideological change of users before and after the launch of ChatGPT on 
November 30, 2022. Thus, we collect all the comments in r/politics three months before and three 
months after the launch date. Due to the seasonal pattern in individuals’ political ideologies (12, 
35), following the approach applied in recent economics literature (36-38), we construct the same 
set of comment panels for the same calendar period, one year prior, to serve as our control within 
a difference-in-differences (DID) design, estimating the average treatment effect of ChatGPT’s 
launch. Because we do not know when each user joined Reddit, we focus on users who posted at 
least one comment between June 1, 2021, and December 1, 2021, one year before ChatGPT’s 
release, which ensures temporal balance in our research design and avoids the results being driven 
by new users who joined the platform after the release of ChatGPT. The sample timeline and 
research design are shown in Figure 1. Ultimately, we collected 6,882,091 posts and comments by 
334,002 unique authors in total, splitting them into the control and treated samples.  
 
Figure 1: Sample Timeline and Research Design 
 
To obtain a continuous measure of political slant for each comment, we use a bag-of-words method 
that has been extensively applied in the literature (19-21). The slant score continuously ranges 
from -1 to +1, with lower values indicating stronger liberal orientations and higher values 
indicating stronger conservative orientations. The core justification for this approach is that liberals 
and conservatives tend to discuss different topics and prefer different vocabularies; even when 
referring to the same concept, the phrases they use can differ due to strategic intent. For example, 
when describing the federal tax on assets of the deceased, congressional Republicans prefer using 
“death tax” much more than “estate tax” as “estate tax sounds like it only hits the wealthy, but 
‘death tax’ sounds like it hits everyone” (19). For the present study, we leverage the 2021 Congress 
Records to generate the keyword dictionary that liberals and conservatives frequently use; details 
are presented in SI Appendix Table S1. A key contribution of our approach is to move beyond the 
conventional reliance on the 2005 Congress Records (19-21) by employing the 2021 Congress 
Records—the same year as our author selection—to better capture contemporary political 
dynamics and to re-estimate the keyword dictionary and coefficients.

Results 
In this section, we first demonstrate that political discourse on Reddit became more polarized 
following the launch of ChatGPT: liberal-leaning authors became more liberal and conservative-
leaning authors more conservative, with little change among neutral and centrist users. We then 
decompose authors on Reddit into active versus inactive ones based on their commenting 
frequency and show that the primary source of this polarization is previously inactive authors, 
whose linguistic patterns indicate greater reliance on ChatGPT and whose political expressions 
became substantially more extreme after its release. Next, we identify a mechanism consistent with 
algorithmic sycophancy: ChatGPT-generated replies systematically align with the ideological 
stance of the content to which they respond, thereby amplifying polarization in same-partisan 
interactions. Finally, we show that affective polarization declined over the same period.

C.3 Algorithm Sycophancy as the Mechanism 
To examine the mechanism through which ChatGPT contributes to increased political polarization 
on the platform, we first use the human score described above to classify all posts and comments 
in the subreddit as either human-generated or ChatGPT-generated.7 Political discussions on the 
platform take two primary forms: comments responding to posts and comments responding to 
other comments. Based on this structure, we organize the data into pairs consisting of an original 
thread (a post or a comment) and its subsequent reply. We then further classify these pairs into 
four categories: human-generated replies to human-generated threads, human-generated replies to 
ChatGPT-generated threads, ChatGPT-generated replies to ChatGPT-generated threads, and 
ChatGPT-generated replies to human-generated threads. For each category, we compare the

Discussion 
This study offers a first empirical investigations into how the release of ChatGPT, a widely adopted 
LLM, has influenced political discourse on social media platforms. Drawing on large-scale, 
detailed Reddit data and a robust measurement of ideological stance derived from congressional 
speeches, we demonstrate that the release of ChatGPT significantly intensified political 
polarization among users. Importantly, this increase in polarization does not stem from the creation 
of more persuasive or ideologically extreme original content. Instead, it appears to originate from 
how ChatGPT-generated comments respond to existing posts. By scaling reply-based expression, 
ChatGPT amplifies users’ responses in ways that align with the prevailing narrative of each 
discussion thread, thereby reinforcing existing positions. As a result, polarization is amplified in 
same-partisan post–comment interactions, while only modestly moderated in counter-partisan 
exchanges—effects that are insufficient to offset the overall rise in ideological polarization. 
Despite this increase in ideological polarization, our findings reveal a surprising and 
counterintuitive result—ChatGPT did not exacerbate affective polarization. In fact, indicators of 
hostility and toxicity declined following ChatGPT’s release. This suggests that while users became 
more ideologically divided, they did so in a more civil manner, possibly due to the structured, 
measured, and less inflammatory language characteristic of ChatGPT-generated content. This 
pattern aligns with recent literature indicating that exposure to balanced or clearly articulated 
viewpoints can reduce partisan misperceptions and emotional hostility (31). 
These findings contribute to a growing body of research exploring the societal consequences of 
generative AI. The findings underscore that the political implications of LLMs are multifaceted: 
while such tools may amplify ideological signals, they can also support less emotionally charged 
political exchanges. This dual effect challenges the simplistic narrative that AI tools necessarily 
degrade the quality of public discourse (46) and instead suggests they may reshape the nature of 
polarization itself—intensifying ideological differentiation while simultaneously reducing 
antagonism. More broadly, the results highlight how relatively simple and low-cost technological 
interventions can foster meaningful improvements in political dialogue, in contrast to large-scale, 
government-driven regulatory approaches that often risk unintended consequences or public 
backlash. This suggests that modest, decentralized tools may in practice achieve more sustainable 
progress in enhancing the quality of democratic discourse than sweeping policy “big hammers.” 
However, several important limitations remain. First, we do not directly observe whether a given 
comment was generated by ChatGPT; our identification strategy relies on behavioral shifts in 
linguistic patterns, which may be subject to measurement errors. Second, our study captures only 
the early effects of ChatGPT’s release; as both user familiarity and model capabilities evolve, the 
long-term dynamics of AI-mediated discourse may diverge from the initial patterns we observe 
here. 
Future research should explore how LLMs influence political expression across different platforms 
and cultural contexts, especially in regions where political sensitivities and AI regulations differ 
substantially. It is also crucial to examine how design choices—such as content safeguards, prompt 
engineering, and model fine-tuning—affect the nature and direction of AI’s impact on public 
dialogue. As generative AI tools become increasingly integrated into digital communication, 
understanding their role in shaping democratic discourse will be essential for both platform 
governance and public policy.
