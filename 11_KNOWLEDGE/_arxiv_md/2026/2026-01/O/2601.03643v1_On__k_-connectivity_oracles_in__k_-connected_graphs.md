On k-connectivity oracles in k-connected graphs
Zeev Nutov
The Open University of Israel
nutov@openu.ac.il
Abstract
A k-connectivity oracle for a graph G = (V, E) is a data structure that given s, t ∈V determines
whether there are at least k + 1 internally disjoint st-paths in G. For undirected graphs, Pettie,
Saranurak & Yin [10] proved that any k-connectivity oracle requires Ω(kn) bits of space. We will
show by a very simple proof that this is so even if G is k-connected, answering an open question
posed in [10].

Introduction
Given a directed or undirected graph G = (V, E) let κ(s, t) denote the maximum number
of internally-disjoint st-paths in G; G is k-connected if κ(s, t) ≥k for all (s, t) ∈V × V .
A k-connectivity oracle for G is a data structure that given s, t ∈V determines whether
κ(s, t) > k. We will consider the following question – what is the minimum number of bits
that a k-connectivity oracle of an n-node graph needs?
It is not hard to establish that for both directed and undirected graphs at least n bits are
needed even for k = 0. For undirected graphs and k = 0 we just need to give a different label
for every connected component, and thus O(n log n) bits suffice. More generally, in the case
of edge-connectivity, the relation “there are k-edge-disjoint st-paths” is an equivalence, which
implies that also in this case O(n log n) bits suffice. This bound is also achievable by the
Gomory-Hu cut-tree [2], that can also return a minimum st-edge-cut. In contrast, at least
n2/4 bits are needed to answer reachability queries (the case k = 0) for a directed acyclic
graph (DAG), c.f. [5, 7, 1]; this follows from the fact that the number of distinct posets on n
elements is at least 2n2/4 [5].
Now let us consider node-connectivity in undirected graphs. It is well known that any
graph G has for any k has a “sparse certificate” – a spanning subgraph with O(kn) edges,
that preserves pairwise connectivities (and also pairwise minimum node-cuts) of G up to k,
see [8]. Such a subgraph can serve as a k-connectivity oracle. Pettie, Saranurak & Yin [10]
proved that this is essentially the best possible – a k-connectivity oracle for an undirected
graph needs Ω(kn) bits of space. They asked whether Ω(kn) bits are still necessary if G is
k-connected. We will answer this question by proving the following.
▶Theorem 1. For any integers k ≥1 and 1 ≤p ≤2k2/4 there exists a k-connected graph
G = G(k, p) with n = k(p + 1) nodes such that any k-connectivity oracle for G requires at
least p · k2/4 =

Proof of Theorem 1
We will use a construction of [6], that for any digraph D = (R, A) constructs an undirected
graph G = (R ∪R′, E) (where R′ is a copy of R) that “shifts” the connectivities by exactly
|R|. Formally, this undirected graph is defined as follows.
▶Definition 2. The connectivity-shift graph of a directed graph D = (R, A) is an
undirected graph G = (V, E) obtained as follows.
1. Add a copy R′ of R and replace every arc uv ∈A by the undirected edge uv′, where
v′ ∈R′ denotes the copy of v ∈R.
2. Add a clique on each of R, R′ and the matching M = {vv′ : v ∈R}.
Let κ(G) = min{κ(s, t) : (s, t) ∈V × V } denote the (node-)connectivity of a directed or
an undirected graph G. The following was proved in [6].
▶Lemma 3 ([6]). Let G be the connectivity-shift graph of a digraph D = (R, A). Then the
following holds.
(i) κG(u, v′) = κD(u, v) + |R| for all u, v ∈R.
(ii) κ(G) = κ(D) + |R|.
As was mentioned, supporting reachability queries in an n-node DAG requires at least
n2/4 bits of space, c.f [5, 7, 1]. Note that κ(D) = 0 for any DAG D. Combining with
Lemma 3 we get the following.
▶Corollary 4. Let G be the connectivity-shift graph of a k-node DAG D = (R, A). Then:
1. κG(u, v′) = κD(u, v) + k for all u, v ∈R.
2. G has 2k nodes and κ(G) = k.
3. Supporting k-connectivity queries in G requires at least k2/4 bits of space.
Corollary 4 already implies that a data structure for answering k-connectivity queries
for a k-connected graph with n = 2k nodes requires at least k2/4 = kn/8 bits of space. We
would like to show that this is so also when n is much larger than k. A common technique to
achieve this is just to take p “gadgets” as in Corollary 4, of different DAGs that correspond
to different posets; it is known that there are at least 2k2/4 distinct posets on k elements
[5]. Then the graph will have n = 2pk nodes and the lower bound will be pk2/4 = nk/8 bits.
This technique works for general graphs, namely, we get a simple proof that the lower bound
of kn/8 bits applies for general graphs with n = 2pk nodes. But it does not apply directly to
k-connected graphs, as the obtained graph is not even connected.
To adopt this construction for k-connected graphs we modify it as follows. Let the p
gadget graphs have node sets R∪R1, R∪R2, . . . , R∪Rp. We identify the set R of all p gadgets.
The obtained graph G has n = k(p + 1) nodes and a lower bound of pk2/4 = 1−1/p

Proof. It is known that a graph G is not k-connected if and only if there exists a proper
partition S, C, T of the node set of G such that |C| < k and such that G has no ST-edge.
Suppose to the contrary that the constructed graph G has such a partition. If S ∩R ̸= ∅
then T ∩R = ∅and T ∩Ri = ∅for all i, since for all i, the subgraph G[R ∪Ri] induced by
R ∪Ri is k-connected; this contradicts that T ̸= ∅. Similarly, we cannot have T ∩R ̸= ∅. It
follows therefore that R ⊆C, giving the contradiction |C| ≥|R| = k.
