ONSAGER’S MEAN FIELD THEORY OF VORTEX FLOWS WITH
SINGULAR SOURCES: BLOW UP AND CONCENTRATION WITHOUT
QUANTIZATION.
D. BARTOLUCCI, P. COSENTINO, L. WU
Abstract. Motivated by the Onsager statistical mechanics description of turbulent Euler flows
with point singularities, we make a first step in the generalization of the mean field theory in
[Caglioti, Lions, Marchioro, Pulvirenti; Comm. Math. Phys. (1995)]. On one side we prove
the equivalence of statistical ensembles, on the other side we are bound to the analysis of a
new blow up phenomenon, which we call “blow up and concentration without quantization”,
where the mass associated with the concentration is allowed to take values in a full interval of
real numbers. This singular behavior may be regarded as lying between the classical blow up-
concentration-quantization and the blow up without concentration phenomenon first proposed
in [Lin, Tarantello; C.R. Math.
Acad.
Sci.
Paris (2016)].
A careful analysis is needed to
generalize known pointwise estimates in this non standard context, resulting in a complete
description of the allowed asymptotic profiles.
1. Introduction
In a pioneering paper ([61]) L. Onsager derived a statistical mechanics description of large vortex
structures observed in two dimensional turbulent flows.
Among other things he concluded
that the maximum entropy (thermodynamic equilibrium) states could be realized by highly
concentrated configurations, where like vortices attract each other. These states are negative
temperature states, meaning that, above a certain energy E0 ∈(0, +∞), the equilibrium entropy
S(E) is decreasing as a function of the energy E. Those physical arguments has been later turned
into rigorous proofs ([26], [27], [44], [45], [59]). We refer to [39], [40] and references therein for
a complete discussion about the Onsager theory and a more detailed account of the impact of
those ideas and to [46], [59] for the Onsager model with random vorticity.
Following the Onsager ideas and assuming the vorticity to be positive, in the framework of mean
field theory and in a certain sharp range of temperatures one can prove ([26], [27]) equivalence of
statistical ensembles where the thermodynamic equilibrium is attained by entropy maximizing
configurations which solve the so called mean field equation (which is (1.5) below with σ = 0 and
λ < 8π). However, when defined on S2, as far as λ < 16π solutions of the mean field equation are
just the trivial solutions (see [53], [54] and [42]). Therefore, with the aim of describing vorticity
fields of geophysical interest [56], it seems natural to introduce in the Onsager mean field model
a fixed, macroscopic co-rotating or counter-rotating vortex. However, with the unique exception
of a short comment in [29], it seems that this case has not been discussed in literature. Indeed,
all the works at hand so far about point singularities in mean field equations has been concerned
with the so called “conical singularities” see [5, 10, 11, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 20, 33, 34, 36, 49,
65, 71, 72, 76, 77, 79, 81] and Remark 1.3 below. It is our aim here to make a first step in the
generalization of the mean field theory in [27] to cover this singular case.

8.1. Known results about problem (3.7).
We list some well known facts about the planar problem (3.7). For ϵ0 = 0, the set of solutions
has been completely classified in [65] for every α∞,σ > −1 and eβ = 8π(1 + α∞,σ). Moreover, if

On the other hand, for ϵ0 > 0 the situation is much more involved (see [54]):
• If α∞,σ ∈(−1, 1], problem (3.7) is solvable if and only if
eβ ∈(8π min{1, 1 + α∞,σ}, 8π max{1, 1 + α∞,σ})
and the solution is unique, radially symmetric and non degenerate.
• If α∞,σ > 1, there exists a unique, radial and non degenerate solution, whenever
eβ ∈(8πα∞,σ, 8π(1 + α∞,σ)).

any solution of (3.7) is radially symmetric. In general the problem is still open, and we refer to
