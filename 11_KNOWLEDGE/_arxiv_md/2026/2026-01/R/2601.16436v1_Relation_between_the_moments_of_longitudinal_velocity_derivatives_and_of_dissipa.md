I.
INTRODUCTION
Velocity derivatives, ∂iuj, are essential quantities to describe the properties of turbulent ﬂows. In particular, they
are crucial to understand dynamical aspects, including ﬂuid deformation, vorticity-strain interaction, stretching
and ampliﬁcation of scalar gradients. Moreover, velocity derivatives are related to the fundamental parameter
characterising turbulence, the energy dissipation rate ǫ = 2νSijSij, where Sij ≡1/2(∂iuj + ∂jui) is the rate of
strain. Therefore, the statistical properties of velocity derivatives have been investigated extensively [1–3]. In
fact, the very strong ﬂuctuations of the rate of energy dissipation in turbulent ﬂows, which are at the root of the
phenomenon of intermittency, leading to deviations from the famous Kolmogorov theory [4], were ﬁrst revealed by
studying the fourth moment of the longitudinal velocity derivative ∂1u1 from hot-wire measurements in a wind
tunnel [5]. For historical and practical reasons, the quantity ǫs ≡15ν(∂1u1)2 deﬁned with the longitudinal velocity
derivate only has been widely used as a surrogate of the energy dissipation rate ǫ because S11 = ∂1u1 can be
obtained using a single hot-wire probe in intense turbulent ﬂows with large mean velocities such as wind tunnels
and atmospheric boundary layers, and because their averages ⟨ǫs⟩and ⟨ǫ⟩are equal in homogeneous and isotropic
turbulence (HIT). In the case n = 2, an exact relation between ⟨ǫ2⟩and ⟨ǫ2
s⟩establishing a proportionality between
⟨(∂1u1)4⟩and ⟨(SijSji)2⟩has been obtained by Siggia [6]. This is an important information, since an outstanding
question in turbulence theory is to characterize the nature of the ﬂuctuations of ǫ, or equivalently, the moments of
the distribution. Whether the moments of order n ≥3 of ǫ and ǫs are simply related to each other is the question
asked in this note.

all integers n, which agree with the known values when n = 1 and n = 2. In this short article, we show that the
general relation in [7] is actually not exact.

(* =========================*)

(* =========================*)
(*1. Double factorial and angular average formula*)
(* =========================*)
(*double factorial, with (-1)!!=1*)

angleAvgK[a_Integer, b_Integer, c_Integer, n_Integer] :=
df[2 a - 1] df[2 b - 1] df[2 c - 1] / df[4 n + 1];
(* =========================*)
(*2. Compute F[n] = <S11^(2n)>*)
(* =========================*)
(* Use: S11 = \[Lambda]1 c1^2 + \[Lambda]2 c2^2 + \[Lambda]3 c3^2,
k1 + k2 + k3 = 2n, sum over k1,k2 with k3=2n-k1-k2 *)

angleAvgK[k1, k2, k3, n] *

];
(* incompressibility constraint: \[Lambda]3 = -\[Lambda]1 - \[Lambda]2 *)
Expand[expr /. \[Lambda]3 -> -\[Lambda]1 - \[Lambda]2]
];

(*3. T2, T3 and basis functions G[n,p]*)
(* =========================*)
(* For a 3x3 incompressible symmetric traceless matrix:
T2 = tr(S^2) = 2(\[Lambda]1^2 + \[Lambda]2^2 + \[Lambda]1 \[Lambda]2)
T3 = tr(S^3) = 3 \[Lambda]1 \[Lambda]2 \[Lambda]3

T2expr = 2 (\[Lambda]1^2 + \[Lambda]2^2 + \[Lambda]1 \[Lambda]2);
T3expr = -3 \[Lambda]1 \[Lambda]2 (\[Lambda]1 + \[Lambda]2);
