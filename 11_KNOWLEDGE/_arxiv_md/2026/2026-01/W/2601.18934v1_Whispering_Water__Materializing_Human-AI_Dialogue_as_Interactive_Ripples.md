Abstract
Across cultures, water has served as a recipient of human confession,
a yielding medium that receives vulnerability where rigid surfaces
cannot. We present Whispering Water, an interactive installation
that materializes human-AI dialogue through cymatic patterns on
water. Participants confess secrets to a water surface, triggering a
four-phase ritual: confession, contemplation, response, and release.
The user’s speech sentiment is directly transmitted into the water to
prime its state, while semantic content enters a multi-agent system,

through discourse and voice profiles are chosen based on what they

rendering machine reasoning as emergent physical phenomena,
the installation explores possibilities for emotional self-exploration
through ambiguous, sensory-rich interfaces.

1
Introduction
People have long whispered to water. Celtic peoples believed spirits
in sacred wells could grant wishes spoken aloud [12]. In Japanese
misogi, practitioners chant while immersed, asking kami to cleanse
impurities [21]. Greek hydromancy interpreted ripples as divine
messages [7]. Daoist philosophy understands water as yin, receptive
yet powerful through softness [3]. Across these traditions, water
functions as a listener, responding through subtle material changes
before returning to stillness.
We present Whispering Water, an interactive installation that
distills the cultural phenomenon of confessing to water into dia-
logue with a multi-agent AI system responding through water as
its medium. Participants speak to the water surface; their voice
undergoes sentiment analysis to prime the water’s state, while se-
mantic content is forwarded to six AI agents engaged in parallel
dialogue. The agents respond to both the participant and one an-
other, producing moments of convergence and divergence before
reconciliation. Each agent’s synthesized speech is decomposed into
harmonic components and reconstructed through acoustic super-
position, translating machine voices into cymatic patterns: from
voice to vibration, sentiment to frequency, linguistic meaning to
material form. This work makes the following contributions:
• We present an interactive installation that materializes
human-AI conversation through water as a medium.
• We propose a novel algorithmic framework that decom-
poses speech into component waves and reconstructs them
in water, establishing a translation between speech and the
physics of material form.
• We implement a multi-agent system contextualized through
water’s materiality, where agent identities emerge dynami-
cally through situated discourse.
2
Related Works
Water as a responsive material has drawn sustained attention from
creative practitioners. Prior work has explored water across physi-
cal forms including droplets [9, 11, 17], bubbles [5, 13], streams [10],
and mist [19]. Many approaches explore water as an acoustic medium
through cymatics [4, 6]. Turczan’s Mirror Speak uses low-frequency
vibrations to make water surfaces "speak" [18]; Sonic Water invited
participants to generate patterns with their voices [14]; Stanford’s
Cymatics let material properties dictate sound composition [15].
These works focus on translating sound energy into visible surface
waves, though rarely connected to AI. Our project treats water not
merely as a display medium but as an active translator between
spoken intimacy and machine reasoning.
3
Whispering Water
We distill the cultural phenomenon of confession to water into
a four-phase process: confession, contemplation, response, and
release. This sequence moves from vocal input to material activa-
tion and finally to dissipation, shifting the participant’s role from
speaker to observer as water becomes the primary site of meaning
(Figure 3).
Confession. Participants lean forward to speak close to the
water surface in a posture of intimacy. The system records the
voice while the water remains still.
Contemplation. After recording ends, overhead light slowly
rises as a standing wave forms on the surface. The wave’s frequency
reflects extracted sentiment, priming the medium before linguistic
response begins.
Response. The system engages six AI agents across four rounds.
First, each agent independently reflects on the utterance. Second,
agents select one among them to comment on the emerging dis-
course. Third, the selected agent responds. During these rounds,

4
Design & Technical Implementation
4.1
Installation and Physics of Water
The installation consists of a metal shelf frame (72 × 12 × 32 inches),
enclosed with Mylar film and mirrored Plexiglass to form a water
basin. The elongated tunnel creates an intimate space for sharing
secrets; mirrored surfaces reflect the viewer while the Mylar mem-
brane vibrates with the water, creating interference patterns that
resonate with cymatic formations. The basin holds approximately
2 gallons of water mixed with UV-reactive dye. Color-changing
LEDs above and UV LEDs below activate the dye and accentuate
surface motion, controlled by Arduino and TouchDesigner. A con-
cealed microphone records spoken input for up to 15 seconds. The
cymatics system comprises six subwoofers driven by three ampli-
fiers, routed through a Focusrite Scarlett 18i20 using multi-channel
output. Subwoofers are arranged beneath the bottom shelf, spaced
12 inches apart, and mechanically coupled to the structure. Low-
frequency vibrations from TouchDesigner propagate through the
frame into the water vessel. Due to standing wave physics, cymatic
patterns are most pronounced in the central region, a consideration
integrated into our algorithmic design.
4.2
Sentiment Analysis of Human Speech
Unlike human speech, synthesized machine voices tend toward
uniform rhythm and constrained emotional range [20]. We there-
fore use the participant’s vocal quality as a primer, establishing an
initial cymatic state during contemplation before agent interaction.
We deploy emotion2vec+ to analyze speech acoustics, capturing
pitch, energy, speaking rate, timbre, and prosody. Emotional out-
put is translated into excitation frequencies: low (20–40 Hz) for
slow undulations, mid-range (50–70 Hz) for regular standing waves,
and high (80–100 Hz) for dense, energetic surface activity, emitted
simultaneously across all subwoofers.
4.3
Multi-agent Conversation
Agent-to-agent communication in multi-agent systems typically
assigns role identities through task-based allocation [8] or frame-
works like the BDI model [2]. However, Bakhtin’s dialogism [1] and
Suchman’s situated action theory [16] suggest that meaning and
agency emerge through interaction rather than residing in individ-
uals. Inspired by these perspectives, our system treats agent roles as
dynamically constituted through discourse rather than predefined.
Our agent pool comprises multiple untuned LLMs (Claude Son-
net 4.5, GPT-4.5, Gemini 2.0, etc.) to introduce heterogeneity in rea-
soning patterns and linguistic styles. In the response stage, the par-
ticipant’s speech is transcribed via ASR and forwarded to six agents
processing in parallel. Each agent produces a brief response (150
characters) while selecting a voice persona encoded in JSON. We im-
plement real-time concurrent streaming where all agents execute in
parallel and outputs are interleaved through asynchronous queue
multiplexing. Following text generation, agents produce speech
through their chosen voice profiles. Rather than assigning fixed
vocal identities, agents dynamically select TTS personas based on
content and tone, allowing vocal identity to emerge from discourse
rather than being imposed as a stable attribute. Personas draw
from expressive voice profiles in ElevenLabs spanning diverse ages,

4.4
Speech to Wave Translation
In our design, an agent’s voice is decomposed into multiple wave
components recomposed within water; when multiple agents speak
simultaneously, their waves form superpositions. We decompose
each speech signal into one to six wave components played through
six subwoofers and physically reconstructed in water, performing
the core translation: converting semantic speech into physical vi-
bration through signal decomposition and acoustic reconstruction
(Figure 5).

Conclusion

stallation that materializes human-AI conversation through water.
Structured as a process where humans confess and AI agents re-
spond through cymatic patterns, the system employs algorithms
that decompose and reconstruct speech in water through signal
processing and speech language models. The work operates as
