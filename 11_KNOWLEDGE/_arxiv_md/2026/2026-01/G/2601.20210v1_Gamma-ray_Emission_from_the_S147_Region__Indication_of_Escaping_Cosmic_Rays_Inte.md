ABSTRACT
We present a detailed analysis of γ-ray emission from the middle-aged supernova remnant (SNR)
S147 (G180.0−1.7) using approximately 16.5 years of Fermi-LAT data. Spatially, a new extended
γ-ray component distinct from the emission associated with the Hα filaments of the SNR shell is
identified.
This new component exhibits a strong spatial correlation with dense molecular clouds
(MCs) identified in CO emission at Local Standard of Rest velocities of 0–5 km s−1. Spectrally, the
cloud-associated emission implies an underlying cosmic-ray (CR) proton population described by a
hard power law with an index of Γ ≈2.1, compatible with the standard diffusive shock acceleration
prediction.
We interpret the γ-ray emission in this region with a hadronic scenario involving two
distinct CR populations: trapped CRs reaccelerated within the radiative SNR shell as proposed in
previous work, and escaping CRs illuminating the nearby MCs.
The derived CR proton intensity
in the MC region significantly exceeds the local Galactic background measured by AMS-02, strongly
suggesting that the cloud is illuminated by particles accelerated by S147.
These findings provide
observational evidence for CR escape during the earlier evolutionary phases of this middle-aged SNR
and highlight S147 as a promising candidate for detection at TeV energies by LHAASO.

1. INTRODUCTION
Cosmic rays (CRs) were discovered more than a century ago, yet their origin remains one of the most fundamental
questions in high-energy astrophysics. The “knee” in the all-particle CR spectrum around 3×1015 eV marks a transition
in acceleration mechanisms or source populations, below which CRs are widely believed to be of Galactic origin (A. M.
Hillas 2005). Among the proposed Galactic accelerators, supernova remnants (SNRs) have long been considered the
primary candidates, capable of efficiently accelerating particles to high energies via the diffusive shock acceleration
(DSA) mechanism (A. R. Bell 1978; R. Blandford & D. Eichler 1987; P. Blasi 2013). The observed γ-ray emission from
the vicinity of SNRs generally originates from two fundamental processes involving these accelerated particles: leptonic
and hadronic (F. A. Aharonian 2013). In the leptonic scenario, relativistic electrons produce γ-ray through inverse
Compton (IC) scattering off ambient photon fields or via non-thermal bremsstrahlung when interacting with gas. This
mechanism tends to dominate in SNRs expanding into low-density environments, as exemplified by RX J1713.7−3946
and RCW 86 (F. Aharonian et al. 2006; M. Ajello et al. 2016). In contrast, collisions between accelerated protons
and the interstellar medium produce neutral pions that subsequently decay into γ-rays (π0 →2γ). This hadronic
process yields a characteristic spectral feature known as the “pion-decay bump” in the 50–200 MeV energy range
(C. D. Dermer 1986; M. Ackermann et al. 2013) and is prominently observed in middle-aged SNRs interacting with
dense molecular clouds (MCs), such as W44, IC 443, and W51C (M. Ackermann et al. 2013; T. Jogler & S. Funk
2016).

In the hadronic scenario, the spectral and spatial characteristics of the γ-ray emission depend critically on the
confinement status of the interacting protons and the distribution of target material. Particles accelerated at the shock
front can either remain trapped within the SNR shell or escape into the surrounding medium. The trapped population,
confined by magnetic turbulence near the shock, typically exhibits a source spectrum reflecting the acceleration process.
In contrast, the escaping population propagates into the surroundings via diffusion. Because the diffusion coefficient
is energy-dependent (D(E) ∝Eδ), high-energy particles escape more efficiently and traverse the distance to remote
clouds faster than low-energy ones (F. A. Aharonian & A. M. Atoyan 1996; V. S. Ptuskin & V. N. Zirakashvili 2005; S.
Gabici et al. 2007). Consequently, MCs located at a distance from the shock are illuminated primarily by these higher-
energy escaping particles, leading to a γ-ray spectrum that is often harder than that of the shell-confined emission
and the ambient Galactic CR sea, with the detailed spectral shape depending on the diffusion distance and time (S.
Gabici et al. 2009; H. Li & Y. Chen 2012; L. Nava et al. 2016).
Simeis 147 (S147, G180.0−1.7) is a large, evolved shell-type SNR (J. R. Dickel & R. R. McKinley 1969) located
toward the Galactic anticentre, with an angular diameter of approximately 180′ (D. A. Green 2019). The remnant
is renowned for its prominent filamentary morphology in Hα line emission (T. A. Lozinskaya 1976), which correlates
well with radio observations (Y. Sofue et al. 1980; L. Xiao et al. 2008). Spatially associated with the remnant is the
pulsar PSR J0538+2817, located approximately 40′ west of the geometrical center. Its parallax distance of 1.3+0.22
−0.16 kpc
(S. Chatterjee et al. 2009) is consistent with the recent measurement of 1.37+0.10
−0.07 kpc derived from background stars
(C. S. Kochanek et al. 2024), establishing a reliable distance scale for the system. Consequently, we adopt a distance
of 1.3 kpc for all physical parameter estimations throughout this work. Regarding the evolutionary state of S147,
while standard Sedov-Taylor models suggest an older age (C. S. Kochanek et al. 2024), recent X-ray observations by
SRG/eROSITA support a supernova-in-a-cavity scenario with an age of approximately 35 kyr (I. I. Khabibullin et al.
2024; M. Michailidis et al. 2024). This revised age is consistent with the kinematic age of the associated pulsar (M.
Kramer et al. 2003), providing a coherent picture of the remnant’s history. Crucially for CR propagation studies, 3D
dust extinction mapping has identified a large dust structure at a distance of d = 1.22 ± 0.21 kpc, which spatially
coincides with the SNR (B.-Q. Chen et al. 2017).
Together with MCs identified in the Local Standard of Rest
(LSR) velocity range of −14 to +5 km s−1 (I.-G. Jeong et al. 2012), these structures provide a potential reservoir of
target material for CRs. While conclusive dynamical evidence for shock-cloud interaction (such as OH masers or line
broadening) has been elusive, this complex environment is suitable for testing particle escape scenarios.
In the high-energy regime, previous studies using Fermi Large Area Telescope (LAT) data detected a spatially
extended γ-ray source (0.2–10 GeV) coincident with S147 (J. Katsuta et al. 2012). The emission morphology in this
energy band shows a possible spatial correlation with the prominent Hα filaments, leading to the conclusion that
the emission is dominated by CRs interacting with the dense gas within the filaments. However, observations above
10 GeV reveal a different picture. In this higher energy band, a simple flat disk template provides a better fit to the
data than the Hα-based template used by J. Katsuta et al. (2012). Furthermore, this high-energy component exhibits
a hard spectrum with an index of approximately 2 (M. Ajello et al. 2017; M. Ackermann et al. 2017), which contrasts
significantly with the softer spectrum measured at lower energies (J. Katsuta et al. 2012). These discrepancies in
both morphology and spectral index strongly suggest the presence of a distinct emission component in the region,
separate from the CRs trapped in the filaments. Given the complex environment surrounding S147, where MCs have
been detected in the vicinity (I.-G. Jeong et al. 2012) and dust structures suggest dense material at the remnant’s
distance (B.-Q. Chen et al. 2017), this hard component may arise from escaping particles accelerated by the remnant
illuminating nearby molecular material. The diffusion of these escaping CRs into the surrounding clouds would generate
significant hadronic γ-ray emission with precisely such a distinct hard spectrum, warranting a dedicated investigation
into unassociated residuals near the SNR boundary.
In this work, we present a detailed analysis of the γ-ray emission from S147 using approximately 16.5 years of Fermi-
LAT data. We combine these data with CO emission templates to systematically search for gas targets of escaping
CRs. We identify a γ-ray component spatially correlated with MCs in the 0–5 km s−1 LSR velocity range, distinct
from the Hα-correlated emission. We demonstrate that the CO template provides a statistically superior description
of this component compared to geometric models, and that this result is robust against different background modeling
assumptions. Finally, we interpret the γ-ray spectra using a unified hadronic scenario in which a population of trapped
CRs interacting with the SNR shell corresponding to the Hα component and a population of escaping CRs illuminating
nearby MCs corresponding to the CO component.

2. MOLECULAR CLOUDS DISTRIBUTION AROUND S147
Recently, a 3D dust extinction study by B.-Q. Chen et al. (2017) identified a prominent dust structure (the “S147 dust
cloud”) at a distance range of ∼1.0–1.6 kpc, which is consistent with the SNR’s distance. Several dense clumps within
this dust structure exhibit a strong spatial correspondence with the CO features observed in the region. Assuming
these MCs are associated with the dust structure and thus located in the immediate environment of S147, they could
constitute ideal targets. Protons escaping the acceleration site could diffuse into these clouds, producing hadronic
γ-ray emission detectable by Fermi-LAT.
Given the location toward the Galactic anticenter, the kinematic distances of the MCs around S147 derived from
Galactic rotation are highly uncertain (J. Brand & L. Blitz 1993). Thus, we prioritized morphological correspondence
to identify the gas associated with the γ-ray emission. Using CO survey data from T. M. Dame et al. (2001), we
inspected the CO gas distribution across a broad velocity range of vLSR ∈[−15, +10] km s−1, a range in which I.-
G. Jeong et al. (2012) found MCs that partially overlap with S147 along the line of sight. Applying a conversion
factor of XCO = 1.8 × 1020 cm−2 (K km s−1)−1 (T. M. Dame et al. 2001), we derived the molecular hydrogen column
density in different velocity intervals. The molecular gas distributions, overlaid with the contours of the Hα emission
from S147 (J. Katsuta et al. 2012), are illustrated in Figure 1. A comparison between the molecular gas morphology
and the Fermi-LAT source positions reveals a notable spatial correlation in the 0–5 km s−1 velocity interval (see
Figure 1e). Specifically, the dense region of the MC in this range spatially overlaps with the unassociated source
4FGL J0534.2+2751 (J. Ballet et al. 2023). Moreover, this molecular feature also coincides with the extended source
3FHL J0537.6+2751e detected above 10 GeV (M. Ajello et al. 2017), which represents a hard-spectrum component
with an extended disk morphology reported in previous analyses (e.g., M. Ackermann et al. 2017) and is indicated
by the white circles in Figure 1. This spatial coincidence between the dense CO clump, the persistent unassociated
source, and the reported high-energy extended emission may imply a physical association between the molecular gas
and escaping CRs. Thus, the molecular gas distribution in the 0–5 km s−1 velocity interval as presented in Figure 1e
is adopted as the spatial template in the subsequent likelihood analysis to quantitatively test this association.
3. FERMI-LAT DATA ANALYSIS
3.1. Data Selection and Preparation
In this work, we utilize approximately 16.5 years of Fermi-LAT Pass 8 data, covering the observation period from
August 4, 2008 to March 18, 2025. We select SOURCE class events (evclass=128) with both FRONT and BACK con-
version types (evtype=3) in the energy range 100 MeV–1 TeV. To minimize contamination from the Earth’s limb,
we apply a maximum zenith angle cut of 90◦. Standard data quality selections are imposed using (DATA QUAL>0 &&
LAT CONFIG==1). Given the angular extent of S147, angular diameter of ∼3◦, a sufficiently large analysis region is
required to encompass the remnant and account for the Point Spread Function (PSF) containment. We therefore
adopt a 14◦× 14◦square region of interest (ROI) centered on the geometrical center of S147 (J2000: R.A. = 84.75◦,
Decl. = 27.83◦). We bin the data using a spatial pixel size of 0.1◦and 10 logarithmic energy bins per decade. The
baseline background model is constructed based on the Fermi-LAT 14-year Source Catalog (4FGL-DR4; J. Ballet et al.
2023), including all point-like and extended sources located within 20◦of the ROI center. The Galactic diffuse emission
is modeled using the standard template (gll iem v07.fits), and the isotropic diffuse background is described by the
spectrum file (iso P8R3 SOURCE V3 v1.txt) provided by the Fermi-LAT Collaboration. Energy dispersion effects are
accounted for by enabling the correction on the spectrum (edisp bins = -2).
We perform binned maximum likelihood analyses over the selected energy range using the Fermitools package.
Unless otherwise specified, the following fitting strategy is applied to all global likelihood analyses presented in this
work (including Sections 3.2 and 3.3): the spectral parameters of sources located within 7◦of the ROI center with a
detection significance > 15σ are left free to vary; additionally, the normalizations of both the Galactic and isotropic
diffuse emission components are treated as free parameters. All other spectral parameters are fixed to their catalog
values to ensure fit stability. We verified that modest variations in these selection criteria (e.g., changing the significance
threshold) do not significantly impact the separation of the Hα and CO components or the spectral results.

The quantitative comparison (Table 1) yields three key results. First, simply removing 4FGL J0534.2+2751 from
the Baseline drastically degrades the fit (∆AIC = −45.6), confirming the significance of the excess emission. Second,
while the geometric templates (Disk and Gaussian) improve the fit, they yield lower ∆AIC values in Scenario B
compared to Scenario A (e.g., for the Gaussian, 44.8 vs. 53.2). This implies that these simple geometric shapes cannot
fully account for the emission, necessitating the retention of the point source to minimize residuals. In contrast, the
physical CO (0–5 km s−1) template provides the best overall description. Uniquely among the tested models, it achieves
a higher ∆AIC in Scenario B (66.6) than in Scenario A (61.3). This statistical preference implies that the point source
4FGL J0534.2+2751 is redundant when the molecular gas distribution is accounted for, suggesting that the point-
like emission listed in the catalog is likely an artifact of modeling the local density peaks within the extended cloud
structure as a distinct source. Consequently, we identify the emission previously attributed to the unassociated source
as part of extended hadronic emission from the cloud, and we adopt the model Baseline (S147 Hα) + CO(0–5 km s−1)
−4FGL J0534.2+2751 for the subsequent spectral analysis.
3.3. Spectral Analysis
Adopting the optimal spatial model determined in Section 3.2 (Baseline + CO(0–5 km s−1) −4FGL J0534.2+2751),
we proceeded to characterize the spectral properties of the γ-ray emission around S147.
We performed a global
maximum likelihood fit over the full energy range of 100 MeV–1 TeV using the same fitting strategy described in
Section 3.1. In this configuration, the spectral shape of the extended SNR S147 (traced by Hα) was modeled as a
LogParabola function, consistent with the 4FGL catalog definition. The CO component was modeled as a simple
Power Law. The global fit yields a spectral index of ΓCO = 2.1 ± 0.05 for the CO component, consistent with the
hard component listed in the 3FHL catalog. Both components are detected with high significance: TSHα ≈274 and
TSCO ≈147, respectively.
To derive the spectral energy distributions (SEDs), we divided the energy range into 10 logarithmically spaced bins
to perform bin-by-bin analyses. The analysis at the lowest energies requires special treatment due to the large PSF
of Fermi-LAT, which causes severe source confusion between the spatially overlapping Hα and CO components. To
mitigate this, we merged the first two low-energy bins (100–631 MeV) into a single interval and restricted the likelihood
analysis to evtype=32 (PSF3) events, utilizing the data class with the best angular resolution. This stringent selection
minimizes the leakage of the SNR shell emission into the MC region caused by the broad PSF at low energies.
Accordingly, the isotropic diffuse emission for this bin was modeled using the specific PSF3 tabulated spectrum
(iso P8R3 SOURCE V3 PSF3 v1.txt). For the subsequent higher energy bins, the standard data selection was applied.
In each spectral bin, we performed a fit where the normalizations of both the Hα and CO components, as well as the
Galactic and isotropic diffuse backgrounds, were left free to vary. The spectral shape parameters were fixed to their
global best-fit values to ensure stability within narrow energy bands. We imposed a detection threshold of TS ≥4
(∼2σ). For energy bins where a component was not significantly detected, we calculated the 95% confidence level
upper limit.
The resulting SEDs are presented in Figure 3. For comparison, we also derived the SED of the Hα emission using
the original baseline model (i.e., excluding the CO component), shown as the blue curve. As evident in the figure, the
inclusion of the CO component significantly reduces the flux attributed to the Hα component (red curve) compared
to the Hα-only case, suggesting that a significant portion of the emission previously modeled as SNR shell emission
may be actually associated with the MCs. Furthermore, the CO component (green curve) exhibits a notably harder
PowerLaw spectrum compared to the curved LogParabola spectrum of the SNR emission. This spectral distinction may
support a scenario where energy-dependent CR escaping from the SNR illuminates the surrounding dense molecular
material. The physical implications of these spectral features and the derived CR densities will be discussed in detail
in Section 4.
4. DISCUSSION
The hadronic origin of the γ-ray emission in S147 has been previously established for the SNR shell component. This
conclusion was supported not only by the striking spatial correspondence between the γ-rays and the Hα filaments but
also by the spectral consistency with the decay of neutral pions produced in proton-proton interactions (J. Katsuta
et al. 2012). In this work, the strong spatial correlation identified between the newly detected extended emission
and MCs (Section 3.2) further reinforces the hadronic scenario, suggesting that γ-ray emission throughout the entire
region, both the shell and the cloud interaction sites, results from inelastic collisions between CR protons and ambient

As illustrated in Figure 5, the derived CR proton intensities in both the SNR shell and the MC regions significantly
exceed the local measurements over a broad energy range. We note that this conclusion is robust against parameter
uncertainties for both components. For the SNR shell, reconciling the γ-ray emission with the local Galactic CR
background would require a target gas mass orders of magnitude higher than our reference value of 1000 M⊙, which
far exceeds current estimates (J. Katsuta et al. 2012; I. I. Khabibullin et al. 2024). For the MC, even allowing for
plausible uncertainties in the distance estimation and the CO-to-H2 conversion factor, it is difficult to account for the
observed excess solely by the local CR background. Notably, the inferred spectra are considerably harder (α ≈2.1−2.2)
than the local measurements (α ≈2.7). To rigorously test the background contribution, we modeled the expected
γ-ray emission using the locally Galactic CR spectrum as a fixed input. As demonstrated in Figure 4 (blue dashed
curve), the Galactic CR background alone fails to reproduce the data, significantly under-predicting the high-energy
flux.
The detection of a harder and enhanced CR proton population strongly suggests an additional CR component
associated with S147.
A plausible scenario is that high-energy protons were accelerated by the SNR shock and
subsequently escaped into the surrounding MCs. This interpretation is supported by the spectral index of the protons
illuminating the clouds (α ≈2.12), which is consistent with the theoretical prediction for DSA mechanism (α ≈2.0)
and significantly flatter than the softened Galactic sea spectrum. Since S147 is a mature SNR, these high-energy
particles likely escaped during an earlier evolutionary phase when the shock velocity was higher.
These escaping
particles have since propagated to the nearby dense gas, producing the observed enhanced γ-ray emission. These
results provide a consistent picture where S147 acts as a source of CRs that illuminate the surrounding molecular
environment.

5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION
In this work, we performed a comprehensive study of the γ-ray emission from the middle-aged SNR S147 using
approximately 16.5 years of Fermi-LAT data. By utilizing event types with the best angular resolution at low energies
and combining the data with CO molecular line surveys, we have resolved the complex morphology of the region. A
key result of our analysis is the identification of an extended γ-ray component that is spatially distinct from the Hα
filaments of the SNR shell. This emission exhibits a strong correlation with dense MCs identified in the 0–5 km s−1
velocity interval. Spatial modeling confirms that a CO-based template provides a statistically superior description
of this emission compared to geometric models or the unassociated point source previously reported in this region,
suggesting that the point source was likely a misidentified density peak within a larger extended structure.
Our spectral analysis indicates that the emission from the SNR shell is well explained by the hadronic interactions
of CRs with the ambient gas, consistent with the interpretation in J. Katsuta et al. (2012). More importantly, we
find that the emission associated with the MCs in the vicinity of S147 exhibits a hard power-law spectrum with
an index of Γ ≈2.1. The CR intensity derived for this region significantly exceeds the local Galactic background.
This enhancement, combined with the hard spectral index, supports a scenario where the clouds are illuminated by
a population of escaping CRs efficiently accelerated via DSA during the remnant’s earlier, more active evolutionary
phases.
Notably, the hard spectrum of the MC component is consistent with extending to hundreds of GeV, showing no
significant evidence of a spectral cutoff within current uncertainties. This suggests that the maximum energy of the
escaping particles may extend well into the very high energy regime. Although S147 is not included in the first LHAASO
catalog (Z. Cao et al. 2024), the distinct hard spectral properties identified here make it a promising candidate that
may be detectable with further accumulated exposure. Future detection of emission at TeV energies would not only
constrain the maximum particle energy achieved during the remnant’s early active phase but also establish S147 as a
unique laboratory for studying how middle-aged SNRs continue to impact the surrounding interstellar medium through
escaping CRs.
