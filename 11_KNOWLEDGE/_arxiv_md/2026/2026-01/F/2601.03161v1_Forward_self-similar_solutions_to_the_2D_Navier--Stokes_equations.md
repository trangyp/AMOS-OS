Non-trivial −1–homogeneous velocity fields do not belong to the critical space

Lebesgue space Ld,∞, where only small-data global well-posedness results are known.1
Therefore, we regard −1–homogeneous velocity fields as on the borderline of the
well-posedness theory. We examine this borderline through the lens of self-similar
solutions. At a glance, this approach seems more tractable, since (1.4) is elliptic
and bears some resemblance to the steady Navier–Stokes equations, for which non-
uniqueness of solutions to the boundary-value problem is common, even expected,
see, e.g., [44, 29, 49].
Perhaps the simplest non-uniqueness scenario is that multiple self-similar so-
lutions evolve from the same self-similar initial data u0. In [42], Jia and ˇSver´ak
speculated that this might arise due to a bifurcation in the set of solutions to (1.4)-
(1.5) as the size of u0 is increased. Subsequently, such a bifurcation was reported
numerically by the second author and ˇSver´ak in [36].
The uniqueness question has been largely ignored in dimension d = 2, since
Leray-Hopf solutions are unique in 2D. However, in the infinite-energy class, there
is nothing to prevent the above scenario from occurring, as we illustrate below.

6.2. Continuation. A numerical continuation is performed in σ: in [0, 10] we

class of R-symmetric solutions.

6.3. Spectrum. For each value of σ, the first 10 smallest eigenvalues of the lin-
earization of (6.2) around eUσ are calculated using the Krylov–Schur algorithm [37]
implemented in SLEPc [38].
At σ = 0, the eigenvalues found numerically are given up to a very high precision
by 1 + n
2, n ∈N with multiplicity n + 1, and correspond exactly to the discrete
spectrum of the Stokes operator in self-similar coordinates.
The real part of the eigenvalues closer to the real axis are represented on Fig-
ure 6.2. In particular, a real eigenvalue crosses the real axis near σ0 ≈39.2 whereas
all the other eigenvalues have a strictly positive real part on the range σ ∈[0, 80].
Interestingly, the eigenvalue λ = 1 is unchanged with respect to σ; the explana-
tion is that one can prove that any eigenvector with non-zero mean has λ = 1.
6.4. Bifurcation. Standard bifurcation theory indicates that when a real eigen-
value is crossing the imaginary axis at σ0, then another solution should bifurcate
from eUσ at σ = σ0. This new branch of solution can be found numerically: for
a value of σ slightly bigger than σ0, Newton’s iterations are performed with the
initial guess eUσ + αϕ, where ϕ is the eigenvector corresponding to the crossing
eigenvalue, and α ∈R is some real parameter to be adjusted. Then a continuation
described above can be performed.
This leads to the numerical construction of a non-symmetric solution eUσ + eVσ
for σ ∈[σ0, 80] represented on Figure 6.3.
