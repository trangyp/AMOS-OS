REAL SUTURED HEEGAARD FLOER HOMOLOGY
FRASER BINNS, GARY GUTH, AND YONGHAN XIAO
Abstract. We develop a theory of real sutured manifolds and a real Heegaard Floer theory for
these manifolds, in line with [GM25]. We develop a notion of real nice diagrams, and prove that
our invariant is combinatorially computable. Our theory shares many structural properties with
Juh´asz’s sutured Floer homology [Juh06], as does the topological theory of real sutured manifolds
with Gabai’s original sutured manifold theory [Gab83]. We also show that our invariant has several
new structural properties differentiating it from sutured Floer homology.

1. Introduction
In recent years, there has been a growing interest in low-dimensional real manifolds: pairs
(Y, τ) where Y is a smooth manifold and τ is an orientation-preserving involution with some extra
assumption on the codimension of the fixed set. Gauge theoretic invariants of such pairs were first
introduced by Tian-Wang [TW09] and Nakamura [Nak13].
To define them, one counts invariant solutions to the Seiberg-Witten equations, and thereby
obtains an invariant of (Y, τ). There has been much development since: a real Seiberg-Witten Floer
homotopy type was defined by Konno, Miyazawa and Taniguchi in [KMT25] and real monopole
Floer homology was defined by Jiakai Li in [Li22]. These theories fit into the general structure of a
TQFT, assigning objects to real 3-manifolds and morphisms to real cobordisms between them.
While the study of symmetry as such has long been of interest to low-dimensional topologists,
these invariants do also give rise to new invariants of unreal objects, the paradigmatic example being
branched covers. Of course, any branched double cover comes with a natural real structure, given

Invariants of manifold-involution pairs have recently been used to answer important open questions
regarding sliceness of knots. The (2n, 1)-cables of the figure-8 knot are known not to be ribbon,
by work of Miyazaki [Miy94]. However, it was only recently shown that these knots are not slice,
using a slice-disk obstruction coming from branched double-covers equipped with their covering
involutions [DKM+24, KPT25, KPT26]. The involution plays a vital role in these proofs.
In another direction, Miyazawa recently produced an (infinite family of) exotic RP2-knots in
S4 [Miy23]. These 2-knots are distinguished by real Seiberg-Witten invariants of their branched
double covers equipped with their covering involutions. It was proved in [HKM25] that these knots
have double branched cover diffeomorphic to CP

fruitful.
Despite their successes, the drawback of gauge theoretic invariants is their difficulty to compute.
In the classical (unreal) setting for 3-manifolds, monopole Floer homology [KM07] is equivalent to
Ozsv´ath and Szab´o’s Heegaard Floer homology [OS04d], by [KLT20d, KLT20e, KLT20c, KLT20a,
KLT20b, CGH24a, CGH24c, CGH24b]. Here, Heegaard Floer homology is a family of invariants
defined using symplectic topology for which computations have proven more tractable. Indeed, the
hat version of Heegaard Floer homology is algorithmically computable [SW10].
In [GM25], the second author and Manolescu introduced real Heegaard Floer homology aiming to
provide a more computable counterpart to Li’s real monopole Floer homology. One of our main
results is that the hat version of this invariant is indeed algorithmically computable:
Theorem 1.1. For any pointed closed real 3-manifold (Y, τ, w), [

combinatorially from a nice real Heegaard diagram.
A nice real Heegaard diagram is a natural extension of Sarkar and Wang’s definition of a nice
diagram in the classical setting; see [SW10, Definition 3.1] for a precise definition in the unreal
setting and Definition 8.1 in the real setting. Given a nice diagram, the differential of the associated
Floer chain complex can be computed combinatorially. The existence of diagrams in the real settings
follows much of the same way as in [Juh08, Section 6], though some added care is needed in the
presence of the involution. While the real Heegaard Floer chain complex’s differential initially seems
more complicated, we prove that it can still be computed combinatorially. An independent proof
that the hat version of real Heegaard Floer homology is computable for closed real 3-manifolds with
connected fixed set is given in [LO26], using the framework of bordered Floer homology.

In the classical case, the interplay of topology and Floer homology is exemplified by that of
Gabai’s theory of sutured manifolds [Gab83] and Juh´asz’s sutured Floer homology, a generalization of
the hat version of Heegaard Floer homology to the sutured manifold setting [Juh06]. Here, a sutured
manifold is a manifold equipped with a particular decomposition of its boundary. Foundational
results in sutured manifold theory include the existence of sutured hierarchies for taut sutured
manifolds [Gab83, Theorem 4.2]. Here, a taut sutured manifold is an irreducible sutured manifold
with non-degeneracy assumptions on its sutured structure [Gab83, Definition 2.10].

To study real sutured manifolds, we define a version of sutured Floer homology that we call real
sutured Floer homology. This invariant generalizes the hat version of real Heegaard Floer homology,
and can likewise be computed combinatorially using nice real Heegaard diagrams; see Theorem 8.33.
Similarly to the case of the 3-manifold invariants, [Li22, GM25], real sutured Floer homology splits
over relative real Spinc structures. See Section 2.3 for details.
Sutured Floer homology is well-behaved under a variety of natural operations on sutured manifolds.
In particular, there is a decomposition theorem describing the change in the sutured Floer homology
under surface decompositions [Juh08, Theorem 1.3]. There is an analogue of this result in the real
setting.

2. Preliminaries
In this section, we define real sutured manifolds, real sutured Heegaard diagrams, and relative
real Spinc structures.
2.1. Real sutured manifolds. Recall that a sutured manifold (Y, γ) is a compact oriented 3-
manifold Y with boundary together with a subset γ ⊂∂Y consisting of pairwise disjoint annuli A(γ)
and tori T(γ). In each component of A(γ), there is a suture, i.e., a homologically non-trivial oriented
simple closed curve. This collection of curves form a set denoted s(γ). We require that ∂Y \ int(γ)
decompose as a union R+(γ) ⊔R−(γ), in which R+(γ) (R−(γ)) consists of connected components of
∂Y \ int(γ) whose normal vector fields point out of (into) Y . These manifolds were introduced by
Gabai to study taut foliations in [Gab83]. As is standard when studying sutured manifolds using
Floer theoretic invariants, we assume that T(γ) = ∅henceforth.
Definition 2.1. A real sutured manifold is a triple (Y, γ, τ), where (Y, γ) is a sutured manifold and
τ is an orientation-preserving involution whose fixed locus is a (possibly empty) properly embedded
codimension-2 submanifold of Y . We require that τ|∂Y interchanges R±(γ) and that fix(τ|∂Y ) is
a finite set of points in s(γ). A real sutured manifold is balanced if the inclusion map induces a
surjection π0(s(γ)) →π0(∂Y ).

4. Real Surface Decompositions
In this section, we begin by defining a real analogue of Gabai’s notion of a decomposing surface,
in Section 4.1. We then show that in various simple cases this operation leaves the rank of real
sutured Floer homology unchanged in Section 4.2. We will return to more general cases in Section 9.
4.1. The sutured theory. We begin with the following topological definition.
Definition 4.1. Let (Y, γ, τ) be a real balanced sutured manifold. A properly embedded oriented
surface S in Y is called a real decomposing surface if the following conditions hold:
• S is fixed setwise by τ and has its orientation reversed by τ;
• every component λ of S ∩γ is either a properly embedded non-separating arc in γ or a simple
closed curve in an annulus A ⊂A(γ) which shares same homology class with A ∩s(γ).
Combining the two conditions in Definition 4.1 and Remark 2.6, components of S ∩γ can be
classified as follows: For each component λ of S ∩γ one of the following holds:
(1) λ is a properly embedded arc in γ which is fixed as a set by τ and λ ∩s(γ) consists of a fixed
point of τ;
(2) λ is a properly embedded arc in γ and there is another component λ′ in S ∩γ such that τ
interchanges λ and λ′;
(3) λ is a simple closed curve in a strongly invertible component of γ and λ passes through both
fixed points on that component of s(γ);
(4) λ is a homologically non-trivial simple closed curve in a connected component γ0 ⊂γ with
s(γ0) a strongly invertible knot, and there exists a component λ′ ⊂γ0 of S ∩γ so that τ
interchanges λ and λ′;
(5) λ is a homologically non-trivial simple closed curve in a connected component of γ that is not
fixed setwise by τ and λ′ = τ(λ) is another component of S ∩γ living in another component
of the suture.
As we do not consider real sutured manifolds with toroidal sutures in this paper, we do not give a
real analogue of case (3) in [Gab83, Definition 3.1].
Definition 4.2. Let S be a real decomposing surface in a real sutured manifold (Y, γ, τ). The real
sutured manifold obtained by a real decomposition along S is the real sutured manifold (Y ′, γ′, τ ′)
where Y ′ := Y \ ν(S) and τ ′ := τ|Y ′. Here, ν(S) is a τ-equivariant neighborhood of S.1 The suture
γ′ is specified by:
(1) γ′ = (Y ′ ∩γ) ∪ν(S+ ∩R−(γ)) ∪ν(S−∩R+(γ)).
(2) R+(γ′) = ((R+(γ) ∩Y ′) ∪S+) \ int(γ′).
(3) R−(γ′) = ((R−(γ) ∩Y ′) ∪S−) \ int(γ′).
Here, S+ and S−are the images of the positive and negative push-offs of S in ∂(ν(S)).

The 2-sphere has two

8.2.2. Tiling structures. In [SW10], Sarkar and Wang made repeated use of the following observation:
if u : F →Σ is an immersion, then the α- and β-curves in Σ can be pulled back to 1-manifolds
(which are also called α- and β-arcs). These arcs divide the source curve F into elementary regions,
which will be called a tiling of F. This structure is useful in their proof that all holomorphic curves
which contribute to the differential in a nice diagram are embedded. We will make use of the same
strategy, though the analysis is more involved, as there are many more (and more complicated)
domains that appear in real nice diagrams. For this reason, we formally define tiling structures, and
a few useful operations on tilings.
Definition 8.10. A tiling structure T = {αa, βa} on a source S is a pair of sets αa and βa each
consisting of pair-wise disjoint, properly embedded arcs in S such that each connected component of
S \ (αa ∪βa) is a bigon or a rectangle. A rectangular tiling is one without bigons.
We say T ′ = {αa′, βa′} is a refinement of T = {αa, βa}, if αa ⊂αa′ and βa ⊂βa′. We say that
a tiling structure is real if there is an involution on S which interchanges αa and βa. A tiling is
minimal if it is not a refinement of another tiling.
There are two operations that we will use to construct new tilings from old ones. Firstly, we can
push tilings forward under diffeomorphisms of S. In particular, if S has non-trivial fundamental
group and c ∈π1(S) is non-trivial, we can consider Dc ∈MCG(S, ∂S), the Dehn-twist along c.
Given a real tiling T = {αa, βa = τ(αa)} of S, we define
Dc(T) := {Dc(αa), Dτ(c)(βa) = τ(Dc(αa))}.

8.3.1. A summary of results. For disconnected domains, the source S is always a pair of disks S1 ⊔S2.
The tiling structure is unique up to refinement. Each Si must be embedded as can be seen from
[SW10, Theorem 3.1], but the image of S1 and S2 may intersect each other along rectangles.
For disk domains, the tiling structure is again unique up to refinement. The rectangles from
row (1) of Table 5 must be embedded, which also follows from [SW10, Theorem 3.1]. For bigons,
hexagons and octagons, immersions may occur in general. Fortunately, the regions of multiplicity
bigger than one must take a standard form.
For annular domains, the situation is more complicated.

Proof of Theorem 11.5. At each stage, first apply finitely many real product disk decompositions until
there are no real product disks. If for the resulting manifold (Yi, γi, τi), the homology H1(∂Yi; Z)−τ∗
is already zero, we are done. If not, we use Proposition 11.1 to find a real taut decomposing surface,
say S. Let (Yi+1, γi+1, τi+1) be the real sutured manifold obtained by decomposing (Yi, γi, τi) along
S. By Proposition 11.7, we can apply Proposition 11.6 to conclude that (Yi+1, γi+1) has strictly
lower complexity than (Yi, γi) as sutured manifolds (forgetting the real structure). The finiteness of
allowed complexity reductions implies that the hierarchy must conclude in finitely many steps.
□
The real sutured manifolds at the ends of the real sutured hierarchies as in Theorem 11.5 satisfy
strong topological restraints.
Proposition 11.8. Suppose (Y, γ, τ) is a real-taut real sutured manifold with H1(∂Y )−τ∗= 0. Then
(Y, γ, τ) is the disjoint union of rotating and interchanging balls, and real-irreducible manifolds with
toroidal boundary, on each connected component of which τ acts by rotation.
Proof. If two non-spherical components of ∂Y are interchanged by τ, then H1(∂Y ; Z)−τ∗̸= 0.
Moreover, the only orientation-preserving involutions, τ, of connected orientable surfaces S with
H1(S)−τ ∗= 0, are rotations of S2 and T 2 [Dug19, Proposition 5.7]. It follows that ∂Y consists of
spheres and rotating tori. Moreover, if a connected component Y ′ of Y has a spherical boundary
component, then the irreducibility assumption on Y implies that Y ′ is a 3-ball. The unique real
structure on a 3-ball that is fixed setwise by τ is rotation about an axis. There is a unique real
structure on a pair of 3-balls interchanged by τ. The remaining components of (Y, τ) have boundary
given by collections of rotating tori, as required.
□
It is not necessarily the case that real sutured manifolds as in the statement of Proposition 11.8 have
non-vanishing real sutured Floer homology. This occurs in the case of the real sutured manifolds at
the end of any real sutured hierarchy for the real sutured exterior of 83, for instance. See Section 10.5.
One could ask if one modified the definition of real sutured hierarchy to allow arc decompositions
in addition to surface decompositions, would it then be true that there always exists a sutured
hierarchy terminating in a real sutured manifold with non-vanishing real sutured Floer homology.
Some limited evidence for this is provided by Remark 5.5.
