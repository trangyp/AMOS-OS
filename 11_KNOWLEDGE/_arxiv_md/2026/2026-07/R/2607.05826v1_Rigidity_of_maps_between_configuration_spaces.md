RIGIDITY OF MAPS BETWEEN CONFIGURATION SPACES
RODRIGO DE POOL, PETER HUXFORD, DANIEL MINAHAN, AND JEROEN SCHILLEWAERT
Abstract. Let n ≥5 and m ≥3. Let Φ: Bn →Bm be a homomorphism of braid
groups. We prove that if the image of Φ is irreducible and not cyclic, then m = n and Φ
agrees with an automorphism modulo the center Z(Bm). This resolves in the affirmative
a conjecture of Chen, Kordek, and Margalit. It also provides a partial resolution to a
problem on the K3 problem list. As a consequence, we prove that every holomorphic
map UConfn(C) →UConfm(C) for n ≥5 and m ≥3 is affine equivalent to either a
constant map or the identity map. This resolves a conjecture of Farb for n ̸= 4.
1. Introduction
Let n ≥3 and let Sn be the symmetric group on n letters. Let
UConfn(C) := {(z1, . . . , zn) ∈Cn : zi ̸= zj when i ̸= j}

This is the configuration space of n unordered points in the complex plane C. Note
that UConfn(C) is a complex manifold. Let Aff ∼= C ⋊C∗be the group of affine trans-
formations of C. The group Aff acts on UConfm(C) in a natural way. Two holomorphic
maps UConfn(C) →UConfm(C) are affine equivalent if they become equal after post-
composing with the topological quotient map UConfm(C) →UConfm(C)/ Aff. We prove
the following rigidity theorem.
Theorem A. Let n ≥5 and m ≥3. Let Ψ : UConfn(C) →UConfm(C) be a holomorphic
map that is not affine equivalent to a constant map. Then m = n and Ψ is affine equivalent
to the identity.
Ferrari map. The lower bound on n is sharp, due to the existence of a map UConf4(C) →
UConf3(C) dating back to Ferrari in 1540. His method that solves for the roots of a quartic
polynomial begins with a construction that can be regarded as a holomorphic map from
the space of quartic polynomials to the space of cubic polynomials. Remarkably, if the
initial quartic polynomial has no repeated roots, i.e., is square-free, then the resulting cubic
polynomial will also be square free. Thus, Ferrari’s construction defines a holomorphic
map R: UConf4(C) →UConf3(C).
Farb conjectured [Far24, Conjecture 2.1] that for n ≥4 and m ≥3, any holomorphic map
UConfn(C) →UConfm(C) must, up to affine equivalence, be constant, the identity, or
n = 4 and the map factors through R. Theorem A resolves this conjecture for n ̸= 4.

Braid groups. A crucial tool in the study of UConfn(C) is the fundamental group
Bn = π1(UConfn(C)),
called the braid group on n strands. The group Bn is isomorphic to the mapping class
group Mod(Dn) of the n-times punctured disk Dn [BB05, Theorem 1.1]. We say that a
homomorphism Φ : Bn →Bm is irreducible if Φ(Bn) does not preserve any non-empty
multicurve in Dm. If Ψ : UConfn(C) →UConfm(C) is a holomorphic map that is not
affine equivalent to a constant map, then the pushforward Ψ∗: Bn →Bm is irreducible
as a consequence of the work of the first author and Souto [DS24, Theorem 1.2] which
extends work of Daskalopalous–Wentworth [DW07, Theorem 5.7].
Central equivalence. The analog of affine equivalence in the setting of braid groups is
the notion of central equivalence. Let G be a group and let Z(G) denote its center. If
Φ : Bn →G is a homomorphism, then let Φ: Bn →G/Z(G) denote the composition
of Φ with the quotient map G →G/Z(G). Two homomorphisms Φ1, Φ2 : Bn →G are
centrally equivalent if there is some ψ ∈Aut(G) for which Φ1 = ψ ◦Φ2.
The bulk of this paper is devoted to proving the following result.
Theorem B. Let n ≥5 and m ≥3. Let Φ: Bn →Bm be an irreducible homomorphism
with non-cyclic image. Then m = n and Φ is centrally equivalent to the identity.
Chen–Salter, alongside many other results about holomorphic maps between other config-
uration spaces, show that Theorem B implies Theorem A [CS26, Theorem 1.5]. Theorem B
was formulated as a question by Chen–Kordek–Margalit [CKM26, Question 1.5] and then
later as a conjecture by the same authors [Mar22, pg. 70]. Theorem B was also later
conjectured by Chen–Salter [CS26, Conjecture 1.4], and this conjecture was included in
the K3 problems list [BKR26, Problem 2.7, Remark (2)]. Problem 2.7 on the K3 problem
list asks to classify all homomorphisms Φ : Bn →Bm. Our Theorem B resolves Problem
2.7 for all n ≥5 and for Φ irreducible. The pushforward R∗: B4 →B3 of Ferrari’s map R
is irreducible and has non-cyclic image, so the bound n ≥5 is also sharp in Theorem B.

Holomorphic maps between moduli spaces. Theorems A and C are examples of results

Question D. Let g, r, h, s ≥0 with 2g + r ≥3 and 2h + s ≥3. What are the holomorphic
maps Mg,r →Mh,s?

[BS24, Corollary 1.4] classify isometric embeddings Mg,r →Mh,s when dim(Mg,r) ≥2.

egy similar to ours. They use group theoretic results, of Aramayona–Souto [AS12], and

3. Nielsen–Thurston classification
Our goal now is to describe the Nielsen–Thurston classification for braid groups and some
consequences of this classification for homomorphisms between braid groups.
Preliminaries on curves. Hereafter, a curve in Sb
g,n refers to an isotopy class of essential
simple closed curves unless otherwise specified. Recall that a curve is inessential if it
is homotopic to a boundary component or to a point (including a puncture). Otherwise
we say that the curve is essential. We will say that two curves γ and δ are distinct
if no representative of γ is isotopic to any representative of δ. An arc is a homotopy
class of embedded intervals in Sb
g,n where the endpoints of the interval map into punctures
or boundary components of Sb
g,n. Here we require homotopies to fix the punctures and
boundary components of Sb
g,n.
If γ, δ ⊂Sb
g,n are curves, we denote their geometric
intersection number by ι(γ, δ) ∈Z≥0, which is the minimum number of intersections
over all representatives of γ and δ. We say δ and γ are disjoint if they have disjoint
representatives, i.e. ι(γ, δ) = 0. Otherwise, we say δ and γ intersect. Note that a curve
γ is always disjoint from itself. If N is a nonempty set of curves, we say that a curve
γ is disjoint from N if γ is disjoint from every γ′ ∈N. Likewise, two sets of curves N
and N ′ are disjoint if each γ ∈N is disjoint from N ′. A multicurve M is a set of
pairwise disjoint curves. An Euler characteristic argument implies that multicurves are
always finite. For a more thorough discussion, see [FM12].
