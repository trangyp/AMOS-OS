REDUCTION MODULO p OF CRYSTALLINE GALOIS
REPRESENTATIONS VIA µp-EQUIVARIANCE
BHARGAV BHATT, TOBY GEE, AND MARK KISIN
Abstract. For a crystalline representation of Gal(Qp/Qp), with given Hodge–
Tate weights, we obtain new constraints on the inertial weights of its mod
p reduction. This allows us to formulate an explicit Serre weight conjecture,
in the generality of L-parameters for unramified connected reductive groups
over Qp, and to prove the elimination direction of this conjecture. The proof
uses prismatic techniques to show that the reductions modulo p of the Breuil–
Kisin modules attached to crystalline representations of Gal(Qp/Qp) acquire a
natural µp-equivariant structure. Combining this with results on the geometry
of the µp-fixed points of affine Grassmannians leads to our new constraint.

1. Introduction
The main theorem. Write GalQ for the absolute Galois group of Q, and let p be a
prime. The weight part of Serre’s conjecture [Ser87] predicts the weights of mod p
modular forms giving rise to a modular Galois representation
ρ : GalQ →GL2(Fp).
This has been generalized to reductive groups G other than GL2 and general number
fields F; see in particular Herzig’s thesis [Her09] and [GHS18] (the introduction to
which gives a brief overview of the history of this problem with further references
to the literature). For simplicity of exposition we mostly stick to the case F = Q
and G = GLn in this introduction; the results and proofs are formulated uniformly
for L-groups of unramified connected reductive groups G over Qp, as explained
below. This may then be applied to the weight part of Serre’s conjecture for any
such G, and F/Q unramified at all primes v|p.
The form of these conjectures is that the set of predicted weights depends only
on the restriction ρ|GalQp , or even on ρ|IQp , where IQp is the inertia subgroup at p.
This is a form of local-global compatibility for the Galois representations associated
to mod p automorphic forms. It is closely related to the reduction modulo p of the
corresponding statements in characteristic zero, and thus to the following question.

Question 1.1. Let r : GalQp →GLn(Fp) be a continuous representation. What are
the possible Hodge–Tate weights of crystalline representations ρ : GalQp →GLn(Zp)
such that the mod p reduction ρ of ρ satisfies ρ ≃r?
Turning the question around, we may reformulate this as asking for all the
representations r : GalQp →GLn(Fp) such that r arises as the reduction modulo p
of a crystalline representation ρ with given Hodge–Tate weights. The main result
of this paper is the following necessary condition on the restriction to IQp of the
semisimplification of r.

ρss|IQp ≃τ(λ, w).

The weight part of Serre’s conjecture. We now explain the connection with Serre
weights more precisely. In Section 6, under the usual hypotheses on an unramified
group G/Zp recalled there, the Serre weights are the irreducible Fp-representations
of G(Fp), parameterized as Fλ−η for suitable p-restricted highest weights λ and a
fixed twisting element η.
For a semisimple L-parameter ρ : GalQp →LG(Fp) we define two sets of Serre
weights.
The set W cris(ρ) consists of the weights Fλ−η for which ρ admits a
crystalline lift with Hodge–Tate cocharacter λ. The set W explicit(ρ) consists of the
weights Fλ−η for which there are λ′ ↑λ and w ∈W with
ρ|IQp ∼= τ(λ′, w).
Theorem 6.3.3 gives the inclusion
W cris(ρ) ⊆W explicit(ρ),
and therefore gives a weight elimination result whenever the expected inclusion
from the true Serre weight set W(ρ) into W cris(ρ) is known. We make the following
optimistic conjecture.
Conjecture 1.6 (Conjecture 6.3.4). Suppose that ρ is semisimple. Then
W(ρ) = W cris(ρ) = W explicit(ρ).
This gives a uniform candidate set of Serre weights, with no genericity assumptions,
and a corresponding weight elimination result, for general unramified reductive
groups.
We also show that our results are compatible with the existing literature on
the weight part of Serre’s conjecture. In particular, for generic ρ, we compare
our set W explicit(ρ) with the predicted set W Her(ρ) of Gee–Herzig–Savitt, which
generalizes Florian Herzig’s conjectural set for GLn. Proposition 6.4.6 proves that
W explicit(ρ) = W Her(ρ) under an explicit genericity hypothesis. Combined with
standard local-global compatibility, this improves existing weight elimination results
for automorphic forms on unitary groups; see Remark 6.4.10. Note also the key
structural theorem in the proof of the Buzzard–Diamond–Jarvis conjecture [GLS14]
is an immediate corollary of our results (see Corollary 5.2.11).

2. µp-fixed points in affine Grassmannians
Our main aim in this section is to prove some results about the µp-fixed lo-
cus for the loop rotation action on the affine Grassmannian. We first establish
Proposition 2.3.12, a combinatorial result which we then combine with results of
Riche–Williamson [RW22] to study the closure relations in this µp-fixed locus. We
have made an effort to give a self-contained treatment of this material, recalling
some standard results along the way.
2.1. Coxeter groups. We briefly recall some more or less well-known results about

consisting of pairs (F, ι) where F is an object of C and ι is a map of V -algebras
V ′ →EndF. Then we have the following.
Corollary A.3.3. Let X be a stack over V which has a flat cover by a formally
smooth, formally finite type formal V -scheme Y , and let (X, O) be the quasi-syntomic
site of X. Then there is a natural equivalence
G′-(Vect(X, O) ⊗V V ′) ≃G-Vect(X, O).
Proof. We will deduce this from Proposition A.3.1. Bearing in mind Corollary A.2.3,
we see that it suffices to show that the natural functor
(A.3.4)
G′-Vect(X, O′) →G′-(Vect(X, O) ⊗V V ′)
is an equivalence.
An object of Vect(X, O) ⊗V V ′ is a vector bundle M on X equipped with an
action of V ′. We first remark that, as an OX ⊗V V ′-module, M is locally projective.
Indeed, this can be checked on the cover Y of X. Since M is locally free over OY , it

locally has depth equal to dim Y. As OY ⊗V V ′ is a sheaf of regular rings which is
finite flat over OY , this implies that M is a projective OY ⊗V V ′-module.
Next, note that the inclusion
Vect(X, O′) ⊂Vect(X, O) ⊗V V ′
identifies Vect(X, O′) with the subcategory of locally projective OX ⊗V V ′-modules
M that are locally free. Local freeness is equivalent to asking that for any point
m of Y, the rank of M at the finitely many maximal ideals of the semi-local ring
(OY )m ⊗V V ′ is constant.
In order to see that (A.3.4) is an equivalence, it is therefore sufficient to note that
if ω is in G′-(Vect(X, O) ⊗V V ′), then for any W in RepV ′G′, ω(W) has constant
rank equal to rk V ′W. To see this, note that after pulling back to Y and then to
the residue field κ(n) of a maximal ideal n of (OY )m ⊗V V ′, we obtain an exact
tensor functor ωn : RepV ′G′ →Vectκ(n). We have ωn(∧iW) ∼= ∧iωn(W) for all i
