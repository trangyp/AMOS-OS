Room-temperature inversionless diamond nitrogen-vacancy electronic spin maser
Ali Fawaz1 and
Sarath Raman Nair1, ∗
1School of Mathematical and Physical Sciences, Macquarie University, North Ryde, NSW 2109, Australia
(Dated: July 9, 2026)
We propose a method to create a room-temperature maser operating at approximately 2.9 GHz
frequency using an ensemble of negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy electronic spins (NV) in dia-
mond, without requiring population inversion. Our method considers a DC magnetic field of a few
milli-Tesla (mT) applied along the perpendicular direction of an ensemble of NV spins aligned along
a common axis. This perpendicular magnetic-field creates superposition states of |ms = −1⟩and
|ms = +1⟩of the NV spin’s ground state triplet levels and thereby makes it possible to drive all
three transitions in the NV spin ground state. We model the system by including optical pump-
ing of the NV spins, near-resonant driving of two transitions, and coupling the third transition to a
near-resonant microwave resonator. Numerical estimates using experimentally realizable parameters
show that inversionless masing can be achieved inside the microwave resonator using our method.
As an application, we show that the output intensity of an inversionless maser (1.1×1014 spins) can
be used for magnetic field sensing with a DC sensitivity on the order of a hundred pT/
√
Hz. Our
study opens a new direction in room-temperature diamond NV maser devices for quantum techno-
logical applications without the requirement of a strong bias magnetic field, as in conventional NV
diamond masers.
Introduction —
The room-temperature continuous-
wave (CW) maser using an optically pumped negatively
charged nitrogen-vacancy electronic (NV) spin ensemble
in diamond [1–6] offers a platform for quantum technolog-
ical applications [7]. For example, a diamond NV maser
can be used as a magnetic-field sensor [1, 7].
On the
other hand, the conventional diamond NV maser [1–7]
requires a large magnetic field, typically provided by a
heavy and bulky magnet, which limits the practical ap-
plicability. The state-of-the-art diamond maser device in
terms of compactness and lightness is the one reported in
[6], to the best of our knowledge, which uses a microwave-
oven-sized electromagnet with a mass of approximately
30 kg to generate a 400 mT magnetic field for a maser
frequency of around 9.6 GHz. Though this magnetic field
requirement can be reduced if the maser frequency is
smaller, as the diamond NV maser frequency is tunable
via magnetic-field, for example, a 3 GHz maser requires
around 210 mT [1], the required field remains substan-
tial. In this work, we propose a method to create a room-
temperature inversionless maser [8, 9] in CW mode and
at a frequency around 2.9 GHz, using diamond NV spins.
We show that this maser can be used for magnetic field
sensing via changes in the output intensity induced by the
target magnetic field, similar to the room-temperature p-
terphenyl pentacene maser magnetic field sensor [10] or
the proposed diamond room-temperature NV laser mag-
netometer [11]. Inversionless masing has been theoret-
ically studied in a gain medium of diamond NV spins
strongly hyperfine coupled to nearby nuclear spins [12].
Here, we focus on inversionless masing using NV spins
alone, which is advantageous in the reproducibility of re-
sultant technological devices.

Numerical estimation —
We numerically model the
system to demonstrate the feasibility of the model. For
this, we keep only Λ, ωy, Ωy, ωz, and Ωz as free param-
eters within experimentally achievable ranges and fix all
the other parameters. We consider experimentally rel-
evant values for the fixed parameters g/2π = 18 mHz,
N = 1.1 × 1014, κx/2π = 130 kHz, Γ/2π = 330 kHz [32]
(Note that the temperature in [32] is 293 K; here we as-
sume 300 K for simplicity), and γl/2π = 200 Hz [33].
All the numerical values used for fixed parameters in our
system, including, intrinsic NV rates, are tabulated in
[20].
We consider Λ/2π between 0 to 1000 Hz. For a laser
spot-size of A = 0.09 cm2 and absorption cross-section
of σ = 7.8 × 10−17 cm2 as in reference [32], the optical
power P = Λ¯hνA/σ, where ν is the optical excitation
frequency, Λ/2π = 1000 Hz, corresponds to laser pow-
ers up to 0.4 W, which is experimentally achievable. For
both Ωy/2π and Ωz/2π, we consider the numerical values
between 0 to 10 MHz. This range for homogeneous classi-
cal MW driving of the NV spin ensemble can be achieved
with special MW antennas [34–38]. On the other hand,
Ωz/2π ∼1 MHz has been experimentally shown for an
ensemble of NV spins in reference [19].

Magnetic-field sensing —
The inversionless maser
can be used to detect a weak magnetic field along the
x-axis, through the change in the maser output inten-
sity. To find the optimal sensing point, we tune the Bx
field with an additional x-field denoted by Bs. Bs modi-
fies nx through ∆12 and ∆23 with ∆23 = ∆13 −∆12 and
the basis considered in this work remains the same for
γe(Bx + Bs) ≪D and Bs ≪Bx, where Bx = 5 mT. As-
suming that the maser output is detected in the same way

example, in [32], the sensitivity is estimated for the noise

S.IV.
EFFECTS OF MAGNETIC FIELD DETUNING UNDER STRONG DRIVING FIELD
To investigate the response under strong microwave driving, we numerically calculate the nx as a function of the
driving strength Ωy and magnetic field offset Bs. The results are shown in Fig. S.1 for fixed Ωz/2π = 500 kHz and
Λ/2π = 500 Hz. In the weak driving regime, where Ωy remains smaller than the intrinsic spin dephasing rate Γ, the
response is primarily governed by the spin transition frequency. Consequently, the gain exhibits a maximum close to
zero magnetic detuning, with the response gradually degrading as the spin transition is shifted away from resonance.
Although we expect a single resonance in this region, we observe two small peaks likely formed due to the presence of
the other drive Ωz. Near the region of maximum gain, the linewidth also narrows. As the driving strength is increased
beyond the spin dephasing rate (Ωy > Γ), the system enters the strong-driving regime, where the coherent driving
field dominates over dissipative processes. In this regime, the microwave field produces a splitting of the dressed-state
energy levels. This splitting becomes more pronounced with increasing Ωy, leading to two distinct resonant features
in the gain spectrum. The magnetic field detuning δB shifts the spin transition frequency relative to the dressed-state
resonances. As a result, the photon number response evolves from a nearly single resonance at weak driving to a
clearly resolved double-peaked structure at strong driving.
