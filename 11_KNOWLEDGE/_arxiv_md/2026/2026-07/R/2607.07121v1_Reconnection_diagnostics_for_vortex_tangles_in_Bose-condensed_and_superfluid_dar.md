ABSTRACT
Bose-Einstein-condensed (BEC) and superfluid dark-matter (SFDM) halos can contain coherent, wave-supported cores whose
angular momentum is carried by quantized vortices. When vortices form a tangle, reconnections convert part of the vortex kinetic
energy into dark phonons, density waves, Kelvin waves, and vortex loops, providing a microscopic channel by which SFDM
vortex structure can affect halo-core evolution. We estimate the dynamical importance of this channel by combining the local
Gross-Pitaevskii reconnection law with a halo-scale vortex-line density calibrated against Schrödinger-Poisson simulations. In
the minimal model the released energy stays in the dark sector, and standard-model luminosity requires additional portal physics.
Key words: methods: analytical — galaxies: haloes — galaxies: kinematics and dynamics — cosmology: theory — dark matter.
1 INTRODUCTION
Cold dark matter gives a successful description of the distribution of
matter on large scales (Planck Collaboration et al. 2016). The same
framework, however, leaves room for alternatives on galactic scales,
where the internal structure of halos is affected by baryonic feedback,
dark-sector microphysics, and the uncertain formation history of in-
dividual systems (Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin 2017; Del Popolo & Le
Delliou 2017). Ultralight bosonic dark matter is one of the simplest
ways of changing the small-scale behavior without modifying the
large-scale success of CDM (Hu et al. 2000; Goodman 2000; Silver-
man & Mallett 2002; Böhmer & Harko 2007a). For a boson of mass
𝑚∼10−22 −10−20 eV the de Broglie wavelength in a galactic halo is
of order a kiloparsec (Böhmer & Harko 2007b; Chavanis 2011; Hui
et al. 2017). The wave nature of the field then produces quantum pres-
sure, suppresses structure below a characteristic scale, and replaces
a collisionless cusp by a solitonic core in the simplest Schrödinger-
Poisson description (see Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin 2017; Hui 2021;
Ferreira 2021, for reviews).
A natural microphysical candidate for such an ultralight boson
is the axion, whose cosmological abundance from vacuum realign-
ment was worked out early on (Preskill et al. 1983). Whether ax-
ion dark matter forms a genuine Bose-Einstein condensate, built
up by gravitational or self-interaction-driven rethermalization into
a single coherent macroscopic wavefunction, or instead behaves as
a classical, coherently oscillating field without true condensation,
has been debated. Sikivie & Yang (2009) argued that gravitational
rethermalization drives cold axion dark matter into a condensate
before galaxy formation; this claim was examined critically (David-
son 2015; Davidson & Elmer 2013) and challenged on kinetic-rate
grounds (Guth et al. 2015). For the present calculation, the key re-
quirement is a coherent coarse-grained wavefunction that supports
phase singularities. Both a true condensate and a classical scalar
field obeying Gross-Pitaevskii-Poisson dynamics can provide that
description.
A related class of superfluid dark matter models has a different
motivation (Khoury 2015; Berezhiani & Khoury 2015, 2016; Khoury
2016). In these models the dark particles form a finite-density con-
densed phase inside galaxies (see Khoury 2022; Berezhiani et al.
2026, for reviews). The phonon excitation of this phase can couple
to baryons and produce forces that mimic modified Newtonian dy-
namics (MOND; see Famaey & McGaugh 2012, for a review), while
the same dark matter behaves as pressureless matter on cosmologi-
cal scales (Khoury 2015; Berezhiani & Khoury 2015, 2016; Khoury
2016). Both Bose-Einstein-condensed dark matter (BECDM) and
superfluid dark matter (SFDM) employ a coherent bosonic medium,
but they are not the same model. The fuzzy or BECDM picture relies
mainly on wave pressure and self-gravity. The SFDM program adds
an equation of state, a phonon-mediated force, and a baryon coupling
as part of the galactic phenomenology (Khoury et al. 2018; Hertzberg
et al. 2021; Lisanti et al. 2023; Favero & Bernardo 2025). In this paper
the local reconnection framework applies to any coherent condensate
with quantized vortices; the numerical halo calibration used below
comes from the non-interacting Schrödinger-Poisson branch.
Quantum fluids are irrotational (Landau 1941) and carry angu-
lar momentum through quantized vortices (Onsager 1949; Feynman
1955), arranged as an Abrikosov lattice in the simplest rotating equi-
librium (Abrikosov 1957; Yarmchuk et al. 1979; Fetter 2009). A
singly quantized vortex carries the circulation 𝜅= ℎ/𝑚, following
from the single-valuedness of the phase (see Donnelly 1993; Fetter
& Svidzinsky 2001; Fetter 2009, for reviews). Since halos acquire
angular momentum through tidal torques and mergers, a rotating con-
densed halo should contain quantized vortices (Silverman & Mallett
2002), either in the BECDM (Yu & Morgan 2002; Kain & Ling 2010;
Zinner 2011; Rindler-Daller & Shapiro 2012) or the SFDM scenario
(Mauland & Elgarøy 2022). Turbulent vortex tangles in BECDM
simulations (Mocz et al. 2017), and baryonic kinematic imprints of
vortex lines (Álvarez-Rios et al. 2025) have also been investigated.
Brax & Valageas (2025a,b) recently followed the formation of vortex
lines from stochastic, rotating initial conditions directly in two- and
three-dimensional simulations with quartic self-interactions, finding

Vortices in terrestrial classical fluids (Kida & Takaoka 1994;

2008; Paoletti et al. 2010; Fonda et al. 2019; Minowa et al. 2022;
Stasiak et al. 2025). This irreversible process (Villois et al. 2020) has
analytical descriptions in nonlinear Schrödinger or Gross-Pitaevskii
theory (Nazarenko & West 2003; Boué et al. 2013) and appears in
Gross-Pitaevskii simulations (Zuccher et al. 2012; Villois et al. 2017;
Galantucci et al. 2019). In the numerical simulations of Mocz et al.
(2017), virialized BECDM halos contain a turbulent vortex tangle
instead of a regular Abrikosov lattice. Once such a tangle exists, re-
connections are difficult to avoid (see Nemirovskii 2013; Barenghi
et al. 2014; Paoletti & Lathrop 2011; Tsubota 2013; Tsatsos et al.
2016; Skrbek et al. 2021; Madeira et al. 2020, for reviews on quantum
turbulence).
In this work, we ask whether such reconnections can become
dynamically and energetically relevant once a halo core actually
contains quantized vortices.
We track three quantities: the event-count parameter 𝜒≡𝑡dyn/𝑡rec,
that is, the number of core reconnection events during one dynam-
ical time; the dissipation time 𝑡diss of the vortex-energy reservoir;
and the dimensionless virial-impact parameter Πrec, which compares
the heat deposited over one dynamical time with a simple virial
energy density. These quantities classify reconnections as a small
correction, a secular internal-heating process, or a backreaction ef-
fect that can change the dark-sector state of the core. We emphasize
that the calculation here is conditional: A relaxed non-interacting
soliton without central vortices has no core reconnection heating.
On the other hand, a merger-perturbed, tidally stressed, externally
confined, or self-interacting core may instead contain a vortex pop-
ulation whose precise line density could be determined by future
simulations or by an angular-momentum model.
The plan of the paper is as follows. In Sec. 2 we construct the
semi-analytical estimate, state the reconnection law, import the halo
calibration from Mocz et al. (2017), estimate the rate and energy
scales, and discuss the release channels. In Sec. 3 we present the
parameter scan and the energetic diagnostics. In Sec. 4 we discuss
the limitations of using laboratory and numerical scalings in a halo
problem. In Sec. 5 we summarize the implications.
2 SEMI-ANALYTICAL HEATING MODEL
We consider a condensed dark-matter core that may be threaded
by quantized vortices. The model uses a local reconnection law,
a halo-scale line density, and dimensional analysis for the rate of
reconnections in a vortex tangle. We keep the uncertain line-density
normalization explicit, because the available Schrödinger-Poisson
calibration supplies a spectral scale rather than a direct count of
phase singularities.
2.1 Local reconnection physics
Vortex reconnections were conjectured by Feynman (1955) and
Schwarz (1985, 1988). They have been observed in superfluid helium
(Bewley et al. 2008) and atomic condensates (Serafini et al. 2015),
and were already present in early Gross-Pitaevskii simulations (Ko-
plik & Levine 1993).
In ideal classical fluids described by the Euler equations, Kelvin’s
circulation theorem and Helmholtz vortex freezing prevent recon-
nections of material vortex lines. Helicity conservation is closely
related to this topological constraint; Kelvin circulation and vor-
tex freezing provide the local obstruction to reconnection (Moffatt
1969). Viscous classical fluids governed by the Navier-Stokes equa-
tion can reconnect vortex lines and dissipate kinetic energy (Yao &
Hussain 2022). Because classical vorticity is a continuous field, re-
connection between antiparallel vortices can create smaller bridges
and threads that undergo further reconnections. Quantized vortices
are discrete phase singularities. Their reconnections evolve smoothly
in the Gross-Pitaevskii equation at fixed total energy, while part of
the kinetic energy is converted into sound near the reconnection point
(Zuccher et al. 2012). Bundles of quantized vortices may still form
bridge-like structures at a coarse-grained level. The relaxation of the
reconnecting cusps has been studied in detail using both the vortex
filament model (VFM, e.g. Schwarz 1985; Wacks et al. 2014) and
the GPE (Kursa et al. 2011).

2.5 Energy-release channels
We interpret 𝑄rec as energy transferred out of coherent vortex mo-
tion. In the minimal condensate description this energy remains in
the dark sector. Gross-Pitaevskii simulations show sound emission
from reconnections (Leadbeater et al. 2001; Proment & Krstulovic
2020), Kelvin-wave production (Minowa et al. 2025; Scollo et al.
2026), vortex-loop formation (Zuccher et al. 2012; Villois et al. 2017;
Andryushchenko & Kondaurova 2018), and irreversible transfer out
of coherent vortex degrees of freedom (Villois et al. 2020; Stasiak
et al. 2025). In a dark-matter halo the analogous channels are dark
phonons (Balatsky et al. 2022), density waves, Kelvin-wave cascades,
vortex loops, incoherent scalar excitations, and gravitational-cooling-
like scalar waves (Guzmán & Ureña-López 2006).

Forcing and regeneration: The dissipation time 𝑡diss treats the vor-

halo can also regenerate vorticity through tidal torques, mergers,
accretion, bar-like disturbances, or an inverse transfer from larger-

a Vinen-type balance between forcing and decay (Vinen 1957a,b,c;
Skrbek et al. 2021). In that language, Πrec ≳1 can mark a forced-
turbulence state in which injection, reconnection, Kelvin-wave cas-
cades, and phonon emission reach a new steady state. Direct simu-
lations should therefore measure both the reconnection sink and the
vortex-regeneration rate.
BECDM versus superfluid dark matter: The calibration of Mocz
et al. (2017) is a Schrödinger-Poisson result. Applying it to superfluid
dark matter assumes that vortex kinematics controls the reconnec-
tion rate more strongly than the detailed equation of state. This as-
sumption is not automatic. In non-self-interacting fuzzy dark matter,
Schobesberger et al. (2021) argue against vortices in relaxed soli-
tonic cores for typical halo spins. With repulsive self-interactions,
Brax and Valageas find that rotating cores instead favor an ordered
vortex lattice and solid-body rotation rather than a turbulent tangle
(Brax & Valageas 2025a,b). A clean lattice has few reconnections
unless shear, Kelvin waves, mergers, or boundary forcing generate
crossings and disorder. Equation (7) should then be replaced by a
lattice-disturbance model; applying the Vinen-type tangle rate to an
ordered lattice would overestimate the event rate. The tangle-scale
line density adopted here may therefore overestimate L, and hence
𝜒, 𝑡diss, and Πrec, for relaxed cores in either branch. This first es-
timate should be followed by simulations of the specific superfluid
model with its phonon sector and baryon coupling. In that case the
energy-transfer channels and the equilibrium core relation may both
differ.
Interpretation of large backreaction parameters: The contours in
Fig. 2 diagnose internal consistency of the passive-vortex picture.
Claims about flattened density profiles, rotation curves, photons,
neutrinos, or gravitational waves require additional dynamics or por-
tal physics. A large value of 𝜒would imply that reconnections are
frequent. A large value of Πrec means that, under the assumed 𝜂v,
reconnection energy over one dynamical time is comparable to the
simple virial energy density. It does not by itself predict disruption,
expansion, or mass loss. The stronger statement is that the assumed
vortex network cannot be evolved as a passive tracer. A natural next
step is to simulate rotating or merger-driven condensate halos in a
Gross-Pitaevskii-Poisson code and measure L, Γrec, Δ𝐸rec, the forc-
ing rate, and the change in the core energy budget directly. This is
beyond the scope of the present work.
Macroscopic vortex signatures: Although the microscopic vortex
core is small, a vortex can leave a macroscopic wave signature in
the surrounding quantum fluid (Galantucci et al. 2026). If the same
mechanism operates in a galactic condensate, a vortex line could
carry a correlated density or phase perturbation over scales larger
than the mean intervortex spacing. This would affect how one infers
L from a spectral peak, how much energy belongs to the vortex
sector, and how a reconnection pulse couples to halo-scale phonons.
In the present estimate, these effects are hidden inside 𝑑peak and 𝜖rec.
A simulation that tracks both phase defects and the surrounding wave
field is needed to separate the microscopic reconnection event from
its macroscopic halo response.
Transport in quantum turbulence: Tang et al. (2025) studied zero-
temperature turbulent diffusion and relative dispersion of quantized
vortices and superfluid parcels in ultraquantum and quasiclassical
regimes. Their result is relevant because mergers, tides, and coherent
rotation can produce different tangle geometries in a dark-matter halo.
Mergers may create an ultraquantum-like tangle, while tidal torques
or coherent rotation may polarize the vortices into quasiclassical
bundles. In the first case, vortex segments and fluid parcels can mix
in a similar way, so reconnection heating may be distributed through
the core. In the second case, the vortices can reconnect while the bulk
parcels follow a different dispersion law, so the heating and phase
mixing become anisotropic and history-dependent. The parameters
L and 𝐶rec compress this distinction into a single line-density scale
and a single rate coefficient. Future halo simulations should classify
the tangle as ultraquantum-like or quasiclassical-like and measure
two-body dispersion for both vortex lines and dark-fluid parcels.

connection heating is absent in the core rather than merely small. An
angular-momentum estimate for slowly rotating cores gives a compa-
rable or smaller vortex-line density along the same soliton sequence,
moving the soliton diagnostics farther from virial backreaction. Re-
connection may therefore be a secular ingredient only when mergers,
tides, external confinement, or self-interactions actually thread the
core with a disordered vortex population, with virial-energy impact
remaining small for the fiducial microphysics.
These masses overlap the ultralight-boson range already con-
strained by structure formation, soliton-core arguments, and Lyman-
𝛼data (Hu et al. 2000; Hui et al. 2017; Iršič et al. 2017; Ar-
mengaud et al. 2017; Kobayashi et al. 2017; Hui 2021; Ferreira
2021), and by a model-independent dwarf-galaxy kinematic bound
of 𝑚≳2.2 × 10−21 eV derived from Leo II (Zimmermann et al.
2025). Reconnection heating supplies an internal consistency test for
the passive-vortex picture and identifies when vortex energy must be
included in the halo-core energy budget.
The energy released by reconnections remains in the dark sec-
tor in the minimal model. Any visible photon or neutrino signal
requires additional portal physics, and gravitational-wave emission
from individual nonrelativistic reconnections is negligible. In the
specific phonon-mediated SFDM branch, the next calculation should
compare 𝑄rec with the phonon heat capacity and the local critical
temperature. A direct Gross-Pitaevskii-Poisson simulation should
measure phase singularities, L, Γrec, Δ𝐸rec, vortex regeneration, and
the evolution of 𝑢vort/𝑢vir in both fixed-radius perturbed cores and
equilibrium soliton cores. The central uncertainty is no longer hidden
in the fiducial numbers: it is the unknown 𝜂v.
