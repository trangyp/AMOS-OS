Reconstruction of Formal Schemes from Categories of Nuclear
Modules
Hisato Matsukawa∗
Abstract
We provide a partially functorial and constructive reconstruction procedure for formal schemes
from symmetric monoidal categories of nuclear modules.
More precisely, for a formal scheme X,
we show that the derived category of torsion modules Dtors(X) can be recovered as the maximal
strongly compactly generated localizing tensor ideal of Efimov’s category NucEf(X), and similarly for
the Clausen–Scholze category NucCS(X). Combining this with the Balmer spectrum, we reconstruct
X from the corresponding symmetric monoidal category of nuclear modules. Moreover, for formal
schemes topologically of finite type over a field or over Z, the contravariant functor X 7→NucEf(X) is
fully faithful; in the affine case, the analogous statement holds for X 7→NucCS(X).

Introduction
The aim of this paper is to establish a reconstruction result for formal schemes from Efimov’s and
Clausen–Scholze categories of nuclear modules.
For a formal scheme X, let NucEf(X) denote Efimov’s category of nuclear modules [Efi25], and let
NucCS(X) denote the Clausen–Scholze category of nuclear modules in the sense of [Sch26a; And23; AM24].
For an ∞-category C, we denote its homotopy category by Ho(C).
Theorem 1.1 (Corollary 5.2). Let X, Y be Noetherian formal schemes.
(1). If there is a symmetric monoidal triangulated equivalence Ho(NucEf(X)) ∼= Ho(NucEf(Y)), then
X ∼= Y.

Assumptions and Notation
We assume the existence of a Grothendieck universe.
All rings are assumed to be commutative and unital.
All formal schemes are assumed to be quasi-compact and quasi-separated and to admit a finitely
generated ideal of definition locally.
Unless otherwise specified, all triangulated categories are assumed to admit small coproducts and to
be well generated.
By an ∞-category, we mean an (∞, 1)-category. We regard ordinary categories and (2, 1)-categories
as ∞-categories in the usual way.
For a functor F, we denote its right adjoint, when it exists, by F R.

2.1
Tensor triangulated categories
In this section, we recall some basic facts on triangulated categories and tensor triangulated categories.
See [Nee01] and [Bal05] for details.
Let T, T ′ be triangulated categories. A functor F : T →T ′ is called continuous if it is exact and pre-
serves small coproducts. Such a functor has an exact right adjoint. If its right adjoint is also continuous,
then F is called strongly continuous. Strongly continuous functors preserve κ-compact objects for every
regular cardinal κ. The full subcategory of κ-compact objects is denoted by T κ.
Let I ⊂T be a thick subcategory. It is called a localizing subcategory if it is closed under small
coproducts. A localizing subcategory I is well-generated as a triangulated category if and only if it is
generated by a set of objects of T. In this case, the Verdier quotient LI : T →T/I is continuous and

functor ιI is strongly continuous if and only if LI is strongly continuous. In this case, Iκ = T κ ∩I.
The localizing subcategory generated by a subset S ⊂T is denoted by ⟨S⟩. If each s ∈S is compact
in T, then ⟨S⟩is called compactly generated. If, moreover, S is finite, then ⟨S⟩is called finitely compactly

A tensor triangulated category, or tt-category for short, is a triangulated category T equipped with
a symmetric monoidal structure −⊗−: T × T →T which is exact in each variable and preserves small
coproducts in each variable. The unit object is denoted by 1T . A homomorphism between tt-categories
is a symmetric monoidal continuous functor.
A localizing subcategory I of a tt-category T is called a localizing ideal if, for all a ∈T and b ∈I,
one has a ⊗b ∈I. For a subset S ⊂T, the localizing ideal generated by S is denoted by ⟨S⟩⊗. If each
s ∈S is compact, then ⟨S⟩⊗is called (weakly) compactly generated. If, moreover, S is finite, then ⟨S⟩⊗
is called (weakly) finitely compactly generated. If I is a well-generated localizing ideal, then the Verdier
quotient T/I inherits a natural tt-structure such that LI : T →T/I is symmetric monoidal. A localizing
ideal I is called a smashing ideal if the inclusion ιI : I →T is T-linear, that is, if ιI(ιR
I (a) ⊗b) ∼= a ⊗ιI(b)
for all a ∈T and b ∈I. For a smashing ideal I, the inclusion ιI is always strongly continuous. Moreover,
I⊥is a localizing ideal and ιR

a natural tt-structure such that ιR

A left idempotent in a tt-category T is a morphism γ : e →1T such that γ ⊗e : e ⊗e →e is an
isomorphism. Dually, one defines a right idempotent. For a left idempotent e →1T , the essential image
of e ⊗−: T →T is a smashing ideal, and the cone of e →1T gives a right idempotent. Conversely, for a
smashing ideal I, the counit ιIιR
I (1T ) →1T is a left idempotent. These correspondences give bijections
between smashing ideals, left idempotents, and right idempotents; see [BF11].

Lemma 2.4. Let T be a tt-category such that T ω ⊗T ω ⊂T ω, 1T ∈T ω, and T ω is rigid, that is, every

2.2.1
Derived category of torsion modules
Let R be a commutative ring, and let I ⊂R be an ideal. An R-module M is called an I∞-torsion
module if, for every m ∈M, there exists n ≥1 such that Inm = 0.
Let D(R) be the derived ∞-
category of R-modules. A complex F ∈D(R) is called I∞-torsion if its cohomologies are I∞-torsion.
Let DI-tors(R) be the full subcategory of D(R) spanned by the I∞-torsion objects. If I = (a1, . . . , an) is
finitely generated, then DI-tors(R) is a smashing ideal of D(R), and it is generated by the single compact
object Kos(R; a1, . . . , an). The quotient D(R)/DI-tors(R) is equivalent to D(SpecR \ V (I)), where V (I)
is the closed subset of SpecR corresponding to I. We denote the classical I-adic completion of R by R∧
I .
For an affine formal scheme U = Spf(R∧
I ), we set Dtors(U) = DI-tors(R). This construction does
not depend on the choice of R and satisfies Zariski descent. For a general formal scheme X, we define
Dtors(X) by Zariski descent. Then Dtors(X) is finitely compactly generated as a localizing subcategory
of itself. For an open subset U ⊂X, the restriction functor Dtors(X) →Dtors(U) is a quotient functor
whose kernel is a compactly generated smashing ideal.
2.2.2
Category of nuclear modules of a tt-∞-category
Let PrL
st be the ∞-category of presentable stable ∞-categories and continuous, that is, colimit-preserving,
functors. A commutative algebra object in PrL
st is called a tt-∞-category. A homomorphism between
tt-∞-categories is a morphism in CAlg(PrL
st), that is, a symmetric monoidal continuous functor. The
symmetric monoidal structure on a tt-∞-category is automatically closed. We denote the right adjoint
of −⊗x by RHom(x, −).
Let PrL
st,ω be the ∞-category of compactly generated presentable stable ∞-categories and continuous
functors which preserve compact objects. A commutative algebra object in PrL
st,ω is called a compactly
generated tt-∞-category. A homomorphism between compactly generated tt-∞-categories is a morphism
in CAlg(PrL
st,ω), that is, a symmetric monoidal continuous functor which preserves compact objects.
Definition 2.5 ([CS26, Definitions 8.5]). Let C be a compactly generated tt-∞-category. An object
x ∈C is called nuclear if, for every compact object c ∈C, the natural map RHom(c, 1C)⊗x →RHom(c, x)
is an isomorphism. The full subcategory of nuclear objects in C is denoted by Nuc(C).
This subcategory Nuc(C) is closed under tensor products and colimits, and is a presentable stable ∞-
category. Every homomorphism between compactly generated tt-∞-categories preserves nuclear objects.
See [CS26, Theorem 8.6] and [MW24, Theorem 2.4].

Balmer spectrum of a weakly compactly generated tensor tri-
angulated category
In this section, we introduce Balmer’s tensor triangular spectrum of a tt-category, as defined in [Bal05].
We will apply this construction to the derived category of torsion modules on a formal scheme in Theorem
4.10. However, the unit object of this category is not compact, so we need a slight modification. Roughly
speaking, our spectrum gives a spectrum of localizing ideals of a compactly generated tt-category.
