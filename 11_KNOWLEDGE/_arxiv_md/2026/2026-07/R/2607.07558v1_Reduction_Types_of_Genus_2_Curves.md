Abstract. Tate produced a table for elliptic curves over local fields that beautifully summarises their
arithmetic invariants in terms of the Kodaira type. We present an analogous set of tables for curves of
genus 2. The invariants addressed include: reduction type of the minimal regular model, reduction type
of the minimal regular model with normal crossings, N´eron component group of the Jacobian, conductor
exponent, valuation of the discriminant of a minimal Weierstrass model, and cluster pictures.

1. Introduction
The aim of the present paper is to classify the main arithmetic invariants of genus 2 curves over local
fields in terms of their reduction type. Our presentation is modelled on Tate’s classical table for elliptic
curves in [34], which was reproduced by Silverman in his books [30, 31]. The Kodaira symbols used in
the elliptic curves classification are replaced by 106 families of “reduction types”, which are essentially
the labels introduced by Namikawa and Ueno [22]. We have split these in seven tables — one for each
(expected) potential stable type, which are the genus 2 analogues of “potentially good” and “potentially
multiplicative” reduction.
As for elliptic curves, a key feature is that this classification is essentially independent of the underlying
local field or DVR, except for some cases of residue characteristic ≤5. Reduction types can be thought
of as being purely combinatorial objects. The associated curve invariants are given in terms of the types
and the parameters of the families (analogously to how an elliptic curve with Kodaira type In has N´eron
component group Z/nZ for every n ≥1), rather than through an algorithm that relies on the equation
of the curve.

K and an algebraically closed residue field k of characteristic p ≥0. Throughout this work, C/K denotes

Beyond the sheer number of different reduction types, genus 2 curves present some new features and
challenges when compared to elliptic curves. We would like to highlight and briefly discuss three of these:
(i) what we mean by a reduction type, (ii) our approach to computing N´eron component groups, and (iii)
cluster pictures, which serve as a replacement for the valuations of the j-invariant and discriminant.
1.1. Reduction types. Formally, by the reduction type of a regular model C/R we mean the following
data (see Definition 2.1):
• The list of components of the special fibre Ck with their multiplicity and geometric genus;
• The intersection pairing on components; and
• The list of singular points of Ck, whether they are normal crossings singularities, and the number
of times that each component passes through each singular point.
The MRM (respectively, MRNC) reduction type is the reduction type of the minimal regular model of C
(respectively, minimal regular model with normal crossings).
For semistable and MRNC models, this data is equivalent to the dual graph of the special fibre (with
genera and multiplicities). Our definition of the MRM reduction type is fairly crude for general curves,
as one could keep better track of the types of singularities that appear. However, it recovers the Kodaira
types for elliptic curves and the description of special fibres given by the Namikawa–Ueno classification
for genus 2 curves (Lemma 2.5).
As there appears to be a shift from using MRM to MRNC reduction types (e.g. [7, 11]) we have
decided to include both in our tables. The special fibre of the minimal regular model and of the minimal
regular model with normal crossings do not in general agree. However, there is a 1-to-1 correspondence
between them.
Theorem 1.2 (see Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.7). The MRNC reduction type of a curve determines
its MRM reduction type. For curves of genus 2 this is a 1-to-1 correspondence.
Schrettner [28] has recently proposed a refinement for the definition of MRM reduction type, and
showed that these are in 1-to-1 correspondence with MRNC reduction types in general.

1.2. N´eron component groups. The N´eron component group of the Jacobian of a curve can be expli-
citly computed from the intersection pairing matrix on the special fibre of a regular model of the curve
due to Raynaud’s work [3, Thm. 1.1] and is therefore a natural invariant of the reduction type. Unfortu-
nately, manipulating these types of matrices is troublesome for whole families of reduction types, as the
dimension of the matrix grows with the underlying parameters, for example the Kodaira type In results
in an n × n matrix. We propose the following alternative approach, which keeps to 2g × 2g matrices,
where g is the genus.
Theorem 1.3 (see Theorem 4.4). Let C/K be a curve, and suppose that C′/C[t] is a curve with the
same MRM reduction type at t=0 as C/K. Then the N´eron component group of C/K is isomorphic to
the torsion subgroup of the co-invariants of the endomorphism of H1(C′, Z) induced by the monodromy
operator at t=0.
As the N´eron component group only depends on the intersection pairing matrix, the auxiliary curve C′
in fact only needs to have the same intersection pairing rather than MRM reduction type. Such a curve
always exists by a result of Winters [37]. However, for our classification in genus 2 we need to work
with families of reduction types, rather than individual ones. The corresponding monodromy matrices,
in terms of the family parameters, were already computed by Namikawa and Ueno in [22], and turn out
to be suitable for our purposes.
1.3. Cluster pictures as an analogue of v(j) and v(∆). Classically, Kodaira types of elliptic curves
can be obtained from the valuation of the discriminant and j-invariant of the curve when p ̸= 2, 3 (see
[34, p.46]). Unfortunately, there is no direct analogue of such invariants in the genus 2 case [6, Thm. 1.2].
As all genus 2 curves are hyperelliptic, following [9] we instead use cluster pictures, which requires us
to restrict to residue characteristic p ̸= 2.
For a hyperelliptic curve y2 = f(x) its cluster picture is simply the data consisting of the matrix of
v-adic distances between the roots of f(x) in Ksep and the valuation of its leading coefficient (note that
including the valuation of the leading coefficient in the data of a cluster picture strays from the usual
convention in [2, 9, 11]). In the context of tame curves and p ̸= 2 cluster pictures are arguably better
than MRM types: the cluster picture recovers both the MRM reduction type and the ℓ-adic Galois
representation (Theorem 3.2 and [9, Thm. 1.20]).
Moreover, just like the valuation of the j-invariant and the discriminant for elliptic curves, cluster
pictures change in a very simple and predictable manner in field extensions. In particular, we get for free
the following consequence of Theorem 1.1. For an explicit illustration, see Example 1.9.
Corollary 1.4. Suppose p ̸= 2, and C/K is a tame curve of genus 2. Then the MRM reduction type
of C over a finite extension L/K is determined by its MRM reduction type over K, its potential stable
type, and the degree of L/K.
The obvious weakness of cluster pictures is that they are model dependent, although this is, of course,
also true of the valuation of the discriminant in the elliptic curve case. In the genus 2 case, we introduce
a purely combinatorial equivalence relation on cluster pictures, with the property that a curve admits a
K-model with a specific cluster picture if and only if it admits K-models for all the cluster pictures in the
equivalence class. It turns out that there are 106 (families of) equivalence classes, and they correspond

1.5. Outline of the paper. In §2, we formally define the reduction type of a model of a curve and we
describe how the MRNC reduction type determines the MRM reduction type (Theorem 1.2), and vice
versa in the genus 2 case. This covers the proof of part (1) of Theorem 1.1.
In §3, we prove part (8) of Theorem 1.1, that our tables give the complete list of cluster pictures that
gives rise to each reduction type in the tame case. We also detail the explicit K-isomorphisms to go
between models of curves with different cluster pictures (Proposition 3.14), and show that, for a curve
with a given reduction type, each cluster picture is realised by some Weierstrass equation of the curve
(Theorem 3.3).
In §4, we prove Theorem 1.3, which relates the N´eron component group of the Jacobian of a general
curve C/K to the homology of a curve C′ defined over C[t] with the same reduction type as C/K. We
use this to calculate the N´eron component groups associated to the reduction type families in our tables,
giving part (2) of Theorem 1.1.
In §5 we review results from literature used to determine the tame part of the conductor exponent,
BSD fudge factor v(ωmin/ω0) and the valuation of the minimal discriminant. This proves parts (3), (6)
and (7) of Theorem 1.1.

configuration of discs containing the roots, and the tame inertia action; they do not use finiteness of
the residue field or Frobenius. Accordingly, the relevant cluster picture arguments carry over unchanged
to our setting. Any statements we reference that involve Frobenius are used only after forgetting the
Frobenius action.

2. Reduction types
In this section we formalise what we mean by MRM and MRNC reduction types, show that they are in
1-to-1 correspondence for genus 2 curves, and that they are essentially equivalent to the Namikawa–Ueno
labels.
Kodaira and N´eron developed a classification of reduction types of elliptic curves over DVRs [13, 23].
For genus 2 curves, the standard classification has become that of Namikawa and Ueno [22]. There are a
few subtleties, however, as they worked over C[t] and the precise objects that they classified rely on this.
We take a slightly different approach: for us the reduction type should be described purely combinatorially
in terms of the special fibre and apply over any DVR. In view of the general usage of the Namikawa–Ueno
classification, we have chosen a definition that as closely as possible recovers it (Lemma 2.5).
Definition 2.1. For a curve C/K with a regular model C/R, by the reduction type of C we mean the
following data:
• A set X that enumerates the components of the special fibre Ck, and functions that record their
multiplicities m : X →Z≥1 and geometric genera g : X →Z≥0;
• The intersection pairing X × X →Z;
• A set S that enumerates the singular points of Ck, together with a function that records the
number of times each component passes through this point f : S →Z[X], and another that
records whether the singularities are normal crossings.
The MRNC reduction type of C/K is the reduction type of the minimal MRNC model of C/R. The
MRM reduction type is the reduction type of the minimal regular model.
Remark 2.2. The MRNC reduction type of a curve determines its N´eron component group (Raynaud
[25]), the tame part of its conductor exponent (Lemma 5.1) and whether the curve has tame reduction
(Saito [27, Thm. 3.11], [15, Thm. 10.4.47]), i.e. the invariants in part (2)–(4) of Theorem 1.1.

3. Cluster pictures
For a hyperelliptic curve C : y2 = f(x), the cluster picture, introduced by Dokchitser, Dokchitser,
Maistret and Morgan in [9], records the v-adic configuration of the differences of roots of f(x). Cluster
pictures were used in [9] to compute the MRM reduction type of semistable hyperelliptic curves and
many other local invariants. Faraggi and Nowell later proved that the cluster picture also determines the
MRNC reduction type for hyperelliptic curves with tame reduction [11]. We note that, although some of
the cluster picture results cited in this section are stated for local fields, the results from [2, 9, 11] that
we use apply in our setting by Remark 1.13.
Throughout this section we assume that the residue characteristic p of K satisfies p ̸= 2.
Notation 3.1. Recall from §1.4 that the cluster picture associated to a Weierstrass equation C : y2 = f(x)

5.1. Conductor exponent. Let C be a curve over K. Recall that the conductor exponent nC is divided
