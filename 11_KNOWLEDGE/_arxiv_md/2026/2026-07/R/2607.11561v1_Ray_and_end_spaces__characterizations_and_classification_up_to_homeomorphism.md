RAY AND END SPACES: CHARACTERIZATIONS AND
CLASSIFICATION UP TO HOMEOMORPHISM
MATHEUS DUZI, GABRIEL FERNANDES ∗, AND PAULO MAGALHÃES JÚNIOR
Abstract. We provide a combinatorial characterization for pairs of order-theoretic
trees with homeomorphic ray spaces, answering an open problem proposed by
Kurkofka and Pitz. This solution is inspired by the introduction of a transfinite
topological game, which allows us to characterize not only ray spaces through the
existence of winning strategies for one of the players, but also their homeomorphic
classes. As applications of these results, we obtain a new topological character-
ization for graph-theoretic end spaces (thus obtaining yet another solution to a
recently solved problem of Diestel), as well as for edge-end spaces and completely
ultrametrizable spaces. We also introduce a generalization of the class of ray spaces
(which is strict, as witnessed by the Sorgenfrey line). Furthermore, we establish
that, for subspaces with cardinality less than continuum of end spaces, the scat-
tered property is equivalent to the property of being, itself, an end space. At last,
we determine that ray spaces in a couple of classes fail to have their product with
any non-discrete space as a ray space.
Keywords: ray space, tree order, transfinite game, end space, infinite graph
1. Introduction
The so called ends of graphs, as classes of rays which cannot be separated by
finitely many vertices, have been first introduced in [10] and have since been used to
study important problems in infinite combinatorics, as it can be seen in, e.g.:
• The network flow theory is extended in [1] to infinite graphs by using the
concept of ends to understand and control flows that “escape to infinity”;
• It is shown in [17] that ends are essential for extending the Nash-Williams
tree-packing theorem to locally finite graphs;
• Ends are demonstrated in [5] to be a necessary component for the Faithful
Cycle Cover Conjecture to hold in locally finite graphs.

We are further able to obtain a handful of applications from this equivalence, the
main of which we briefly elaborate in this section.
For starters, let us say that a space X is an end space if it is homeomorphic to
the end space of some graph G. Recall that Diestel asked in Problem 5.1 of [7]
for topological properties over a space X which could characterize X being an end
space. An answer for this question came in [14] (Theorem 3.6) with the existence of
a subbase satisfying, again, a certain list of properties. We can also find an alternate
answer in [2] (Theorem 2.1), where one of the properties of the first characterization
is replaced by the existence of a winning strategy for one of the players in a certain
topological game over the space X. With our Theorem 3.15, on the other hand, we
are able to provide a completely different answer to Diestel’s problem:
Theorem 3.18. A topological space X is an end space if, and only if, there is a
winning strategy σ for Alice in RayGame(X) such that succ(dom(σ)) is a special
tree.
As completely ultrametrizable spaces comprise another class of spaces which are
homeomorphic to specific kinds of ray spaces, we also obtain:
Theorem 3.23. A topological space X is completely ultrametrizable if, and only if,
there is some strategy σ for Alice in RayGame(X) which always wins at the ωth
inning.
Theorem 3.16 further allows us to generalize the concept of ray spaces in Section 4:
let us say that a topological space (X, τ) is a quasi-ray space if there is some coarser
topology τ ′ ⊆τ such that (X, τ ′) is a ray space. Then, by defining a transfinite
topological game QuasiRayGame(X) which is “easier” for Alice in comparison to
RayGame(X), we show that
Theorem 4.8. For a non-empty topological space X, the following are equivalent:
(a) X is a quasi-ray space.
(b) Alice has a winning strategy in QuasiRayGame(X).
We are thus able to determine that a few spaces, such as the Sorgenfrey line,
2ω1 or subspaces of ordinals, are quasi ray-spaces, despite not satisfying the strict
requirements to be ray spaces.

in [2] that these results can be generalized to Gδ subspaces, where this problem was
recently formalized in Question 4.6. The applicability of our characterizations is then
