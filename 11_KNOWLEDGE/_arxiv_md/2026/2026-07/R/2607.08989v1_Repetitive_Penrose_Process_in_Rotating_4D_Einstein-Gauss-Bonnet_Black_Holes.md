Abstract
We investigate the repetitive Penrose process for neutral particles in a rotating four-dimensional
Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet black hole obtained through the modiﬁed Newman–Janis algorithm, devel-
oping a nonlinear iterative scheme in which the mass, angular momentum, and irreducible mass are
updated after each extraction event. Imposing the triple turning-point condition, we obtain a closed-
form solution of the conservation equations for energy, angular momentum, and radial momentum
that reduces to the Kerr result in the limit of vanishing coupling. The distinctive feature of this back-
ground is that, although the Gauss–Bonnet coupling α is a ﬁxed constant of the action and is not
carried by the infalling fragments, the dimensionless coupling ˆα = α/M 2 grows at every iteration as
the mass decreases, so that the eﬀective Gauss–Bonnet correction is self-ampliﬁed along the sequence.
We ﬁnd that a larger coupling lowers the extremal spin, contracts the ergosphere, and reduces the
number of admissible decays, forbidding the process near the horizon at strong coupling while permit-
ting it at larger decay radii; the termination is controlled throughout by the incident particle. The
energy return on investment decreases monotonically with ˆα and the growth of the irreducible mass
is suppressed relative to Kerr, whereas the energy utilization eﬃciency is non-monotonic: below a
critical coupling the parameter space exhibits a four-region structure with a bounded window in which
the EGB black hole is more eﬃcient than Kerr; this four-region structure collapses into a three-region
one above a critical coupling. This coupling-driven reorganization of the eﬃciency landscape has no
analogue in the Kerr, Reissner–Nordström, Kerr–de Sitter, accelerating Kerr, or Kerr–Newman cases.

Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet black hole; repetitive Penrose process; Gauss–Bonnet coupling;
energy utilization eﬃciency; irreducible mass

Introduction
The possibility of extracting energy from a rotating black hole was established by Penrose, who showed
that a particle entering the ergosphere and decaying there can send a negative-energy fragment across
the horizon while a second fragment escapes to inﬁnity with more energy than the original [1, 2]. The
kinematics of the mechanism, the bound on the escaping energy, and the constraints on the decay were
subsequently clariﬁed by Bardeen, Press, and Teukolsky and by Wald [3, 4], and Denardo and Ruﬃni ex-
tended the idea to charged particles in the Reissner–Nordström geometry, where an analogous generalized
ergosphere supports negative-energy states [5, 6].
The energetics of these processes are governed by the irreducible mass. Christodoulou and Ruﬃni
showed that a black hole possesses a mass-energy formula in which Mirr sets the part of the mass that
cannot be extracted by classical means, and that transformations are reversible when Mirr is held ﬁxed
and irreversible when it grows [9, 10]. The connection between the irreducible mass and the horizon
area, A = 16πM 2
irr, together with Hawking’s area theorem [11] and the identiﬁcation of the area with
entropy [12], ﬁxes the amount of energy that can be liberated and the thermodynamic cost of doing so.
For a long time the Penrose process was treated as a single event, and attempts to repeat it were
formulated linearly, with the black-hole parameters held ﬁxed between decays. Ruﬃni and collaborators
recently pointed out that such a treatment violates energy conservation, and that a consistent repetitive
process must update the mass and angular momentum after every decay [7, 8]. Once this is done the
sequence becomes strongly nonlinear: most of the lost rotational energy is converted not into escaping
radiation but into irreducible mass, the spin approaches a positive lower bound, and the process terminates
after a ﬁnite number of steps with only a small fraction of the mass extracted.
This framework has since been extended to a variety of backgrounds. Hu, Cai, and Wang studied
the repetitive electric Penrose process in Reissner–Nordström spacetime and found that the black-hole
charge cannot be driven exactly to zero through a ﬁnite sequence of decays, a behavior they interpreted
as a thermodynamic third-law analogue [13]. In Kerr–de Sitter black holes, Wang and Zeng showed that
a larger cosmological parameter enhances both the single-extraction capability and the energy return on
investment [14], while Zeng and Wang found that, for accelerating Kerr black holes, the energy utilization
eﬃciency can exceed the 50% bound obtained in the Kerr case when the decay occurs suﬃciently close to
the horizon [15]. The process has also been generalized to charged particles in an initially extremal Kerr–
Newman black hole, where two independent electromagnetic couplings separately control the incident
particle’s access to the ergoregion and the depth of the negative-energy states available to the captured
fragment [16]. An attractive black-hole–fragment coupling enhances both the extraction eﬃciency and
the energy return on investment and, unlike in the Reissner–Nordström case, allows the horizon charge
to pass through zero and reverse sign without violating the area theorem or cosmic censorship. Also, the
process has been extended to the Konoplya–Zhidenko rotating non-Kerr metric, where a purely geometric
deformation parameter—unrelated to any physical hair—was likewise shown to raise both the energy
return on investment and the energy utilization eﬃciency relative to the Kerr case, particularly at larger
decay radii [17]. In each of these extensions, the additional spacetime parameter—charge, cosmological
constant, acceleration, or metric deformation—tends to make the repetitive process either more eﬃcient
or easier to sustain than in the Kerr case.
Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet gravity provides a natural setting in which to test how a purely gravitational
modiﬁcation of the background aﬀects this picture. The theory supplements General Relativity with
the quadratic Gauss–Bonnet invariant, which is topological in four dimensions but, through a consistent
regularization, contributes to the ﬁeld equations and leaves an imprint on black-hole solutions controlled
by a coupling α, with General Relativity recovered as α →0. A rotating solution can be generated
from the static one by the modiﬁed Newman–Janis algorithm [18], giving a stationary, axisymmetric,
asymptotically Kerr-like geometry whose horizon and ergosphere depend on α.
Because the Penrose
process is sensitive precisely to the size of the ergoregion and to the location of the horizon, this spacetime
is a suitable arena for studying how quadratic curvature corrections modify repetitive energy extraction.
In this work we develop the repetitive Penrose process for neutral particles in the rotating four-
dimensional Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet black hole. Imposing the triple turning-point condition, we obtain
a closed-form solution of the conservation laws and build a nonlinear iterative scheme that updates the
mass, angular momentum, and irreducible mass after every decay. A feature speciﬁc to this background
is that, although α is a ﬁxed constant of the action and is not carried by the infalling fragments, the
dimensionless coupling ˆα = α/M 2 grows as the mass decreases, so that the eﬀective strength of the
correction changes along the sequence and the geometry must be recomputed at each step. We show that
this running reduces the extractable reservoir and the number of admissible decays, that the termination
is set throughout by the incident particle, and that the utilization eﬃciency reorganizes into a four-region
structure below a critical coupling and a three-region structure above it, with a bounded window in which
the Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet black hole outperforms Kerr.

Conclusion
We have studied the repetitive Penrose process for neutral particles in a rotating four-dimensional
Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet black hole, treating every decay self-consistently through the triple turning-point
condition and updating the mass, angular momentum, and irreducible mass at each step. The analytic
solution of Sec. 3 reduces to the Kerr result of Ref. [8] as ˆα →0, which served both as a consistency
check and as the baseline for all comparisons.
What sets this case apart from the earlier extensions of the process is the status of the coupling. In the
charged Kerr–Newman geometry [16] the electromagnetic interaction enters through a quantity carried by
the fragments, while in the Kerr–de Sitter [14] and accelerating Kerr [15] cases the additional parameter
is an external constant ﬁxed once and for all. The Gauss–Bonnet coupling α belongs to neither class: it
is a constant of the action, yet the quantity that actually controls the geometry, ˆα = α/M 2, increases
at every iteration because the mass decreases. The correction is thus self-amplifying, and the eﬀective
potentials, the horizon, and the ergosphere must be recomputed at each step with a progressively larger
ˆα.
This running has direct numerical consequences. A larger initial coupling lowers the extremal spin and
contracts the ergosphere, so the extractable reservoir shrinks and the sequence stops after fewer decays. At
ˆr = 1.2 the number of admissible events drops from eight in the Kerr limit to a single decay at ˆα0 = 0.0006,
and the process is forbidden outright for ˆα0 ≳0.0012; moving the decay radius outward restores it, and
the strongly coupled regime that is closed at ˆr = 1.2 reopens at ˆr = 1.5. Over the entire parameter range

The two eﬃciency measures respond diﬀerently, and this is our main result. The energy return on
investment falls monotonically with ˆα0, while the growth of the irreducible mass is suppressed relative to
Kerr, so that a smaller fraction of the lost rotational energy is locked into the horizon. The utilization

now adjacent to the minimum radius. This reorganization across ˆα0,change is a genuine change in the
topology of the eﬃciency landscape, driven by the coupling alone. It has no counterpart in the geometries
examined so far: in Kerr [7, 8] the rotational energy is largely converted into irreducible mass, in Reissner–
Nordström [13] the charge cannot be reduced to zero, and in the accelerating case [15] the utilization
eﬃciency can exceed one half, but in none of them does the eﬃciency structure itself change character as
a parameter is varied.

work, combining the charge-sign selectivity of the Kerr–Newman process with the geometric running of
