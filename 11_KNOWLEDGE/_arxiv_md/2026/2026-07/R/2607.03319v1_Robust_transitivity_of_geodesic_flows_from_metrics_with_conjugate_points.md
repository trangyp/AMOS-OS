This dynamical system establishes deep connections between dynamical properties
and the geometry of the underlying manifold. This interplay goes in both directions,
where geometric properties imply dynamical properties, and vice versa.
For example, a classical result states that if a metric g has negative sectional
curvature, then its geodesic flow is uniformly hyperbolic (an Anosov flow) [1]. This
means that the tangent bundle along orbits splits into complementary invariant
directions in which the dynamics is simplified: distances are exponentially contracted
in one direction and exponentially expanded in the other (see Section 2.3 for precise
definitions). On the other hand, Klingenberg [25] proved that metrics with Anosov
geodesic flow on compact manifolds cannot present geodesics with conjugate points
(a purely geometrical feature). This result was later extended to complete metrics
on not necessarily compact manifolds by Ma˜n´e [27] and for general non-compact
surfaces in [29] by Melo and Roma˜na.
We remark that it is not true that metrics with Anosov geodesic flows must
present only non-positive curvature, explicit examples of Riemannian metrics with
curvature with both signs were constructed in [14] and [19] (see also [15]).
Given these rich interplays between the Anosov property of geodesic flow and the
geometry of the manifold, it is natural to pose the following general question:
Question 1. Which geometric properties can or can not coexist with which dynam-
ical property of the geodesic flow?
More specifically:
Question 2. Which “interesting” dynamical properties of the geodesic flow can
occur outside the class of Anosov geodesic flows?
This second question became a focal point in several works published in the early
2000s. For instance, every metric on the sphere S2 must admit conjugate points,
hence no geodesic flow on S2 can be Anosov (similar result for T2). On the other
hand, significant progress was made in constructing metrics on S2 whose geodesic
flows exhibit rich dynamics—such as positive topological entropy and nearly dense
geodesics—as shown in [5], [9], and [23]. Geodesic flows with complex dynamics on
spheres were already known from the work of Donnay [12, 13], whose arguments
relied on the presence of negative curvature. In contrast, the other constructions
mentioned above were achieved even within the space of positively curved metrics,
demonstrating that complicated dynamics for the geodesic flow do not require neg-
ative sectional curvature to even appear.
Another way to relax the Anosov condition is to consider partially hyperbolic sys-
tems (see Section 2.3), which still exhibit contracting and expanding directions, but
these are no longer complementary. In addition, they possess center directions along
the orbits where the dynamics may be neutral (neither contracting nor expanding),
making the analysis considerably more delicate.
In the setting of geodesic flows, the first examples of partially hyperbolic geodesic
flows were constructed by Carneiro and Pujals in [6]. Recently, we extended their
results in a subsequent work [10], where we produced metrics whose geodesic flows
display rich dynamical properties, including ergodicity with respect to the Liouville
measure (and hence topological transitivity), uniqueness of the measure of maximal
entropy, and expansivity. All these properties occur for metrics that lie outside the
class of Anosov geodesic flows, but on the boundary of metrics without conjugate
points (see [39]).

In the present work, we further extend the properties of such examples by estab-
lishing their robust transitivity, which leads to our first main result:
Theorem A. There exists a manifold M that admits a C2-open set of Riemannian
metrics with conjugate points and topologically transitive geodesic flow.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result establishing the existence
of robustly transitive geodesic flows outside the class of Anosov metrics. This par-
tially addresses the questions posed above and also provides a complete answer to a
question left open by Carneiro and Pujals [Question 1, [6]] regarding the existence
of transitive partially hyperbolic geodesic flows for metrics that admit conjugate
points.
The study of robustly transitive dynamical systems beyond uniform hyperbolic-
ity dates back to the 70s with the work of Ma˜n´e [26], and was further developed by
Bonatti and D´ıaz [2]. A common feature of their examples is that they are partially
hyperbolic systems. Later, Bonatti, D´ıaz and Pujals characterized robustly tran-
sitive diffeomorphisms, proving that every C1-robustly transitive diffeomorphism
must admit a dominated splitting—a necessary condition for partial hyperbolicity.
For flows, a similar result for the Poincar´e map was obtained by Bonatti, Gourmelon,
and Vivier [3].
In our setting, we say that a Riemannian metric g is robustly transitive if it
admits a C2-neighbourhood U such that for every ˜g ∈U, the geodesic flow of ˜g
is topologically transitive. In light of the aforementioned results, it is natural to
conjecture that an analogous characterization should hold in the context of geodesic
flows, although the perturbation techniques required would be of a fundamentally
different nature:
Conjecture 1.1. Let g be a robustly transitive Riemannian metric. Then, its geo-
desic flow must admit a dominated splitting.
More generally,
Conjecture 1.2. Let X be a robustly transitive contact flow (inside the class of
contact flows); then it must admit a dominated splitting.
Our result provides the first evidence supporting the above conjectures. More-
over, by a result of Contreras [7], a dominated splitting for the geodesic flow (more
generally contact flows) already implies partial hyperbolicity.
The proof of Theorem A combines three main ingredients. First, the construc-
tion in [10] furnishes the required examples and several of their essential properties.
Second, we introduce a key property in the context of flows, called the SH-saddle
property, where “SH ” stands for “Some Hyperbolicity”. This notion was originally
introduced by Pujals and Sambarino [37] for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms
as a criterion to obtain C1-robust minimality of the stable foliation. It was later ex-
tended by the second author [36] to a broader class of partially hyperbolic systems—
including symplectic diffeomorphisms—yielding new examples of C1-robustly tran-
sitive, non-Anosov partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on Tn.
The SH-saddle property requires the existence of a uniform constant L > 0 such
that every strong stable (resp. strong unstable) leaf of length at least L contains a
point whose backward (resp. forward) iterates exhibit uniform expansion in some
center direction (see Section 3 for the precise definition).

1.2. Strategy of the proofs. Altogether, the proof of Theorem A goes as follows:
the examples in [10] are partially hyperbolic, not Anosov, expansive, topologically
transitive and the metrics have no conjugate points. We establish the SH-saddle
property for the examples and prove that this is a C2-open property in the set
of Riemannian metrics with partially hyperbolic geodesic flow. We can then ap-
ply Theorem B to find a C2-neighbourhood consisting of Riemannian metrics with
topologically transitive geodesic flows, call it U. By a result by Ruggiero [39], the
metrics given by the examples must lie in the boundary of the set of metrics with
no conjugate points; thus, we find a smaller C2-open set inside U of Riemannian
metrics with topologically transitive geodesic flow and conjugate points, and the
proof is complete.
The proof of Theorem B is the most delicate part of this article. The arguments
rely heavily on the dynamics and topological aspects of the setting rather than
the Riemannian geometry. Moreover, we will use that sufficiently close to the initial
metric, some properties persist, then we will restrict ourselves to smaller and smaller
neighborhoods whenever necessary. Let us describe the steps of the proof:
Step 1: Let g1 be a Riemannian metric and φt as in the hypothesis. Expansivity
and non-existence of conjugate points guarantee by [41] the existence two
continuous foliations with C0 leaves given by the stable and unstable sets.
Moreover, this pair of foliations presents a local product structure. This
implies transitivity for φt.

2.1. Expansive geodesic flows. Let φt : X →X be a continuous flow on a metric
space (X, d). The flow φt is said to be expansive if there exists a constant χ > 0
such that for every x ∈X we have the following property: if for a given y ∈X there
exists a continuous and surjective map ry : R →R with ry(0) = 0 such that
d(φt(x), φry(t)(y)) ≤χ for every t ∈R,
then there exists t0 ∈R such that φt0(x) = y. We call χ the expansivity constant.
In other words, every two different orbits of an χ-expansive flow are χ-separeted
eventually in time.
We say that a continuous flow φt : X →X is topologically stable if there exists
a C0 neighbourhood V of φt such that, for every flow ψt ∈V there are continuous
and surjective functions h : X →X and r : X × R →R with r(·, 0) = 0 such that
h ◦ψt(x) = φr(x,t) ◦h(x) for every t ∈R, x ∈X.

4.2. A criterion for openness. In this section, we are going to state a result that
we are going to apply in the main theorem. Roughly speaking, it says that given a
continuous map between topological spaces of the same dimension, and such that the

open set. The version we are going to use comes from [30], which is a quantitative
version of a result in [4]. We begin with a few definitions.
Definition 4.3. Suppose f : X →Y is a continuous map between metric spaces.
We say that y ∈Y is a stable value if there is ϵ > 0 such that if dC0(f, g) < ϵ then
y ∈Im(g).

then Im(f) contains an open set. To see this, take ϵ > 0 from the definition of

Remark 4.16. Let us bring attention to the elements that are crucial for our proof.
Indeed, the proof is purely topological and dynamical and does not rely heavily on the
nature of the geodesic flow. The first one is partial hyperbolicity and the SH-Saddle
property for flows. Proposition 3.12 gives us that these are C1 open properties inside
the class of flows. The second element is expansivity with local product structure for
the stable and unstable sets. These imply topological transitivity, density of periodic
points, and topological stability. The same proof we have presented for Theorem 4.13
works for a more general result for the general class of flows, and we will state it
for future use.
Theorem 4.17. Let φt : M →M be a partially hyperbolic flow of class C1 on a
compact and connected differentiable manifold. Assume that φt satisfies the following
conditions
(1) φt is expansive with local product structure for the stable and unstable sets.
(2) k := dim Ess = dim Euu.
(3) φt has SH-Saddle property of index (n −k, n −k).
Then, φt is C1-robustly transitive.

Theorem 5.1 (Theorem B in [10]). Let (M, g) be a compact K¨ahler manifold of holo-

5.2. Conclusion of the proof. We now finish the proof of Theorem A by following
the steps we stated in the introduction.
Let ˜g be the Riemannian metric given by Corollary 5.2, i.e., ˜g has no conjugate
points and its geodesic flow φt is expansive, partially hyperbolic but non-Anosov.
By Lemma 5.3 the geodesic flow φt has the SH-saddle property of index (d, d), where
d = n −1 −k and k = dimEss = dimEuu. Then by applying Theorem B, we obtain
a C2 neighborhood of ˜g, say U, such that every Riemannian metric g ∈U has a
topologically transitive geodesic flow.
Ruggiero proved in [39] that the C2 interior of the set of Riemannian metrics
with no conjugate points coincides with the set of Riemannian metrics with Anosov
geodesic flow. Since the geodesic flow of ˜g is non-Anosov, the metric ˜g must lie in the
C2 boundary of the set of Riemannian metrics with no conjugate points. Therefore,
there is a metric ˆg ∈U that has conjugate points.
Moreover, by a corollary of
[39], we know that the set of Riemannian metrics with conjugate points is C2 open.
Then there exists V ⊂U a C2 neighborhood of ˆg consisting of metrics with conjugate
points.
Therefore, we conclude that every Riemannian metric in V has conjugate points
and partially hyperbolic geodesic flow that is topologically transitive.
