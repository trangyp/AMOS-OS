ABSTRACT
We present multi-epoch, multi-frequency radio observations of the tidal disruption event (TDE)
AT 2022wtn, obtained with the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) and Giant Metrewave Radio
Telescope (GMRT), spanning 97-866 days after optical detection. The peak radio flux density increases
until 300 days post optical discovery, flattens out for several hundred days, then begins to decrease
at 534 days.
Utilizing an updated equipartition analysis framework, we estimate several physical
parameters of the event and the surrounding medium.
We model AT 2022wtn with two different
geometries: a spherical and a conical emitting region. The spherical outflow model gives an expansion
velocity of v ≈0.21c and a kinetic energy of ∼3.8 × 1049 erg, and the conical outflow model yields
a higher energy (∼1.8 × 1050) and velocity (v ≈0.41c) than the spherical case. After ruling out
the possibility of a relativistic jet, we consider several potential origins for sub-relativistic outflow
regions in TDEs including unbound debris streams, collisionally-induced outflows, an accretion-driven
wind, and an outflow from an accretion disk state transition, and find only an accretion disk state
transition outflow to be consistent with the high energy and velocity found in our equipartition results.
AT 2022wtn is a uniquely powerful non-relativistic radio-emitting TDE, and joins a growing population
that display a diverse range of outflow properties.

1. INTRODUCTION
On occasion, a star will venture too close to a su-
permassive black hole (SMBH) and be ripped apart
by tidal forces overcoming the star’s self-gravity (M. J.
Rees 1988; J. G. Hills 1975). In the aftermath, a lumi-
nous transient is produced that can be observed across
the electromagnetic spectrum; such an occurrence is
known as a tidal disruption event (TDE). During this

process, roughly half of the resulting stellar debris is
accreted onto the black hole, while the other half es-
capes the gravitational pull of the SMBH and is ejected
into the surrounding environment (C. R. Evans & C. S.
Kochanek 1989; J. Guillochon & E. Ramirez-Ruiz 2013).
Most TDEs are observed in locations coincident with the
nucleus of their host galaxies (S. van Velzen et al. 2020;
but see also, D. Lin et al. 2018; I. Sfaradi et al. 2025; Y.
Yao et al. 2025; C. C. Jin et al. 2025; W. Li et al. 2025;
W. Zhang et al. 2025).

TDEs provide an excellent way to study accretion
disk formation, relativistic jets, and outflow mechanisms
around black holes. A variety of outflow mechanisms
have been proposed in TDEs to explain radio observa-
tions, which constrain physical properties such as the
outflow velocity, blast wave radius, and the density of
the surrounding environment (R. A. Chevalier 1998;
B. D. Metzger et al. 2012; B. Margalit & E. Quataert
2021; T. Matsumoto & T. Piran 2023; F. De Colle et al.
2012).
A small portion (≲1%) of the known TDE popula-
tion have launched prompt on-axis relativistic jets (e.g.,
B. A. Zauderer et al. 2011; S. Bradley Cenko et al.
2012; T. Eftekhari et al. 2018; L. Rhodes et al. 2023; F.
De Colle et al. 2012) that generate extremely luminous
(νLν ≳1040 erg/s) radio emission still detectable sev-
eral years after initial disruption. Most (≳99%) radio-
detected TDEs exhibit less-luminous outflows (νLν ≲
1040 erg/s), commonly attributed to a quasi-spherical
Newtonian outflow interacting with the circumnuclear
environment (e.g., K. D. Alexander et al. 2016; C. T.
Christy et al. 2025; Y. Cendes et al. 2021). These out-
flows have been well described by accretion-driven wind,
collisions in the debris stream, the unbound debris in-
teracting with the surrounding medium, or state transi-
tions in the accretion disk launching an outflow (K. D.
Alexander et al. 2016; J. Krolik et al. 2016; C. Bonnerot
& W. Lu 2020; S. C. Wu et al. 2025; L. Dai et al. 2018),
but the relative importance of these outflow mechanisms
in powering the observed radio emission remains uncer-
tain.
By deriving the inferred physical properties of
the event, we can constrain the outflow mechanism in
the context of these models, though several (e.g., A. J.
Goodwin et al. 2022) or none (e.g., C. T. Christy et al.
2024) of them may sufficiently explain the outflow prop-
erties. As many as 40% of radio-TDEs only display radio
emission at late times, hundreds to thousands of days af-
ter the initial disruption (K. D. Alexander et al. 2020;
Y. Cendes et al. 2024).
In this paper we present multi-epoch, multi-frequency
radio observations of the TDE AT 2022wtn extending
over 2 years after discovery. F. Onori et al. (2025) pre-
viously studied the optical, UV, and X-ray emission and
found evidence for a prompt accretion disk formation
and an edge-on viewing angle. We consider outflow mod-
els that can self-consistently explain the radio emission
in the context of the available multiwavelength data for
this event.
The paper is organized as follows. In §2 we describe
our radio data and the reduction methods used. In §3
we model the radio spectral energy distribution as syn-
chrotron radiation. In §4, we carry out an equipartition
analysis to derive physical properties of the outflow and
the surrounding environment. In §5 we discuss impli-
cations of our modeling and look at AT 2022wtn in the
context of multiwavelength observations. In §6 we sum-
marize our results and present conclusions. All times
and frequencies reported are in the observer frame, un-
less otherwise noted.

3. EMISSION MODELING
3.1. Light Curve
AT 2022wtn is a low-luminosity (νLν ≲1040 erg/s)
radio TDE, comparable to a majority of radio-TDEs
(K. D. Alexander et al. 2020). This is in contrast with
the ultra-luminous jetted TDEs Swift J1644+57 (T.
Eftekhari et al. 2018; B. A. Zauderer et al. 2011) and
AT 2022cmc (L. Rhodes et al. 2023), and the candidate
off-axis jetted TDE AT 2018hyz (Y. Cendes et al. 2025).
The Ku-band radio light curve of AT 2022wtn exhibits
a fast initial rise after the first detection at δt = 167
days, followed by a monotonic decline for the remainder
of the observations (Figure 1). The Ku-band luminos-
ity rises by a factor of ∼4 between the first and sec-
ond detections. Due to the continued rise in peak flux
from 197 days to 300 days (Figure 1), the optically thin
SED above the peak at 197 days (Figure 2), and an ab-
sence of steep spectra below the peak (Appendix B), we
consider an origin in free-free absorption an unlikely ex-
planation for the rising light curve. Coupled with the
non-detection at δt = 97 days, this rapid rise is instead

γ is the electron Lorentz factor, and γ > γm, where
γm is the minimum electron Lorentz factor. We con-

4.3. Earlier Outflow?
In Section 3.2, we excluded the data points corre-
sponding to an excess transient component from our
modeling of the first SED at δt = 197 days. If we instead
take these points as comprising a distinct SED from an
entirely separate outflow launched at ≤197 days, we can
use the lowest frequency detection as an upper limit on
νp and a lower limit on Fp of this component. We can
then calculate all of the relevant equipartition quanti-
ties, obtaining a lower limit on the radius and energy of
the outflow (which are time-independent in the Newto-
nian regime). These values are extremely high with re-
spect to the values seen in the δt = 197 days epoch, sug-
gesting that if this is indeed a separate (perhaps prompt)
outflow, it would have been moving extremely fast with
a velocity of v > 0.3c, even with a launch date of δt = 0
days. Given the non-detection at δt = 97 days, it is un-
likely that an extremely fast and energetic outflow was
launched before this time, implying an even higher ve-
locity.
While the implications of such an outflow are
interesting, it is very difficult to draw any conclusions
on it given the paucity of data, since this component
disappears almost completely in the second SED at 233
days. We therefore opt to exclude it from the model-
ing, instead focusing on the post-138 days outflow in
our analysis.
5. DISCUSSION
Using the results obtained in our equipartition analy-
sis, we will now discuss the possible outflow mechanisms
behind AT 2022wtn, and compare its physical properties
to that of other TDEs.
5.1. Outflow Velocity and Kinetic Energy
We plot the equipartition energy as a function of out-
flow velocity for AT 2022wtn and several other TDEs
in Figure 5. To standardize our comparison, we recom-
pute the equipartition values for these other TDEs fol-
lowing the method described in Section 4.1. Our non-
relativistic spherical model predicts a roughly constant
velocity for the first five epochs (δt = 197−534d), before
decelerating in the final three (δt ≳534d; see Figure
4). This corresponds to β ≈0.21 ± 0.03 for a spher-
ical geometry, and β ≈0.41 ± 0.04 for a conical ge-
ometry. These values are quite high in the context of
other thermal TDEs, which usually have velocities of
β ≈0.05 −0.1 (K. D. Alexander et al. 2020, 2016; C. T.
Christy et al. 2025; A. J. Goodwin et al. 2022). When
modeled as a spherical outflow, AT 2018hyz displayed
velocities of β ≈0.33 and an energy comparable to that
of AT 2022wtn (Y. Cendes et al. 2025); however, there
is strong evidence that AT 2018hyz possesses an off-axis
relativistic jet, which is not the case for AT 2022wtn (see
Section 5.3.1).
AT 2022wtn exhibits a higher kinetic energy than any
other thermal (i.e., non-relativistic) TDE (see Figure


## Page 10


10
5; B. Mockler & E. Ramirez-Ruiz 2021), but does not
reach the energies seen in the relativistic on-axis TDEs
Swift J1644+57 and AT 2022cmc (T. Eftekhari et al.
2018; L. Rhodes et al. 2023). Furthermore, our inferred
equipartition energy increases by roughly ∼5× between
δt = 197 −300 days, driven by the increase in peak flux
(see Figure 3), at which point it plateaus at ∼3.7×1049
erg. The conical model sees a similar evolution, but with
a higher energy at all times, plateauing at ∼1.8 × 1050
erg.
Such an increase in energy has previously been
observed in TDEs (e.g., A. J. Goodwin et al. 2022; Y.
Cendes et al. 2021; E. Berger et al. 2012; C. T. Christy
et al. 2024), and could be caused by energy injection
into the outflow due to accretion onto the SMBH (Y.
Cendes et al. 2021). An energy increase could also be
due to a range of ejecta velocities present in the outflow
(E. Berger et al. 2012), but this is unlikely in our case
since the expected contemporaneous decline in velocity
is not seen (see Figure 4).
5.2. Circumnuclear Density Profile
Figure 5 shows the circumnuclear density next as
a function of the radius of the emitting region for
AT 2022wtn compared to other TDEs in the litera-
ture.
The density profile for the spherical case fol-
lows a slope of next ∝R−2.08 throughout the observa-
tion period. This falls within the range of other non-
relativistic TDEs, some of which display a profile of
next ∝R−1.5 consistent with spherical Bondi accre-
tion (H. Bondi 1952), and others displaying a steeper
next ∝R−2.5 profile (K. D. Alexander et al. 2016; M. M.
Anderson et al. 2020).
5.3. Outflow Mechanism
Armed with the extensive radio analysis presented
thus far, we can now discuss implications for the out-
flow mechanism behind AT 2022wtn.
We first con-
sider a relativistic jet scenario before looking at sev-
eral non-relativistic outflow models, including unbound
debris, collisionally-induced outflows, accretion-driven
wind, and an outflow driven by a state transition in the
accretion disk. We analyze these scenarios in the con-
text of the multiwavelength data presented in F. Onori
et al. (2025), including their discovery of a prompt ac-
cretion disk formation and presence of outflows at early
times, and their calculation of the Eddington ratio.
5.3.1. Relativistic Jet
So far, we have assumed that the outflow from
AT 2022wtn is non-relativistic.
Instead, if we assume
a collimated relativistic outflow (Γ ≈2; K. D. Alexan-
der et al. 2016; A. J. Goodwin et al. 2022), we obtain
fA ≲10−5 and an opening angle of θj ≲0.01◦by solv-
ing for fA with equation 24 in R. Barniol Duran et al.
(2013). This is far narrower than typical jets, even in
GRBs (D. A. Frail et al. 2001). In addition, if the radio
emission from AT 2022wtn were caused by a relativistic
jet, we would expect to see energies and radio luminosi-
ties similar to that of Swift J1644+57 (∼1052 erg and
νLν ≳1040 erg/s; T. Eftekhari et al. 2018), which are
not observed.
An on-axis relativistic jet is further ruled out by con-
sidering the multiwavelength properties of AT 2022wtn,
as outlined in F. Onori et al. (2025). Due to the strong
Bowen lines present in the optical and UV spectra, and
the lack of X-ray emission, it is likely that we are see-
ing the accretion disk from an off-axis viewing angle
(θ > 0◦). However, for the high energies (∼1050 erg)
seen in AT 2022wtn, we would expect a more extended
light curve (i.e., a slower evolution) than we observe (see
right panel in Figure 1) if the radio emission instead
arose from a promptly-launched off-axis jet (J. Granot
et al. 2018).
In summary, the evolution of the light curve, the low
luminosity and energy, and the unphysical opening angle
make the possibility of a relativistic jet being the source
of the observed radio emission in AT 2022wtn unlikely.
5.3.2. Unbound Debris
During a TDE, roughly half of the debris from the
disrupted star will be gravitationally unbound from the
SMBH, escaping at a speed dependent on the ratio of the
SMBH’s tidal radius and the pericenter distance (A. Ya-
linewich et al. 2019). This debris can form a bow shock
as it interacts with the CNM, producing synchrotron
emission. The unbound material is expected to possess
energies of ∼1047 −1048 erg (J. Krolik et al. 2016) and
travel at speeds of ≲0.06c (A. Yalinewich et al. 2019).
The energy found in our spherical analysis of ∼3.7 ×
1049 erg exceeds the allowed range expected for an un-
bound debris stream. Additionally, our inferred velocity
(β ≈0.21) is far higher than expected in an unbound
debris model.
This model also requires a collimation
(fA ∼0.2) higher than in the spherical case; increasing
the collimation would only further increase the energy
and velocity seen in our analysis. Therefore, an unbound
debris stream causing the radio emission is unlikely.
5.3.3. Collisionally-induced Outflow
W. Lu & C. Bonnerot (2020) demonstrated that
stream-stream collisions in the bound stellar debris
can cause a spherical outflow of gas, referred to as a
collisionally-induced outflow (CIO). The expected en-
ergy in a CIO model is ∼1050 erg, consistent with the
values obtained in our equipartition analysis.


## Page 11


11
However, the velocity we derive for a spherical geome-
try (β ≈0.21) exceeds the expected values for this model
(β ≈0.01 −0.1). In addition, the CIO scenario predicts
that a radio-emitting outflow would be launched around
the time of optical discovery, as the optical emission in
this model is primarily driven by stream-stream inter-
actions (W. Lu & C. Bonnerot 2020). This is disfavored
by our inferred launch date of δt = 138 days, and the
radio upper limit at δt = 97 days.
5.3.4. Accretion-Driven Wind
The radiation pressure generated by SMBH accre-
tion can produce winds traveling at velocities of β ≈
0.01 −0.1; this would generate radio emission as the
winds shock the surrounding CNM (L. E. Strubbe &
E. Quataert 2009). TDE outflows have been explained
with this mechanism before (K. D. Alexander et al.
2016; Y. Cendes et al. 2021), but the outflow velocity
of AT 2022wtn (β ≈0.21) is inconsistent with the ex-
pected values (β ≈0.01 −0.1) in an accretion wind
scenario (L. E. Strubbe & E. Quataert 2009).
There is evidence for a spherical outflow found in
the multiwavelength data presented by F. Onori et al.
(2025); however, this outflow would be launched near
the time of optical discovery, far earlier than our pro-
posed launch date of δt = 138 days; it would also be
much slower than our expected velocity, only reaching
v ≈0.007c. Further evidence for an outflow is found at
δt ≈80 days, where broad optical spectral lines are de-
tected. However, the emission lines seen in this outflow
are still much narrower than would be expected for an
outflow with v ≈0.21c (F. Onori et al. 2025), indicat-
ing that this is a separate component not related to the
radio emission.
5.3.5. Outflow Driven by an Accretion State Transition
We finally consider the possibility that the radio emis-
sion is caused by a delayed, sub-relativistic outflow re-
sulting from a state change in the accretion disk, first
proposed in F. De Colle et al. (2012). The outflow could
be approximated with either of our equipartition mod-
els; a conical geometry would display a higher energy,
velocity, and radius than in the spherical case.
Such
a scenario is consistent with what is proposed in S. C.
Wu et al. (2025), where instabilities or state transitions
within the accretion disk lead to the launch of an outflow
with velocities of 0.05c −0.3c, in line with our inferred
value of β ≈0.21 for the spherical case. The predicted
energy of ∼1050 in this model is consistent with our
values (∼3.7 × 1049, spherical; ∼1.8 × 1050, conical) as
well.
This model is attractive because of its ability to ex-
plain the delayed radio emission seen in AT 2022wtn.
F. Onori et al. (2025) find evidence for a prompt cir-
cularization process, far earlier than the first radio de-
tection at δt = 167 days; if the accretion flow were to
undergo a state transition (e.g., into a delayed phase of
super-Eddington accretion) around our inferred launch
date, we would expect an outflow to be launched at
roughly the same time (K. D. Alexander et al. 2025).
This state transition could be caused by the promptly-
circularized debris forming a hot pressure-supported en-
velope, which would delay rapid accretion until the en-
velope cools (B. D. Metzger 2022), as suggested in K. D.
Alexander et al. (2025). Another possible explanation
for this delayed phase of accretion is a larger debris self-
intersection radius; in this case, the debris would take
longer to reach the event horizon, delaying peak accre-
tion onto the SMBH (M. Nicholl et al. 2022).
Such a transition into super-Eddington accretion is
consistent with the findings in F. Onori et al. (2025),
who report an Eddington ratio of 0.06 −0.09 (i.e. sub-
Eddington) for δt ≲120 days. Additionally, if the radio
emission were produced by an outflow launched due to a
new phase of super-Eddington accretion, the rapid rise
of ≲100 days in the light curve of AT 2022wtn (see Fig-
ure 1) would be expected, as the shock collides with the
surrounding CNM (S. C. Wu et al. 2025). We there-
fore conclude that a transition into a phase of delayed
super-Eddington accretion can explain the outflow in
AT 2022wtn.
6. CONCLUSIONS
We present the results of our detailed study of the
TDE AT 2022wtn, observed in the radio from δt =
97 −866 days after its optical discovery. Transient ra-
dio emission was first observed several months after the
peak of the optical flare, rising to a peak flux density at
δt = 300 days, before beginning to decline. The radio
luminosity of AT 2022wtn is comparable to that of other
thermal TDEs (see Figure 1).
In our analysis, we modeled the radio emission with
two distinct non-relativistic geometries: a spherical and
conical outflow launched at δt = 138 days after opti-
cal discovery. We find that the energy displays a ∼5×
increase between δt = 197 −300 days, possibly indica-
tive of energy injection from accretion onto the central
SMBH. The energy then plateaus at ∼3.7 × 1049 erg, a
value higher than most other thermal TDEs, but still far
lower than the energy expected in a relativistic jet (e.g.,
Swift 1644+57; T. Eftekhari et al. 2018). We rule out
the possibility of the radio emission originating in a rela-
tivistic jet, as the opening angle of the jet would have to
be extremely small (≲0.1◦); additionally, AT 2022wtn
displays a lower luminosity than jetted-TDE candidates


## Page 12


12
such as Swift J1644+57 and AT 2018hyz (T. Eftekhari
et al. 2018; Y. Cendes et al. 2025).
We find that the outflow propagates at v ≈0.21c in
the spherical case, and v ≈0.41c in the conical case,
which is far too fast to be explained by several of the
current non-relativistic TDE models; unbound debris
streams, collisionally-induced outflows, and accretion-
driven winds all require a velocity of v ≲0.1c. A de-
layed outflow caused by an accretion state transition,
which would have similar dynamical properties to either
of our equipartition model geometries, offers a viable ex-
planation for the observed radio emission. This model
additionally explains the delayed launch date of δt ≈138
days, and the fast rise in the Ku-band light curve (see
Figure 1). In summary, we favor an outflow caused by
an accretion state transition as the origin of the radio
emission.
AT 2022wtn possesses a uniquely fast and ener-
getic outflow, more powerful than that of other non-
relativistic TDEs. Our analysis provides insight into the
outflow properties of this event, and also emphasizes the
diversity of the non-relativistic radio-emitting TDE pop-
ulation. We encourage continued future modeling that
considers powerful and evolving outflows, and we also
highlight the need for extended radio and multiwave-
length monitoring campaigns to constrain the mecha-
nisms behind TDE outflows.

B. CONSTRAINTS ON FREE-FREE ABSORPTION IN THE EARLY RADIO LIGHT CURVE
The VLA Ku-band (≈15 GHz) observations at 97 days (upper limit) and 169 days and 197 days (detections) reveal
a very rapid rise in the radio light curve of at least ∼t2.4 (from 97–169 days) and ∼t9 (from 169–197 days). As
discussed in Section 4.2, one possibility is that this rise is associated with the delayed launch of an outflow. Here,
we consider the alternate scenario where the outflow is launched earlier but the radio emission is initially suppressed
by free-free absorption (FFA). The free-free optical depth at frequency ν through a path-length L for a fully ionized
medium is given by (G. B. Rybicki & A. P. Lightman 1986)
τff = 1.8 × 10−2gff(T/K)−1.5Z2(n/cm−3)(L/cm)(ν/Hz)−2,
where we set the Gaunt factor, gff ∼5 and ion charge, Z = 1 (assuming pure hydrogen composition) following I.
Sfaradi et al. (2025) and A. J. Nayana et al. (2025). Setting the radius to the equipartition value inferred from the
first full SED at 197 days yields
τff = 0.5(T/105K)−1.5(n/106cm−3)(L/4 × 1016cm)(ν/15GHz)−2.


## Page 15


15
A suppression corresponding to the observed flux ratio of eτff ≈4 between subsequent pairs of the first three epochs
corresponds to τff ≈1.4, requiring an average density of ≈2 × 106cm−3 within the absorbing region and an enclosed
mass of ≈0.4 M⊙within a spherical volume of radius L.
This large enclosed mass hints that the suppression is
unlikely to arise from FFA. Although this calculation remains subject to the unknown geometry of the system and the
properties (e.g., temperature, size, and density structure) of the absorbing medium, yet more evidence against FFA
suppression is afforded by the SEDs. If the Ku-band flux were subject to strong FFA, this suppression would increase
rapidly at lower frequencies (as e−1/ν2); however, the SED rises below Ku-band at 197 days and there is no evidence
for a steep (free-free absorbed) spectrum below the peak. The FFA model can be rescued if the FFA is assumed to
entirely disappear by 197 days; however, the flux on the optically thin segment of the SED continues to rise from 197
days to 300 days while the spectrum remains peaked at lower frequencies (Fig. 2). This is very difficult to explain as
a further reduction in τff, since the expected change in the spectral shape this should cause is not seen. Whereas we
cannot rule out a combination of rapidly dropping τff and a rising flux due to, e.g., energy injection into the outflow,
such scenarios appear increasingly contrived and a delayed outflow launch remains the simpler, favored interpretation.

D. EVOLVING ELECTRON ENERGY INDEX
In Figure 7, we present the results of fitting the SEDs leaving p as a free parameter. We find a large change in p
from 197 days to 233 days, with modest variations in p thereafter. These variations in p would significantly impact the
equipartition quantities, in particular, implying a very large energy increase by a factor of ≈100 between δt = 197–233
days. While energy injection has been proposed for TDEs before (see Section 5.1), this extremely rapid energy increase
appears implausible. Fitting all SEDs with a fixed optically thin spectral slope still yields good fits, suggesting that
this variation is not strongly supported by the data. Finally, we find that this variation is substantially reduced on
fixing the smoothing parameter to s = 1 (which otherwise depends on p in the J. Granot & R. Sari 2002 framework)
and is therefore likely not a real effect. To avoid imprinting this unphysical variation on the equipartition quantities,
we undertake our analysis with p fixed to the mean value of 3.26.
E. CONSTRAINTS ON EMISSION FROM THERMAL ELECTRONS
For completeness, we explore the impact of thermal electrons on our SED modeling in section 4 using the semi-

of ϵT , these parameters have similar meanings as described in section 4.
