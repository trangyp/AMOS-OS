CHANGE OF ENRICHMENT FOR MONOIDAL MODEL CATEGORIES
KIMBALL STRONG
Abstract. A classical theorem of category theory says that given an adjunction L : V ⇄
W : R between monoidal categories with lax monoidal right adjoint, there is an induced ad-
junction LCat : V-Cat ⇄W-Cat : RCat between categories of enriched categories. We extend
this result to monoidal model categories (with some model categorical assumptions), improv-
ing on previous theorems which required the derived functor of L to be strong monoidal.
As an application, we consider the category of chain complexes over a varying groupoid of
operators, equipped with two different monoidal structures: the standard tensor product,
and the cartesian product. We show that both of these are monoidal model categories,
and use our theorems to reproduce the square-zero extensions adjunction for augmented
dg-categories.

Suppose V and W are monoidal categories, and that
L : V ⇄W : R
is an adjunction of underlying categories. If L is “strong monoidal,” that is, if there is a
natural isomorphism L(X ⊗Y ) ∼= L(X) ⊗L(Y ), then this adjunction defines an adjunction
in a 2-category of monoidal categories, and it then follows that it induces a change-of-
enrichment adjunction
LCat : VCat ⇄WCat : RCat
which is given by applying L and R locally (i.e. by applying L and R to the hom-objects).
This was generalized to homotopical settings by Muro in [Mur12] in which the correspond-

Before stating our main theorem, we need some amount of terminology: recall
that for V a monoidal category, the category V-Cat is equipped with a canonical functor
π0 : V-Cat →Cat, which sends C ∈V-Cat to the category π0C which has the same objects
as C, and for which
π0C[x, y] = HomV(IV, C[x, y])
where IV is the unit of V. We can extend this to the model categorical setting; if V is a
model category, then we can define π0 : V-Cat →Cat by setting
π0C[x, y] = HomHo(V)(IV, C[x, y])
Definition 0.1. Let V be a model category equipped with a symmetric monoidal structure.
We call a functor f : C →D of V-categories a Dwyer-Kan equivalence if it satisfies:
• (homotopical full faithfulness): for any x, y ∈C, the map C[x, y] →D[fx, fy] is
a weak equivalence in V.
• (homotopical essential surjectivity): the induced functor π0f : π0C →π0D is
essentially surjective.
Note that these together imply that π0f is in fact an equivalence of categories, as homotopical
full faithfulness implies that the map
HomHo(V)(IV, C[x, y]) →HomHo(V)(IV, D[fx, fy])
is an isomorphism.

0.2. Organization of the Paper. We will begin in Section 1 by reviewing some background
on change of enrichment for ordinary (i.e., non-model) categories, as well as the model
category case when the object set is fixed (which is significantly easier to analyze). In Section
2 we prove the second of our change of enrichment theorems for monoidal model categories
(Theorem B). In Sections 3 and 4, we apply this theorem to the category of chain complexes
with action by a (varying) groupoid, and in Section 5 show that the resulting adjunction
recovers the noncommutative 1-forms of an augmented dg-category. In Section 6—by far the
most technical section—we prove Theorem A. In Section 7 we give a “counterexample” to
demonstrate the necessity of one of our seemingly extraneous hypotheses, showing that even
when the model structures involved are trivial the induced adjunction can fail to be Quillen.

As this list of axioms is fairly long, we will say a brief word on the necessity of each:
(1) We will prove this theorem by analyzing the effect of LCat on the generating (acyclic)
cofibrations of the model structure on V-Cat as constructed in [Mur12], hence the
necessity for V to satisfy the hypotheses there.
(2) W does not need to satisfy all of the same axioms, though it does still need to be a
monoidal model category.
(3) The assumption that the unit of V is cofibrant could likely be omitted provided one
is willing to make the other axioms more complex (phrased in terms of a cofibrant
replacement of the unit), but as all of the examples we are interested in have cofibrant
unit we have chosen not to pursue this slightly more complex statement.
(4) The unit-cylinder axiom is the most technical, and its assumption is very specific
to our method of proof analyzing the generating acylic cofibrations of the model
structure as constructed by Muro in [Mur12]. Thus, we expect it is the least necessary
axiom. However, it is satisfied in many examples of interest: for instance, it is always
satisfied if every object of V is fibrant. It is also satisfied by sSet and various models
of spectra.
(5) The assumption that R is lax monoidal is of course necessary in the classical theorem,
and thus also necessary for the homotopical analog.
(6) Finally, we have in addition to lax monoidality of R the weak preservation of the
unit by L, which appears to have no analog in the classical theorem.
However,
as we note in Section 7, some condition on the unit is necessary for the change-of-
enrichment adjunction to be well behaved, even when V and W have the trivial model
structure. While the condition of weak preservation of the unit is certainly stronger
than necessary, it holds for many examples of interest, and furthermore our method
of proof is fairly dependent on this fact.
