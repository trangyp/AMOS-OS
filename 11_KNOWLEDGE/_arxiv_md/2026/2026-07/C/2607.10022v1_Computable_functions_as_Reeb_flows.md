COMPUTABLE FUNCTIONS AS REEB FLOWS
KAI CIELIEBAK, ´ANGEL GONZ´ALEZ-PRIETO, AND EVA MIRANDA
Abstract. We prove that, given any contact 3-manifold and any computable function f : N 99K N,
there exists a defining contact form and a Poincar´e section of its Reeb flow whose partially defined
return map computes f.
1. Introduction
The realization of computation within continuous dynamical systems is a very active research topic
at the crossroads of logic, topology, and analysis. Despite its seemingly simple nature, computation
is known to exhibit highly complex dynamics, including chaos, mixing, and undecidability. For this
reason, the ability to simulate computability within a family of continuous dynamical systems provides
strong evidence that very intricate dynamics can arise.
The most celebrated model of computation is given by the notion of a Turing machine.
This is
an abstraction of a computer consisting of a simple read-write head inspecting a two-sided tape of
symbols. At each step of the computation, the head reads one symbol and, according to the symbol
read and its internal state, replaces it with another symbol, transitions to another internal state, and
shifts either to the right or to the left along the tape in order to continue the read-write process.
This procedure continues until the machine reaches one of its halting states, leaving the result of the
computation written on the tape. Turing machines were conceived as a way to formalize the notion
that a partially defined function f : N 99K N can be effectively computed by a mechanical algorithm.
Such a function f is said to be computable if there exists a Turing machine M such that, when we
write any n ∈N on a tape in binary and run M on it, then M halts if and only if f(n) is defined, in
which case the result of the computation is f(n) written in binary on the tape. From this perspective,
M is the “algorithm” computing f, the piece of code that is executed to calculate f mechanically.
Despite their apparent dependence on Turing machines, computable functions are first-class citizens
in computability theory. As Kleene proved in [12], they can be fully characterized as partial recursive
functions through a set of axioms independent of the notion of Turing machines. In fact, in line with
the Church–Turing thesis, computable functions turn out to be models of computation fully equivalent
to Turing machines. In this spirit, there exist computable functions with very complex behaviour, such
as universal functions, capable of simulating any other computable function, or undecidable functions,
for which there exists no algorithm able to determine their domain of definition.

Partially defined functions also arise naturally in continuous dynamical systems. Consider a manifold
M with a smooth vector field X and a Poincar´e section D ⊂M, i.e., an embedded 2-disk transverse
to X. This defines a partially defined first return map Φ : D 99K D as follows: if the forward X-orbit
of x ∈D hits D again we set Φ(x) to be the first return point; otherwise, Φ(x) is undefined. In
particular, if we embed N ,→D via the binary expansion of an integer encoded in the square Cantor
set (see §2.4), then we obtain a partially defined function Φ|N : N 99K N.
A particularly interesting family of dynamical systems arises from contact geometry.
Given a 3-
dimensional manifold M, a contact form is a 1-form α ∈Ω1(M) such that α ∧dα ̸= 0. This contact
form induces a maximally non-integrable coorientable distribution ξ = ker α of planes, called the
contact structure, as well as a unique smooth vector field R characterized by α(R) = 1 and dα(R, ·) = 0,
called the Reeb field. Reeb dynamics is a very active area of research, with important milestones such
as Taubes’ proof of the Weinstein conjecture [19], and the celebrated “two or infinity” theorem by
Hofer, Wysocki, and Zehnder [11].
In this setting, the main result of this note is the following.
Theorem A. Let (M, ξ) be a coorientable contact 3-manifold. For any computable function f : N 99K
N, there exists a contact form β ∈Ω1(M) with ξ = ker β and a 2-dimensional disk D ⊂M such that
the Reeb flow of β is transverse to D and its first return map Φ satisfies Φ|N = f.
The proof of this result combines ideas from computability and contact topology. On the compu-
tational side, it builds on the work [10], where models are constructed in which computable partial
functions are encoded by return maps of volume-preserving vector fields on dynamical handlebodies.
More precisely, given a computable function f : N 99K N, consider a Turing machine Mf computing
it. One then places a 0-cell for each internal state of Mf, and connects these cells by 1-handles rep-
resenting the transitions of Mf between states, thereby constructing a handlebody W. Alternatively,
one may think of W as the result of “thickening” the graph representing Mf as a finite state machine.
Then a volume preserving vector field X is defined on W so that the dynamics on each 1-handle
correspond to the read-write-shift operation performed by the Turing machine Mf, in such a way
that the global continuous dynamics simulate the symbolic dynamics of Mf on transverse sections of
the 0-handles. Furthermore, by gluing the loose ends of W corresponding to the starting and halting
states of Mf we can arrange that W has all its 1-handles attached on both sides to 0-handles, and the
gluing locus D0 becomes a Poincar´e section for X whose first return map restricts to f on N ⊂D0.
On the geometric side, the main task is thus to realize this dynamical handlebody as a Reeb flow.
We achieve this through a series of results that may be of independent interest. First, we construct a
contact form α on W whose Reeb vector field is a rescaling of X. This form is handcrafted in such a
way that it agrees with the standard form −y dx + dt on a neighborhood of the 0-handles. Secondly,
we prove that we can find an embedding ϕ : W ,→M into the given contact 3-manifold, and a contact
form β ∈Ω1(M) with ker β = ξ, such that ϕ∗β = α. This implies that ϕ(D0) ⊂M is a Poincar´e
section for the Reeb flow of β whose first return map Φ satisfies Φ|N = f.
This note should be seen as a generalization of [1], where Turing machines are encoded as Euler flows
through generalized shifts, but with the focus shifted to computable functions. In [1], the authors
constructed a Reeb flow on any 3-manifold whose return map on a Poincar´e section represents one
step in the computation of a given Turing machine. They achieved this by embedding the contact
mapping torus of any generalized shift inside the given contact manifold. In contrast, the construction
developed in this note realizes the whole computable function as the return map of the flow, in

3.3. Contact forms and structures. In this subsection, we briefly review some basic concepts from
contact topology. For a more thorough treatment see e.g. [8].
Let M be a 3-manifold. A contact form on M is a 1-form α ∈Ω1(M) such that α ∧dα is nowhere
zero. It defines a (coorientable) contact structure ξ = ker α, which is a maximally non-integrable
distribution of planes. Any contact form α′ with ξ = ker α′ is said to be a contact form defining ξ.
Associated to a contact form α is its Reeb vector field R defined by the properties
ιRdα = 0,
α(R) = 1.

asserts that, given a smooth 1-parameter family of contact forms αt ∈Ω1(M) for 0 ≤t ≤1 with
αt = α0 outside a compact set, there exists an isotopy φt : M →M such that φ∗

4.6. Undecidable recurrence and periodic Reeb orbits. We finish by outlining two immediate

The first application concerns recurrence to a fixed transverse section and its decidability. Recall that

halting function of a universal Turing machine. By Theorem A, the contact form and section D ⊂M
can be chosen so that the first return map Φ : D 99K D realizes f on the encoded copy of N ⊂D.

would therefore decide the domain of f, a contradiction.

hence it cannot be an iterate of an orbit with smaller return period. Since the primitive periods are
unbounded, the corresponding simple closed Reeb orbits are distinct, proving the claim.
□
Remark 4.16. The former result should be compared with the two-or-infinity periodic orbits theory
for Reeb flows in dimension three, initiated by Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder [11] and developed further
by Cristofaro-Gardiner–Hutchings–Pomerleano [5] and Cristofaro-Gardiner–Hryniewicz–Hutchings–
Liu [4]. Under the hypotheses of these results, a Reeb flow has either two or infinitely many simple
periodic orbits. The exceptional case of exactly two simple orbits is highly rigid: it can occur only on
S3 or on a lens space, where the two orbits are the core circles of a genus-one Heegaard splitting [3].
Thus Corollary 4.15 is not meant as a new abstract existence theorem in the range already covered
by this theory. Its point is different: it gives a prescribed contact form whose infinitely many simple
Reeb orbits arise from an explicit computational mechanism. The two-symbol subsystem produces
primitive periodic points of unbounded return period, and their suspensions rule out, by construction,
the exceptional two-orbit dynamics.
