layers by mechanical exfoliation is also hindered by this coupling, preventing
experimental examination of monolayer’s properties. Here we show that 2M-
WS2 undergoes amine intercalation through a simple wet-chemical reaction,

challenges the long-held view that group VIB dichalcogenides are inert toward
neutral-molecule intercalation, and demonstrates molecular intercalation as a
general chemical route for realizing monolayer-like topological-insulator physics
and enabling chiral van der Waals superlattices in bulk single crystals.

1 Introduction
Since the isolation of graphene initiated the field of two-dimensional (2D) materi-
als [1, 2], a wide range of atomically thin crystals have been identified, exhibiting
exotic quantum phenomena that arise from reduced dimensionality. Among them, 2D
topological insulators (TI), also known as quantum spin Hall (QSH) insulators, com-
bine an insulating bulk with conducting helical edge states protected by time-reversal
symmetry, and offer considerable potential for low-dissipation spintronics.[3, 4] The-
ory predicts that monolayer 1T′-MX2 (M = W, Mo; X = S, Se, Te) transition-metal
dichalcogenides (TMDs), built from distorted-octahedral zig-zag metal chains, are
promising 2D QSH insulators (Fig. 1a).[5] Topological edge states have since been
reported in monolayer 1T′-WTe2 [6, 7] and 1T′-WSe2.[8] However, they have not yet
been observed in 1T′-WS2, which has the advantage of enhanced air stability compared
to tellurides and thermal robustness among metastable phases.[9, 10]
Although monolayers across the 1T′-MX2 family share similar in-plane struc-
tures, their bulk parent phases exhibit fundamentally different stacking patterns
of 1T′ layers, including the bulk 1T′, 1Td, and 2M polymorphs associated with
distinct interlayer couplings.[11, 12] For instance, bulk 1Td-WTe2 adopts a non-
centrosymmetric orthorhombic stacking of relatively weakly coupled 1T′ layers (space
group Pmn21),[13] whereas 2M-WS2, the bulk phase of 1T′-WS2, crystallizes in a base-
centered monoclinic structure (space group C2/m) with two 1T′ layers per unit cell.[14]
Adjacent layers are related by an (a+b)/2 translation, where a and b denote lattice
vectors, but remain tightly stacked, forming a centrosymmetric bilayer motif (Fig. 1b).
Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) and first-principles calculations

electronic dispersion rather than quasi-2D behavior.[12] Consistently, bulk 2M-WS2
stays metallic and becomes superconducting at low temperature even in samples as

transport on high-quality 1T′-WS2 monolayers remains elusive, and the intrinsic insu-

than pursuing cleaner monolayers with physical exfoliation, we introduce a chemical
route: using neutral-molecule intercalation as an internal spacer to suppress interlayer
coupling and recover monolayer-like electronic behavior within bulk crystals.

Neutral-molecule intercalation has a long history. Since the 1970s, TaS2 has been
known to spontaneously intercalate Lewis-basic molecules, forming superlattices of
intact TMD layers separated by insulating molecular spacers.[19] This chemistry
enabled the early discovery of quasi-2D superconductivity prior to the development of
mechanical exfoliation,[20, 21] establishing a chemical route to electronically decoupled
layered systems. However, such intercalation chemistry has largely been confined to
metallic group IVB/VB TMDs, whereas group VIB compounds such as 2H-MoS2 and
3

2H-WS2 are generally considered inert toward neutral amines.[22] More recently, some
amine-intercalated 1T′-MoS2 nanoparticles derived from (NH4)2MoS4 precursors have
been reported for chiral transport and electrocatalytic applications,[23] hinting that
metastable group VIB systems may exhibit a broader intercalation chemistry than
previously recognized. The metastable 2M-WS2, recently isolated by wet-chemical
synthesis,[14, 24] is metallic and therefore a plausible host, yet its intercalation
chemistry remains unexplored.
In this work, we intercalate 2M-WS2 with a series of neutral amines, namely R-, S-,
and rac-methylbenzylamine (R/S/rac-MBA; rac denotes the racemate), n-hexylamine
(HexA), and n-dodecylamine (DDA), forming superlattices of electronically decoupled
yet chemically intact 1T′-WS2 layers (Fig. 1c). The intercalated phases are insulating,
consistent with the ground state predicted for the monolayer, and intercalation with
chiral amines imprints chiroptical activity onto the WS2 layers, providing a chemical
handle for tuning electronic chirality. More broadly, these results demonstrate how
wet-chemical intercalation can engineer van der Waals (vdW) heterostructures with
tunable properties through host-guest interfacial proximity effects.[25]
2 Results
2.1 Synthesis and Chemical Characterization
WS2-amine hybrids were obtained by soaking 2M-WS2 crystals in anhydrous amines
under heating for two weeks (Fig. 2a). The WS2 stoichiometry before and after
intercalation was determined by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spec-
troscopy (ICP-OES, Table S1) and corroborated by scanning electron microscopy and
scanning-transmission electron microscopy with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy
(SEM/STEM-EDS, Fig. S12 to S18). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA, Extended
Data Fig. 1) indicates amine contents of approximately 0.5 molecules per W atom
for all intercalates except HexA (0.3, see Methods), consistent with nearly complete
intercalation.[22] Correspondingly, the low-angle (h00) reflections in powder X-ray
diffraction (PXRD) reveal substantially expanded interlayer spacings along the a
axis (Fig. 2b; for example, from 5.9 to 17.2 ˚A for MBA), matching the expansion
expected for an amine-bilayer arrangement[26] and supporting the formation of fully
intercalated phases.[27]
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) shows no significant shifts in the W and
S core levels, excluding substantial reductive charge transfer from the amines to
WS2 during intercalation (Fig. 2e, S8, Extended Data Fig. 2c). Differential scanning
calorimetry (DSC) reveals the characteristic 1T′/2M-to-2H phase transition (Fig. 2f,
S21, Extended Data Fig. 2d),[24] confirming that the metastable 1T′-WS2 framework
is preserved. The elevated transition temperatures of the intercalated phases point
to enhanced thermal stability, consistent with earlier work on mono- and few-layer
1T′-WS2,[10] and indicate that the mild wet-chemical treatment does not intro-
duce substantial defects, which would otherwise lower the transition temperature.[24]
Raman spectra retain the characteristic modes of 1T′/2M-WS2 but show several
peak shifts, including a pronounced red shift of the mode near 120 cm−1 (Fig. 2d,

2.2 Structural Characterization at Atomic Resolution
Despite the long history of molecular intercalation, only recently have amine-
intercalated TMDs crystals such as 1T-TiS2,[26, 32] and 2H-TaS2[33] been examined
by HRSTEM. Since these hosts are trigonal or hexagonal, previous studies have
focused mainly on interlayer expansion. For monoclinic 2M-WS2, we instead find a
much larger structural reconstruction upon intercalation, in which sliding of individ-
ual layers does not necessarily introduce disorder but effectively resets the interlayer
symmetry (Fig. 3a).
Cross-sectional high-angle annular dark-field STEM (HAADF-STEM) images
taken along the b-axis clearly resolve the ordered W dimers of the zig-zag W chains in
the bc plane, for both pristine and intercalated phases, confirming that the intralayer
1T′-WS2 framework remains intact at atomic resolution (Fig. 3b, c). The images also
show expanded interlayer spacings of approximately 17 ˚A along the a-axis, consistent
with electron diffraction (Fig. 3d) and PXRD (Fig. 2b). More importantly, the stack-
ing changes markedly upon intercalation: the monoclinic β angle decreases from 114◦
in pristine 2M-WS2 to nearly orthogonal (93◦) in the intercalated phases (Fig. 3b, c),
indicating substantial interlayer shifts along the c-axis. The same behavior is seen,
even more clearly, in WS2(HexA)0.3, despite different interlayer expansion (Fig. S20).
The stacking rearrangement also has a direct, visible consequence in plan-view
imaging. In pristine 2M-WS2 with C2/m symmetry, AB stacking offsets the zig-zag
W chains of neighboring layers such that, along the a∗projection (perpendicular to
the bc plane), chains from successive layers overlap rather than appearing as sepa-
rated features (Fig. 3e, S19). By contrast, in an isolated 1T′ layer (P21/m, Fig. 3f),
the W chains project as well-separated parallel lines. The intercalated superlattices
exhibit this monolayer-like pattern of clearly separated parallel chains across the entire
flake and even in multilayer regions (Fig. 3g,h),[34] suggesting that layers are aligned
directly above one another. This monolayer-like bulk structure indicates substantial
layer shifts along both the b- and c-axes, together with a pronounced reconstruction
of the interlayer registry (Fig. 3a).
The symmetry lowering from C2/m to P21/m suggested by PXRD refinements is
also confirmed by in-plane electron diffraction. Diffraction spots that are forbidden in
C2/m (odd k, Fig. 3i) but allowed in P21/m (where only 0k0 reflections with odd k are
forbidden, Fig. 3j) emerge in the intercalated phases (Fig. 3l), closely matching those
of the monolayer (Fig. 3k). We note that P21/m is also the space group of monoclinic
1T′-MoTe2,[35] a prototypical weakly coupled 1T′ layered system. [12]

Overall, molecular intercalation reconstructs the interlayer stacking while pre-

in pristine 2M-WS2, and the formation of weakly interacting WS2-molecule units that
favor near-parallel layer alignment. Because the WS2 layers stay intact, these materials
let us probe monolayer-like physical properties within bulk single crystals.
2.3 Physical Characterization
Four-probe transport was measured on single crystals of pristine 2M-WS2 and interca-
lated WS2 superlattices (Fig. 4a, Extended Data Fig. 3a). Pristine 2M-WS2 is metallic,
with resistance decreasing upon cooling and dropping to zero at 8.7 K as superconduc-
tivity emerges. Its residual resistance ratio (RRR, R300 K/R9 K) reaches 91, confirming
the high crystalline quality of the parent phase. In contrast, all amine-intercalated
samples are insulating (dR/dT < 0), with resistance increasing upon cooling and no

superlattices are nonmagnetic with no detectable superconductivity (Fig. 4b, Extended

not caused by permanent defects or chemical decomposition. Consistently, partially
intercalated and de-intercalated samples that contain mixed pristine and interca-
lated phases also retain superconductivity (Extended Data Fig. 4, 5), indicating that
superconductivity does not require perfect bulk 2M-WS2 stacking and can survive in
regions of only a few imperfectly stacked 1T′-WS2 layers, in line with earlier reports
of superconductivity in films restacked from 1T′-WS2 monolayer inks.[34] There-
fore, the insulating behavior of the fully intercalated superlattices most likely arises
from the increased interlayer separation, which electronically isolates the 1T′-WS2
layers and suppresses interlayer coupling. Molecular intercalation thus drives an elec-
tronic crossover from a metallic superconducting state to an electronically decoupled,
monolayer-like insulating state.
Notably, the reversible behavior we observe differs from the recently reported
amine-intercalated TiSe2 system,[36] where sacrificial intercalants thermally decom-
pose, irreversibly resulting in a new superconducting phase after deintercalation. This
behavior is plausibly linked to the chemical sensitivity of TiSe2,[37–39] in which decom-
position and side reactions with amines can accompany intercalation. By contrast,
2M-WS2 is exceptionally robust: it can be synthesized under strongly oxidative acidic

conditions with chromate in air[9, 14, 24] and has been used as an electrode for aqueous
potassium-ion storage.[40] Consistent with this robustness, the 2M-WS2 intercalation
reactions maintain colorless solutions throughout, in contrast to the brown coloration
observed for the TiSe2 system, indicating the absence of comparable degradation
pathways. Thus, this framework stability supports the reversible changes in physical
properties arising primarily from intercalation-induced stacking rearrangements rather
than irreversible chemical transformation.
Together, these results show that molecular intercalation suppresses the metal-
lic state of bulk 2M-WS2, and that the resulting insulating superlattices match
the insulating ground state predicted for isolated monolayer 1T′-WS2.[5, 16] The
chemical robustness of this system makes amine-intercalated WS2 superlattices an
attractive platform for probing intrinsic electronic states without complications from
environmental degradation.
2.4 Theoretical Calculations
To understand the electronic structure of the superlattices, we carried out density func-
tional theory (DFT) calculations on monolayer, bilayer, and intercalated 1T′-WS2.
Modeling bulky chiral amines requires large supercells, leading to significant compu-
tational costs; more importantly, low-symmetry intercalants lower the space group to
P1, eliminating the inversion center required for evaluating the topological indices.
We therefore constructed a WS2-NH3 model in which NH3 molecules are placed near
the WS2 layers while the interlayer spacing is fixed to the experimental value (∼17 ˚A)
refined from WS2(rac-MBA)0.5 (Fig. S7). This model reproduces the experimental
decoupling while preserving P21/m symmetry and its inversion center. In agreement
with previous work,[5, 16, 41] the monolayer is gapped, whereas the bilayer, the
structural unit of 2M-WS2, is metallic (Fig. 5a, b). For the WS2-NH3 superlattice,
intercalation restores a gapped band structure close to that of the monolayer (Fig. 5c),
with molecular states lying well below the Fermi level and contributing negligibly near
the gap (Extended Data Fig. 6). The gap persists in the lower-symmetry WS2-R-MBA
model (P1, Fig. S26), and charge-density analysis shows negligible charge transfer
between molecules and layers (Fig. S27), consistent with experimental characteriza-
tion. Because the decoupled layers behave as independent two-dimensional sheets, we
evaluate the 2D Z2 invariant of a single layer, which remains nontrivial (Z2 = 1), indi-
cating that the WS2-NH3 superlattice preserves the quantum spin Hall character of
the monolayer (Fig. 5a, c).[5]

2.5 Chiral Intercalation
Up to this point, the intercalation studied here is essentially non-destructive to the
WS2 host, preserving the local chemical environment and the crystallographic sym-
metry of the layers, aside from the spatial rearrangements described above. However,
beyond acting as electronically inert spacers, chiral intercalants have previously been
shown to induce chiral transport in R/S-MBA-intercalated TaS2,[33, 42] TiS2,[32] and
MoS2.[23] We therefore examine the host-guest interaction more closely, focusing on

Free R/S-MBA molecules show circular dichroism (CD) only below 280 nm
(Fig. 6a). In contrast, exfoliated WS2(R/S-MBA)0.5 dispersions in isopropanol (IPA)
exhibit pronounced CD across the entire measured range (Fig. 6b), closely follow-
ing the broad absorption features of 1T′-WS2 (Fig. 6c). The chiral amines therefore
imprint a chiroptical response onto the electronic transitions of otherwise achiral WS2
layers. Similar chiroptical responses imparted by chiral molecules to inorganic lay-
ers have also been observed in covalently modified TiNCl[43] and amine-intercalated
1T-TiS2.[32] Two controls confirm that this response is intrinsic rather than arising
from scattering or linear dichroism: (1) the R and S samples produce mirror-image
CD spectra (Fig. 6b), whereas the achiral intercalates, WS2(rac-MBA/DDA/HexA)x,
and pristine 2M-WS2 show similar absorption but no measurable CD signal (Fig. 6b,
Extended Data Fig. 7d); (2) the alternating positive and negative CD features,
together with the offsets between CD and absorption peaks (Extended Data Fig. 7c),
resemble those observed in quantum dots capped with chiral ligands,[44] where they
have been attributed to chiral-induced splitting of excitonic states into sublevels with
opposite angular momentum.[45]
Notably, the inorganic layers themselves appear centrosymmetric: in-plane diffrac-
tion of WS2(R-MBA)0.5 is consistent with P21/m, which retains an inversion center
even after chiral intercalation. Therefore, rather than arising from a chiral WS2
11


## Page 12


lattice, the observed chiral optical responses reflect coupling between the WS2 elec-
tronic transitions and the chiral molecular environment, analogous to the induced CD
observed in achiral quantum dots capped with chiral ligands, where the atomic struc-
ture of the inorganic core remains largely intact.[44, 45] We describe this coupling as
long-distance σ-donation, a weak molecular interaction mediated by epitaxial N coor-
dination that locally perturbs the WS2 electronic states without significant atomic
displacement (Fig. 6d). Such a σ-donation model is consistent with the original pro-
posal for neutral molecular intercalation in 2H-TaS2,[31, 46] where donation from the
nitrogen lone pair to the metal dz2 orbital was proposed based on small shifts in
guest-molecule vibrational frequencies (<50 cm−1),[22, 47] characteristic absorption
spectra,[48] and stoichiometric host-guest molecular ratios.[19] The minimal net charge
transfer and the preserved WS2 framework observed in our system are inconsistent
with later interpretations invoking redox chemistry associated with water or impurity
incorporation.[49–51] Overall, the results of this work support weak σ-donation as the
dominant host-guest interaction, locally modifying the electronic states of WS2 while
preserving the overall lattice structure.
Beyond the local chemistry, these superlattices, built from alternating topological
and insulating layers, realize within a single crystal the vdW topological field-
effect-transistor (vdW-TFET) heterostructure proposed earlier (Fig. 6e).[5] Given the
coexistence of chiral polarization and the 2D TI state in 1T′-WS2, a natural ques-
tion is whether chirality can affect the spin-channel velocity or population of the QSH
state. The abundant interfaces between inorganic layers and chiral spacers are well
suited to proximity effects from chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS)[25, 52, 53] or
Rashba spin-orbit coupling (Fig. 6f).[54, 55] Finally, while mechanical exfoliation of
bulk 2M-WS2 is hindered by strong interlayer interactions, intercalation weakens these
interactions and facilitates exfoliation: preliminary experiments already yield few-
micrometer flakes down to monolayers, opening the way to low-dimensional transport
studies of the interplay between topology and chirality (Extended Data Fig. 8).
3 Conclusion
In conclusion, we establish neutral-molecule intercalation of 2M-WS2 as a wet-chemical
route to electronically decoupled, monolayer-like 1T′-WS2 superlattices. The interca-
lation drives pronounced interlayer expansion and ordered layer shifts while preserving
the intrinsic 1T′-WS2 framework. In contrast to the metallic, superconducting par-
ent 2M phase, the resulting superlattices are insulating and, according to DFT, retain
the topological character expected for the monolayer, thereby providing access to
monolayer-like electronic regimes within bulk crystals.
Beyond serving as a platform for exploring the electronic behavior of monolayers,
these superlattices constitute vdW heterostructures within single crystals, providing
an alternative to mechanically assembled stacks of alternating active and insulating
layers. The host-guest interaction introduces an additional degree of freedom for tuning
the electronic structure through interfacial proximity effects, as demonstrated by the
chirality imprinting. More generally, molecular intercalation of vdW crystals offers
12


## Page 13


a distinct chemical route to engineering emergent electronic functionality in layered
quantum materials.
4 Methods
4.1 Synthesis
2M-WS2 crystals were synthesized by acid treatment of KxWS2 according to pre-
viously reported procedures.[24] The obtained crystals were isolated from the acid
matrix, washed with water and methanol, and dried under vacuum. All amines were
purchased from Oakwood Chemical and stored over activated 4 ˚A molecular sieves
to minimize residual water content. All synthetic procedures were carried out in an
Ar-filled glovebox, and all glassware, including vials and pipettes, was oven-dried at
120 ◦C for at least 48 h prior to transfer into the glovebox. Although the isolated 2M-
WS2 crystals are chemically robust and air-stable, stringent precautions were taken
to minimize exposure to water, oxygen, and other impurities to ensure well-controlled
reactions with the amines.
A typical intercalation procedure for all amines is described below. First, 20 mg of
2M-WS2 crystals in a 24 mL vial were rinsed with 1 mL of the corresponding amine,
and the washing solution was discarded. Subsequently, 4 mL of fresh amine was added,
and the capped vial was sealed with electrical tape and heated at 65 ◦C for 7 days
using a heating block on a temperature-controlled hot plate. The reaction solution was
then removed and replaced with 3 mL of fresh amine, followed by resealing and heating
at 100 ◦C for an additional 7 days. After completion of the reaction, the resulting
crystals were isolated from the liquid and dried under vacuum at room temperature
until no visible residual solvent remained.

of 0.02 M HNO3 solution and shaken gently at 300 rpm for 12 h using a Scilogex
SK-0330-Pro shaker. The resulting de-intercalated crystals were isolated, washed with
water and methanol, and dried in air prior to characterization. Partial de-intercalation
was achieved by limiting the acid treatment time to 2 h.

XPS measurements were performed on a ThermoFisher K-Alpha spectrometer.
Samples were transferred directly from the glovebox into the XPS chamber using
a vacuum-transfer module. Spectra were analyzed and fitted using CasaXPS and
referenced to the adventitious C 1s peak at 284.5 eV.
Raman spectra were collected using a Horiba Raman spectrometer equipped with
a 532 nm laser and a neutral-density filter (ND = 0.6).
Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were acquired in reflection mode on a
Thermo Nicolet 6700 FTIR spectrometer.
SEM images and EDS data were collected using a Quanta 200 FEG environmental
SEM equipped with an Oxford energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer.
Cross-sectional TEM lamellae were cut out of bulk single crystals using a Helios
DualBeam focused ion beam (FIB)/SEM system and thinned to approximately 80 nm

air exposure. Plan-view TEM samples were prepared by Scotch-tape exfoliation and
transferred directly onto Si3N4 TEM grids. SAED and HAADF-STEM images were
collected using a Titan Cubed Themis 300 double-Cs-corrected S/TEM operated at
300 kV, providing a spatial resolution of 0.07 nm and an energy resolution of 0.8 eV.
The microscope is additionally equipped with a Super-X EDS system for elemental
mapping and a Gatan Quantum SE/963 P post-column energy filter for energy-filtered
TEM measurements.
DSC measurements were performed using a PerkinElmer HyperDSC DSC8500
calorimeter. Heating rates of 10 ◦C min−1 were used for WS2(MBA)0.5 samples and
20 ◦C min−1 for WS2(Hex)0.3 and WS2(DDA)0.5 samples. Cooling rates were fixed at
20 ◦C min−1.
TGA measurements were conducted under flowing nitrogen using a PerkinElmer
TGA-8000 thermogravimetric analyzer.

erty Measurement System (PPMS, Quantum Design) using the ETO option. After
mechanical cleavage with adhesive tape (Scotch Magic tape), crystals were fixed to
sapphire substrates with GE varnish and contacted using four gold wires and silver
paint (Fig. S22).

operating in vibrating-sample magnetometer (VSM) mode. Pressed pellets of crystals

of 50 nN. KPFM measurements were performed in sideband KPFM mode with an AC
bias voltage of 1.5 V applied at a modulation frequency of 1.5 to 3 kHz. Mechanical

glovebox.
4.3 Calculations
DFT calculations were performed on proposed crystal structures based on experi-
mental data using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) 6.4.2. [56–59] The
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA)[60] func-
tional was used for exchange-correlation functional, with the recommended potpaw-
PBE.64 projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials provided by VASP.[61, 62]
The calculations used a plane-wave energy cutoff of 550 eV, a Γ-centered 1 × 8 × 8
Monkhorst-Pack k-point grid, [63] and included spin-orbit coupling (SOC); the elec-
tronic self-consistency criterion was 10−6 eV. Band structures and density of states
were calculated using the sumo Python package. [64] Band structures were computed
with full paths (Extended Data Fig. 6) as well as the selected in-plane path connect-
ing Γ = (0, 0, 0) and Y= (0.5, 0.5, 0) for better comparison with previous literature
results.[5, 16].
Topological classification was performed within the framework of topological
quantum chemistry.[65] Irreducible representations of the occupied bands at the
time-reversal-invariant momenta were extracted from the VASP wavefunctions with
vasp2trace and analyzed with the Check Topological Mat. tool of the Bilbao Crystal-
lographic Server, [66–68] yielding the symmetry-indicator invariants, Z2.
