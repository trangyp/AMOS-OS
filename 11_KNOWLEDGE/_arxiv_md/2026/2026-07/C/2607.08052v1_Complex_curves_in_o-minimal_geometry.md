COMPLEX CURVES IN O-MINIMAL GEOMETRY
SPENCER DEMBNER
Abstract. There has recently been considerable progress relating o-
minimality to complex analytic geometry. Yet almost nothing is known
about coherent cohomology or the classification of vector bundles, even
for curves. In Ran and similar structures, we show that cohomology of
noncompact curves is concentrated entirely at punctures. As an applica-
tion, we compute the cohomology of the structure sheaf on the affine line
and describe a connection to Diophantine approximation. Finally, we use
similar techniques to characterize which definable Riemann surfaces have
definable compactifications. The proofs are based on a careful analysis
of boundary behavior for definable holomorphic functions.
1. Introduction
Let S be an o-minimal structure on R. Peterzil and Starchenko [PS10]
developed a theory of definable complex analysis, by replacing holomorphic
functions with S-definable ones. Building on their work, Bakker-Brunebarbe-
Tsimerman [BBT23] introduced definable analytic spaces and definable co-
herent sheaves, proved definable analogues of Oka’s coherence theorem and
of GAGA, and used them to resolve a longstanding conjecture of Griffiths
about the images of period maps in Hodge theory.
It is tempting to define the cohomology of a definable sheaf by simply
taking derived functors of the definable global sections functor. But this has
hardly been considered for coherent sheaves, because it appears completely
pathological: In the usual structure Ran,exp even the unit disk D has nontrivial
cohomology, and in any o-minimal structure the punctured disk D∗does. This
diverges sharply from ordinary complex geometry, since coherent cohomology
on noncompact Riemann surfaces always vanishes.
Even so, in this paper we show that in many o-minimal structures, the
cohomology of curves is well-behaved. Call S branching if, roughly speaking,
all singularities of one-variable definable holomorphic functions are branch
points. Examples, as we will show later, include Ran, RK
an, and Ran∗.
Theorem A. Let S be a branching o-minimal structure that contains Ran, let
Y be an irreducible compact complex curve, and let X ⊊Y be a noncompact
definable open subset whose normalization has no punctures. Then:
(1) Every definable coherent sheaf on X has vanishing higher cohomology.
(2) Every definable vector bundle on X is definably trivial.

One could justifiably wonder how general these theorems are: Concretely,
given a definable Riemann surface X, does it definably embed as an open
subset of a compact Riemann surface Y ? For ordinary Riemann surfaces, the
answer is essentially yes: By a theorem of Stout [Sto65, Theorem 8.1], every
finite-type Riemann surface embeds into a compact one, and we can even
require its complement to be a union of points and disks.
In the definable setting, things are more complicated. We will see that
there exist “twisted forms” of the punctured disk D∗, whose puncture cannot
be definably filled due to a monodromy obstruction. There is also a subtler
obstruction, coming from the fact that definable holomorphic functions
(usually) extend continuously to the boundary: There could be an end of Xan
which analytically is a disk, but which definably “appears” as a puncture,
and such an end cannot be definably compactified.

2. Some definable topology
Here is an impressionistic outline for the proof of Theorem A:
(1) Cohomology classes are represented by Čech cocycles. Fix a cocycle
s with transition functions sI.
(2) For a fixed boundary point x ∈∂X, use properties of the structure S
to show that the sI extend globally near x. It follows that s is trivial
near x.
(3) Therefore, there exists a cover (Vi) of ∂X such that s is trivial on
(Vi ∩X). We will see that s extends holomorphically past almost all
points of ∂X ∩Vi.
(4) We can refine (Vi) to eliminate the finitely many singular points,
making the sI overconvergent. Thus, s extends past the boundary.
(5) Since S contains Ran, any trivialization of s on a larger domain
restricts definably to X.
In this section, we isolate the purely topological aspects of this argument,
since they also play a role in Section 7. First, we review the definition and
basic properties of o-minimal sheaf cohomology.
2.1. Definable sheaf cohomology. Given a definable space X, we would
like to define sheaves of definable continuous functions on X. In general,
definable functions do not satisfy gluing for the Euclidean topology: For
example, in Ran, every analytic function is “locally” definable for the Euclidean
topology, but not globally. On the other hand, suppose the cover (Ui) is a
finite cover by definable open sets. Then definable functions do satisfy the
sheaf condition, simply because a finite union of definable sets is definable.
In this section we explain this notion of a definable sheaf, the corresponding
notion of sheaf cohomology, and some of its basic properties.
O-minimal sheaves were first considered by Pillay [Pil88], and o-minimal
sheaf cohomology was defined by Edmundo, Jones, and Peatfield [EJP06].
Their results are stated only for definable sets X ⊆Rn, and a priori this is
not quite enough for us: We want to consider for example Riemann surfaces
given by charts. Thus we have the following definition of van den Dries.

3. Boundary behavior of holomorphic functions
Let f : U →C be a definable holomorphic function of one variable. If
z ∈∂U is an isolated singularity, then f is not essential at z. But at a general
point z ∈∂U, its behavior can be more subtle: For example, e1/z has an
essential singularity and is Ran,exp-definable on an open set near zero, though
not on a punctured neighborhood. The proofs of our main theorems will
require a detailed understanding of such phenomena, because they proceed
essentially by reduction to the boundary.
In this section, we survey the boundary properties of definable holomorphic
functions. As we will indicate, many of these results are due to Peterzil–
Starchenko and Wilkie, while others appear to be original. We also introduce
the class of branching structures which form the context for the rest of the
paper, and prove some basic properties.
3.1. Continuity at the boundary. The first observation we will need is
the following, which slightly adapts a theorem of Peterzil and Starchenko. It
says definable holomorphic functions extend continuously to their boundary
unless there is a topological obstruction.

open set connected at x ∈∂U, and let f : U →Y be a definable holomorphic
map to a definable compact Riemann surface (for example, P1). Then f has
a unique limiting value at x. If U is boundary-connected, then there exists a
definable continuous extension
f : U →Y.
