I.
INTRODUCTION
In addition to conventional baryons and mesons, QCD al-
lows any color-neutral hadronic state to exist, including mul-
tiquark states (tetraquarks and pentaquarks) [1, 2]. Since the
ﬁrst experimental report of the hidden-charm tetraquark can-
didate X(3872) [3] in 2003, subsequent experiments have re-
vealed many more tetraquark candidates. The most represen-
tative ones include the ﬁrst charged tetraquark Zc(3900) [4],
the ﬁrst open-heavy-ﬂavor tetraquark candidate Tcc(3875) [5],
as well as the pentaquark Pc states [6–10]. Meanwhile, the-
oretical discussions on the internal structures of these states
have arisen, focusing mainly on two distinct pictures: the
molecular conﬁguration and the compact multiquark conﬁg-
uration [11–29].

Another insight comes from the bag conﬁne-

single conﬁned object (i.e., a compact state)?
We propose a uniﬁed variational formulation of the bag
model that incorporates chromoelectric interactions between
heavy quarks. First, using this uniﬁed variational framework,
we reﬁne the results for doubly heavy baryons previously
obtained with perturbative methods, and further predict the
masses of fully heavy baryons. Second, we estimate the con-
ﬁnement energies of tetraquark systems with diﬀerent num-
bers of light quarks. Our results show that the bag conﬁne-
ment energy exhibits a strong dependence on the number of
light quarks; for the cqqq (q = u, d, s) system, it is already
very close to the bag conﬁnement limit. Subsequently, tak-
ing into account both chromoelectric and chromomagnetic in-
teractions, we calculate the mass spectra of the nQ¯n¯n, nQ¯s¯n,
¯Q¯snn, sQ¯s¯n, and sQ¯s¯s systems, and analyze their potential to
form compact structures as well as their strong stability.

the calculated mass spectra of singly heavy tetraquark states,
along with a discussion of their compactness potential and
strong decay properties. Finally, Sec. IV gives a brief sum-
mary of the computational results and analyses.

B.
Variational form of the CEI
In the mean-ﬁeld description of the MIT bag model, the
long-range conﬁnement of quarks is captured by the vacuum
pressure and the Casimir energy, but the short-range dynam-
ics among quarks is not fully accounted for. This is partic-
ularly relevant for systems containing heavy quarks, where
the short-range chromoelectric interaction may need to be in-
cluded as an additional term beyond the mean-ﬁeld approx-
imation. In other non-perturbative approaches, such eﬀects
have been parametrized through short-range binding energies
between heavy quarks to reconcile the baryon and meson
spectra [68–73]. It was also observed that, for a given ﬂa-
vor conﬁguration, the short-range binding energy in baryons
is half of that in mesons, a relation that follows directly from
the ratio of their color factors. The short-range interaction be-
tween heavy quarks is additionally enhanced relative to that
between light quarks, and this enhancement arises from the
chromoelectric interaction (CEI).

C.
The bag conﬁnement limit and compact stability
There are various models for discussing conﬁnement, such
as the lattice QCD string/ﬂux-tube picture [48–50, 77], the
soliton bag model [78], the cloudy bag model [79], the NJL
model [80], holographic QCD [81], and the dual supercon-
ductor model [82], each of which describes the mechanism of
quark conﬁnement from its own perspective. In the MIT bag
model, the long-range binding of quarks is encoded in the bag
conﬁnement energy ECON, which consists of two competing
contributions [56, 57]
ECON = 4
3πR3B −Z0
R ,
(16)
where the ﬁrst term is the volume energy, originating from
the diﬀerence in vacuum energy density between the interior
and exterior of the bag, and the second term is the Casimir
zero-point energy, which opposes the expansion of the bag
and thus plays a stabilizing role [60, 61]. The parameters B
and Z0 are determined by ﬁtting the masses, magnetic mo-
ments, and charge radii of light-ﬂavor hadrons [62]. In the
variational procedure, the volume energy tends to enlarge the
bag, while the Casimir term counteracts this expansion; their
balance determines the optimal bag radius R0 that minimizes
the total mass.
From the perspective of conﬁnement, the sign of ECON has
a clear physical meaning: when ECON < 0, the bag experi-
ences an eﬀective binding potential and can maintain a stable
compact conﬁguration; conversely, if ECON > 0, the bag is
no longer energetically bound and tends to split into several
color-singlet clusters. Setting ECON = 0 yields the critical
radius

When the bag radius R > Rc, the conﬁnement energy becomes
positive, implying that the conﬁnement mechanism of the bag
model itself breaks down. It is worth noting that this critical
scale is qualitatively consistent with the string-breaking dis-
tance obtained from lattice QCD (about 1.1–1.3 fm) [48–50].
Thus, the conﬁnement limit of the bag model can be viewed as
another description of the same nonperturbative energy scale

Here Rc serves as the upper bound for compact multiquark
states. Conventional hadrons—mesons and baryons—all have
equilibrium bag radii well below Rc [63]. For tetraquark sys-
tems, however, the bag radius depends sensitively on the num-
ber of light quarks, since light quarks carry larger kinetic ener-
gies and tend to expand the bag. As shown in Table IV, even
before including chromoelectric and chromomagnetic inter-
actions, the bag radius of the cqqq system already approaches
Rc. Once full interactions are switched on, some states may
exceed this limit and thus lose the potential to form compact
structures.
It should be clariﬁed that a negative conﬁnement energy
ECON < 0 may be a necessary but not suﬃcient condition
for the existence of a compact tetraquark. A system satisfy-
ing ECON < 0 could still manifest as a molecular state rather
than a single conﬁned bag, depending on the actual dynam-
ics and the hadronization process [57]. For instance, the c¯cq¯q
system, even if it has the potential to be compact, cannot be
ruled out as a D ¯D molecule. In the bag picture, a compact
multiquark state is a single conﬁned entity containing multi-
ple valence quarks, whereas a molecular state consists of two
or more color-singlet hadrons interacting weakly. These two
scenarios are not mutually exclusive.
In this work, we use ECON as a primary criterion to as-
sess whether a singly heavy tetraquark state has the poten-
tial to form a compact structure. States with ECON < 0 and
R0 < Rc are considered as compact candidates, while those
with ECON > 0 are disfavored.

SUMMARY
In this work, based on the MIT bag model, we construct a
uniﬁed variational formulation that incorporates a short-range
chromoelectric interaction (CEI) explicitly dependent on the
bag radius R. Using this model, we ﬁrst verify the mass spec-
tra of singly, doubly, and fully heavy baryons (Table II), ﬁnd-
ing good agreement with lattice QCD results and experimental
data.
Furthermore, we calculate the mass spectra of the singly
heavy tetraquark states nQ¯n¯n, nQ¯s¯n, Q¯s¯n¯n, sQ¯s¯n, and sQ¯s¯s,
and analyze their stability against strong decays.
In the
calculations, we consider two color representations, namely
6c ⊗¯6c/¯3c ⊗3c and 8c ⊗8c/1c ⊗1c, and the mass spectra ob-
tained from these two representations are strictly consistent.
Subsequently, we analyze the S -wave strong decay stability
by evaluating the weight of the color-singlet component in the
eigenstates of the 8c ⊗8c/1c ⊗1c representation, and provide
the partial width ratios.
We use the bag conﬁnement energy ECON as a criterion for
judging the formation of compact structures and analyze the
compactness potential of each system. For tetraquark systems,
the conﬁnement energy exhibits a strong dependence on the
number of light quarks. In particular, for the singly charmed
tetraquark system, the bag radius has already approached the
critical region near the conﬁnement limit Rc = 5.615 GeV−1.
Our results indicate that some singly heavy tetraquark states,
especially the high-spin S = 2 states in the singly charmed
system, have positive conﬁnement energies, which makes it
diﬃcult for them to form compact structures. Nonetheless,
some states still possess the potential to form compact struc-
tures. For the singly bottom tetraquark system, due to the
suppression eﬀect of the bottom quark on the bag radius, the
compactness is enhanced.
Our results indicate that for singly heavy systems, the
masses tend to lie above their corresponding lowest thresh-
olds, and such states are generally unstable against strong de-
cays. In the nc¯s¯n system, the state Tc¯sn¯n(0+, 2.925) (the second
state in Table VII) has a mass of 2.925 GeV, which is close
to the experimentally observed T a
c¯s0(2900) (M = 2908 MeV,
JP = 0+). Its dominant decay channel is Dsπ, with k · |ci|2 =
211 MeV, a value comparable in magnitude to the large ex-
perimental width of T a
c¯s0(2900) (Γ = 136 MeV). We therefore
suggest that T a
c¯s0(2900) could be a candidate for a compact
singly heavy tetraquark state, with its large decay width nat-
urally understood as arising from an OZI-superallowed decay
mechanis.
Using the MIT bag model, we systematically predict
the mass spectra and decay properties of various singly
heavy tetraquark states, and identify a set of candidates
with signiﬁcant potential to form compact structures (e.g.,
Tnc¯s¯n(0+, 2.472), Tnc¯s¯n(1+, 2.624), Tsb¯s¯n(1+, 6.523), etc.). In
our model, the conﬁnement energy is obtained under the
spherical-cavity approximation, which provides a macro-

a deeper understanding of the model and further investigation
of the quark–quark interactions.
