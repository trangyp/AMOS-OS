Channel Estimation and Beamforming for
Microwave Linear Analog Computers
(MiLACs)-Aided Multiuser MISO Systems
Qiaosen Zhang, Matteo Nerini, Senior Member, IEEE, Bruno Clerckx, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Microwave linear analog computers (MiLACs) have
recently gained attention for future gigantic multiple-input
multiple-output (MIMO) systems by enabling beamforming with
greatly reduced hardware and computational cost. However,
channel estimation for MiLAC-aided multiuser systems remains
an open problem. Conventional channel estimation requires many
radio-frequency (RF) chains to access full-dimensional received
signals, followed by massive digital processing, which undermines
the advantages of MiLAC-aided systems in reducing the number
of RF chains and computational complexity. In this paper,
we propose computationally efficient channel estimation and
beamforming schemes for MiLAC-aided multiuser multiple-input
single-output (MU-MISO) systems with a limited number of RF
chains. We consider the general case where different user groups
experience different channel correlation matrices. By exploiting
the rank deficiency of these matrices, the proposed schemes use
MiLAC to compress the full-dimensional received signals in the
analog domain, making them compatible with the available RF
chains while preserving the essential channel information. Then,
in the digital domain, only low-dimensional channel estimation is
performed based on these compressed observations, substantially
reducing computational cost. We further show how regularized
zero-forcing beamforming (R-ZFBF) can be efficiently realized
from the low-dimensional channel estimates through a cascade
of two MiLACs, which offers greater computational flexibility
than a single MiLAC. Numerical results show that the proposed
schemes reduce computational complexity up to 1540× and
16108×, for channel estimation and beamforming, respectively,
while achieving performance comparable to digital baselines.
Index Terms—Channel estimation, Beamforming, microwave
linear analog computer (MiLAC), multi-user multiple-input
single-output (MU-MISO).

Uture wireless networks are expected to support ex-

reliability. To meet these requirements, gigantic multiple-input
multiple-output (MIMO) with up to thousands of antennas
has emerged as a promising paradigm beyond massive MIMO
[1], [2]. By substantially enlarging the antenna array, gigantic
MIMO can provide enhanced spatial multiplexing, beam-
forming gains, and ultra-massive connectivity. However, such
scaling makes conventional digital beamforming increasingly

impractical. In particular, it requires one dedicated radio-
frequency (RF) chain per antenna and incurs computational
complexity that scales rapidly with the array size, leading to
prohibitive hardware cost, power consumption, and latency.
To address this issue, recent research has pivoted toward
alternative beamforming strategies that offload intensive com-
putations from the digital domain to the analog domain. In
this context, microwave linear analog computer (MiLAC) has
been proposed in [3], [4]. A MiLAC is a multiport microwave
network composed of tunable admittance components that
process microwave signals in the analog domain. Once the
admittance components are configured, MiLAC realizes the
desired beamforming matrix. When an input signal is applied,
MiLAC directly generates the corresponding beamforming
output through wave propagation inside the network, without
requiring any digital matrix-vector multiplication. As a result,
MiLAC-aided beamforming provides flexibility comparable to
digital beamforming, while requiring only a minimal number
of RF chains, supporting the use of low-resolution analog-
to-digital/digital-to-analog converters (ADCs/DACs), and sub-
stantially reducing computational complexity.
Existing studies on MiLAC have mainly focused on mod-
elling [3], beamforming design [4]–[8], architecture design
[9], [10], and prototyping [11]. Specifically, the concept and
modelling of MiLAC were first introduced in [3], followed
by its application to computationally efficient beamforming in
[4]. By further imposing lossless and reciprocal constraints
on MiLAC, beamforming designs for point-to-point MIMO
systems to achieve capacity and for multiuser multiple-input
single-output (MU-MISO) systems to maximize sum rate were
investigated in [5]–[8]. Reduced-complexity and two-layer
MiLAC architectures were studied in [9], [10]. In addition,
a hardware prototype based on hybrid couplers and phase
shifters was developed in [11].
Although the above-mentioned works [4]–[10] rely heavily
on accurate channel state information (CSI) to configure the
tunable admittance components in MiLAC, none of them
addresses channel estimation for MiLAC-aided systems. Only
one recent work [12] developed computationally efficient
channel estimation schemes for MiLAC-aided systems. How-
ever, it focused on point-to-point MIMO systems and assumed
that the number of transmit and receive RF chains equals the
number of transmit and receive antennas, respectively. This
represents an important first step, but remains limited in scope.
To fully exploit the potential of MiLAC-aided beamforming
in gigantic MIMO, it is essential to acquire accurate CSI for

multiuser (MU) systems while preserving the key advantages
of MiLAC, namely low computational complexity and a
minimal number of RF chains. However, this is non-trivial
due to two key challenges. First, there is a mismatch between
the operation that can be efficiently computed by MiLAC and
the operations required by conventional channel estimation.
Specifically, MiLAC is well suited for beamforming because,
once configured, it can apply an instantaneous linear trans-
formation to an input signal and generate the corresponding
beamforming output directly in the analog domain. However,
conventional channel estimation in MU systems requires a
different type of processing. The base station (BS) first collects
the received signals from all users at the M BS antennas
over Tu training symbol durations to form the received signal
matrix Y ∈CM×Tu. Then, the BS post-multiplies Y by
the conjugate of user-specific training sequences to isolate
the corresponding user channels [13, Section 3.1]. Therefore,
conventional channel estimation requires both storing all re-
ceived signals into Y and post-multiplying it by the training
sequences. These operations are fundamentally different from
the instantaneous linear transformation that can be efficiently
computed by MiLAC. Hence, it remains unclear how to exploit
the computational capability of MiLAC to perform channel
estimation in MU systems while preserving low computational
complexity. Second, the limited number of RF chains pre-
vents the MiLAC-aided BS from directly acquiring the full-
dimensional received signals. Hence, channel estimation must
rely on low-dimensional observations produced by MiLAC,
which makes accurate CSI acquisition challenging.
To address these challenges, in this work, we investigate
computationally efficient channel estimation for MiLAC-aided
MU-MISO systems with a limited number of RF chains, and
further study the corresponding beamforming design based on
the estimated channels. The main contributions are as follows.
First, we present a MiLAC-aided MU-MISO system model
integrating uplink training and downlink beamforming. The
model employs only as many RF chains as users, explicitly
accounting for the limited number of RF chains in MiLAC-
aided systems. It also incorporates different channel correla-
tion matrices across user groups, leading to low-dimensional
channel representations that underpin the proposed channel
estimation and beamforming schemes.
Second, we propose computationally efficient channel es-
timation schemes for the MiLAC-aided MU-MISO system.
The key idea is to leverage the computational capability of
MiLAC to perform analog compression before digital channel
estimation. Specifically, in the analog domain, MiLAC exploits
the rank deficiency of the correlation matrices to compress
the full-dimensional received signals into low-dimensional
observations, thereby preserving essential channel information
while matching the limited number of RF chains. Then, in
the digital domain, only low-dimensional channel represen-
tations are estimated from these compressed observations
using least squares (LS) and minimum mean square error
(MMSE) estimators, avoiding massive digital processing of
full-dimensional received signals. Analytical results show that
the proposed LS and MMSE schemes achieve, respectively,
lower and identical mean square error (MSE) compared with
their digital counterparts, while requiring greatly lower compu-
tational complexity, at the expense of extra training overhead.
Third, we develop MiLAC-aided regularized zero-forcing
beamforming (R-ZFBF) schemes based on low-dimensional
channel estimates. We show that a single MiLAC cannot
realize R-ZFBF from such estimates with low computational
complexity. To address this limitation, we propose a cascade
MiLAC architecture formed by connecting two MiLACs in se-
ries, which enables efficient implementation of R-ZFBF from
low-dimensional channel estimates. Compared with conven-
tional digital R-ZFBF, the proposed schemes are analytically
shown to achieve the same sum rate with significantly reduced
computational complexity. When uplink training overhead is
taken into account, a minor loss in effective sum rate occurs.
Fourth, we provide numerical results to validate the pro-
posed schemes. The results confirm the predicted channel esti-
mation performance and computational complexity reductions
over digital LS and MMSE. They also show that MiLAC-
aided R-ZFBF achieves the same sum rate as digital R-ZFBF
when training overhead is omitted, while greatly reducing
computational complexity. The loss of effective sum rate due
to extra training overhead is further quantified.
Organization: Section II presents the channel model, the
signal model, and the transmission protocol of a MiLAC-

MiLAC-aided channel estimation schemes. Section IV intro-

schemes based on estimated channels. Section V provides nu-

to digital baselines. Section VI concludes this work.

III. MILAC-AIDED CHANNEL ESTIMATION
In this section, we propose computationally efficient channel
estimation schemes for the MiLAC-aided MU-MISO system
with a limited number of RF chains.
A. Training Protocol and Overall Observation Matrix
With only L RF chains available, where L = K, the
MiLAC-aided BS acquires the L-dimensional observation zt at
each symbol duration t ∈Tu, rather than the M-dimensional
received signal yt. To obtain sufficient observations for chan-
nel estimation under this RF-chain constraint, we first intro-
duce a training protocol.
Specifically, following the training approach in [19], [20],
we divide the training phase of Tu symbol durations into F
frames, each consisting of L symbol durations, as illustrated

VI. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose computationally efficient channel
estimation schemes for MiLAC-aided MU-MISO systems with
a limited number of RF chains. Considering users partitioned
into G groups with different correlation matrices, we develop
virtual channel estimation for small G and global virtual
channel estimation for large G. Analytical results show that
the proposed schemes achieve lower LS MSE and the same
MMSE MSE as digital baselines, while greatly reducing com-
putational complexity, at the cost of extra training overhead.
Based on low-dimensional channel estimates, we further
develop MiLAC-aided R-ZFBF schemes. A cascade MiLAC
is introduced to realize the R-ZFBF precoder directly from the
virtual channel for small G or the global virtual channel for
large G, without reconstructing the full-dimensional channel.
Compared with digital R-ZFBF, the proposed schemes are
analytically shown to achieve the same sum rate with greatly
reduced computational complexity, while incurring a minor
sum-rate loss when uplink training overhead is considered.
Finally, numerical results validate the proposed schemes,
confirming the predicted estimation and beamforming perfor-
mance with substantial computational savings. Particularly, the
proposed schemes reduce computational complexity by up to
1540× for channel estimation and 16108× for beamforming.
When training overhead is considered, a trade-off between the
hardware complexity and the effective sum rate is observed.
In this work, we consider a fully-reconfigurable MiLAC
to characterize the fundamental performance and complexity
limits of MiLAC-aided channel estimation and beamforming.
Extending the proposed framework to lossless and reciprocal
MiLACs remains an important direction for future research.
