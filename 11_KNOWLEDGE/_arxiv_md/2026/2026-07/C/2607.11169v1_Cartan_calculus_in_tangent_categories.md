CARTAN CALCULUS IN TANGENT CATEGORIES
LORY AINTABLIAN AND CHRISTIAN BLOHMANN
Abstract. We determine the structure needed in a tangent category in the sense of
Rosick´y and Cockett-Cruttwell to construct the Cartan calculus on all objects.
The
missing ingredient is a scalar multiplication by a commutative ring object R, playing
the role of the smooth real line, which equips the tangent bundle of every object with
the structure of an R-module compatible with the tangent structure.
We show that
under these axioms the Lie algebra of vector fields acts by derivations on the ring of
R-valued functions and satisfies the Leibniz rule. In other words, the tangent bundle is
an abstract Lie algebroid, so that the Lie algebra of vector fields is a Lie-Rinehart algebra
over the ring of functions. Consequently, every object carries a Cartan calculus of Lie-
Rinehart forms, given by the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex together with its differential,
inner derivative, and Lie derivative. Examples include the tangent categories of smooth
manifolds, G-manifolds, Lie groupoids, log manifolds, pro-manifolds, elastic diffeological
spaces, affine and general schemes, graded manifolds, and affine C∞-schemes.

1. Introduction
A considerable part of the local computations in differential geometry is carried out in
terms of the Lie algebra of vector fields, the graded commutative algebra of differential
forms, the de Rham differential, the inner derivative, and the Lie derivative. On a smooth
manifold, these structures together with their algebraic relations form the Cartan calculus
(Definition 6.5).
The purpose of this paper is to determine the structure needed in a tangent category
to construct the Cartan calculus on all objects.
A tangent category, in the sense of
Rosick´y [Ros84] and further developed by Cockett and Cruttwell [CC14], axiomatizes the
tangent functor and the natural transformations that are needed to abstractly define the
Lie bracket of vector fields. What was missing is a scalar multiplication by a ring object
R in the category satisfying the appropriate axioms to allow for the construction of the
Cartan calculus.

What are these axioms? It is rather obvious that the tangent bundle TX →X should
be equipped with the structure of an R-module in the slice category over X. Rosick´y
remarks as much in his original paper [Ros84]. It is also clear that we have to require
that TR ∼= R × R as R-modules, so that we have a projection onto the fiber of TR →R,
which is needed for the definition of the differential of a function. Similarly, we should
require the vertical lift to be R-linear (cf. [CL23]). It is not clear, however, what further
compatibility conditions are needed to ensure that vector fields act by derivations on the
ring of R-valued functions and that the Lie bracket satisfies the Leibniz rule.

The concept of scalar multiplication of Definition 4.20 and the results of this paper
apply to a wide range of examples (Section 7), including the Cartan calculus of
(1) smooth vector fields and de Rham forms on a smooth manifold,
(2) G-equivariant vector fields and forms on a G-manifold,
(3) multiplicative vector fields and multiplicative forms on a Lie groupoid,
(4) log vector fields and log forms on a log manifold,
(5) pro-vector fields and ind-forms on a pro-manifold such as the infinite jet manifold,

used in Lagrangian Field Theory,

(8) degree zero vector fields and forms on a graded manifold,
(9) C∞-derivations and forms on an affine C∞-scheme.
The list is not exhaustive and can easily be extended.
1.1. Future directions. In several of the examples listed above, the Lie-Rinehart forms
as defined in (1.1) are not the kind of forms that are usually considered. This is the
case for non-smooth schemes and C∞-schemes, where the Lie-Rinehart forms are not
the usual K¨ahler forms, and on elastic diffeological spaces, where the Lie-Rinehart forms
are generally not the usual diffeological de Rham forms. It turns out that there is a
smaller Cartan calculus given by vector fields and cubical forms (called singular forms
in [CLW18]), which are the expected forms in these examples. This is the subject of a
forthcoming paper. In the case of graded manifolds, there is a different issue: The usual
graded Cartan calculus is given by graded vector fields in all degrees and forms with the
total degree given by the sum of the form degree and the additional grading. To obtain
this we have to enrich the tangent structure over graded vector spaces, which is a topic
for future research. Finally, we conjecture that the concept of scalar multiplication can be

1.2. Related work. Tangent categories were introduced by Rosick´y [Ros84] and later
rediscovered and substantially developed by Cockett and Cruttwell [CC14], who broad-
ened the framework by replacing abelian groups with commutative monoids. They are
closely tied to the differential categories arising from the semantics of differential linear
logic. The Cartesian differential categories of Blute, Cockett, and Seely [BCS09] axioma-
tize the directional derivative of multivariable calculus and coincide with the differential
objects of a tangent category, while their integral counterpart was studied by Cockett
and Lemay [CL19]. Cockett, Lemay, and Lucyshyn-Wright [CLLW20] showed that the
co-Eilenberg-Moore category of a differential category is a tangent category; in partic-
ular, this exhibits the opposite of the category of commutative rings—the basic setting
of algebraic geometry—as a tangent category. We build on this result to show that the
category of affine schemes is a tangent category with scalar multiplication by the affine
line (Section 7.7).
Two structural results clarify the meaning of the axioms of a tangent category. Le-
ung [Leu17] classified tangent structures as strong monoidal functors out of a category of
Weil algebras, making precise the connection with synthetic differential geometry; building
on this, Garner [Gar18] proved that every tangent category embeds into a representable
one, in which the tangent functor is given by exponentiation by an infinitesimal object.
A substantial body of work develops differential geometry synthetically within an ar-
bitrary tangent category. This includes vector fields and their Lie bracket, whose Ja-
cobi identity was established by Cockett and Cruttwell [CC15]; differential bundles and
fibrations [CC18], the tangent-categorical analogue of vector bundles, further character-
ized by MacAdam [Mac21] and by Ching [Chi24]; connections, together with curvature,
torsion, and the Bianchi identities [CC17]; solutions of differential equations and their
flows [CCL21]; and differential and sector forms, from which Cruttwell and Lucyshyn-
Wright [CLW18] extracted two notions of cohomology.
The framework of tangent categories accommodates a broad range of examples beyond
smooth manifolds. Cruttwell and Lemay [CL23] analyzed differential bundles in commu-
tative algebra and algebraic geometry, identifying them with modules and quasi-coherent
sheaves; Ikonicoff, Lanfranchi, and Lemay [ILL23] exhibited categories of algebras over
an operad as tangent categories, and Lanfranchi studied the resulting operadic geom-
etry [Lan23a] as well as a Grothendieck construction for tangent fibrations [Lan23b].
Vooys [Voo23] showed that ind-categories of tangent categories are again tangent cate-
gories. In the higher-categorical direction, Bauer, Burke, and Ching [BBC21] extended
the theory to ∞-categories, making precise the analogy between Goodwillie’s calculus of
functors and the differential calculus of manifolds.
Most closely related to the present paper are developments concerning Lie theory in
tangent categories. Burke and MacAdam [BM19] introduced involution algebroids as a
tangent-categorical generalization of Lie algebroids. In [AB25] we defined differentiable
groupoid objects and their abstract Lie algebroids, the notion we use here. While vector
fields and forms have each been considered separately, the full algebraic structure of the
Cartan calculus has, to the best of our knowledge, not previously been constructed in the
framework of tangent categories.

2. Abstract tangent structures
2.1. Bundles with algebraic structure. In category theory and related areas of math-
ematics, the most basic meaning of “bundle” is a morphism A →X. The morphism is
often required to have additional good properties and the fibers are equipped with addi-
tional structure. This generalizes various kinds of fiber bundles in geometry. The algebraic
structures we will consider are given by finite product algebraic theories, which we recall.
Definition 2.1. Let T be a small category with finite products (including a terminal
object as empty product). A T-algebra in a category C is a functor T →C that preserves
finite products. A morphism of T-algebras is a natural transformation that preserves
finite products.
Examples of algebraic structures that are T-algebras in the sense of Definition 2.1
are semigroups, monoids, commutative monoids, groups, abelian groups, semirings, rigs,
rings, R-modules, k-vector spaces, R-algebras, and C∞-rings. When T is the theory of,
say, abelian groups, a T-algebra in C is called an abelian group internal to C or in C for
short.
A T-algebra A: T →C/X in the slice category over X ∈C will be called a bundle of
T-algebras over X. If the pullback Ax := ∗×X A: T →C over a point x: ∗→X exists
in C, then Ax is a T-algebra in C, called the fiber over x.

7.2. G-manifolds. Let G be a fixed Lie group. A G-manifold is a smooth manifold M
together with a smooth left G-action ρ: G × M →M [Lee13, Ch. 9]. A morphism is a
smooth G-equivariant map. The tangent functor is given by TM with the induced G-
action T(2)ρ: G×TM →TM. Fiber products of the tangent bundle are equipped with the
diagonal G-action. Since all structure transformations π, 0, +, τ, λ, and κ of the tangent
structure are natural, they are G-equivariant.
We conclude that G-manifolds form a
cartesian tangent category with scalar R-multiplication, in agreement with the general
construction of MacAdam [Mac21, Prop. 4.1] for categories of bundles over a fixed base.
The vector fields of this tangent category are the G-equivariant vector fields; the ring
of scalar functions is Hom(M, R) ∼= C∞(M), and the Lie-Rinehart forms are the G-
invariant de Rham forms Ω(M)G. In geometry, G-invariant vector fields and differential
forms play an important role in symplectic reduction [MW74] and geometric invariant
theory [Kir84]. If G is compact and connected, a standard averaging argument shows
that the inclusion Ω(M)G ,→Ω(M) induces an isomorphism in de Rham cohomology
(see, e.g., [Lee13, Ch. 9]).

7.3. Lie groupoids. Let us consider the category of Lie groupoids and smooth homomor-

7.6. Elastic diffeological spaces. Diffeological spaces are concrete sheaves on the site

7.11. Differentiable stacks. The category of differentiable stacks [BX11] is a 2-category,
so, strictly speaking, it is outside the scope of this paper, but within the scope of the
framework of higher tangent categories developed by Kristine Bauer, Matthew Burke, and
Michael Ching [BBC21]. We would still like to indicate how, conjecturally, differentiable
stacks are equipped with the structure of a (2, 1)-tangent category.
The category of differentiable stacks is equivalent to the (2, 1)-category of Lie groupoids,
right principal groupoid bibundles, and biequivariant diffeomorphisms [Blo08]. In this
category, the usual methods of differentiation on smooth manifolds are at our disposal.
By applying the tangent functor to a Lie groupoid G, we obtain the tangent groupoid
TG. By applying the tangent functor to a G-H bibundle P, we obtain a TG-TH bibundle
TP. The bibundle P is H-principal if and only if the endpoint map of the H-action,
P ×H0 H0 →P ×G0 P is an isomorphism. As is the case for any functor, T preserves
isomorphisms, so if P is right principal, then TP is right principal. Similarly, the natural
transformations of the tangent structure on manifolds induce natural transformations on
Lie groupoids and bibundles. We posit that this equips the category of differentiable stacks
with a higher tangent structure. Verifying this rigorously is a task for future research.
