COMPACTIFYING REAL ANALYTIC FUNCTIONS AND
RESULTING REEB SPACES
NAOKI KITAZAWA
Abstract. We formulate compactifications of continuous maps naturally. We
consider real analytic functions mainly. We are interested in topological prop-
erties and combinatorial ones of explicit resulting maps. For understanding
them, we use their Reeb spaces, being quotient spaces of the spaces of the
domains of the functions and defined by the equivalence relation identifying
two points in same components of their level sets. They are known to be 0- or
1-dimensional (metrizable) cell-complexes, in our situations or more general
certain tame cases.
Reeb spaces have been important in understanding topological properties
and combinatorial ones of functions and spaces roughly, since the last century.
These compactifications have been explicitly studied by the author pre-
viously and recently. We have obtained real algebraic functions whose Reeb
spaces are not so complicated and which seem to be of most natural and sim-
plest. We present new discussions and examples.
1. Introduction.
1.1. Reeb spaces? The Reeb spaces Rc of continuous functions c : X →Y where
Y is a real number field R (the case of real-valued functions) or the unit circle
S1 := {(x1, x2) ∈R2 | x12 + x22 = 1} ⊂R2 (the case of circle-valued functions)
on topological spaces X are fundamental and strong tools in understanding X and
topological properties and combinatorial properties of the functions. [23] is one of
related pioneering papers.
We give its rigorous definition. Let c : X →Y be a continuous map. We have the
equivalence relation ∼c on X and the quotient space Rc := X/∼c: for x1, x2 ∈X,
x1∼cx2 if and only if they are in a same connected component of a preimage c−1(y).
A level set of the function c : X →Y := R, S1 is a preimage c−1(y). A contour
of c is a connected component of a preimage of of c. We have the quotient map
qc : X →Rc and the unique continuous map ¯c : Rc →Y with c = ¯c ◦qc.
They have been shown to be graphs for certain nice real-valued functions on
(compact) manifolds such as Morse(-Bott) functions and naturally generalized ones,
in [7, 20], and shown in [24, 25], for real-valued functions, more generally. We can
apply this for circle-valued cases, similarly, where we do not need to know the
arguments.
Key words and phrases. Smooth, real analytic, or real algebraic (real polynomial) functions
and maps. Compactifications of spaces and (differentiable) smooth, real algebraic, real analytic,
or continuous maps. Reeb spaces. Cell complexes. 1-dimensional cell complexes whose edges are
oriented. Peano continua. (Di)graphs. Reeb (di)graphs.
