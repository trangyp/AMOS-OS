COMB SMOOTHING AND LOCAL TRIVIALITY OF HOMOGENEOUS SPACES
OVER A RELATIVE CURVE
MARGOT BRUNEAUX AND FEDERICO SCAVIA
Abstract. Let R be a Henselian local ring, let κ be the residue field of R, let C be a smooth projective
curve over R with geometrically connected fibers, let G be a reductive C-group with isotrivial radical
torus rad(G), and let E →C be a G-torsor. We show that, if either the kernel of the central isogeny
Gsc ×C rad(G) →G is ´etale over C or κ is large, the Zariski-local triviality of Eκ →Cκ implies the
Zariski-local triviality of E →C. We also prove an averaged form of this result, assuming only that
rad(G) is isotrivial, as well as a variant for projective homogeneous spaces under no restrictions on G. As
consequences, we obtain a local-global principle for torsors over function fields of curves over Henselian
discrete valuation rings, strengthening work of Gille–Parimala–Suresh, a Henselian version of a theorem
of Drinfeld–Simpson, and an injectivity result for the Brauer–Azumaya group of C not covered by earlier
work of Colliot-Th´el`ene–Ojanguren–Parimala. Our proofs are geometric and rely on compactifications
of torsors and on a relative and arithmetic version of the comb smoothing technique, which we develop
in detail, building on work of Koll´ar and Graber–Harris–Starr.

1. Introduction
Let C be a smooth projective relative curve over a scheme S, and let G be a reductive group scheme
over C.
A natural question is whether G-torsors over C become Zariski-locally trivial after passage
to a cover S′ →S in some Grothendieck topology. In the ´etale setting, this statement is related to
uniformization results for the stack of G-torsors on a curve; in the Nisnevich setting, it provides a key
geometric input for local-global principles for torsors over semi-global function fields.
1.1. Local triviality and uniformization. The starting point is the work of Beauville–Laszlo [BL94]:
given a section Σ ⊂C of C →S, they proved [BL94, Lemma 3.5] that every SLn-torsor on C is trivial
on C \ Σ Zariski-locally on S, and then used this to prove the uniformization of moduli spaces of vector
bundles with trivial determinant on a curve.
Drinfeld–Simpson [DS95] extended the uniformization
statement to split semisimple groups G: given a section of C →S, every G-torsor becomes trivial on the
complement of the section after a suitable fppf base change S′ →S; if the order of π1(G) is invertible
on S, then one may take S′ →S to be ´etale. This was extended by Heinloth [Hei10]. For a parahoric
group scheme G over a fixed curve C0 over a field k, Heinloth proved: if x ∈C0 is a closed point, then
every family of G-torsors over C0 ×k S becomes trivial on (C0 \ {x}) ×k S′, for some faithfully flat base
change S′ →S. The theorems of Beauville–Laszlo, Drinfeld–Simpson and Heinloth underlie the one-point
uniformization theorem for the stack of G-torsors over a curve [Zhu17, Theorem 4.1.6], and show how
questions about the local triviality of torsors fit in a broader framework involving loop groups, patching,
and moduli stacks of bundles.
For an arbitrary reductive C-group G, one cannot expect the G-torsor E →C to become trivial on
the complement of a given section Σ of C →S, not even after a faithfully flat base change S′ →S, as can
already be seen for the case of Gm-torsors (equivalently, line bundles) on constant 1-parameter families
of elliptic curves. In the arbitrary reductive case, the most natural replacement of this condition that one
can aim for is Zariski-local triviality. This is already considered in the work of Drinfeld–Simpson, who
show that, for a split reductive group G, every G-torsor on C admits a reduction to a Borel subgroup
of G after a suitable surjective ´etale base change on S, and hence in particular becomes Zariski-locally
trivial after such a base change.
1.2. Local triviality over a Henselian base. There is also a finer local triviality problem. Let C →S
be a smooth projective curve with geometrically connected fibers, let G be a reductive C-group, let s ∈S,
and let E be a G-torsor on C. If the fiber Es →Cs is Zariski-locally trivial, does this local triviality spread
to a neighborhood of s in a suitable Grothendieck topology? For this question, the natural topology on
the base scheme S is not the ´etale topology but the Nisnevich topology. Indeed, an ´etale neighborhood of
s may replace the residue field κ(s) by a finite separable extension, potentially adding new sections, while
a Nisnevich neighborhood preserves the residue field at s. Moreover, considering the Nisnevich topology
is relevant for local-global principles over semi-global fields. Indeed, if R is a Henselian discrete valuation
ring and C →Spec R is a regular proper curve with function field F, then local-global questions for
GF -torsors are governed by the relation between the generic fiber, the special fiber, and the completions
of F. A Nisnevich-local triviality theorem for torsors on the model C is therefore a natural geometric
input for proving local-global principles over semi-global function fields: this is the perspective taken by
Gille–Parimala–Suresh [GPS21].
Since the question is Nisnevich-local at the point s ∈S, we may pass to the Henselization of the local
ring of S at s. The problem then takes the following form. Let R be a Henselian local ring with residue
field κ, let C →Spec R be a smooth projective relative curve, and let E be a G-torsor over C. If the
closed fiber Eκ →Cκ is Zariski-locally trivial, does it follow that E is Zariski-locally trivial?
Let rad(G) be the radical torus of G, let Gsc be the simply connected cover of the derived subgroup
of G, and let µ(G) be the kernel of the central isogeny Gsc ×C rad(G) →G, which is a finite flat C-
group. Under the assumption that µ(G) is ´etale and that rad(G) becomes quasi-split after a finite ´etale
morphism C′ →C of degree invertible in κ, Gille–Parimala–Suresh [GPS21, Theorem 7.2] prove that
E →C is Zariski-locally trivial. As a consequence, they prove the local-global principle for G-torsors
over a Henselian discrete valuation ring in many new cases; see [GPS21, Corollary 7.8].
The assumption that µ(G) be ´etale is a mild restriction on the residue characteristic. In the semisimple
case it amounts to requiring that the order of π1(Gκ) be invertible in κ, and is the Nisnevich analogue
of the corresponding hypothesis in Drinfeld–Simpson’s ´etale-local uniformization theorem. By contrast,
the hypothesis on the radical torus is of a different nature. Gille–Parimala–Suresh assume that the torus
rad(G) becomes quasi-split after a finite ´etale cover eC →C of degree prime to the residue characteristic.
This condition enters through their treatment of the toral case [GPS21, Theorem 2.4] via the proper base
change in ´etale cohomology.

1.3. Main results. In this paper, we study the problem of Nisnevich-local triviality using geometric
methods. Our first theorem is the following strengthening of the main result of [GPS21].
Theorem 1.1. Let R be a Henselian local ring with residue field κ, and let C be a smooth projective
R-curve with geometrically connected fibers, and let G be a reductive C-group whose radical rad(G) is
isotrivial and such that the natural central isogeny Gsc ×C rad(G) →G has ´etale kernel. If two G-torsors
E1 and E2 over C are Zariski-locally isomorphic over the special fiber Cκ, then they are Zariski-locally
isomorphic over C.
Theorem 1.1 was proved by Gille–Parimala–Suresh [GPS21, Theorem 7.2] when rad(G) becomes quasi-
split over a finite ´etale extension C′ →C of degree invertible in κ; note that this implies that rad(G) is
isotrivial. In particular, Theorem 1.1 is new already if G is an isotrivial C-torus (in which case µ(G) is
trivial).
When κ is large (that is, every κ-curve with a smooth κ-point has a Zariski-dense set of κ-points), our
methods allow us to remove the assumption on µ(G).
Theorem 1.2. Let R be a Henselian local ring with large residue field κ, and let C be a smooth projective
R-curve with geometrically connected fibers, and let G be a reductive C-group whose radical rad(G) is
isotrivial. If two G-torsors E1 and E2 over C are Zariski-locally isomorphic over the special fiber Cκ,
then they are Zariski-locally isomorphic over C.
We also prove an averaged version of Theorem 1.1, assuming only that the radical torus of G is
isotrivial.
Theorem 1.3. Let R be a Henselian local ring with residue field κ, and let C be a smooth projective
R-curve with geometrically connected fibers, and let G be a reductive C-group whose radical rad(G) is
isotrivial. If two G-torsors E1 and E2 over C are Zariski-locally isomorphic over the special fiber Cκ,
then there exists a finite collection of finite ´etale Henselian local R-algebras {Ri}i∈I of collectively coprime
degrees such that, for each i ∈I, the base changes (E1)Ri and (E2)Ri are Zariski-locally isomorphic.
Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 have the following consequence for the local-global principle over function fields
of curves over Henselian discrete valuation rings.
Corollary 1.4. Let R be a Henselian discrete valuation ring with residue field κ, let C be a smooth
projective R-curve with geometrically connected fibers, and let F be the function field of C. Let G be a
reductive C-group whose radical rad(G) is isotrivial. Assume that at least one of the following conditions
holds:
(i) the isogeny Gsc ×C rad(G) →G has ´etale kernel;
(ii) the field κ is large.
Then the local-global principle holds for GF -torsors over F with respect to the discrete valuations associ-
ated with the codimension-one points of C.
When R = Zp is the ring of p-adic integers, for some prime p, Corollary 1.4 is due to Colliot-Th´el`ene,
Parimala and Suresh; see [CTPS12, Theorem 4.8]. It was proved by Harbater–Hartmann–Krashen under
the assumption that GF is F-rational; see [HHK09, Theorem 3.7]. When µ(G) is ´etale and the radical
torus of G becomes quasi-split over a finite ´etale extension C′ →C of degree invertible in κ, it is due to
[GPS21, Corollary 7.8].
1.4. Projective homogeneous spaces. Our methods also apply to relative projective homogeneous
spaces.
Theorem 1.5. Let R be a Henselian local ring with residue field κ, let C be a smooth projective R-curve
with geometrically connected fibers, let Y →C be a smooth projective morphism such that, for every
c ∈C, the fiber Yc is a homogeneous space under a smooth affine connected k(c)-group, and such that
there exists a rational section Cκ 99K Yκ.
(1) There exists a finite collection of finite ´etale Henselian local R-algebras {Ri}i∈I of collectively
coprime degrees such that, for each i, the base change YRi →CRi admits a section.
(2) If κ is a large field, then Y →C admits a section.
As a first consequence, we prove a Henselian analogue of the aforementioned theorem of Drinfeld–
Simpson [DS95, Theorem 1].
Corollary 1.6. Let R be a local Henselian ring with residue field κ, let C be a smooth projective R-curve
with geometrically connected fibers, let G be a quasi-split reductive group over C, and let B be a Borel
subgroup of G. Let E be a G-torsor over C, and suppose that Eκ →Cκ is Zariski-locally trivial.

1.5. Comb smoothing and proofs of the main results. The works described above studied uni-
formization and local triviality via techniques involving moduli stacks, loop groups, and patching. In
contrast, our proofs of Theorems 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 are geometric; more precisely, they are based on a rela-
tive and arithmetic version of the comb smoothing technique, whose development takes up the majority
of this paper.
For the proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, by a twisting argument it suffices to show that, for every
G-torsor E →C such that Eκ →Cκ is Zariski-locally trivial, the G-torsor E →C is Zariski-locally
trivial. Since rad(G) is isotrivial, a theorem of Nath [Nat26] implies the existence of a fiberwise dense
smooth proper compactification E of E over C; see Section 6 and Appendix A. By the valuative criterion
for properness, a rational section of Eκ →Cκ extends uniquely to a section s: Cκ →Eκ. Equivalently, s
defines a κ-point of the Weil restriction ResC/R(E), which by a theorem of Grothendieck is represented
by an R-scheme locally of finite type. If this κ-point were smooth, we could lift it by Hensel’s lemma
to an R-point of ResC/R(E). The strategy is therefore to replace this κ-point by a smooth point whose
corresponding section agrees with the initial one on any prescribed finite closed subset of Cκ. In order
to accomplish this, we prove an arithmetic version of the comb smoothing technique of [GHS03].
Theorem 1.8. Let k be an infinite field, let D be a geometrically connected smooth projective k-curve,
let Z ⊂D be a finite subscheme, let p: X →D be a smooth projective morphism with separably rationally
connected fibers, and let s: D →X be a section of p. Then, for every integer N ⩾0, there exists an
integer mN ⩾N, a comb C with handle D and teeth C1, . . . , CmN attached to D at points distinct from
those of Z, and a morphism f : C →X smoothable fixing f(Z), such that f|D = s and such that, for all
1 ⩽i ⩽mN, p ◦f|Ci is constant and the restriction f|Ci : Ci →X is almost very free.
To prove Theorem 1.8, we first establish a smoothing theorem for combs embedded in X satisfying a
certain cohomological vanishing condition; see Theorem 4.6. We then construct a comb satisfying this
condition inside X with handle D; see Lemma 4.8. Koll´ar [Kol04, Theorem 16] has already established a
result very close to Lemma 4.8, but not exactly in the form required for our purposes. Since the precise
form we need does not appear in the literature, we provide complete proofs, following the strategy of
[Kol04] with the modifications required by our setting.
To construct teeth contained in the fibers of X →D, we study in Section 2 free rational curves in the
fibers of X →D. This also allows us to treat the case when D has genus zero without separate arguments.
Since a rational curve that is very free inside a fiber of X →D is not very free when viewed as a curve
in the total space X (the tangent directions coming from the base D contribute trivial summands to the
pullback of the tangent bundle of X) we keep track of almost very free curves and of the number of trivial
summands in the splitting type of the restricted tangent bundle. In this way, we relate very free curves of
X →D in the fibers to smoothness properties of the relevant evaluation maps. In Section 3, we recall the
relationship between separable rational connectedness and very free rational curves, and use it to produce
vertical rational curves in many tangent directions through prescribed points of the fibers of a smooth
morphism with separably rationally connected fibers. In Section 4, we first establish Theorem 4.6. Then,
using the abundance of very free curves in the fibers proved in Section 3 and following the strategy of
[Kol04, Theorem 16], we construct a comb with the required properties inside X (see Lemma 4.8), and
apply Theorem 4.6 to this comb to prove Theorem 1.8.

The idea of using comb smoothing on a compactification of a torsor, and then interpreting sections
of this compactification as points of a Weil restriction, was first suggested by Jason Starr [Sta23] in the
context of the Drinfeld–Simpson theorem. In that setting one works ´etale-locally on the base, and the
fact that a smooth morphism admits sections ´etale-locally allows the geometric argument to be carried
out after passing to algebraically closed residue fields. This removes most of the technical difficulties we
face in this paper.
