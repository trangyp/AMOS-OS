COMBINATORIAL INTERPRETATION OF THE COEFFICIENTS OF THE ORDER
POLYNOMIAL OF FENCE POSETS
YAKOB KAHANE
Abstract. Given a fence poset P, we define a new statistic on permutations, denoted by blP , that provides a
combinatorial interpretation of the coefficients of the order polynomial of P, answering a question of Ferroni, Morales,
and Panova (2025). Using the fact that the base polytope of a lattice path matroid can be decomposed into order
polytopes of fence posets, we also obtain a combinatorial interpretation of the coefficients of the Ehrhart polynomial
of the base polytope of Schubert matroids, answering a question of Stanley (1999). As an application of this statistic,
we establish the first nontrivial lower bound for the linear coefficient of the Ehrhart polynomial of an order polytope.
Finally, we conjecture generalizations of this statistic to skew-shape posets and circular fence posets.

3. Statistic for fence Poset
Given a fence poset P of size n, recall that Pn
k=1 ak = n!, which is the number of labelings of P. Thanks to
Lemma 2.5, those coefficients are nonnegative. The goal of this section is to give a statistic of the labeling of the
vertices which gives an interpretation of the coefficients ak.
The interpretation of [tn] n! Ω(P; t) = e(P) as counting linear extensions of P, together with the formula for
[t1] Ω(P; t) from Lemma 2.5 and the decomposition into convex subposets in Theorem 2.9, inspired the following
statistic describing the coefficients ak. This statistic uses the notions of block partitions that will be defined in this
section. Figure 6 gives an example of blocks and the statistic.
Definition 3.1 (Fence blocks). Let P be a fence poset and let σ be a labeling of the elements of P. A blocks is a
subposet such that :
• The leftmost element x of the block is called the root,
• For each other element y of the block, if y ∈asc(P), then σ(y) < σ(x),
• For each other element y of the block, if y ∈desc(P) then σ(y) > σ(x).
A block partition of a poset and labeling (P, σ) is a partition B = {B1, . . . , Bk} into blocks.
In a similar
way as for decomposition into subposets, given two blocks Bi, Bj we say that Bi < Bj if and only if there exists
y ∈Bi, z ∈Bj such that y <P z. We denote xi the root of Bi.
Definition 3.2. Given a fence poset with a labeling (P, σ) and a block partition B = {B1, . . . , Bk}. Define the order
relations on the blocks by Bi≺Bj if and only if σ(xi) < σ(xj).
We say that a block partition B = {B1, . . . , Bk} is valid if i ̸= j, Bi < Bj implies Bi ≺Bj.

Definition 4.6. A circular fence poset P of size n is a poset whose Hasse diagram is a cycle. It can be described
by a collection of position 2 ≤α1 < · · · < αk ≤n on the ground set {z1, . . . , zn} such that
• ∅̸= {α1, . . . , αk−1},
• For all 1 ≤i < n, andi /∈{α1, . . . , αk}, zi < zi+1,
• for all 1 ≤i < n, andi ∈{α1, . . . , αk}, zi > zi+1,
• zn < z1.
Figure 13. A circular fence poset of size 14.
We extend the definition of desc(P) and asc(P) on cycle cover naturally and saying that z1 ∈asc(P) (since it is
greater that the ”element at his left” which is zn).
Convex subposets of a circular fence poset P are either fence posets or P itself. We therefore extend the definition
of block fences by saying that P is a block if and only if there exists an element x ∈P, such that for all y ∈P,
y ∈desc(P) if and only if σ(y) > σ(x). In this case x is the root of the block P.
As for the skew shape case, given a partition into blocks B = {B1, . . . , Bk}, we say that Bi ≺Bj if and only if
σ(xi) < σ(xj) where xi and xj are respectively the roots of Bi and Bj. We say that B is a valid block partition if
and only if Bi < Bj implies that Bi ≺Bj.
