COMPLEX DYNAMICS PERSPECTIVE FOR BIRATIONAL MAPS OF THE PLANE
ARISING FROM CLUSTER ALGEBRA MUTATIONS
ANDREI GRIGROREV, KRISHNA CHAITANYA KALIDINDI, ANDRES QUINTERO SANTANDER,
AND ROLAND ROEDER
Abstract. Using the methods of holomorphic dynamics we investigate planar birational mappings that arise
from the theory of cluster algebras and integrable systems. Computing dynamical degrees of these mappings,
many of which are greater than one, allows us to show that many of the mappings do not have a conserved
quantity (nor an invariant fibration).
In most of the examples, invariant fibrations can also be ruled out by
finding superattracting periodic points. This answers a question posted by Machacek and Ovenhouse [MO24] and
by Chen and Li [CL24]. Moreover, having found a good algebraically stable model for the mappings and having
computed the dynamical degree, we can then apply results from the ergodic theory of birational maps to produce
invariant measures with positive entropy and positive Lyapunov exponents.

We will need to develop some terminology in order to state our main results. We will typically compactify
C2 by the complex projective plane CP2 and sometimes by more complicated rational complex surfaces. Any
rational self-mapping f : CP2 99K CP2 can be expressed in homogeneous coordinates [X : Y : Z] as a triple
f([X : Y : Z]) = [f1(X, Y, Z) : f2(X, Y, Z) : f3(X, Y, Z)],
where f1, f2, and f3 are homogeneous polynomials of the same degree that are chosen so that there is no
common factor of positive degree. With such a choice the common degree of these polynomials is called the
algebraic degree of f and denoted dalg(f). One can also associate to any such f the topological degree dtop(f)
which is the number of preimages of a sufficiently generic point in CP2 under f.
It is important to note that dalg(f) is not invariant under birational conjugacy and also that it does not
always behave well under iteration; i.e. one may have that dalg(fn) < (dalg(f))n for some natural number
n ≥2. (Here fn denotes the n-th iterate of f.) For this reason, one introduces the dynamical degree of f
denoted by λ1(f) and defined as
λ1(f) := lim (dalg(fn))1/n .
This limit always exists and it is unchanged under birational conjugacies. The main dynamical properties of
a dominant rational self-map of a K¨ahler surface (and in particular of CP2) are conjectured to be determined
by the two invariants λ1(f) and dtop(f); see, e.g. [Gue05]. For this reason, the first thing one does when
encountering a new rational self-map of CP2 is to ask “what are λ1(f) and dtop(f)?”
The mutation mappings µp,q are birational, so dtop(µp,q) = 1.
It is much more subtle to compute the
dynamical degree λ1(µp,q) and this computation (and its corollaries) is one of the main achievements of our

1.4. Ergodic Theory and Lyaponov Exponents of the maps. Our final result concerns the ergodic theory
of the mappings µp,q and it is relevant for p, q ≥1 as well as for p, q ≤−1.
Theorem E. Suppose p, q ≤−2 or p, q > 0 s.t. pq > 4 and let λ1 ≡λ1(µp,q) > 1 denote the dynamical degree
of µp,q, as expressed in Theorem A. The mapping µp,q : CP2 99K CP2 has
(i) Topological entropy equal to log(λ1) > 0.
(ii) An invariant measure ν of maximal entropy; i.e. whose measure-theoretic entropy equals log(λ1).
(iii) The Lyapunov exponents χ+(ν, µp,q) and χ−(ν, µp,q) of ν under µp,q satisfy
χ+(ν, µp,q) ≥log(λ1)/2 > 0 > −log(λ1)/2 ≥χ+(ν, µp,q).
(iv) Saddle-type periodic orbits for µp,q are asymptotically equidistributed with respect to ν.
The meanings of the terms used in Theorem E will be explained in Section 2.5. Using the vast number
of powerful results on the ergodic properties of birational mappings mentioned earlier in the introduction,
Theorem E will follow easily from the fact that λ1(µp,q) > 1 after checking a few additional technical details.
We present it here as an illustration of what the methods of holomorphic dynamics in several variables can
achieve when applied to a rational mapping whose motivations came from another area.

Appendix B. Local Indices Method
In this appendix we provide an alternate proof of Theorem A using the local indices method developed in
[ASW23a] and [ASW23b]. This is an alternative method to the computation in the Picard group that allows
to obtain the dynamical degree of a birational map of CPN. We leave the analogous computations for p, q < 0
to the reader.
The idea of the method is to compute deg(fn) by means of the local indices which are numbers associated
to a polynomial and a blow-up point in I(f−1) (see Definition B.6) and the computation of what is called by
