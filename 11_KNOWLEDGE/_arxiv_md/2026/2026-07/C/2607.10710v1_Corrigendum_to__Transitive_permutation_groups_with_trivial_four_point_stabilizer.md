Corrigendum to “Transitive permutation groups with
trivial four point stabilizers”
Paula Hähndel, Christoph Möller and Rebecca Waldecker
Abstract
This article revisits earlier work by the last author together with Kay Magaard.
We correct mistakes in results about the Sylow 2- and 3-structure of groups that
act with fixity 3 and one result about possible components, we improve some
results and we add details.
Keywords:
Permutation group, fixed points, fixity, simple group
1. Introduction
The motivation to investigate permutation groups that act with low fixity stems
from applications to Riemann surfaces. Following Ronse (see [21]), we say that a
group G has fixity k ∈N on a set Ωif and only if k is the maximum number
of fixed points of elements of G# on Ω. More background on our motivation
and on applications can be found in [15], and in this article we correct mistakes
in [16]. One error occurred when we analysed the 2-structure, which fortunately
did not lead to mistakes in the classification of finite simple groups that act with
fixity 3. We made another mistake when it comes to a more general situation
and possible components. This will be discussed separately, and in both cases
we explain exactly what parts of our initial work are affected by the mistakes,
we correct the mistakes, and we address all potential consequences. Towards
the end, we also refer to our recent work on soluble groups that act with low
fixity. These results were not available when Kay Magaard and the third author
wrote the original article, but in the end we decided that we wanted to use them
directly rather than repeating the arguments, which would have been necessary
in the revision of some of the specialised results in [16].
Here is the main hypothesis for the remainder of this paper:
Hypothesis 1.1. Suppose that G is a finite, transitive permutation group with
permutation domain Ω. Suppose further that G acts with fixity 3 on Ω.
We prove results that replace several lemmas in [16], mostly along two themes:
The 2- and 3-structure, where we made small mistakes that, fortunately, did not
have severe consequences and can easily be fixed, and components, where we
actually found new cases. These new cases are relevant in the general analysis,
and also for applications in ongoing work on groups that act with fixity 4, but

This paper is organised as follows:
After a few preliminary results about specific groups and action with fixity 3, we
prove results about the Sylow structure that will serve as a replacement of the
2- and 3-structure lemmas in [16]. We briefly discuss abelian minimal normal
subgroups, and then we turn to components of groups that act with fixity 3.

that we have overlooked in [16]. Then we prove an adapted version of our result

A general lemma, before we begin:
Lemma 2.1. Let G be a finite group, let E := E(G) and let x ∈G be such that
|CE(x)| = 3. Then x induces an inner automorphism on E.
Proof. Without loss G = E⟨x⟩, and we assume for a contradiction that x /∈
ECG(E). Our hypothesis implies that |CG(x)| = 9 and that, therefore, x does
not centralise an involution. According to Theorem 3 in [6], G must be one of
the almost simple groups in [6, Table 1], and since E ̸= G, we know that G
cannot be simple. Now G is isomorphic to PGL3(q) or PGU3(q), where q ≡ϵ
modulo 3 if ϵ = 1 or ϵ = −1, respectively. In both cases, x is contained in
an irreducible torus T such that NG(T) ∼= Cq2+ϵq+1 : C3. Since x has order
3, it follows that x ∈Z(NG(T)). In particular, CG(x) contains a subgroup of
order 3 · (q2 + ϵq + 1) and therefore 3 = |CE(x)| ≥q2 + ϵq + 1. If follows that
(q, ϵ) = (2, −1) and that G ∼= PGU3(2), which is a soluble group of order 216.
But 1 ̸= E(G) is not soluble, hence this impossible.
