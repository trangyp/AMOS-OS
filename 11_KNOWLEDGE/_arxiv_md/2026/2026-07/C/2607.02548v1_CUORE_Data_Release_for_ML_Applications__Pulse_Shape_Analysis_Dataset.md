I.
INTRODUCTION
Neutrinoless double-beta (0νββ) decay is a hypo-
thetical lepton-number-violating process whose discov-
ery would confirm the Majorana nature of the neutrino
and provide a mechanism for explaining the matter-
antimatter asymmetry of the universe [1, 2].
The
CUORE (Cryogenic Underground Observatory for Rare
Events) experiment searches for 0νββ decay of 130Te us-
ing an array of 988 TeO2 crystal calorimeters operated
at millikelvin temperatures at the Laboratori Nazionali
del Gran Sasso (LNGS) in Italy [3].
CUORE has accumulated over 2 tonne·yr of TeO2 ex-
posure, setting a lower limit on the 130Te 0νββ decay
half-life of T1/2 > 3.5 × 1025 yr (90% C.I.) [4].
The
data release presented here is an excellent resource for the
AI/ML community as it combines the high-quality, time-
series data from CUORE with well-understood, physics-
based labels.
In response to the growing demand for open scientific
datasets [5] and the development of new analysis tech-
niques, the CUORE Collaboration is releasing a curated
subset of its data. This release specifically targets the
problem of pulse classification: distinguishing clean sin-
gle pulses from pile-up pulses, where two or more pulses
are present in the same detector event window.
II.
THE CUORE DETECTOR AND PULSE
FORMATION
A.
Detector Overview
The CUORE detector consists of 988 5 × 5 × 5 cm3
TeO2 crystal calorimeters. The crystals are arranged in
19 towers of 52 crystals each; the active detector mass is
742 kg. The CUORE detector is hosted inside a custom
dilution refrigerator, which cools the crystals to a base
temperature of ∼10 mK. At these temperatures, the heat
capacity of the crystals follow the Debye law C(T) ∝T 3,
making the temperature fluctuations from energy depo-
sitions measurable.
The crystals serve as both ββ source (containing 130Te
at ∼34% natural isotopic abundance) and as the calori-
metric absorbers [6]. An NTD (Neutron Transmutation
Doped) germanium thermistor [7] is glued to each crystal
to measure the temperature change resulting from energy
depositions.
B.
Pulse Shape
When a particle deposits energy E in a TeO2 crys-
tal, the crystal temperature increases proportionally by
∆T ∝E/C(T). This temperature rise is measured as
the change in voltage across the biased NTD thermistor.
The resulting detector response is a thermal pulse with
a characteristic shape that is determined by the ther-
mal couplings and capacitances of the calorimeter com-
ponents.
Typical CUORE pulses have a rise time of ∼50 ms and
a thermalization (decay) time of ∼1 s. The continuous
data stream is formed by passing the thermistor volt-
age through a room-temperature preamplifier and anti-
aliasing filter, and digitizing at 1 kHz. A representative
clean pulse and pile-up pulse are shown in Figure 1.
C.
Pile-Up Events
Pile-up occurs when two or more separate interactions
deposit energy in a single crystal calorimeter, within an
event window of 10 s. In CUORE, pile-up events are a
source of background in the 0νββ decay search because
they can mimic the energy of a single-particle event or
misrepresent the background near Qββ, where the posited
0νββ decay signature peak is expected.

B.
Pulse Array Format
Each pulse is stored as a one-dimensional NumPy array
of length 10000, corresponding to a 10-s event window.
The window is defined such that the triggered pulse rise
midpoint occurs at approximately index 3000, providing
∼3 s of pre-trigger baseline [8]. The full window captures
both the rising edge and a sufficient portion of the decay-
ing tail for pulse shape characterization. Notably, each
triggered pulse is associated with its own event window,
so a set of pile-up pulses may be present in multiple event
windows.

V.
AI/ML CHALLENGE
To encourage engagement with this dataset, we pro-
pose a pulse classification challenge with the following
task:
Classification Task: Develop a machine learning model
that takes a raw pulse array as input and predicts the
binary label (0 = clean, 1 = pile-up).
Models will be
evaluated on the held-out test set using the following
metrics:
• AUC-ROC: Area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve.
• Accuracy: Fraction of correctly classified events.

• False Positive Rate at fixed True Positive
Rate: Specifically, the false positive rate at a true
positive rate of 90%, relevant for physics analyses
where signal efficiency must be controlled.
Participants are invited to submit predictions on the
test set (where labels are withheld) to vis224@pitt.edu.
The submission format should be a CSV file or NumPy
array with one predicted probability per test event (prob-
ability of Class 1 / pile-up).

Appendix A: Technical Details of the Data Release
Because the pulse shapes can change for different de-
tector operating conditions and are largely dependent
on the individual calorimeter components, the 10000
CUORE waveforms provided in this data release come
from a single CUORE dataset-calorimeter. Only the cal-
ibration runs of the single CUORE dataset were used in
the event selection. Using calibration runs ensured ade-
quate statistics of pile-up pulse events since radioactive
sources are deployed around the detector during these
measurements. For the event selection, basic quality cuts
were applied to exclude baseline instabilities and only
triggered pulses with reconstructed energy >500 keV
were accepted.
