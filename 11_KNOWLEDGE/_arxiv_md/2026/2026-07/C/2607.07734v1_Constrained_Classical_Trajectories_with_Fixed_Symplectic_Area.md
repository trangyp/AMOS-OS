Constrained Classical Trajectories with Fixed Symplectic Area
Taisuke Hasegawa∗
Center for Computational Sciences, University of Tsukuba, 1-1-1 Tenno-dai, Tsukuba 305-8577, Japan†
(Dated: July 10, 2026)
We study finite bundles of N classical trajectories subject to a fixed symplectic-area condition
on their phase-space covariance. We derive constraint forces that preserve this condition with zero
bundle-averaged power. Setting κ = ℏ2/4 fixes only the area scale: the trajectories remain classical,
and the finite bundle need not be Gaussian. In the Gaussian large-N limit, the centroid and width
equations coincide with those of variational Gaussian wave-packet dynamics. Here N specifies the
finite-bundle representation, not an order in the Moyal expansion or in an ℏexpansion.
Classical Hamiltonian dynamics can be represented ei-
ther by individual trajectories or by the evolution of a
phase-space density.
In the density description, each
phase-space point is transported along a classical trajec-
tory. A bundle containing a finite number of trajectories
can therefore represent the motion of a localized region
of phase space.
In an anharmonic potential, the force
varies across the bundle and deforms its position and mo-
mentum widths; as a result, the phase-space area scale
inferred from the covariance is not generally preserved.
We therefore construct a collective constraint that keeps
this covariance-based area scale fixed during the motion.
Constraint forces are standard in classical mechanics and
molecular dynamics [1–3]; here, however, the forces ap-
plied to the individual trajectories are determined by the
collective position and momentum widths of the bundle.
We derive the corresponding constraint forces through
a d’Alembert-type construction and obtain constrained
equations of motion for a finite trajectory bundle. We
then choose the prescribed area scale to match that car-
ried by the covariance of a pure Gaussian quantum state,
while the trajectories themselves remain classical. For se-
quences of bundles approaching a Gaussian phase-space
density, the construction has a Gaussian large-N limit
whose centroid and width equations coincide with those
of variational Gaussian wave-packet dynamics [4–7]. Fi-
nally, we illustrate the calibrated finite-bundle dynamics
numerically.

Calibration to the quantum area scale.— Up to this point,
√κ has specified the prescribed symplectic-area scale of
the covariance in a finite-bundle dynamics built from
classical trajectories. We now set κ = ℏ2/4, so that this
area scale is √κ = ℏ/2.
This choice does not change
the classical form of the trajectory-level equations: each
bundle member remains a constrained classical trajec-
tory. The resulting covariance relation then has the same
form as that of a pure Gaussian quantum state [10–15].
This is only a calibration of the second moments; it does
not impose a Gaussian form on a finite bundle. With
this calibration, the finite-bundle equations describe con-
strained classical trajectory dynamics whose covariance
carries the quantum symplectic-area scale ℏ/2. We next
examine the Gaussian large-N limit of these calibrated
finite-bundle equations.

Discussion.— The central result is a finite-bundle dy-
namics of labeled classical trajectories subject to a col-
lective fixed-area constraint on the bundle covariance.
The fixed-area condition constrains the second moments
and has zero bundle-averaged power, while the detailed
finite-bundle shape is left to the trajectory evolution.
The formulation therefore treats the symplectic-area cal-
ibration and the shape of the phase-space distribution
as separate ingredients. The finite-bundle equations can
be implemented using force evaluations along the trajec-
tories.
For the coupled Morse-oscillator model, paired
propagations over ten initial-bundle realizations show a
systematic influence of the constraint on both centroid
and covariance dynamics. For the selected sequence of
bundles approaching the same target Gaussian density,
the realization-averaged finite-bundle dynamics moves
toward the variational Gaussian wave-packet trajectory
as N is increased.
These calculations show the direct
force-based propagation of the constrained bundle and
illustrate the finite-bundle behavior approaching the an-
alytically identified collective limit. At finite N, the dy-
namics consists of a finite set of labeled constrained tra-
jectories and imposes no closure on the bundle shape; in
