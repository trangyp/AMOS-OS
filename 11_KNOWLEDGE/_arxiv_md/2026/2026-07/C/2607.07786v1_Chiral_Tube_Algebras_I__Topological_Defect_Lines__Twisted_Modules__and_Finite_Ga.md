Abstract
Chiral algebras and topological defect lines (TDLs) represent two complementary notions
of symmetry in 2d conformal field theories. In this paper, we introduce chiral tube algebras
to unify and extend these two notions. Chiral tube algebras generalize chiral algebras in
two ways. First, they extend the action of chiral algebras beyond the local Hilbert space
to include defect Hilbert spaces twisted by TDLs. Second, they allow for non-local chiral
currents attached by TDLs and thus can map between different defect Hilbert spaces, anal-
ogous to the tube algebras of TDLs. Since local chiral currents can become non-local after
finite gauging, chiral tube algebras provide a natural framework for describing the image of
chiral algebras under such gauging. We illustrate this framework through a variety of ex-
amples that generalize familiar chiral algebras, including Kac-Moody algebras, W algebras,
superconformal algebras, and their orbifolds/bosonizations. We construct their irreducible
modules, which are isomorphic to twisted modules of the corresponding chiral algebras, and
use them to organize local and defect Hilbert spaces. In a subsequent paper, we will study
chiral tube algebras generated by non-local chiral currents with fractional spins, which have
no counterparts in chiral algebras.

In answering these questions, a natural unifying symmetry structure emerges, which we refer

Chiral tube algebras generalize ordinary chiral algebras by extending their action beyond
the local Hilbert space to include defect Hilbert spaces. In this sense, they are analogous
to the tube algebras associated with TDLs [12, 13], which describe how TDLs act on both
local and defect Hilbert spaces, and hence the name “chiral tube algebras”. Because of this
extended action, chiral tube algebras can incorporate both local chiral currents and non-

unlike ordinary chiral algebras, chiral tube algebras generated by non-local chiral currents
necessarily mix the local and defect Hilbert spaces, similar to the tube algebras of non-
invertible TDLs. Since local chiral currents often become non-local under finite gauging,
chiral tube algebras also provide a natural framework for describing the image of chiral
algebras under finite gauging. In Table 1, we compare chiral tube algebras with ordinary

1.1
Topological Defect Lines and Tube Algebras
Topological defects are natural generalizations of ordinary global symmetries [14]. They are
invariant under smooth deformations of their underlying submanifolds and commute with
the stress tensor, thereby defining conserved quantities and generalized global symmetries.
An ordinary global symmetry with symmetry group G is described by a set of co-
dimension 1 topological defects Ug labeled by group elements g ∈G, whose fusions obey
the group multiplication law,
Ug × Ug′ = Ugg′ .
(1.1)
In particular, every topological defect Ug has an inverse given by its orientation reversal
Ug = Ug−1, such that Ug × Ug−1 = I, where I is the identity defect. Sweeping a topological

1.3
Chiral Tube Algebras
As discussed in Section 1.1, TDLs give rise to a rich spectrum of defect operators, which
naturally motivates the questions raised at the beginning of the paper. We will explain that
chiral tube algebras provide a natural framework for addressing all of these questions.
How does a chiral algebra act on defect Hilbert spaces?
TDLs, by definition, commute with the stress tensor, so their associated defect Hilbert
spaces always preserve the Virasoro algebra and therefore can be organized into Virasoro
modules. However, these defect Hilbert spaces may not preserve an extended chiral algebra
generated by additional chiral currents V a(z). This happens when the TDLs act nontrivially
on the chiral currents. Since TDLs implement global symmetries, they must preserve the
OPE structure of the chiral algebra and hence act as automorphisms of the chiral algebra
(assuming the TDLs do not map the chiral currents to non-local chiral currents)5. As we
explain below, although the original extended chiral algebra is no longer preserved on the
defect Hilbert spaces, a version of it twisted by these automorphisms still survives. The
defect Hilbert spaces can then be organized into modules of this twisted chiral algebra.

Generally, to construct a chiral tube algebra, one needs to specify the following data: a set

by a subcategory of these TDLs, together with their OPEs; and the action of the TDLs

associated non-local chiral currents may transform as twisted modules of the chiral algebra.

category. In this case, the non-local chiral currents are untwisted modules under the chiral

the end of Verlinde lines becomes the vertex tensor category formulated in [71–75] (see also

Outline
We have introduced the general notion of chiral tube algebras in the introduction. In the
rest of the paper, we will illustrate this framework in various concrete examples.
Section 2 focuses on the three-state Potts CFT and its Z2 orbifold, i.e. the tetracritical
Ising CFT. The three-state Potts CFT is the simplest CFT that hosts a W3 chiral algebra.
We construct the corresponding W3 chiral tube algebra that acts on all defect Hilbert spaces
twisted by TDLs. We then construct the modules of this chiral tube algebra and show that
each of them is isomorphic to either an untwisted or a twisted W3 module. Interestingly, the
Virasoro modules not contained in the untwisted W3 modules naturally combine into twisted
W3 modules.
Thus, the untwisted and twisted W3 modules together cover all Virasoro
modules at this central charge. In the tetracritical Ising CFT, the W3 current becomes non-
local. We explain how to use this non-local current to construct the orbifold of the W3 chiral
tube algebra and describe how the local and defect Hilbert spaces are organized under it.
Section 3 is devoted to chiral tube algebras that generalize the su(2)1 Kac-Moody algebra.
Since the spin 1 currents of the su(2)1 algebra also generate an ordinary SU(2) symmetry,
there is a natural su(2)1 chiral tube algebra describing how the su(2)1 Kac-Moody algebra
acts on the defect Hilbert spaces twisted by these SU(2) TDLs. Its modules are isomorphic
to untwisted or twisted su(2)1 modules, which provide an alternative derivation of spectral
flow [77]. We discuss an explicit realization of this su(2)1 chiral tube algebra in the SU(2)1
WZW model, and then study its ZN orbifold, realized in the corresponding ZN orbifold of
the SU(2)1 WZW model, which is a compact boson CFT.
Section 4 studies the N = 1 superconformal algebra and its bosonization in the example
of the tricritical Ising CFT and its fermionization, which is an N = 1 minimal model. In the
tricritical Ising CFT, the supercurrents are non-local and attached by a Z2 TDL. Unlike the
non-local currents encountered in the previous examples, these supercurrents are themselves
charged under the attached Z2 TDL. This leads to new features in the corresponding chiral
tube algebras.

5
Discussions and Outlook
We highlight a few future directions and open questions.
Representation theory of chiral tube algebras.
For a strongly regular chiral algebra
V, the category of its representations Rep(V) is known to form a modular tensor category
[98,99]. Physically, this modular tensor category describes the anyons in the 3d topological
quantum field theory whose boundary realizes the chiral algebra V. It would be interesting
to systematically develop a representation theory of chiral tube algebras and to identify the
corresponding mathematical structure.
SymTFT for chiral tube algebras.
The representations of tube algebras are encoded
compactly in the 3d SymTFT [14, 22–31, 33–38] associated with the TDLs. They are in
one-to-one correspondence with the anyons in the SymTFT [13, 18–21, 39].
It would be
interesting to develop an analogous SymTFT perspective for chiral tube algebras.
The
continuous spacetime SymTFTs developed in [45] may provide a useful starting point.
Chiral strip algebras.
Recent works have generalized tube algebras to strip algebras
[100–104], which describe the interplay between topological defects and boundaries. It would
be interesting to extend chiral tube algebras in a similar direction by developing “chiral strip
algebras”. Such algebras could perhaps be used to bootstrap the conformal data associated
with the conformal boundaries in rational CFTs.
Chiral tube algebras in 4d SCFTs.
There is an intriguing correspondence between
4d N = 2 superconformal field theories (SCFTs) and chiral algebras [105]. It would be
interesting to connect chiral tube algebras with this SCFT/VOA correspondence. In par-
ticular, under the correspondence, a superconformal surface defect gives rise to a module
of the corresponding chiral algebra, whose character computes the surface defect Schur in-
dex [106]. Similarly, a twisted superconformal surface defects attached by 3d topological
defects gives rise to a twisted module of the corresponding chiral algebra. From the per-
spective of the chiral algebra plane, an ordinary surface defect appears as a local point
operator, whereas a twisted surface defect appears as a non-local point operator attached
by a TDL. This suggests that one might be able to define a chiral tube algebra acting si-
61


## Page 63


multaneously on superconformal surface defects and their twisted counterparts. A natural
setup for exploring this idea is the compactification of a 4d SCFT on S2 × Σ, where the
resulting two-dimensional non-unitary CFT on Σ carries the chiral algebra associated with
the original 4d SCFT [107]. It would also be interesting to study twisted defects attached
by non-invertible symmetries [59,60,108–110] in 4d SCFTs, and to explore their interactions
with the corresponding chiral algebras. An example of such non-invertible twisted defects
has been studied in Maxwell theory [111]. Another hint that chiral tube algebras might
arise naturally in the SCFT/VOA correspondence is that the characters of twisted modules
appear in the lens space index of 4d N = 2 SCFTs [112].
