Coherence of Smyth powerspaces
Rongqi Xiao, Xiaodong Jia∗
School of Mathematics, Hunan University, Changsha, Hunan, 410082, China
Abstract
In this paper, we study when the Smyth powerspace Q∗
v(X) of a topological space X is coherent,
and prove that X is coherent and weakly Hausdorff if and only if Q∗
v(X) is coherent and weakly
Hausdorff. We give examples to show that neither coherence nor weak Hausdorffness of X solely
implies that Q∗
v(X) is coherent or weakly Hausdorff. As a byproduct, our work gives an affirmative
answer to a question raised by Xu [15, Question 7.6].
Keywords:
Smyth powerspace, coherence, weak Hausdorffness, Scott space.
1. Introduction
The Smyth powerspace Q∗
v(X) of a topological space X consists of all nonempty compact
saturated subsets of X and equips with the upper Vietoris topology. It is one of the classical
powerspace constructions in non-Hausdorff topology, and especially in domain theory that is used
for denotational semantics of demonic nondeterminism in theoretical computer science [1, 2, 4].
To serve as semantic models, one will need the Smyth powerspace be defined on the cor-
responding semantic category. This purpose has led to many research in this direction. That
is, given a space X, one asks which properties of X are preserved by the construction Q∗
v(X).
Compactness of spaces is trivially in that preservation list. Goubault-Larrecq showed that if X is
stably compact, then Q∗
v(X) is stably compact [3]; Heckmann and Keimel showed that X is sober
if and only if Q∗
v(X) is sober [7, Theorem 3.13]; Xu, Xi and Zhao showed that X is well-filtered
if and only if that Q∗
v(X) is well-filtered [16]; and Lyu, Chen and the second author showed that
X is locally compact if and only if that Q∗
v(X) is core-compact if and only if Q∗
v(X) is locally
compact [13]. As stably compact spaces are compact, locally compact, sober and coherent spaces,
and motivated by these developments, we naturally ask whether coherence of a space can be
preserved by the Smyth powerspace construction. The main goal of this paper is to answer this
question.
A space X is called to be coherent if the intersection of arbitrary two compact saturated
subsets of X is again compact saturated. Coherence plays an important role in non-Hausdorff
topology and domain theory, where it is used to characterize Lawson compactness [2, 9] and
to locate Cartesian closed subcategories of domains [10]. Our first main result shows that the
Smyth powerspace Q∗
v(X) of a coherent topological space X is indeed coherent if X is additionally

2. Preliminaries
The following definitions can be found in [1, 2, 4].
Let X be a topological space. We denote by O(X) the set of all open subsets of X, ordered
by the inclusion order ⊆. The specialization preorder ≤spec on X is defined by x ≤spec y if and
only if x ∈cl({y}), where cl({y}) is the closure of {y}. The relation ≤spec is a partial order if
and only if X is T0. Intersections of open subsets of X are called saturated subsets. We say X is
coherent if the intersection of any two compact saturated subsets of X is compact saturated. A
T0 space X is called well-filtered if for any filtered family of compact saturated subsets Ki, i ∈I
and open subset U, that T
i∈I Ki ⊆U holds implies that Ki ⊆U holds for some i ∈I.
Write Q(X) for the set of all compact saturated subsets of X. The upper Vietoris topology ν
on Q(X) is generated by the basic opens
□U := {K ∈Q(X) : K ⊆U},
where U ranges over the open subsets of X. We write Qv(X) := (Q(X), ν) as the Smyth pow-
erspace of X, and use Q∗
v(X) to denote the subspace of Qv(X) that consists of nonempty compact
saturated subsets of X and equipped with the subspace topology. Note that the specialization
order on Q∗
v(X) (or on Qv(X)) is reverse inclusion: for K, L ∈Q(X), K ≤spec L ⇐⇒L ⊆K.
3. The weakly Hausdorff case
Weak Hausdorffness is a topological property initially introduced by Keimel and Lawson in [11].
Definition 3.1. X is weakly Hausdorff if for all compact saturated subsets Q1, Q2 of X, and for
every open neighborhood W of Q1 ∩Q2, there are two open neighborhoods U of Q1 and V of Q2
such that U ∩V ⊆W.

Theorem 3.6. Let X be a topological space. The following are equivalent.
1. X is coherent and weakly Hausdorff;
2. Qv(X) is coherent and weakly Hausdorff;
3. Q∗
v(X) is coherent and weakly Hausdorff.
Proof. Assume that X is coherent and weakly Hausdorff. We show that 1 implies 2. We first show
that Qv(X) is coherent. Let A, B ⊆Qv(X) be compact saturated subsets. We must show that
A ∩B is compact in Qv(X). Since A and B are compact, A × B is compact in Qv(X) × Qv(X).
By Lemma 3.4, the image ∩(A × B) = {K ∩L : K ∈A, L ∈B} is compact in Qv(X). We claim
that ∩(A × B) = A ∩B. Take K ∈A and L ∈B. Since K ∩L ⊆K and K ∩L ⊆L, the
specialization order on Qv(X) (reverse inclusion) yields K ≤spec (K ∩L) and L ≤spec (K ∩L).
Because A and B are saturated, they are upward closed with respect to ≤spec. Hence (K ∩L) ∈A
and (K ∩L) ∈B, so K ∩L ∈A ∩B. Therefore ∩(A × B) ⊆A ∩B. Conversely, if M ∈A ∩B,
then (M, M) ∈A × B and ∩(M, M) = M. So M ∈∩(A × B), and hence A ∩B ⊆∩(A × B).
Thus A ∩B = ∩(A × B), which is compact. Since the intersection of saturated sets is saturated,
A ∩B is compact saturated. This shows that Qv(X) is coherent.
To show that Qv(X) is weakly Hausdorff, we let A, B ⊆Qv(X) be compact saturated and let
W ∈O(Qv(X)) satisfy A ∩B ⊆W. Then A ∩B = ∩(A × B) ⊆W, and hence A × B ⊆∩−1(W).
Note also that ∩−1(W) is open in Qv(X)×Qv(X) as the ∩operation is continuous by Lemma 3.4.
By Wallace’s generalized tube lemma (see for example Exercise 9 on Page 171 in [14]), there are
open sets U, V ⊆Qv(X) such that A ⊆U, B ⊆V, and U ×V ⊆∩−1(W). Moreover, if M ∈U ∩V,
then (M, M) ∈U × V, and M = ∩(M, M) ∈W; therefore, we have U ∩V ⊆W, and Qv(X) is
weakly Hausdorff.
That 2 implies 1 is shown in Proposition 3.5, and the equivalence between 2 and 3 is explained
in Remark 3.3.

6. Closing remarks
It is worth emphasizing that the counterexample constructed in Section 4 has very strong
classical properties, as witnessed by Proposition 4.2. Thus, even within the class of compact,
coherent, T1, locally compact and second-countable spaces, the Smyth powerspace Q∗
v(X) need
not preserve coherence. In other words, among these properties, the essential missing condition to
make Q∗
v(X) coherent is precisely weak Hausdorffness. This shows that weak Hausdorffness is not
merely a technical assumption in Theorem 3.6, but is relevant to the preservation of coherence by
the Smyth powerspace construction. A natural direction for further work is to characterize those
coherent spaces X for which Q∗
v(X) is coherent, and to determine whether weak Hausdorffness
can be replaced by other assumptions.
We conclude this paper with listing the following, where we use " =⇒" to mean implication.
1. Q∗
v(X) is coherent =⇒X is coherent (Proposition 3.5, item 1);
2. Q∗
v(X) is weakly Hausdorff =⇒X is weakly Hausdorff (Proposition 3.5, item 2);
3. X is weakly Hausdorff ̸⇒Q∗
v(X) is weakly Hausdorff (Example 3.8);
4. X is coherent ̸⇒Q∗
v(X) is coherent (Theorem 4.8);
5. X is coherent and weakly Hausdorff ⇐⇒Q∗
v(X) is coherent and weakly Hausdorff (Theo-
rem 3.6).
