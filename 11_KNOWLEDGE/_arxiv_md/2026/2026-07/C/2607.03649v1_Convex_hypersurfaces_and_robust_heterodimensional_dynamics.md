CONVEX HYPERSURFACES AND ROBUST HETERODIMENSIONAL DYNAMICS
JULIAN CHAIDEZ AND MICHAEL HUANG
Abstract. We prove that any closed orientable hypersurface in a contact manifold of dimension five
or greater is isotopic to a robustly non-convex hypersurface via an arbitrarily 𝐶0-small isotopy. This
strengthens a recent result of the first author and yields a strong counterpart to the groundbreaking
density theorem of Honda-Huang and Giroux.
This is proven by combining a new convexity
obstruction via heteroclinics and recent advances in robust heterodimensional dynamics due to Li-
Turaev to produce a robust deconvexifying plug, which is a local and robust convexity obstruction.
1. Introduction
A hypersurface in a contact manifold is convex if there exists a contact vector field that is
everywhere transverse to the hypersurface. Convex surface theory was originally pioneered by
Giroux [26], who established several fundamental properties of convex surfaces and applied them
to resolve (in dimension three) a question of Eliashberg-Gromov [18] on the existence of contact
Morse functions. Convex surfaces subsequently developed into a core tool in low-dimensional
contact topology, with many fundamental applications (cf. [20–22, 31, 32, 35–37]). We refer the
reader to the monograph of Etnyre [19] for a more comprehensive reference.
In higher dimensions, convex hypersurface theory has only recently gained traction due to the
groundbreaking work of Honda-Huang [33,34]. Applications have included the completion of
the higher-dimensional Giroux correspondence by Breen-Honda-Huang [13]. A central question
in all of these works regards the abundance of convex hypersurfaces. More precisely, we have
the following question about density and genericity [34, Remark 1.2.4] or [44].
Question 1 (Density/Genericity). Are convex hypersurfaces dense (or even generic) in the space
of closed oriented hypersurfaces in any contact manifold in the 𝐶𝑘-topology?
One of the fundamental results of Giroux [26] states that convex surfaces are 𝐶8-generic in
dimension three, and the main results of Honda-Huang [33] established the 𝐶0-density of convex
hypersurfaces in any dimension. On the other hand, it was long conjectured that Question 1
should have a negative answer in the 𝐶8-topology beyond dimension three. This longstanding
problem was finally resolved by the first author [14] who recently proved the following result.
Theorem 2. [14] There is a closed oriented hypersurface Σ in any contact manifold p𝑌, 𝜉q of dimension
five or greater that cannot be approximated by convex hypersurfaces in the 𝐶2-topology.
The work [14] applied a tool from partially hyperbolic dynamics, namely Bonatti-Diaz blenders
[7–10], to construct a closed hypersurface in any contact manifold with 𝐶2-robustly transitive
characteristic foliations. Any such hypersurface is also 𝐶2-robustly non-convex, since a convex
hypersurface cannot have transitive characteristic foliation. These results revealed a connection
between the topology of hypersurfaces in contact manifolds and partially hyperbolic dynamics.
In this paper, we develop these connections further by establishing a general relationship
between robust non-convexity in contact topology and robust heterodimensional dynamics (cf.
Bonatti-Diaz [9]). As an application, we use recent work of Li-Turaev [38] to construct robust and
local obtructions to convexity, yielding the following dramatic strengthening of Theorem 2.
Theorem 3 (Main Result). Any closed orientable hypersurface Σ in a contact manifold p𝑌, 𝜉q of dimen-
sion five or greater is isotopic to a 𝐶2-robustly non-convex hypersurface via a 𝐶0-small isotopy.
This resolves a conjecture [14, Conjecture 20] of the first author, and proves that the failure of
Question 1 is extreme. Moreover, this work points to a connection between the convex/non-
convex dichotomy and the program of 𝐶1-generic dynamics advocated by Bonatti [6].

Historically, robust heterodimensional dynamics originally appeared in the seminal work of
Abraham-Smale [1] who constructed the first examples of 𝐶1-open sets of diffeomorphisms
that are not Axiom A. This disproved the conjecture that Axiom A diffeomorphisms and flows
are 𝐶1-generic, historically known as Smale’s dream [48]. Further examples were discovered in
[16,17,17,27,39,46,47] and a general approacht to robust heterodimensional cycles was eventually
developed by Bonatti-Diaz [8, 10] using blenders. Robust heterodimensional dynamics, along
with the related phenomenon of robust homoclinic tangencies, subsequently developed into
central phenomena in differentiable dynamics (cf. [9,28,29] for related overviews).
The centrality of robust heterodimensional dynamics is exhibited by the following famous
conjecture of Palis, posed originally in [42, 43], which can be viewed as a revision of Smale’s
dream that incorporates non-hyperbolic robust phenomena.
Conjecture 8 (Palis). A 𝐶1-generic diffeomorphism (or non-singular line field) either satisfies Axiom A,
contains a robust heterodimensional cycle, or contains a robust homoclinic tangency.
In fact, there is a version of this question posed by Bonatti-Diaz [9, Question 1.2] that removes
the case of homoclinic tangencies, and thus conjectures a dichotomy between hyperbolicity and
robust heterodimensional cycles. This variant of the Palis Conjecture is closely related to the
results of this paper, and we will return to it shortly.

1.4. Generic Dynamics For Characteristic Foliations. We conclude this introduction with a
perspective on our results related to the program on 𝐶1-generic dynamics outlined by Bonatti.
In [6], Bonatti articulates a general philosophy for understanding 𝐶1-generic diffeomorphisms,
stating several conjectures similar to the Palis Conjecture. Roughly, each conjecture in [6] posits
that the space of diffeomorphisms divides into two 𝐶1-open sets consisting of (a) a set of diffeo-
morphisms satisfying a global structural property and (b) a set of diffeomorphisms exhibiting a
robust local structure that obstructs the global one. The specific global structural property and
robust local structure vary from conjecture to conjecture.
The results of this paper fit neatly into this program as follows. We consider space of closed
oriented hypersurfaces in contact manifolds equipped with the 𝐶2-topology, which corresponds
to the 𝐶1-topology on the characteristic foliations. On one hand, convexity of the hypersurface
provides a global structural property of the characteristic dynamics. On the other hand, the
results of this paper suggest that the complementary robust local structure should be a certain
type of heterodimensional cycle. More precisely, we fix the following terminology.
Definition 17 (Positive-Negative). A heterodimensional cycle 𝐶in the characteristic foliation of
a hypersurface Σ in a contact 2𝑛` 1-manifold p𝑌, 𝜉q is positive-negative if the indices satisfy
indp𝐶`q ď 𝑛´ 1
and
indp𝐶´q ě 𝑛
We can divide the space of closed oriented hypersurfaces in a contact manifold into two
𝐶2-open sets: the set of convex hypersurfaces and the set of hypersurfaces containing a robust
positive-negative heterodimensional cycle. Corollary 11 states that these sets are disjoint; The-
orems 14 and 15 state that the latter set is non-empty and 𝐶0-dense in higher dimensions; and
the result of Honda-Huang [34] states that the former set is non-empty and 𝐶0-dense. We thus
arrive at the following Palis-type conjecture in the spirit of Bonatti’s program.
Conjecture 18. The space of closed oriented hypersurfaces in a contact manifold p𝑌, 𝜉q decomposes into
two open and 𝐶2-dense sets: the set of convex hypersurfaces and the set of hypersurfaces containing a
robust positive-negative heterodimensional cycle 𝐶.
Phrased differently, Conjecture 18 states that positive-negative cycles are generically the only
obstruction to convexity.
In this paper, all of the positive-negative heterodimensional cycles 𝐶involved in the convexity
obstructions are coindex one. While they are not very well understood, robust heterodimensional
cycles of higher codimension are known to exist [2–4]. It is interesting to ask if there are examples
of robust positive-negative cycles of a different type.
Question 19. Are there examples of robust, positive-negative heterodimensional cycles of coin-
dex greater than one in the characteristic foliations of hypersurfaces in contact manifolds?
