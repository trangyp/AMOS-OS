LUCIEN HENNECART
Abstract. In this paper, we prove that both the seminilpotent and the fully nilpotent
CoHAs of A2 are commutative. This is a surprising result in strong contrast with the
CoHA of A2 without nilpotency conditions, previously studied by Davison, which is
related to the Lie algebra W1+∞of differential operators on C∗. The latter is highly
noncommutative. Our proof combines two constraints on the Lie bracket on the affinized
BPS Lie algebra: it is filtered with respect to the perverse filtration and it is graded
with respect to the cohomological degree. In the case of the Jordan quiver and nilpotent
CoHAs, these constraints force the Lie bracket to vanish. We also describe the equivariant
nilpotent CoHAs in the presence of the action of a one-dimensional torus rescaling the
first coordinate of A2 with weight 1 and the second with weight −1. In this case, one
obtains enveloping algebras of Rees Lie algebras associated with the nilpotent and the
seminilpotent filtrations on the Lie algebra W +
1+∞, reminiscent of the description of the
equivariant non-nilpotent CoHA given by Davison.

with the AGT conjectures. This is a particular case of CoHAs of preprojective algebras

of quivers [YZ18]. CoHAs have since become central objects, with applications in repre-
sentation theory, enumerative geometry and algebraic geometry with descriptions of the
cohomology of quiver varieties [DHM23], proof of the χ-independence conjecture of Toda
[Tod23] for symplectic surfaces [Dav+26], and proof of the P=W conjecture [Hau+22], to

CoHAs of quivers with potentials were defined around the same time as the CoHA of
A2, by Kontsevich and Soibelman in [KS11], in order to give a mathematical meaning to
the physics expectation predicting the existence of an algebra of BPS states [HM98]. The
CoHA of A2, and more generally the CoHA of the preprojective algebra of any quiver,
may be viewed as a CoHA of a quiver with potential via dimensional reduction [Dav17,
Appendix A], [RS17, Appendix by Ben Davison], see also [YZ20].
Despite their many applications, the computation of the algebraic structure of CoHAs
remains a very challenging question. These algebras are fully described by generators and
relations in only a few cases. One can mention the examples of finite type quivers, Jordan
quiver, and more generally cyclic quivers [Dav22; Jin24].
CoHAs come in several variants given by imposing various flavours of nilpotent condi-
tions (see [BSV20, §1.1] for a list). In this paper, we are interested in the two possible
nilpotent versions of the cohomological Hall algebra of A2, namely the fully nilpotent
and the seminilpotent CoHA, corresponding respectively to finite length sheaves on A2
set-theoretically supported on the origin or on the horizontal axis of A2.
The CoHA of A2 without nilpotency conditions, AA2, was studied by Davison in
[Dav22]. It is governed by a degeneration of the Lie algebra W1+∞(Section 1.2.2). We
briefly recall its structure. Let GrF

this paper gives this description for A2, which happens to be simpler than the CoHA of

Appendix A. Milnor–Moore theorem over rings
We recall the following useful lemma [Lan02, Appendix 2, p.880].
Lemma A.1. Let R be a principal ideal domain. Then, any submodule of a free R-module
is free.
We are now ready to prove a version of the Milnor–Moore theorem for algebras over
PIDs.


## Page 18


18
LUCIEN HENNECART
Proposition A.2. Let R be a Q-algebra that is a PID, A a connected cocommutative
R-bialgebra that is free as an R-module and P ⊆Prim(A) a submodule of the space of
primitive elements of A that generates A as an R-algebra. Then, A ∼= UR(Prim(A)).
Proof. It is clear that Prim(A) is a Lie algebra for the Lie bracket induced by the com-
mutator Lie bracket in A. Then, by universality of the enveloping algebra, there is a
natural morphism Φ: UR(Prim(A)) →A. Since P generates A as an algebra, so does
Prim(A).
Therefore, Φ is surjective.
Since A is a free R-module, so is Prim(A) by
Lemma A.1. By the PBW theorem for UR(Prim(A)) [Bou75, Chap. I, §2, no.7, Theo-
rem 1], since Prim(A) is a free R-module the R-module UR(Prim(A)) is also free over
R. In particular, it is torsion free. By tensoring with the fraction field K = Frac(R),
we obtain a surjective morphism ΦK : UR(Prim(A)) ⊗R K →A ⊗R K. Since enveloping
algebras commute with extensions of the base-ring [Bou75, Chap. 1, §2, no.9], we have
UR(Prim(A)) ⊗R K ∼= UK(Prim(A) ⊗R K). Moreover, by freeness of A and Prim(A),
the morphism Prim(A) ⊗R K →AK := A ⊗K is injective and its image consists only of
primitive elements, and so it factors through Prim(A ⊗K). The morphism ΦK therefore
factors as
UK(Prim(A) ⊗K) →UK(Prim(A ⊗K)) →AK .
By the Milnor–Moore theorem for AK, the second arrow is an isomorphism. The first
arrow is injective, and the composition is ΦK and is surjective. This implies that the
composition is an isomorphism and also Prim(A) ⊗R K ∼= Prim(A ⊗R K).
Let L = ker(Φ). Then, L ⊗R K = 0 and L ⊆UR(Prim(A)) is torsion-free by the PBW
theorem. Therefore, L = 0 and so Φ is an isomorphism.
