ASSOCIATED FINITE GROUP
LUCA CHIANTINI,  LUCJA FARNIK, GIUSEPPE FAVACCHIO, BRIAN HARBOURNE, JUAN MIGLIORE,
TOMASZ SZEMBERG, AND JUSTYNA SZPOND
Abstract. For any finite set L of 3 or more skew lines in P3
K over an algebraically closed field K of
arbitrary characteristic, there is a canonical associated subgroup GL of PGL2(K). Given a finite subgroup
G ⊂PGL2(K) we study which configurations of lines have GL = G. We derive an upper bound on the
number |L| of lines in terms of the order |G| of the group G and as an application we classify up to projective
equivalence which sets L in P3
C have GL = G for certain finite nonabelian groups G.
1. Introduction
This paper establishes a new problem in enumerative geometry and shows how work on classifying sets
of skew lines in P3
K over finite fields K (these are called spreads in the literature) can be carried over to any
field, including the complex numbers.
Let K be a field and let K denote its algebraic closure. The basic problem is to classify, up to projective
equivalence, finite sets of skew lines in P3
K over a field K. This is a finite classification problem when K is
finite, since there are only finitely many lines defined by pairs of linear forms in 4 variables over a finite field.
Whereas, over any field, there is only one projective equivalence class of sets of 3 skew lines, over C there
are, even for sets of only 4 skew lines, infinitely many projective equivalence classes. (Given a set L of 4
skew lines in P3
K, there is always a line T ⊂P3
K meeting all of the lines in L. The fact over C that there are
infinitely many projective equivalence classes of 4 skew lines is related to the fact that there are infinitely
many possible values of the cross ratio of the points of intersection of T with the 4 lines in L.)
However, as we explain in more detail below, for each finite set L of 3 or more skew lines in P3
K, there
is an associated group GL which is canonically a subgroup of Aut(L) for each L ∈L. (The group GL is
not finite in general, but it is finite if K is the algebraic closure of a finite field.) If we fix three skew lines
(which we refer to below as L∞, L0 and L1) and consider only sets L containing L∞, L0 and L1, then with
respect to a fixed isomorphism Aut(L) ∼= PGL2(K) and a fixed finite subgroup G ⊂PGL2(K) our main
result, Theorem 3.2, shows that there is a unique minimal finite set of lines MG in P3
K such that whenever
GL = G we always have L ⊆MG.
This shows that the well-studied problem of classifying spreads (i.e., sets of skew lines) over finite fields
can be carried over to any field simply by restricting to lines with a given finite group G ⊂PGL2(K). The
problem then becomes to classify subsets L′ of the finite set MG such that GL′ = G. One can ask all of the
same questions as is done with spreads over finite fields, but now in the context of having a specified group
G: what is the least number of lines for which we get a given group G, what is the maximum number, which
subsets of lines are maximal, which are minimal, how many subsets of lines of given cardinality are there up
to projective equivalence that have the given group G.
Given a finite set L = {L1, . . . , Ls} of s ≥3 pairwise skew lines in P3
K, the paper [2] associates to L a
groupoid GL whose objects are the lines of L. (Recall that a groupoid is a category for which the morphisms
are all invertible.) The basic morphisms are obtained from triples of skew lines: if Li, Lj, Lk are distinct and
p ∈Li, the plane spanned by p and Lk meets Lj in a unique point q, giving an isomorphism fijk : Li −→Lj

all configurations of skew lines L for which GL ∼= G. The matrix description above makes this a concrete
problem about finite sets of matrices whose classes, together with the classes of their differences, generate
the prescribed group.

group is trivial, thereby giving a geometric criterion for triviality [2].

3. A finiteness result on the lines associated to a given group
By [1, Proposition 4.1], finite subgroups of PGL2(K) of the same isomorphism type and of order prime to
the characteristic are conjugate. Thus by Remark 2.6, such a conjugation is realized, in the normalized matrix
model, by a projective change of coordinates. Hence, to study which sets of skew lines have group isomorphic
to a given group G, it is enough, up to projective equivalence, to fix one embedding G ⊂PGL2(K).
For this fixed subgroup G, Theorem 3.2 shows that there are only finitely many normalized configurations
L = {L∞, L0, LI2, ℓ4, . . . , ℓs} with GL = G.
