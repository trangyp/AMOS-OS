Exotic R4’s, RBG Links, and End Floer Homology
Sean Eli and Shunyu Wan
Abstract
We give the first pair of non-diffeomorphic exotic R4’s made by attaching diffeomorphic Casson han-
dles onto diffeomorphic disk complements. Our examples are obtained using the RBG link construction
to find slice knots with diffeomorphic slice disk complements, but whose Whitehead doubled disk com-
plements are not diffeomorphic. We distinguish the exotic R4’s using end Floer homology.
1
Introduction
In this paper we study exotic R4’s: smooth manifolds homeomorphic to R4 but not diffeomorphic to R4. This
bizarre, uniquely 4-dimensional phenomenon was first shown to occur in the 1980s, following the landmark
theorems of Donaldson and Freedman [3, 6]. Despite initial progress on the classification of exotic R4’s,
many elementary questions remain open [5, 7, 9, 10, 15].
We focus on the subfamily of slice R4’s, made by attaching a Casson handle to the boundary of a slice disk
complement and deleting the boundary [1, 13]. The way the smooth structure of a slice R4 depends on the
choices of disk complement and Casson handle is one of the main open questions about exotic R4’s, and has
seen considerable study [1, 5, 8, 13]. To date, all known examples that distinguish slice R4’s require varying
either the slice disk complement or the Casson handle. In particular, it is unknown whether diffeomorphic
disk complements and diffeomorphic Casson handles can be combined to produce non-diffeomorphic exotic
R4’s. Our main result gives the first example of this phenomenon. Thus, the smooth structure of a slice R4
depends not only on the choice of disk complement and Casson handle, but also on the gluing map.
Theorem 1.1. There exist non-diffeomorphic exotic slice R4’s made by attaching diffeomorphic Casson
handles onto diffeomorphic disk complements.
We prove Theorem 1.1 as follows. First, we use the unknotting number 1 RBG link technique introduced
by Piccirillo [14] (see also [11]) to construct slice knots K0 = m(820) and J0 with diffeomorphic 0-traces, but
different maximal Maslov gradings in knot Floer homology (see Proposition 3.2). By the trace embedding
lemma, it follows that K0 and J0 admit diffeomorphic slice disk complements in B4 (see Corollary 2.2).
We extend each disk complement to an exotic R4 by attaching the simplest Casson handle with all positive
clasps, CH+ [10], to a meridian of the deleted knot, and removing the remaining boundary. The resulting
slice R4’s are distinguished using graded end Floer homology [5, 8]. In the present setting, this invariant is
controlled by the maximal nontrivial Maslov gradings of the knot Floer homologies of K0 and J0, due to
recent work of Hom, Lidman, and the first author [5] (see Theorem 3.1).
Remark 1.2. In our example, the diffeomorphism S3
0(K0) ∼= S3
0(J0) coming from the RBG construction
does not send a meridian of K0 to a meridian of J0. Such a diffeomorphism would preserve the Casson handle
attaching region, and then the diffeomorphism of the disk complements would extend over the Casson handle.
In [5], it is shown that if K1 and K2 are nontrivial slice knots with different maximal Maslov gradings
in knot Floer homology, then the maximal gradings of HF +(S3
0(Wh(K1))) and HF +(S3
0(Wh(K2))) differ,
and this grading difference survives in the end Floer homology of the corresponding exotic R4’s. Hence, the
proof of Theorem 1.1 also gives the following phenomenon on the level of disk complements.
Theorem 1.3. There exist slice disks (DK0, K0) and (DJ0, J0) in (B4, S3) that have diffeomorphic com-
plements, but whose Whitehead doubled disk complements are non-diffeomorphic, detected by the maximal
nontrivial grading of HF + of their boundaries.

More generally, starting with an unknotting number 1 slice knot of genus g ≥2 often yields a pair of
exotic R4’s made from the same two pieces, as in Theorem 1.1. We confirm this for the knots m(89), 10129,
and m(11n42). The genus requirement follows from work of Kegel and Spreer [11] who show that this RBG
construction does not construct new knots from twisted Whitehead doubles.
Proposition 1.4. Let K be any of the unknotting number 1 slice knots m(820), m(89), 10129, or m(11n42).
Then K shares a slice disk complement with some 0-friend J, and the two exotic R4’s made by attaching
CH+ to a meridian of either deleted disk are not diffeomorphic.
Remark 1.5. In Proposition 1.4, various knots are mirrored in order to find a positive unknotting crossing.
See Figure 1. In general this procedure can also be done for negative crossings.
Based on our observations, we make a conjecture about \

Conjecture 1.5.1. Let K be a homologically thin slice knot with genus g ≥2, and unknotting number
1. Assume K has a positive unknotting crossing and let J be the corresponding Piccirillo friend. Then
\
HFK(J) is supported nontrivially in exactly g diagonals, shifted up in Maslov grading from K. Hence the
corresponding exotic R4 pairs are distinct.
In Section 2 we find J0 by applying the unknotting number 1 RBG construction to K0 = m(820), and show
these knots admit diffeomorphic slice disk complements. In Section 3 we prove Theorem 1.1, Theorem 1.3,
and Proposition 1.4. Throughout, we only use positive, untwisted Whitehead doubles, and CH+ denotes
the simplest Casson handle with all positive clasps.

Traces and disk complements
The 0-trace of a knot K is the 4-manifold constructed by attaching a 2-handle to B4 along K with the
0-framing. Given an unknotting number 1 knot K, Piccirillo’s RBG link technique produces another knot J
with the same 0-trace as K. See [11, 14] for details about this construction. The resulting knot J is called
a Piccirillo friend of K, as in [11]. Note K is slice if any only if any Piccirillo friend is slice, by the trace
embedding lemma.

Since K0 and J0 have diffeomorphic 0-traces, they admit diffeomorphic slice disk complements. This fact
is likely known to experts but we are not aware of a proof in the literature. We give a proof based on the
smooth trace embedding lemma, which states that a knot is smoothly slice if and only if its 0-trace embeds
smoothly in S4. Indeed, the complement of the trace embedded in S4 is the desired slice disk complement.
Theorem 2.1. Let K be a knot in S3 and X0(K) its 0-trace. Suppose i : X0(K) ,→S4 is a smooth embed-
ding. Then there exists a smooth slice disk (D, K) ,→(B4, S3) such that S4 \ int(i(X0(K))) is orientation-
reversing diffeomorphic to the slice disk complement B4 \ ν(D).
Proof. The 0-trace X0(K) consists of a 0-handle h0 union a 2-handle h2 attached along K ⊂∂h0 with the
0-framing. Since i : X0(K) ,→S4 is a smooth embedding, its restriction to the 0-handle i|h0 : h0 ,→S4 is a
smooth embedding of a standard compact ball in S4. Thinking of S4 as the double B4 ∪−B4, after smooth
isotopy we may assume i(h0) = B4. Then the complement of int(i(h0)) in S4 is −B4, and i(h2) is a tubular
neighborhood of i(C) in −B4, where C ⊂h2 is the core 2-disk. Since i(K) is the knot m(K) ⊂−B4, we see
an embedding of a slice disk (i(C), m(K)) ,→(−B4, −S3). Thus (−B4) \ ν(i(C)) is a slice disk complement
for m(K), but this is the same as S4 \ int(i(X0(K))).
Corollary 2.2. Suppose K, J are slice knots in S3 with X0(K) ∼= X0(J).
Then there exist slice disks
(DK, K) ,→(B4, S3), (DJ, J) ,→(B4, S3) such that B4 \ ν(DK) ∼= B4 \ ν(DJ).
Proof. Since K is slice, the trace embedding lemma gives a smooth embedding i : X0(K) ,→S4. Letting
ψ : X0(J) →X0(K) be a diffeomorphism, we find that j := i ◦ψ : X0(J) ,→S4 is a smooth embedding with
the same image as i. Applying Theorem 2.1 to i shows there is a smooth slice disk (DK, K) ,→(B4, S3) such
that S4 \ int(i(X0(K))) is diffeomorphic to B4 \ ν(DK). Applying Theorem 2.1 to j instead gives a smooth
slice disk (DJ, J) ,→(B4, S3) such that S4 \ int(j(X0(J))) is diffeomorphic to B4 \ ν(DJ). But then
B4 \ ν(DJ) ∼= S4 \ int(j(X0(J))) = S4 \ int(i(X0(K))) ∼= B4 \ ν(DK).

Theorem 3.1 (Theorem 1.1 of [5]). Let R be a slice R4 built by attaching CH+ to a slice disk complement
(B4, S3)\ν(D2, K) for a nontrivial slice knot K, along a 0-framed meridian of the deleted disk, and removing
the boundary. Then R is not diffeomorphic to R4. Moreover, if K1 and K2 are two nontrivial slice knots
whose knot Floer homology \
HFKred has different maximal nontrivial Maslov gradings, then the exotic R4’s
R1 and R2 built as above are not diffeomorphic with any choice of orientations.
In [5], Theorem 3.1 was used to distinguish exotic R4’s made by attaching CH+ to different slice disk
complements. Theorem 3.1 is proven by computing the graded end Floer homology of the end of R; the
maximal Maslov grading of K controls the maximal grading of HF +(S3
0(Wh(K)) and hence that of the
graded end Floer homology.
Proposition 3.2. Let K0 be the knot m(820), and J0 its Piccirillo friend from Figure 2. Then \
HFKred(K0)
has maximal nontrivial Maslov grading 2 and \
HFKred(J0) has maximal nontrivial Maslov grading 4.
Proof. Working with SnapPy inside Sage, we used Szab´o’s knot Floer calculator to compute \
HFK of both
knots, which are plotted in Figure 3. Inputting the left diagram of Figure 2 into SnapPy’s PLink editor, and
running the following commands, computes \

is where the RBG diagram should be entered.
L = snappy.Manifold()
L.dehn_fill([(0,1),(0,0),(0,1)])
K = L.filled_triangulation()
P = K.exterior_to_link()
P.knot_floer_homology()
Swapping the second line with
L.dehn_fill([(0,1),(-2,1),(0,0)])
and keeping the other lines the same gives \
HFK of J0. Note, SnapPy’s exterior to link() command recov-
ers a knot diagram from the given exterior, based on the algorithm of Dunfield-Obeidin-Rudd [4]. Since
the computation is performed with an oriented triangulation and specified peripheral curves, the resulting
diagram is determined up to this oriented exterior data; in particular, the procedure does not introduce an
ambiguity between a knot and its mirror. Since K0 and J0 are slice, we see that for both knots the maximal
nontrivial Maslov grading of \
HFKred is the same as that of \
