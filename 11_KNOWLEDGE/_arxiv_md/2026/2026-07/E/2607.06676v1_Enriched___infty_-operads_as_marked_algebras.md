ENRICHED ∞-OPERADS AS MARKED ALGEBRAS
MARKUS ZETTO
Abstract. We show that an enriched ∞-operad is completely determined by its category
of right modules together with a ‘marking’ of the representable modules. More precisely,
for any presentably monoidal ∞-category V we construct an equivalence between the
category of colored V-enriched ∞-operads and a certain full subcategory of the category
of presentably symmetric monoidal V-module ∞-categories equipped with a functor from
an ∞-groupoid. This effectively allows us to reduce many aspects of enriched ∞-operad
theory to the theory of presentably symmetric monoidal ∞-categories.
As an application, we describe a notion of univalence (or Rezk-completeness) for
enriched ∞-operads, and directly construct an equivalence between univalent S-enriched
∞-operads in our sense and Lurie’s model of ∞-operads in [Lur17]. We study envelopes
and categories of algebras for enriched ∞-operads and show that, in the S-enriched case,
the resulting notions agree in both models.

1. Introduction
The notion of a (colored) operad, or multicategory, generalizes that of a category by
allowing for k-ary multimorphisms with k sources and a single target, for any k ≥0. Operads
were originally introduced in algebraic topology to encode the algebraic structure carried
by iterated loop spaces [BV06], [May06]. The little cubes operads En introduced for this
purpose already indicated the need for a homotopy coherent notion of operads, as their
multimorphisms spaces are given by configuration spaces and not merely sets. Incorporating
this is one of the main achievements of the notion of ∞-operads introduced in [Lur17].
Many operads in nature however, most prominently the Lie operad and its derived or
spectral variants, do not only have spaces of multimorphisms but rather they are enriched:
their multimorphisms form objects in some symmetric monoidal category, for instance vector

1.3. Fissile categories. In stark contrast to the situation with enriched ∞-categories in
[RZ25], the condition of being a ⊗-atomic marking cannot be checked one object at a time,
i.e. y : X →M being ⊗-atomic is not equivalent to (but does imply) y(x) being ⊗-atomic
for all x ∈X. This is however true under the following additional assumption on M:
Definition 1.3 (Theorem 4.8). A presentably symmetric monoidal V-module ∞-category
M is called fissile if the unit V →M and the multiplication map ⊗: M ⊗V M →M are left
adjoint 1-morphisms in CAlg(RModV(Pr)).
Remark 1.4. This is very similar, but somewhat orthogonal to the notion of rigidity
in [GR17, Chapter 1, §9], where the multiplication is required to admit an adjoint as a
V-V-bimodule map.
We show in Theorem 4.15 that given a fissile category M, a functor y : X →M is a
⊗-atomic marking iff for every x ∈X, the object y(x) ∈M is ⊗-atomic. A key example of a
fissile category is Fun(Sym X, V), since its multiplication is obtained by applying the free
functor P Sym(−) ⊗V to the fold map ∇: X ⊔X →X. This generalizes to the following
theorem:
Theorem B (Theorem 4.16). For every V-enriched operad O, the operadic presheaf category
P⊗
V(O) is fissile. In particular, the image of the functor from Theorem A may equivalently
be characterized as consisting of those functors y : X →M such that
• M is fissile,
• Every x ∈X is sent to a ⊗-atomic object in M,
• The image of y generates M under colimits, V-tensoring and symmetric monoidal
structure.
Remark 1.5. The notion of fissile categories is not technically necessary for most results in
this paper. The reason why it is useful to separate from the notion of being ⊗-atomically
generated is that it only refers to M, and not the existence of some functor X →M. Where
we do require it is for our discussion of colimits over enriched ∞-operads in Theorem 4.23,
and in future work [Zet] to define the Cauchy-completion of an enriched ∞-operad, see
Theorem 7.14.

1.5. Organization of the paper. In Section 2, we define enriched ∞-operads as monads in
the (∞, 2)-category CAlg(RModV(Pr)), and develop some basic intuition and constructions.
Section 3 is concerned with studying internally left adjoint 1-morphisms as well as monadic
1-morphisms in CAlg(RModV(Pr)), including criteria for characterizing and methods to
construct them.
We use this theory in Section 4 to give an alternative description of
enriched ∞-operads using marked algebras, and introduce the notion of fissile categories.
In Section 5 we compare S-enriched ∞-operads with Lurie’s notion of ∞-operads, which
involves the notion of univalence. Section 6 is about enriched envelopes, and Section 7 defines
categories of algebras and for instance examines limits, colimits and free algebras. Finally,
Appendix A constructs the (∞, 2)-category CAlg(RModV(Pr)) we make use of throughout
the paper, establishing several of its properties we use in the main text like the existence of
Eilenberg-Moore objects.
1.6. Relation to other work. In [Hau21], Haugseng proves that ∞-operads in the sense of
Lurie are equivalent to algebras in S-enriched symmetric sequences. However, his construction
of the composition product differs from the one obtained by identifying symmetric sequences
with an endomorphism object in CAlg(RModV(Pr)), and he does not supply a comparison
between the two. As he notes in §1.2, this left open the question of whether this notion of
enriched ∞-operads agrees with this more widely used one. During the preparation of this
work, both monoidal structures were compared in the single-colored case by [Ara26].
There is further unpublished work on our comparison result for S-enriched ∞-operads in
the single-colored case: Lukas Brantner and Gijs Heuts [BH] are preparing a proof using the
theory of analytic monads. Also Thomas Blom, Connor Malin and Niall Taggart [BMT] give
a proof that is closely related to ours and describe Koszul duality in their formalism. Finally,
Shaul Barkan and Jan Steinebrunner were considering a similar framework that extends to
colored enriched ∞-properads, see also the outlook of [BS26].

As a reminder, for D a 2-category and d, d′ ∈D, the category HomD(d, d′) is a left module over
EndD(d′) and a right module over EndD(d) via post- and precomposition respectively. Given

HomD(d, x) ≃LModT (HomD(d, d′))
for any d ∈D. In other words, x represents the Cat-enriched presheaf LModT (HomD(−, d′)).

g : d →y exibits y as a Kleisli object of S if composition with it lifts to an equivalence
HomD(y, d′) ≃RModS(HomD(d, d′))
for any d′ ∈D. See the proof of Theorem A.30 for another perspective.
