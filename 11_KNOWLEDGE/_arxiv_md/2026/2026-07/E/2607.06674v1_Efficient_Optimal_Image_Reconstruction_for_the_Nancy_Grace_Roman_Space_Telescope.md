ABSTRACT
The forthcoming Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope will revolutionize astrophysics by generating
huge amounts of data of unprecedented quality. To properly address the data deluge and fully realize
its potential, analysis tools that are both efficient and optimal are needed. Effortless (previously
known as Fast Imcom) is a new algorithm for linear image reconstruction. Like its predecessor Imcom,
it offers control over point spread functions in output images; by avoiding laborious calculations, it
is tens of times faster and has a smaller memory footprint. In this paper, I apply Effortless to
simulated Roman images and present promising first results. With ideal point sources, I illustrate
that a single image reconstructed by Effortless, combined with the post-measurement calibration
procedure described in a companion paper, can lead to better measurements than a set of ∼6 images
coadded by Imcom. While both algorithms were originally designed for weak gravitational lensing
cosmology, Effortless can benefit studies of static features and dynamic aspects of the Universe
alike. Moreover, the efficiency and interpretability of Effortless provides new possibilities for further
reducing errors in measurements. The implementation of Effortless is detailed in the companion
paper.

and hyperparameter optimization have made it even
more efficient. Besides, the CCAPP condo8 at the Ohio
Supercomputer Center (O. S. Center 1987) has been mi-
grated from the Pitzer cluster ( Ohio Supercomputer
Center 2018) to the more powerful Cardinal cluster (
Ohio Supercomputer Center 2024).

Medium and Wide Tiers of Roman HLIS (with a typical
coverage of ∼6 per band). While introducing Effort-

In most cases, the differences between them (lower

Like in the itera-

The reconstructed versions of input white

impact of bad input pixels, while Effortless simply

work (but see M. Yamamoto et al. 2024; K. Laliotis et al.
2024, for some previous work).
Figure 2 presents two layers of individual images re-
constructed by Effortless. These images are in the
Y106 band, which is the bluest band for most of the
Roman weak lensing program and thus is subject to the
most severe undersampling effects (smallest wavelength-
to-aperture diameter ratio λ/D for the same native pixel
scale). It is worth emphasizing that Effortless can
project individual images onto a common pixel grid and
unify the PSFs; such capability (which Imcom is not
thought to have9) enables pixel-by-pixel comparisons be-
tween different images for obtaining time-domain infor-
mation or removing some exposure-specific errors, al-
though the efficacy of such comparisons can be limited
by PSF residuals due to imperfections of the image re-
construction process. From the distributions of masked
output pixels (shown in white), one can easily see the
positions of bad input pixels and the boundaries of Ro-
man detectors; but outside the corresponding regions,
the Effortless reconstruction is robust against the un-
availability of some input pixels.
The left half of Figure 2 shows injected stars in sym-
metric logarithmic scale. In such a scale, one can clearly
see square edges and diffraction spikes in the outer re-
gions of injected stars, as well as alternating positive
and negative features in the central parts. PyImcom
injects these point sources by drawing (resampled) PSF
images onto that layer. Due to the relative small size
of the PSF images (322 native pixels in K. Cao et al.
(2026a) and this work), the square edges are prominent.
These edges are not a physical feature of Roman PSFs
and are already present in the 'gsstar14' before the re-
construction process. Outer residuals, especially diffrac-
tion spikes, of bright objects (usually stars in the Milky
Way) are worth removing so that their faint neighbors
can be measured; this can be done via either simply
increasing the window size for selecting input pixels or
subtracting properly scaled PSFs from input images (E.
Macbeth et al. 2026, in preparation). Since the former
leads to higher computational costs and may not be ro-
bust against geometric distortions, the latter is probably
preferred; Effortless can simplify and accelerate the

(taken from K. Cao et al. 2026a). While the errors are
within the requirements for the Roman weak gravita-
tional lensing cosmology program (M. Yamamoto et al.
2024),12 they are up to an order of magnitude larger
than those based on Effortless outputs.
Since the
high quality of Effortless results relies on a post-
measurement calibration based on the subpixel positions
of injected stars in different input images, I tentatively
conclude that it may make more sense to conduct mea-
surements on individual reconstructed images (where
PSF residuals are better understood) and combine the
results, rather than to conduct measurements on com-
bined images. A detailed description of the calibration
process and a more comprehensive view of the analysis
results will be presented in K. Cao (2026, in prepara-
tion).
Before concluding this section, it is worth emphasiz-
ing that the above results are based on noiseless point
sources drawn using the same input PSFs that are used
for image reconstruction. Future work is needed to en-
able precise and accurate measurements of noisy ex-
tended sources (like galaxies in real-world observations)
without PSFs known a priori. Nevertheless, K. Cao &
Roman HLIS Cosmology PIT (2026) and this paper lay
the foundation for efficient and optimal image analysis
using Effortless.
4. DISCUSSION
In this paper, I have applied Effortless (EFFicient
Optimal image ReconsTruction using LESS memory,
previously known as Fast Imcom; K. Cao & Roman
HLIS Cosmology PIT 2026), a new algorithm for lin-
ear image reconstruction, to realistic simulated Roman
images ( OpenUniverse et al. 2025). With the same in-

Devising a

puts and target outputs as a suite of previous tests of
PyImcom (B. Rowe et al. 2011; C. M. Hirata et al. 2024;
M. Yamamoto et al. 2024; K. Cao et al. 2025, 2026a),
I have found that Effortless is 50–60 times faster for
the Medium and Wide Tiers of Roman HLIS (R. Obser-
vations Time Allocation Committee & C. Community
Survey Definition Committees 2025). With ideal point
sources measured by the HSM (C. Hirata & U. Seljak
2003; R. Mandelbaum et al. 2005) module of GalSim
(B. T. P. Rowe et al. 2015), I have illustrated that a
single image reconstructed by Effortless, combined
with a simple post-measurement calibration procedure,
can lead to better measurements than a set of ∼6 im-
ages coadded by Imcom.
In addition to being a more efficient and accurate suc-
cessor to Imcom, Effortless opens up a myriad of new
possibilities. For example, the capability of projecting
individual images onto a common pixel grid and unify-
ing the PSFs enables pixel-by-pixel comparisons between
different epochs. Other possibilities include but are not
limited to semi-analytic models for understanding prop-
erties of reconstructed images, simple calculations for
optimization of the tiling pattern, and iterative correc-
tions to astrometry, flux calibration, and PSF modeling.
See Section 6.2 of K. Cao & Roman HLIS Cosmology
PIT (2026) for a fuller discussion about potential appli-
cations of Effortless. Meanwhile, to enable reliable
measurement of noisy extended sources (instead of ideal
point sources), some challenges remain to be overcome
(see Section 6 of K. Cao 2026, in preparation). These
are important directions for future work regarding Ef-
fortless.
