EXISTENCE OF TWO EMBEDDED MINIMAL SPHERES IN S3 WITH AN
ARBITRARY METRIC
ZHICHAO WANG AND XIN ZHOU
Abstract. We prove that S3 endowed with an arbitrary Riemannian metric g admits at least
two embedded minimal spheres. The proof is based on an iterative scheme of relative min-max
constructions.
1. Introduction
In 1982, S. T. Yau posed the following problem [44]:
Problem 1 ([44, Problem 89]). Prove that there are four distinct embedded minimal spheres in
any (Riemannian) manifold diﬀeomorphic to S3 (the 3-dimensional sphere).
Let (S3, g) denote an arbitrary Riemannian manifold diﬀeomorphic to S3. In this paper, we
establish the following result (see Theorem 6.3, where the theorem is restated and proved):
Theorem A. There exist at least two distinct embedded minimal spheres in (S3, g).
Shortly after Yau posed this problem, Simon–Smith [36] proved the existence of at least
one embedded minimal sphere in any (S3, g). Their proof utilized a variant of the min-max
theory for minimal hypersurfaces pioneered by Almgren [1,2] and Pitts [30]; (see also [34] and
[10]). It is also worth noting that branched immersed minimal spheres were obtained earlier by
Sacks–Uhlenbeck [32] via a min-max theory for harmonic maps; (see also [11]).
Although there are naturally four distinct families of embedded spheres that can be plugged
into the Simon–Smith min-max theory to construct embedded minimal spheres, the issue of
integer multiplicity poses a signiﬁcant obstacle to establishing the existence of a second minimal
sphere. For metrics with positive Ricci curvature, White [43] employed degree-theoretic methods
to prove the existence of at least two embedded minimal spheres, and at least four when the
metric is suﬃciently close to the round metric. More recently, Haslhofer–Ketover [17] proved
the existence of at least two embedded minimal spheres for bumpy metrics by demonstrating
that the second width has multiplicity one.
Building on these developments, the authors [40] very recently proved a multiplicity-one
theorem for the Simon–Smith min-max theory, resolving Yau’s conjecture for (S3, g) when g has
positive Ricci curvature or when g is bumpy. We remark that this work utilized the regularity
results established by Sarnataro–Stryker [33] and the authors [39]. In the present paper, we
extend these advancements to prove the existence of at least two embedded minimal spheres
for all Riemannian 3-spheres. Notably, this marks the ﬁrst time that more than one embedded
minimal sphere has been constructed for general metrics, advancing the general problem for the
ﬁrst time since Simon–Smith [36] found the initial minimal sphere over 40 years ago.
More recently, the Simon–Smith min-max theory has seen extensive study, not only concerning
the existence of minimal surfaces with controlled topology but also in the context of broader
problems in geometric topology. Building on the multiplicity-one theorem established by the

1.1. Diﬃculties and ideas of the proof. Recall that the Simon–Smith min-max theory
always yields at least one embedded minimal sphere, denoted by Σ0.
When Σ0 is a stable
minimal sphere with contracting neighborhoods ([37, Page 883]) — that is, a neighborhood
foliated by surfaces with their mean curvature vectors pointing towards Σ0, — we can cut S3
along Σ0 and construct embedded minimal spheres on both sides by applying an argument from
[37]. In what follows, we only consider the case where Σ0 has an expanding neighborhood (that
is, a small neighborhood foliated by constant mean curvature surfaces whose mean curvature
vectors point away from Σ0).
We ﬁrst recall several classical methods for constructing additional minimal spheres and
explain why they fail for a general Riemannian metric.
Recall that there exist four diﬀerent classes of sweepouts (with min-max widths L1 ≤L2 ≤
L3 ≤L4) on the space of embedded spheres in S3 [40, §8.3]. Moreover, by Lusternik–Schnirelmann
theory, it suﬃces to consider the case where
L1 < L2 < L3 < L4.
However, the possibility of higher multiplicity in the Simon–Smith min-max theory becomes the
major barrier in constructing the second minimal sphere. In prior literature, there are three
main methods utilized to construct additional solutions:
• Degree theory. White [43] deﬁned a degree for the space of embedded minimal spheres in
S3 with positive Ricci curvature. Nevertheless, this degree may not be well-deﬁned for
a general S3 due to the lack of compactness of the space of embedded minimal spheres.
• Multiplicity-one theorems. In our previous work [40], we proved a multiplicity-one the-
orem for unstable components produced by the Simon–Smith min-max theory, which
implies the existence of at least four minimal spheres in a Riemannian 3-sphere with
positive Ricci curvature. We also note that Haslhofer–Ketover [17] used catenoid es-
timates (proved by Ketover–Marques–Neves [22]) to establish multiplicity one for the
second min-max width if S3 has positive Ricci curvature. However, in another previous
work [41], we constructed metrics on S3 such that L2 can only be realized by Σ0 with
multiplicity two. In other words, it is impossible to obtain the second minimal sphere
by expecting multiplicity one to hold for all general metrics.
• Symmetry of S3. There are also constructions [14] of embedded minimal spheres on
ellipsoids, but heavily relying on the symmetries of the ellipsoids.

1.2. Organization. This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we set up notations and
review essential lemmas from geometric measure theory. We also state two relative Simon–Smith
min-max theorems that will be applied later. In Section 3, we construct a family of spheres and
present fundamental estimates for the area of the connecting necks. In Section 4, we establish
upper bounds for the area of this family as well as related properties. In Section 5, we smooth

that our Lipschitz family of spheres can be appropriately approximated by a smooth family.
