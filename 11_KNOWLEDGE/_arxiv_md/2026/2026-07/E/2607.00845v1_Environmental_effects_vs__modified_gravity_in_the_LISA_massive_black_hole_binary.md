Environmental effects vs. modified gravity in the LISA massive black hole binary
population
Lorenzo Copparoni and Enrico Barausse
SISSA, Via Bonomea 265, 34136 Trieste, Italy and INFN Sezione di Trieste and
IFPU - Institute for Fundamental Physics of the Universe, Via Beirut 2, 34014 Trieste, Italy
Gravitational-wave signals from massive black hole binaries observed by LISA can carry imprints
of both the astrophysical environment of the source and possible deviations from general relativity.
We investigate whether environmental effects leave a detectable imprint on the LISA binary popula-
tion, and whether they can mimic modified-gravity effects with the same frequency dependence. As
representative channels we adopt accretion and viscous migration in a circumbinary disk for the en-
vironmental sector, and a time-varying Newton constant ˙G for the modified-gravity sector. All three
effects enter the waveform at the same negative post-Newtonian order and are described, at leading
order, by a common phase-deformation parameter, which makes them formally degenerate at the
single-event level. Combining Fisher-matrix forecasts with a hierarchical nested-sampling analysis
of synthetic catalogs from astrophysically motivated population models, we find that, even under
extreme astrophysical assumptions—an active fraction of 50%, together with a super-Eddington
accretion tail—the population-level posteriors remain fully compatible with vacuum. However, a
hierarchical population-wide analysis may yield a non-trivial upper limit on the active fraction
and a mild lower bound on the slope of the Eddington-ratio distribution. Environmental effects
are therefore unlikely to bias LISA’s tests of general relativity with massive black hole binaries in
astrophysically realistic scenarios.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The direct detection of gravitational waves (GWs)
from compact-binary coalescences [1] has turned GW
astronomy into a powerful probe of both astrophysics
and fundamental physics. On the astrophysical side, the
growing catalog of events from the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA
(LVK) collaboration [2, 3] has yielded a wealth of in-
formation on the formation channels, evolutionary histo-
ries and host environments of stellar-mass compact bi-
naries [4–6]. On the fundamental physics side, the ob-
served waveforms—spanning the full inspiral, merger and
ringdown—can be compared in detail with the predic-
tions of general relativity (GR) in its strong-field, highly-
dynamical regime, providing some of the most precise
tests of the theory available to date [7–11]. In the next
decade, the space-based Laser Interferometer Space An-
tenna (LISA) [12, 13] will extend the frequency range of
GW astronomy to the mHz regime, where the expected
primary sources are massive black hole binaries (MB-
HBs) with total masses in the 104–107 M⊙range [14–
18], extreme mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) of stellar-
mass compact objects into central massive black holes
(MBHs) [19, 20], and the early inspiral of stellar-origin
black hole binaries (SOBHBs) [21–23].
At even lower
(nHz) frequencies, pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) have been
observing [24–28] a possible stochastic background of
GWs from MBHBs of mass ≳108M⊙.
The gravitational waveform of an inspiralling binary
is never, in a real astrophysical system, that of a clean
two-body system in vacuum. Gas accretion onto the com-
ponents and dynamical friction/migration torques from
the surrounding gas [23, 29–36] can leave imprints on
the binary’s phase evolution, whose magnitude can be-
come comparable to the radiation-reaction term at low
frequencies. A growing body of work has now shown that
such environmental imprints are in principle detectable
with LISA across a number of source classes, from in-
termediate mass-ratio and EMRI systems [29, 30, 33–36]
to SOBHBs [31, 32] and stochastic backgrounds [23]. For
PTAs, the effects of the surrounding environment (stellar
hardening and triple massive black hole interactions) are
expected to be even more significant [37], possibly ex-
plaining the flatter-than-expected background observed
in the nHz band [24–28].
For MBHBs in the LISA band the situation is more nu-
anced. Order-of-magnitude arguments [30] indicate that
the bulk of these sources should be only weakly affected
(if at all) by their astrophysical environment. It has more
recently been pointed out, however, that this conclusion
may break down at the lower-mass end of the population,
≲105 M⊙[38, 39]. Moreover, even when they are small
in absolute terms, environmental effects can still bias the
tests of GR that LISA MBHBs are expected to enable
with unprecedented precision.
Accretion and gas drag
enter the waveform phase at negative post-Newtonian
(PN) orders, and so do many of the best-motivated mod-
ifications of GR. Both classes of effects therefore affect
the same low-frequency, early-inspiral portion of the sig-
nal. Examples of such GR modifications include a secu-
lar variation of Newton’s constant [40, 41], scalar-dipole
radiation, and violations of the strong equivalence prin-
ciple [42].
In this work we focus on a particularly evident re-
alization of this overlap: a GR modification and a set
of environmental effects that all enter the inspiral phase
at exactly the same −4 PN order, and are therefore for-
mally indistinguishable on a single source. As the GR-
modification channel we adopt a secular drift of the grav-
itational constant,
˙G/G [40, 41].

In the environmental sector, we con-

angular-momentum conservation, induces a −4 PN de-

The second is gas-driven migration, i.e.
the torque exerted on the binary by the disk, which con-
tributes at the same −4 PN order, with the same fre-
quency dependence as accretion but a different overall
prefactor. At leading order, all three effects are therefore
captured by a single −4 PN deformation of the inspiral
phase, and the signal of an individual MBHB cannot dis-
tinguish among them. The two classes of effects, however,
behave differently across a population of sources: envi-
ronmental contributions are expected to vary in magni-
tude from one binary to another, whereas a putative ˙G
drift is universal. A hierarchical analysis of the full pop-
ulation of LISA MBHBs can therefore in principle still
disentangle the two, in analogy with what has been pro-
posed for EMRIs [45].
To answer these questions we combine single-event
Fisher-matrix forecasts with a hierarchical Bayesian anal-
ysis [46] of synthetic MBHB catalogs drawn from astro-
physically motivated population models, spanning both
heavy-seed (HS) and light-seed (LS) formation chan-
nels [47]. The most informative sources turn out to be
the lowest-mass MBHBs at z ≲1, which constitute a
small subset of any realistic population. Even under ex-
treme astrophysical assumptions—an active fraction of
50% together with a tail of the Eddington-ratio distribu-
tion extending well into the super-Eddington regime—
the population-level posteriors remain fully compatible
with vacuum, although they do yield a non-trivial upper
limit on the active fraction and a mild lower bound on
the slope of the Eddington-ratio distribution.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section II reviews the −4 PN dephasing induced by ˙G
and by accretion and viscous migration, and introduces
a common agnostic phase-deformation parameter. Sec-
tion III describes the MBHB population models that
we consider, computes the corresponding Fisher-matrix
parameter-estimation errors across the mass–redshift
plane, and presents per-event parameter-estimation re-
sults on specific 10 yr realizations.
Section IV em-
beds the single-event measurements in a hierarchical
Bayesian framework, derives the ˙G constraint, and per-
forms population-level model comparisons between the
environmental, vacuum and modified-gravity hypotheses.
Throughout the paper we adopt geometrized units with
GN = c = 1 (with GN the present day Newton constant)
unless otherwise stated.
II.
MODIFICATIONS AT −4 PN ORDER
Rather than introducing a dedicated template for
each candidate effect, we adopt the parameterized post-
Einsteinian (ppE) framework [48], in which a single pa-
rameter captures the dominant phase deformation at
a given PN order.
For comparable-mass binaries near
merger—the regime probed most precisely by current
LVK tests of GR—negative-PN corrections of the kind
considered here are subdominant to radiation reaction.
However, at the lower orbital frequencies accessible to
LISA MBHBs, these corrections become, as discussed in
Sec. I, a natural target. We specialize the ppE template
to the ˙G case in Sec. II A, to accretion and viscous migra-
tion in Sec. II B, and present the resulting Fisher-matrix
parameter-estimation results across parameter space in
Sec. III B.
A.

active galactic nuclei (AGN) [53, 54], a duty cycle that we
will use in Sec. IV as order of magnitude for the active-

Galaxy mergers generically drive gas inflows toward the
galactic nucleus [55–57], so MBHBs can plausibly be
embedded in a gas-rich environment in which the sur-
rounding material settles into a circumbinary disk [58].

III.
POPULATION MODELS AND
SINGLE-EVENT ANALYSIS
The parameter space spanned by LISA MBHBs covers
several orders of magnitude in both chirp mass and dis-
tance, and the number of detected events in any given re-
gion of this space depends sensitively on the assumed as-
trophysical history of the MBHB population. We there-
fore rely on a suite of semi-analytic population models,
each encoding different choices for the seeding mecha-
nism, the delay time between the galaxy merger and the
subsequent MBH merger, and the impact of supernova
(SN) feedback on MBH accretion. The catalogs produced
by these models are the basis for the detectability and
bias estimates presented in the rest of this section.
A.
Population models
The formation history of MBHBs is not well con-
strained observationally, and plausible evolutionary se-
quences spanning high redshift to the present yield sub-
stantially different catalog realizations.
We adopt the
semi-analytic model for the formation and evolution of
galaxies and MBHs originally introduced in [16]. Addi-
tions and improvements to specific aspects of the model
were subsequently introduced in [17, 18, 37, 62, 63]. The
catalogs are publicly available as part of the BACH data
release [64].
We consider five population models, three HS and
two LS. In the HS class, the black-hole seeds arise from
the collapse of proto-galactic disks induced by bar in-
stabilities [65], with typical seed masses of the order of
∼105 M⊙:
• HS-nod-SN (from [18]):
no delay between the
galaxy merger and the MBH merger (except for
the dynamical-friction timescale, including tidal ef-
fects, between dark-matter halos), and including
the effect of SN feedback on MBH accretion;
• HS-nod-SN-high-accr (from [18, 47]): as above, but
with the MBH accretion rate boosted by a factor
of ∼4;
• Q3-nod (from [14]): an earlier HS realization with
no delay and no SN feedback on accretion.
In the LS class, the seeds form instead from the remnants
of population III stars in high-redshift, low-metallicity
systems [66]:
• LS-nod-SN (from [18]):
no delay between the
galaxy merger and the MBH merger (except for
the dynamical-friction timescale, including tidal ef-
fects, between dark-matter halos), and including
the effect of SN feedback on MBH accretion;
• popIII-d (from [14]): an earlier LS realization that
includes, on top of the dynamical-friction timescale
with tidal effects between dark-matter halos, fur-
ther delay contributions from stellar hardening,
triple-MBH interactions, and gas-driven migration.
Together, these five population models span a represen-
tative range of astrophysically motivated assumptions
about the seeding mechanism and accretion history of
MBHs across cosmic time.

IV.
POPULATION-LEVEL INFERENCE
For single events, the precision to which a negative-PN
contribution can be measured is limited. The different
physical effects considered in this work leave essentially
the same imprint: they all induce a −4 PN dephasing
and are therefore degenerate at the level of an individ-
ual source. Information at the population level, however,
can break part of this degeneracy. A putative drift of
Newton’s constant is expected to act in the same way
on every source, whereas environmental effects act only
on the fraction of binaries embedded in a gaseous envi-
ronment, with disk properties that vary from event to
event [70].

V.
CONCLUSION
In this work we investigated whether the astrophysical
environment of LISA MBHBs could leave a detectable
imprint at the population level, and whether it could
mimic—and thereby bias—LISA’s tests of GR. We fo-
cused on three −4 PN deviations from vacuum templates:
gas accretion onto the components, viscous migration
in a circumbinary disk, and a secular drift of Newton’s
constant. All three are formally degenerate for a single
source, but the degeneracy is broken at the population
level: a ˙G drift is universal across the catalog, whereas
the environmental effects act only on a fraction of bi-
naries and with a magnitude that varies from event to
event.
Across the five population models considered, the most
informative sources are low-mass MBHBs at low redshift,
which are rare irrespective of the seeding model. Even
at the optimistic LISA 10 yr mission duration, no event
is individually sensitive to accretion at realistic Edding-
ton ratios: at fEdd = 1, vacuum-template recovery shifts
no event’s chirp mass by more than 10% of the statisti-
cal error. Viscous migration is a more promising channel
because of its stronger η−2 mass-ratio scaling: up to ∼8
events per year in the HS models admit a single-event
measurement with σfEdd < 10 at fiducial ξ = 1, and at
the optimistic thin-disk value ξ ∼10 a few HS-catalog
events per year can exhibit a vacuum-template chirp-
mass bias at or above the statistical error. This handful
of relatively noisy detections is, however, not enough to
drive a population-level preference for an environmental
signature.
At the population level we compared three recov-

vacuum, a hierarchical environmen-
tal model parameterized by the active fraction λ and
the power-law slope α of the Eddington-ratio distribu-

For

σG1 ∼10−7 yr−1 across all the catalogs considered—
significantly better than current bounds from ground-
based GW observations, but several orders of magni-
tude weaker than the Solar-System limit and than what
can be reached with longer-lived LISA sources such as
EMRIs and SOBHBs.
For the environmental hypoth-
esis, all the injections considered—including a viscous-
migration injection with active fraction λ = 0.5 and a
super-Eddington Eddington-ratio tail (fmax = 10)—yield
population posteriors that are fully compatible with vac-
uum, although they do produce a non-trivial upper limit
on λ and a 2σ lower bound on α. A marked Bayes-factor
preference for the modified-gravity hypothesis emerges
only for injected G1 several orders of magnitude above
the Solar-System limit; such values are not yet excluded
in the strong-field, dynamical regime probed by ground-
based GW observations, but no realistic theoretical mo-
tivation singles them out.
Taken together, these results indicate that, under as-
trophysically realistic assumptions, environmental con-
tamination of LISA’s tests of GR with MBHBs is
negligible—and, symmetrically, that a population-wide
detection of an environmental imprint in LISA MBHBs
is unlikely in any of the population scenarios we consid-
ered. One important caveat is in order: our analysis is
restricted to circular inspirals, while recent works [77, 78]
have shown that environmental dephasing can be sub-
stantially amplified in eccentric orbits.
Extending the
present population-level framework to eccentric MBHBs
would simultaneously sharpen the prospects for an envi-
ronmental detection and the corresponding risk of ˙G-test
bias, and we leave this investigation to future work.
