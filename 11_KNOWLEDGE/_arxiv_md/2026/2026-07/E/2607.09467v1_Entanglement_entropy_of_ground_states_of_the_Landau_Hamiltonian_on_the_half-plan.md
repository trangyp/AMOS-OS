ENTANGLEMENT ENTROPY OF GROUND STATES OF THE LANDAU
HAMILTONIAN ON THE HALF-PLANE
PAUL PFEIFFER AND WOLFGANG SPITZER
Abstract. We study the entanglement entropy (EE) of ground states of a Hamiltonian defined
on a domain with a boundary. Surprisingly, boundary conditions can change the spectrum and the
nature of the spectrum drastically but not the leading behaviour of the EE. As is well-known, the
Landau Hamiltonian on the full plane has pure point spectrum (the infinitely degenerate Landau
levels) and ground states display a so-called strict area law.
On the other hand, the Landau
Hamiltonian on the half-plane has purely absolutely continuous spectrum and yet we prove a strict
area law for its ground states.
We raise the question of what extra or finer conditions on the
absolutely continuous spectrum are necessary to guarantee a logarithmically enhanced area-law as
we have for the Laplace operator.

1. Motivation
There have been a lot of efforts devoted to the study of entanglement entropy (EE) in general and
to the scaling of this entropy for ground states of many-particle fermionic systems. Even the simplest,
non-interacting systems pose considerable mathematical challenges and lead to interesting questions.
These many-particle systems are completely described by operators H on the corresponding one-
particle Hilbert-space such as the Laplace operator, ´∆, or the Landau operator on L2pΩq, for some
fixed spatial domain ΩĂ Rd. In our case, Ωwill be the half-plane R` ˆ R, which is (cone) invariant
under scaling, that is, L ¨ ΩĂ Ωfor L ą 0. A ground state (of the many-particle) system on the full
configuration space Ωis described by the spectral projection of H, Pµ “ 1pH ď µq for some µ P R.
Such a state is then spatially localized to LΛ, see (2.2). Then we ask about the behaviour of the EE
of this localized state as L becomes large. Since the EE of this state localized to LΛ is identical to

leading order in L with the surface area of the boundary, that is, |BpLΛq| “ Ld´1|BΛ|. If this is the
case we call this a strict area-law (scaling) of EE. There are other notable cases where the EE scales
differently to leading order, namely as Ld´1 lnpLq|BΛ| and this we call a logarithmically enhanced
area-law (scaling). Yet different (faster and slower) scaling behaviours are known for ground states
of quantum spin chain models.
The simplest cases of the Laplace operator or Landau operator on the full Euclidean space Rd
have been studied, see [4,7,10,12,18]. In the case of the Laplace operator one has a purely absolutely
continuous spectrum with a slow decay of the integral kernel of the spectral projection Pµ. This leads
to a logarithmically enhanced area-law. It is relatively simple to deduce a strict leading area-law
bound if the integral kernel of Pµ decays exponentially in all directions. To deduce the precise scaling
in all cases is still another matter. The two-dimensional Landau operator has pure point spectrum
with an exponentially decaying integral kernel Pµ and thus we see a strict area-law.
Building on these results, stability results have been proved for perturbations by magnetic or
electric potentials, see [14, 16]. In [19], we studied the transition between a strict area law and a
logarithmically enhanced area-law as we let the magnetic field tend to 0, or in [11], where we studied
the same transition as the temperature T of an equilibrium state (instead of a ground state) tends
to 0.
In [20], the one-dimensional Laplace operator H “ ´∆` V with a periodic potential V was
studied. It is well-known that the spectrum of H is purely absolutely continuous and one expects a
logarithmically enhanced area-law for the found states Pµ “ 1pH ď µq. This turns out to be true
(for all µ inside the spectrum) and, surprisingly, the leading coefficient of lnpLq does not depend on
the potential V , that is, it is the same as for the one-dimensional Laplace operator. The case of a
periodic potential in higher-dimension is unknown.
For (discrete) random systems with one-particle Hamiltonian H, we mention the following results.
In [5, 15], ground states Pµ “ 1pH ď µq with µ in the spectrum of H with exponentially localized
eigenfunctions were studied and a strict area law was proved. What is unknown is the precise form of
the coefficient of Ld´1|BΛ|, in particular, its dependence on physical parameters such as the disorder.
In [13], the authors study the one-dimensional dimer model (an Anderson model with correlated weak
disorder v) at the two exceptional energies, µ P t0, vu, at which there is delocalization. Surprisingly,
they prove that the EE of ground states Pµ scales at least like lnpLq unlike for µ in the rest of the
spectrum.
In this paper, we take a different configuration space from the start, namely the half-plane
Ω“ R` ˆ R and the Landau operator on this half-plane with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Other
boundary conditions such as Neumann boundary conditions, which are very popular in supercon-
ductivity, could be considered as well. Then it is interesting to ask how the boundary condition
effects the (leading) scaling of the EE of ground states. While we only partially answer this question,
see Theorem 2.1 and the remarks following, our initial interest in this model comes from a different
direction. In contrast to the Landau model on the full plane, the Landau model on the half-plane has
purely absolutely continuous spectrum. Does this now lead to a logarithmically enhanced area-law
or to a strict area law? Our main result says that we have a strict area-law bound in all cases and
the precise strict area-law scaling if the domain Λ is separated from the boundary t0u ˆ R of the
half-plane and it is the same as if we considered the problem on the full plane. In other words, if the
domain Λ is separated from the boundary, then the leading order of EE is uneffected by the Dirichlet
boundary conditions.
So, in all these models there is a relation between spectral properties and the type of area-
law scaling.
While µ in the point spectrum of H with exponentially decaying integral kernel of
Pµ “ 1pH ď µq always leads to a strict area law we have now an example of a Hamiltonian H with
purely absolutely continuous spectrum and yet a strict area law for its ground states. We discuss
the decay properties of Pµ in the present model in Section 8.
