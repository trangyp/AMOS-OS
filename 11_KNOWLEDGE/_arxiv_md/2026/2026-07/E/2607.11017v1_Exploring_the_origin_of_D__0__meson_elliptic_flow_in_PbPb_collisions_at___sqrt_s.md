Exploring the origin of D0 meson elliptic flow in PbPb
collisions at √sNN = 5.02 TeV using event shape
engineering
The CMS Collaboration*
Abstract
The influence of the initial-state geometry on the elliptic flow of prompt D0 mesons
in high-energy heavy ion collisions is explored. A lead-lead (PbPb) data sample at a
center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair of 5.02 TeV and with an integrated luminosity
of 0.607 nb−1 was collected in 2018 with the CMS detector at the CERN LHC. Based
on these data, an event-shape engineering technique is used to isolate collisions with
similar geometrical properties. An asymmetry parameter, q2, is first determined from
the distribution of transverse energy in the forward region of the detector. This pa-
rameter is shown to be linearly correlated with the elliptic anisotropy of inclusive
charged particles measured near mid-rapidity, as characterized by the second-order
Fourier coefficients, v2. Taking the charged particle v2 coefficients as proxies for the
initial-state eccentricity, the correlation of D0 meson v2 values near mid-rapidity with
those of inclusive charged particles is then studied. For D0 mesons with transverse
momenta in the range of 2–30 GeV and for different degrees of collision overlap, the v2
values are found strongly correlated with those for charged particles when selected
based on similar q2 intervals. This correlation suggests that the initial-state geome-
try substantially impacts the development of charm-hadron flow in heavy ion colli-
sions. A model calculation that explores the thermalization and collective motion of
D0 mesons and charged particles is compared to the experimental results.

Introduction
Relativistic heavy ion collisions can create a quark-gluon plasma (QGP), a state of matter com-
posed of deconfined quarks and gluons [1]. The QGP has a shear viscosity to entropy density
ratio that is close to that of an ideal fluid [2] and its evolution can be described by relativistic
hydrodynamics. In a near-ideal fluid, the initial spatial geometry of the QGP can be effectively
transformed into momentum anisotropies in the final state, leading to collective patterns in
azimuthal distributions of emitted particles [3, 4]. This azimuthal asymmetry, which has been
observed in many heavy ion measurements at both the BNL RHIC and the CERN LHC, can be
quantified with Fourier coefficients, vn = ⟨cos[n(ϕ −Ψn)]⟩, where ϕ represents the azimuthal
angle of a particle, Ψn denotes the azimuthal angle of the symmetry plane for the nth order
harmonic [5, 6], and the ⟨⟩is the average taken over all particles of interest in all events. The
v2 coefficient, commonly referred to as elliptic flow, is expected to exhibit a linear relationship
with the corresponding initial-state eccentricity of the QGP for low transverse momentum (pT)
particles [7–10]. While v2 values for inclusive charged particles and for identified particles
containing light quarks have been extensively studied, detailed investigations of v2 values for
particles containing heavy quarks, such as D0 mesons, offer a novel and important handle to
study the properties of the QGP medium. The large mass of heavy quarks renders them sen-
sitive to the early stages of the collision and their subsequent interaction with the medium,
providing unique insights beyond those obtainable from light-quark measurements.
Positive D0 meson v2 values that suggest hydrodynamic flow have been observed at both
RHIC [11–13] and LHC [14–21] for different collisional centralities. Centrality is defined by
the fraction of the total inelastic hadronic cross section, with the 0–10% centrality represent-
ing the 10% most central collisions and, thus, the largest nuclear overlap. Measurements of
pT-differential v2 values of prompt D0 mesons in lead-lead (PbPb) collisions from the CMS ex-
periment demonstrate a clear centrality dependence, which already suggests the influence of
the initial-state geometry on the final-state momentum anisotropy of these particles [18]. A
recent CMS measurement of prompt D±
s meson anisotropic flow reaches a similar conclusion
in the 4 < pT < 10 GeV region [22]. Recent ALICE measurements of the elliptic flow of prompt
D0, D+, D+
s , and Λ+
c v2 in PbPb collisions provide complementary evidence for the partonic
origin of charm-hadron v2 values [23].

measured collision centrality. However, event-by-event fluctuations in the initial-state shape
within a given centrality class lead to a variation in the final-state momentum anisotropy. To

ing technique has been developed to select events according to final-state observables that are
correlated with the initial-state eccentricity. A second-order flow vector Q2 is first defined as

This Letter presents a measurement of the prompt D0 meson v2 values determined at mid-
rapidity (|y| < 1) as a function of q2 values measured at forward η in PbPb collisions at
a center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair of √sNN = 5.02 TeV. The data were collected in
2018 using the CMS detector at the CERN LHC and correspond to an integrated luminosity
of 0.607 nb−1. By classifying events based on q2 values, a systematic variation of the initial-
state eccentricity is achieved, enabling the study of initial-state geometry influence on the D0
meson v2 values. The pT dependent D0 meson v2 values, with 2 < pT < 30 GeV, are measured
in five centrality classes (0–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–40, and 40–50%), each of which is further di-
vided into ten q2-based event-shape classes. The inclusive charged particle v2 values measured
for 1 < pT < 3 GeV are used as an experimental proxy for the event-by-event eccentricity,
motivated by the approximately linear hydrodynamic response of low-pT charged particles to
the initial geometry. These inclusive measurements have the same ranges of rapidity, central-
ity, and q2 as the D0 mesons. The correlation between D0 meson v2 values and the low-pT
charged particle v2 values in the ten event-shape classes for each centrality range is studied
and compared to a hydrodynamic model. Pearson correlation coefficients [8] are calculated to
quantify the linearity of the D0 meson vs. charged particle v2 correlations. Tabulated results
are provided in the HEPData record for this analysis [24].

The CMS detector
The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6 m internal diame-
ter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8 T. Within the solenoid volume, there is a silicon pixel and
strip tracker that measures the trajectories of charged particles in the range |η| < 2.5, a lead-
tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and two forward rapidity hadron calorimeters.
For charged particles with 1 < pT < 10 GeV and |η| < 1.4, the typical track resolution is 1.5%
in pT and 25–90 (45–150) µm in the transverse (longitudinal) impact parameter [25]. The two
hadron forward (HF) calorimeters use steel as an absorber and quartz fibers as the sensitive
material and are segmented to form 0.175×0.175 radians (∆η×∆ϕ) towers. These calorimeters
are located 11.2 m away from the interaction region, one on either side, and together they ex-
tend the calorimeter coverage to 3.0 < |η| < 5.2. In this analysis, the HF information is used
for performing the offline event selection, for determining event centrality, with higher energy
deposits corresponding to more central events, and for calculating the q2 values. A detailed
description of the CMS experiment can be found in Refs. [26, 27].
Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system. The first level, composed of
custom hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to
select events at a rate of around 100 kHz within a fixed latency of 4 µs [28]. The second level,
known as the high-level trigger, consists of a farm of processors running a version of the full
event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing, and reduces the event rate to a few
kHz before data storage [27, 29].

Event selection and simulated event samples
The data sample consists of minimum bias (MB) PbPb events corresponding to an integrated
luminosity of 0.607 nb−1 [30]. The MB selection requires signals above readout thresholds be-
tween 6–12 GeV in both of the HF calorimeters [29]. Events are required to pass selection criteria
designed to reject background events such as beam-gas interactions [31]. The final results in-
clude only events with at least one reconstructed primary vertex (PV) associated with two or
more tracks within 15 cm from the nominal interaction point along the beam direction, and at
least two towers with energy larger than 4 GeV in each of the HF detectors. Finally, in order to
suppress the contamination from events with multiple ion-ion collisions, an event is rejected if
the cluster shapes in the pixel part of the tracker are not compatible with those expected in a
single PbPb collision [19]. The total event selection efficiency is (97 ± 2)%.
Monte Carlo (MC) generators are employed to investigate the kinematics of D0 mesons in PbPb
collisions. The D0 mesons are produced using PYTHIA8.212 [32] with the CP5 tune [33], and
their decays are modeled using EVTGEN 1.3.0 [34]. Samples are generated for both prompt
and nonprompt D0 mesons from b-hadron decays. The decay products of the D0 mesons are
embedded into MB events generated via HYDJET 1.9 [34], which is tuned to reproduce the ob-
served charged particle multiplicity and spectra. The CMS detector response to these combined
events are simulated using the GEANT4 package [35].

Signal reconstruction and extraction
The D0 mesons are reconstructed using the decay channels D0 →K−π+ and D0 →K+π−;
in this Letter, these two channels are combined and collectively referred to as D0. The tracks
used for the reconstruction have pT > 1 GeV and |η| < 1.2. Only tracks passing high-purity
criteria [25] are used. The relative pT resolution of the reconstructed D0 candidate is less than
10%. Other requirements for the tracks include having a minimum of 11 hits along the track
trajectory in the tracker and having the track fit χ2 divided by the total number of degrees of
freedom of the fit, and also divided by the total number of tracker layers having a hit along the
track path, be less than 0.18. The reconstruction is performed by combining pairs of oppositely
charged particle tracks with invariant mass minv within ±130 MeV of the world-average D0
meson mass of 1865 MeV [36]. Since the CMS detector does not provide particle identification
information for high-pT charged particle tracks, both possible particle assignments (pion and
kaon) are considered for each pair of selected candidate decay tracks.
Following the reconstruction of D0 candidates, a boosted decision tree (BDT) algorithm from
the TMVA package [37] is used to optimize the significance of signal, which is given as the
ratio of the signal to the signal uncertainty, obtained from fitting the data using the function
described below and illustrated in Fig. 1. For the training, the prompt reconstructed D0 mesons
from MC simulations that match the D0 particle at the generator level are used as signal candi-
dates, and the side bands from data within the mass range of three to six standard deviations
of the MC signal distribution away from the mass peak are used to simulate the background.
Training is performed separately for each pT range and centrality class. The variables used to
discriminate the signal from the background are the χ2 probability for the D0 vertex, the sig-
nificance of the distance between the PV and secondary vertices, and the angle between the
D0 momentum and the vector pointing from the PV to the secondary vertex (pointing angle).
The variables related to the decay products of the D0 meson used for the training are the sig-
nificance of the distances of the closest approach (DCA) to the primary vertex, both along and
perpendicular to the beam direction, and the pT of the decay tracks. Overtraining checks are

Event shape engineering
An event-shape engineering technique is used to classify events based on the initial-state ge-
ometry of the collision. The HF towers in the range 3 < |η| < 5 are used for calculating the
asymmetry parameter q2. This results in a large η gap with the tracks used for D0 candidate
reconstruction, which are restricted to the range |η| < 1.2, thus suppressing nonflow correla-
tions. Using both the HF+ and HF- detectors, q2 is defined as (|Q2|HF+ + |Q2|HF-)/(∑M
j=1 wj),
where ∑M
j=1 wj is the sum of weights from both detectors. The q2 distribution is dependent
on centrality and can vary significantly over centrality classes covering 10% ranges. This intro-
duces a centrality bias where, e.g., events with smaller q2 values are dominated by more central
events. To reduce this centrality bias when selecting events based on their q2 values, the events
are divided into 1% centrality bins and, subsequently, each of these bins is further divided into
ten separate q2 bins. Since the analysis cannot be performed in 1% centrality classes because of
the limited size of the sample, within a larger 10% centrality range, q2 bins are combined into
a single analysis bin, such that, e.g., all events with the lowest 10% of q2 values in each of the
centrality bins 0–1 to 9–10% are combined into the lowest 10% q2 events of the centrality class
0–10%. A similar approach is followed in an analysis by the ALICE Collaboration [21].

Systematic uncertainty
The sources of systematic uncertainty include the fit to the signal mass distribution, the fit to
the background mass distribution, the sorting of events into q2 ranges, the centrality determi-

7
nation, the nonprompt D0 meson contamination, and the D0 meson acceptance and efficiency
corrections. The systematic uncertainties associated with the extraction of the D0 signal is ob-
tained by changing the signal probability density function (pdf) from a double Gaussian to a
triple Gaussian with the same mean but different widths, and by changing the function for the
combinatorial background from a second- to a third-order polynomial. The systematic uncer-
tainty due to how a q2 range is selected for a given event is found by changing the boundaries
of the range associated with each q2 bin up and down by the uncertainty associated with the
Q2 flow vectors. The systematic uncertainties associated with the centrality selection is ob-
tained by shifting the centrality boundaries based on the energy deposited in the HF towers up
and down. The nonprompt D0 meson fraction after applying the DCA selection is 5%, as dis-
cussed in Section 5.1. Since nonprompt D0 meson v2 values are smaller than those for prompt
D0 mesons in the studied pT and centrality region, as shown in Ref. [43], the maximum rela-
tive uncertainty due to the nonprompt contamination in the v2 value is 5%. This estimation
is based on Eq. (6) from an earlier CMS publication [18]. The effect of the efficiency is stud-
ied by changing the efficiency correction values. In an alternative approach, the efficiency is
based solely on simulation and does not consider any potential differences between the MC
and data pT spectra. The v2 values for D0 candidates passing the BDT selection, and for all
the D0 candidates, are calculated from the MC samples, and the difference in v2 is quoted as
the systematic uncertainty due to BDT selection. In this analysis, no significant effect resulting
from the BDT selection is observed. The summary of the uncertainties is provided in Table 1.
Each of these potential sources was varied as described, with the difference in the experimen-
tal result based on the varied parameter with that found using the nominal value taken as the
associated systematic uncertainty for the slope and intercept. The total uncertainty for each
experimental result is taken as the quadratic sum of the individual uncertainties. For r, the sys-
tematic uncertainty is calculated using a similar approach to that of the statistical uncertainty,
where each individual D0 meson v2 coefficient is varied according to a Gaussian distribution
centered on its measured value, with a standard deviation corresponding to its total system-
atic uncertainty. The final systematic uncertainty was determined using the 68.3% confidence
interval of the resulting distribution.

Summary
The elliptic-flow v2 Fourier coefficient is measured for D0 mesons using an event-shape engi-
neering technique. The data sample consists of lead-lead collisions measured with the CMS


## Page 13


11
detector at the CERN LHC at a center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair of 5.02 TeV and with an
integrated luminosity of 0.607 nb−1. The azimuthal asymmetry of energy deposits in a forward-
rapidity calorimeter is characterized by a q2 parameter that can be related to the initial collision
geometry. A strong linear correlation is found between this parameter and the corresponding
v2 values for inclusive charged particles measured in the pseudorapidity (η) and transverse
momentum (pT) ranges of |η| < 1 and 1 < pT < 3 GeV, respectively. The variation between
D0 meson and inclusive charged particle v2 values obtained at common q2 intervals is then
explored. A strong linear correlation is found over the centrality range of 10–40%, as binned in
10% classes, for D0 mesons at low pT (2–10 GeV). A weaker linear correlation is observed for
centrality ranges of 0–10 and 40–50%. The parton-hadron-string-dynamics model is compared
to the experimental results and found to underestimate the degree of charm quark thermaliza-
tion in the quark-gluon plasma at pT < 4 GeV. These findings provide compelling evidence for
the importance of the initial-state geometry in determining the collective flow of heavy quarks
within the quark-gluon plasma created in heavy ion collisions.
