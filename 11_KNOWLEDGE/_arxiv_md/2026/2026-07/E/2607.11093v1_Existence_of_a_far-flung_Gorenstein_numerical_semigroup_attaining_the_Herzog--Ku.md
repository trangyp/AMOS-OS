EXISTENCE OF A FAR-FLUNG GORENSTEIN NUMERICAL
SEMIGROUP ATTAINING THE
HERZOG–KUMASHIRO–STAMATE BOUND
AKIHIRO SUGAWARA
Abstract. We address a question posed by Herzog, Kumashiro, and Stamate
regarding the upper bound for the multiplicity of any far-flung Gorenstein nu-
merical semigroup. We answer this question in the affirmative by presenting a
method for constructing certain far-flung Gorenstein numerical semigroups with
maximal reduced type. Furthermore, using this method, we consider a question
raised by Herzog, Hibi, and Stamate. More precisely, for any integer t ≥5, we
construct a certain far-flung Gorenstein numerical semigroup with type t, which
is a counterexample to the question.
Introduction
The far-flung Gorenstein property was introduced by Herzog, Kumashiro, and
Stamate to study one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay rings whose trace ideal of the
canonical module is as small as possible (see [6]). Let S be a numerical semigroup,
and let k[[S]] := k[[ts | s ∈S]] be the numerical semigroup ring associated to
S, where k is a field. We say that S is far-flung Gorenstein if k[[S]] is far-flung
Gorenstein.
It is well-known that for any numerical semigroup S ̸= N, the multiplicity m(S)
of S is at least t(S) + 1, where t(S) is the type of S. On the other hand, there is
no upper bound for the multiplicity of a numerical semigroup S ̸= N in terms of its
type in general (for example, see [13, Proposition 1]). Surprisingly, it is shown in
[6] that there exists an upper bound for the multiplicity of any far-flung Gorenstein
numerical semigroup, which comes from the Rohrbach problem (see [10], [12]) in

To prove this theorem, we first show that if S is a far-flung Gorenstein numeri-

observation, we focus on providing a method of constructing far-flung Gorenstein

statement holds true when t(S) ≤3 (see [5, Theorem 2.4]), and that there exists
a counterexample with t(S) = 5 (see [5, Example 2.5]). We show that there ex-
ists a counterexample with type t for any integer t ≥5. In addition, we present a
counterexample to [5, Question 2.6], which corresponds to the case t(S) = 4 in [4,
Question 2.3].
The structure of the article is as follows. In Section 1, we fix the notation, and
we recall some definitions and results about numerical semigroups. In Section 2, we
recall the Rohrbach problem, and we provide a characterization of far-flung Goren-

1. Preliminaries
First, we fix the notation and recall some definitions and basic facts on numerical
semigroups. Let Z and N denote the set of integers and the set of non-negative
integers, respectively.
The cardinality of the set A is denoted by |A|.
For any
integers x ≤y, the set of integers {z ∈Z | x ≤z ≤y} is represented by [x, y].
We define ⟨n1, . . . , ne⟩= {Pe
i=1 λini | λi ∈N}, where n1, . . . , ne are non-negative
integers. A numerical semigroup S is a submonoid of (N, +) such that N \ S is
finite. Let S be a numerical semigroup. The smallest element of S \{0} is called the
multiplicity of S, and it is denoted by m(S). The set N\S is called the set of gaps of
S, and its cardinality is said to be the genus g(S) of S. It is well-known that every
numerical semigroup has a unique minimal system of generators, and it is denoted
by msg(S). The cardinality e(S) of msg(S) is called the embedding dimension of S.
Note that the inequality e(S) ≤m(S) holds. We say that S has maximal embedding
dimension (MED-semigroup) if e(S) = m(S).
The largest integer in Z\S is called the Frobenius number of S, and it is denoted
by F(S). Let PF(S) := {x ∈Z \ S | x + s ∈S for all 0 ̸= s ∈S} be the set of
all pseudo-Frobenius numbers of S. The cardinality t(S) of PF(S) is said to be the
type of S. We say that S is symmetric if x ∈Z \ S implies that F(S) −x ∈S, or
equivalently, if t(S) = 1. We also define
rPF(S) = [F(S) −m(S) + 1, F(S)] \ S.
The cardinality of rPF(S) is called the reduced type s(S) of S (see [7], [9], [13]).
Note that F(S) ∈rPF(S) ⊂PF(S). Thus, we obtain
1 ≤s(S) ≤t(S).
Then, we say that S has maximal reduced type if s(S) = t(S), i.e., rPF(S) = PF(S).
Given two sets A and B, we define A+B = {a+b | a ∈A, b ∈B}. A relative ideal
I of S is a set I ⊂Z such that I+S ⊂I and x+I ⊂S for some x ∈S, where x+I :=
{x+a | a ∈I}. The following three relative ideals are very important: the maximal
ideal M(S) := S\{0} of S, the conductor ideal C(S) := {z ∈Z | z ≥F(S)+1} of S,
and the standard canonical ideal K(S) := {x ∈Z | F(S)−x /∈S} of S. Note that if I
and J are relative ideals of S, then I+J and I−J := {z ∈Z | z+y ∈I for all y ∈J}
are relative ideals of S.
The trace ideal of K(S) is defined as tr(K(S)) := K(S)+(S −K(S)). In general,
the following fact is known:

The Rohrbach problem ([10], [12]). Let A be a finite set of non-negative integers,
and let n(A) be the integer such that 0, 1, . . . , n(A) −1 ∈A + A and n(A) /∈A + A.
If 0 /∈A, then let n(A) = −1. For a positive integer t, the Rohrbach problem asks
to find the integer
n(t) := max{n(A) | A ⊂N, |A| = t}.
Definition 2 ([8, Definition 7]). We say that a finite set A of non-negative integers
is extremal if n(A) = n(|A|).
Example 1.

and n(2) = 3.
(2) Consider {0, 1, 2} and {0, 1, 3}. We see that they are extremal and n(3) = 5.
