Abstract
This paper determines the exact frequency of all consecutive sign patterns of lengths
two and three formed by the quadratic character modulo an odd prime p. Using basic
properties of character sums over finite fields, we derive exact counting formulas for
pairs (n, n+1) and triples (n−1, n, n+1) exhibiting specific sequence patterns in {±1}.
The frequencies of pairs are classified entirely by p (mod 4), whereas triples exhibit a
more complex dependency on p (mod 8) and the Jacobsthal sum Tp = P
x∈Fp χ(x3−x).
2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11L40, 11A15.
Key words and phrases: quadratic character, character sum, quadratic residue, finite
field, Jacobsthal sum.

Introduction
Throughout this paper, for a given odd prime p, we let Fp denote the finite field Z/pZ and
we let χ denote the quadratic character modulo p.
Since the pioneering work of Gauss, quadratic residues and nonresidues modulo a prime
have occupied a prominent place in number theory. One of the fundamental questions in
this area concerns the local behavior of the sequence {χ(n)}n∈Fp. A natural way to study
this sequence is to investigate the occurrence of prescribed sign patterns. In particular, one
may ask how frequently pairs or longer blocks of consecutive residues and nonresidues occur.
The study of such patterns dates back several decades. One of the earliest quantitative
results is due to Carlitz [2], who obtained estimates for the number of pairs of consecutive
quadratic residues and consecutive quadratic nonresidues in the finite field Fp. A fundamen-
tal contribution of Burgess [1] showed that, for sufficiently large primes p, the length of the
longest run of consecutive quadratic residues or consecutive quadratic nonresidues modulo
p is bounded by O(p1/4+δ), where δ > 0 is arbitrary. Subsequent refinements and extensions
of Burgess’s method have led to stronger results; see, for example, [5, 7]. Using elementary

More intricate residue patterns have also attracted considerable attention. Wang and
Lv [8] investigated the number of integers 1 ≤n ≤p −1 for which n, n + n, and n −n
are simultaneously quadratic residues or simultaneously quadratic nonresidues modulo p,
where n denotes the multiplicative inverse of n modulo p.
More recently, Wang and Li
[9] examined the distribution of triples of consecutive quadratic residues and consecutive
quadratic nonresidues modulo p, obtaining exact formulas for their frequencies.
While many existing results concern asymptotic estimates or the existence of long runs
of residues and nonresidues, explicit formulas for the exact frequency of short sign patterns
are less common. The purpose of this paper is to obtain complete and explicit formulas for
all sign patterns of lengths two and three. For (ε1, ε2) ∈{±1}2, we consider the quantity
N(ε1, ε2) = #{n ∈Fp : χ(n) = ε1, χ(n + 1) = ε2},
which counts the occurrences of a prescribed pair of consecutive quadratic character values.
Our first theorem gives an exact evaluation of N(ε1, ε2) and shows that the distribution
depends only on the value of χ(−1), or equivalently on the residue class of p modulo 4.
We then study patterns of length three. For (ε1, ε2, ε3) ∈{±1}3, we define
N(ε1, ε2, ε3) = #{n ∈Fp : χ(n −1) = ε1, χ(n) = ε2, χ(n + 1) = ε3}.
Our second theorem expresses these quantities in terms of Tp = P
x∈Fp χ(x3 −x) the Ja-
cobsthal sum. The resulting formulas depend explicitly on the residue class of p modulo 8,
reflecting the influence of both χ(−1) and χ(2). This yields a complete classification of all
triple patterns of consecutive quadratic residues and nonresidues.
