Dark matter scattering with pre-supernova neutrinos
Sangeetha N. Tallur1, ∗and Nirmal Raj1, †
1Centre for High Energy Physics, Indian Institute of Science, C. V. Raman Avenue, Bengaluru 560012, India
Pre-supernova neutrinos, emitted during the last day prior to core collapse of a massive star,
could provide a unique probe at MeV energies of scattering interactions between dark matter and
neutrinos. Due to attenuation of the flux of electron anti-neutrinos from their scattering on dark
matter on the way to Earth, we expect a deficit of inverse beta decay events at large-volume, low-
threshold detectors such as KamLAND, JUNO, and Super-K. which we use to derive upper limits
on the dark matter-neutrino reduced scattering cross section. Though seemingly weaker than limits
from post-bounce supernova neutrino events, these sensitivities provide an important cross-check,
could help determine the energy dependence of the cross section, and may even be a distinct probe
of certain models. Further, pre-supernova neutrinos may test hints reported of dark matter-neutrino
scattering in the early universe suppressing small-scale power as seen in Lyman-alpha data.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Observing the scattering of dark matter (DM) on Stan-
dard Model (SM) states may reveal its long-sought par-
ticle identity. Extensive constraints have been placed on
nuclear and electron recoils induced by dark matter in
direct detection experiments and other terrestrial set-
tings [1, 2], celestial bodies [3, 4], and cosmic rays [5, 6];
see also the references in Ref. [7]. In this work we con-
sider the possibility that the dominant SM portal to DM
is neutrinos, as realized in various models [8, 9], and es-
timate the sensitivities to DM–ν scattering from a future
measurement of a pre-supernova flux of neutrinos.
Neutrinos from core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe)
have been used to set limits on interactions with DM
and cosmic relics.
During their diffusion out of the
proto-neutron star they can scatter with dark states pro-
duced in the supernova, prolonging supernova (SN) cool-
ing [10, 11], or during their transit to Earth across typi-
cally O(10) kpc distances, they can scatter with the halo
DM or relic background and deviate from the supernova
(SN) line of sight, so that their flux is effectively atten-
uated; however, from scatters on DM away from the SN
line of sight, there may be time-delayed “echoes”; fur-
ther, historic SNe and DM–ν scatters could form a diffuse
Galactic background of neutrinos [12–17]. The scatter-
ing of supernova neutrinos (SNν) on DM may also alter
the density profile of dwarf spheroidals [18]. Moreover,
event spectra of the integrated neutrino emission from
all past CCSNe across cosmic history giving rise to the
diffuse supernova neutrino background (DSNB), recently
seen at the 2.6 σ level at Super-Kamiokande loaded with
gadolinium (SK–Gd) [19] and expected to be observed
at Hyper-Kamiokande, JUNO, and DUNE [20–22], may
carry information about interactions of the DSNB νs
with DM [23–25].
Even prior to the onset of core collapse, massive stars
in their final evolutionary stages – particularly when

burning silicon – emit a steady flux of MeV-energy neu-
trinos created by e+e−pair annihilation, photo-neutrino
production, nuclear e± capture, and β± and plasmon de-
cays. These pre-supernova neutrinos (preSNν), though
less luminous than supernova neutrinos (SNν) by a few
orders of magnitude, carry direct information about the
internal state of the dying star [26], and could provide
an early warning of an impending Galactic supernova by
hours to days [27]; some 30 massive stars in the solar
neighborhood have been identified as progenitor candi-
dates [28], such as α Orionis, a.k.a. Betelgeuse. Due to
their low fluxes and energies, their observation requires
detectors with large target masses and low energy thresh-
olds [29–36]. In this work we estimate the sensitivities of
such detectors to DM–ν interactions from the attenua-
tion of the preSNν fluxes from a future nearby SN event,
typically O(100) pc away. In particular, we derive up-
per limits on the reduced cross section of elastic DM–
ν scatters from estimated event counts of electron anti-
neutrinos in the inverse beta decay (IBD) detection chan-
nel at Super-Kamiokande, KamLAND, and JUNO. In
forthcoming work, we derive similar sensitivities from at-
tenuation and time-delay signals from (the usually stud-
ied post-bounce) neutrinos from a future Galactic su-
pernova event [37]. To our knowledge, ours is the first
work to use preSNν to study physics beyond the SM.
[Ref. [38] studied ultra-light axions produced in the fi-
nal evolutionary stages of nearby SN progenitors from
the conversion of MeV photons.] Though we will have to
be uncommonly fortunate for the occurrence of the next
Galactic supernova – in itself a rare event – to be within
the 100 pc neighborhood, the distinct characteristics of
preSNν make studying their potential in cornering new
fundamental physics worthwhile.
Due to typically smaller DM column densities and
lower statistics, our preSNν sensitivities would appear
weaker than those obtained from post-bounce SNν. How-
ever, they are still of fundamental value: (i) as these are
O(MeV) neutrino energies as opposed to O(10 MeV) SNν
energies, models with resonant scattering peaked near an
MeV may be uniquely probed by preSNν. Further, scat-
tering via light mediators would be infrared-enhanced

and give more value to preSNν-based sensitivities; (ii)
they would provide an important cross-check to the sen-
sitivities derivable from the SNν that would emerge from
the same site, and moreover the ratio of the cross section
limits would give us a clue as to the energy dependence
of the cross section; the slower, hours–days evolution of
the preSNν flux compared to the 10 second evolution of
the SNν flux could in principle also serve to probe the
energy dependence of the DM scattering: a cross section
that rises or falls with neutrino energy would distort/tilt
the observed spectrum in a specific way; (iii) due to sub-
kpc baselines, the preSNν-based limits depend only on
the local DM density, which is relatively well-measured
with a fractional uncertainty of about 100%. In contrast,
for the O(10 kpc) baselines of SNν, the fractional uncer-
tainty on the DM column density can be much higher as
inherited from fits of the Galactic DM density profile to
stellar rotation curves; (iv) on a related note: in models of
dissipative dark matter, a dark disk closely aligned with
the baryon disk of the Milky Way may be formed [39, 40];
it is plausible that the dissipative component of DM,
with its rich interaction structure, is just the one that
connects to the neutrino sector. In such a scenario, the
DM column density of SNν is provided by the dark disk
alone, weakening the SNν-based limits and hence lifting
the significance of preSNν; (v) the modeling of preSNν
and SNν fluxes are of a different nature: the preSN ¯νe
spectrum is set by e+e−annihilation emissivity, in turn
functions of the progenitor core’s temperature, density
and free electron fraction profiles, as tracked by stellar
evolution codes, giving a quasi-thermal spectral shape.
For SNν, there are much larger theoretical systematics:
explosion dynamics, standing accretion shock instability
effects, equation of state-dependent average neutrino en-
ergy and pinching parameter, collective neutrino oscilla-
tions, phase transitions at accretion/explosion, and other
actively debated effects. These again make preSNν sensi-
tivities an important independent probe of new physics;
(vi) in case the progenitor undergoes a “failed” supernova
event that collapses to a black hole soon after bounce,
SNν may be in shortage, and the preSNν flux may carry
much constraining power;
(vii) Further, as we will discuss, there may be an inter-
esting interplay between (pre)SNν-based limits and those
from cosmology. Specifically, there is a 3σ level hint seen
in Lyman-alpha data of DM-ν scattering suppressing the
small-scale matter power spectrum [41]. We show that
preSNν may test this finding for DM-ν scattering cross
sections that are a steeply increasing function of neutrino
energy.
This paper is organized as follows. In Section II we
present the preSNν flux and detector event rates, and
discuss the attenuation of the flux by scattering on dark
matter to derive a criterion for setting limits.
In Sec-
tion III we show and discuss our results. In Section IV
we provide further discussion and conclude.
II.
SETUP
In this section we sketch the signals of DM–preSNν
interactions. From preSNν spectra available online we
first compute the expected event counts at various de-
tectors in relevant detection channels. We then describe
our procedure for setting limits on DM–ν scattering via
attenuation of the preSNν and SNν fluxes.

IV.
DISCUSSION
In this work we used the distinct virtues of pre-
supernova neutrinos to probe new physics in the form of
dark matter-neutrino scattering interactions, with results
summarized in Fig. 2. Due to the unique combination of
their detectably large fluxes, astronomical baselines and
O(MeV) energies, preSNν can probe certain energy de-
pendences in DM-ν scattering and may help test hints
of this process in Ly-α spectral data. Other scenarios of
new physics too may be probed by preSNν, e.g., their
scattering on cosmic relics such as the cosmic neutrino
background or a background of vectors or majorons that
couple to νs [12]; and the invisible decay of neutrinos,
with measurements of the preSNν flux providing an inde-
pendent check of the future limits from a Galactic CCSN
or the DSNB [54]. Beyond the currently operational de-
tectors we had considered, one could also estimate the
sensitivities of DM-preSNν scattering in future detectors
such as DUNE and Hyper-K [30], and those employing
coherent elastic nuclear scattering channels [31, 55–57],
as well as at proposals for kilotonne-scale low-threshold
detectors such as a dual-phase 10 kt “module of opportu-
nity” at DUNE [58–60], or liquid xenon-based kilotonne
detectors designed for 0νββ searches repurposed for DM
searches [61, 62]. We leave these interesting investiga-
tions to future studies.
