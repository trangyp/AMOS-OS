DIFFEOLOGICAL RIEMANNIAN ORBIFOLDS
DAVID MIYAMOTO
Abstract. We show that the data of a Riemannian metric on a differentiable stack presented by an
orbifold groupoid is equivalent to the data of a Riemannian metric on its diffeological orbit space. As
a consequence, we conclude that the classical notion of Riemannian orbifold is equivalent to that of
a Riemannian diffeological orbifold. We use the framework for Riemannian diffeology introduced by
Kuribayashi, Sakai, and Shiobara, and our result answers a problem they posed in the affirmative.
More generally, we show that a Riemannian metric on a Lie groupoid, namely a 2-metric in the sense
of del Hoyo and Fernandes, induces a Riemannian metric on its diffeological orbit space only if the Lie
groupoid is regular, and that properness is a sufficient but not necessary condition.

1. Introduction
The goal of this article is to compare two definitions of a Riemannian metric on a singular space.
This begs the definition of “Riemannian metric” and “singular space.” The former, in the category of
smooth manifolds Mfld, is unambiguous: a Riemannian metric g on a smooth manifold M is a smooth
and positive-definite section of the symmetric square of the cotangent bundle. Equivalently, g is a smooth
map g: TM ×M TM →R that restricts to an inner product on each fibre TxM ×TxM. “Singular space”
is a less precise term. We specialize to quotients of smooth manifolds, viewed as differentiable stacks and
as diffeological spaces.
The category of diffeological spaces Dflg, introduced by Souriau [Sou80], is a quasi-topos that includes
Mfld as a full subcategory. As such, every quotient of a diffeological space, and thus of a manifold, inherits
a natural diffeology even when it does not inherit a manifold structure. For example, the following spaces
carry non-trivial diffeologies:

Differentiable stacks, henceforth simply “stacks,” may be intrinsically defined as categories fibred in
groupoids satisfying sheaf-like and descent conditions. However, conveniently, each stack is also presented
by a Lie groupoid. Put rigorously, there is an equivalence of (weak) 2-categories from the weak 2-category
of Lie groupoids (whose 1-morphisms are principal bibundles), to the strict 2-category of stacks. We
denote the stack corresponding to a Lie groupoid G ⇒M by [M // G].
The relevant Lie groupoids in the example above are the action groupoids R ⋊Z2, R2n ⋊SO(2n), and
R2n ⋊U(n), and the holonomy groupoid Hol(F). The stacks [R // Z2] and [M // F] are isomorphic, but
the stacks [R2n //SO(2n)] and [R2n //U(n)] are not. The Lie groupoid R⋊Z2 is an example of an orbifold
groupoid, namely an ´etale and proper Lie groupoid.

Our findings may be summarized thus:
(a) (Proposition 4.8) If G ⇒M has orbits of different dimensions within the same component of M,
then a Riemannian metric on the stack [M // G] cannot induce a weak Riemannian metric on
M/G. This is the case for the action groupoids R2n ⋊SO(n).
(b) (Theorem 4.22) If G ⇒M is a proper and regular Lie groupoid, then every Riemannian metric
on [M //G] induces a weak Riemannian metric on M/G. Note that M/G is a diffeological orbifold.
(c) (Theorem 4.24) Furthermore, if G ⇒M is an orbifold groupoid, then every Riemannian metric
on M/G is induced by a Riemannian metric on [M // G].
Items (b) and (c), when applied to orbifold groupoids, imply that the notions of Riemannian metric on
the stack [M //G], on the diffeological orbifold M/G, and on the classical orbifold M/G, are all equivalent.
On the other hand, item (a) shows that there is an unavoidable obstruction to generalizing (b) and (c).
Moving beyond orbifolds, we observe (Proposition 4.29) that if Γ is a countable group acting smoothly
and freely on a manifold M, then M ⋊Γ also satisfies the conclusions of (b) and (c). For an example,
we may take M = R and Γ = Z + αZ for irrational α, and gain a complete understanding of the weak
Riemannian metrics on the irrational torus R/(Z + αZ).
It would be interesting to have a uniform
description of those Lie groupoids G that satisfy the conclusions of (b) and (c).
Riemannian metrics on singular spaces have appeared throughout the literature. Satake [Sat57] intro-
duced Riemannian metrics on orbifolds simultaneously with his definition of orbifold. Shortly thereafter
Reinhart [Rei59] introduced Riemannian foliations, which are foliated manifolds (M, F) equipped with
a “bundle-like” metric on M that is intended to model a metric on the leaf space M/F. Indeed, a
bundle-like metric is precisely a 0-metric on Hol(F). Gallego, Gualandri, Hector, and Reventos [Gal+89]
axiomatized the Riemannian structure of Hol(F), producing a first notion of Riemannian groupoid. This
is a Lie groupoid equipped with a 1-metric, in the sense of [dF18]. Pflaum, Posthuma, and Tang [PPT14]
distinguished 0-metrics on Lie groupoids, calling them “transversally invariant Riemannian metrics.” We
already mentioned that we use del Hoyo and Fernandes’s notion of Riemannian groupoid (stack) [dF18;
dF19]. Recently, Holtrop [Hol25] introduced a more general notion of Riemannian groupoid.
Riemannian diffeology is a relatively younger development. Iglesias-Zemmour proposed a definition
of Riemannian metric on a diffeological space in [Igl23]. The distinction between [Igl23] and [KSS25] lies
in the notion of definiteness (cf., Remarks 3.5 and 3.8). Goldammer and Welker [GW21] gave another
definition of Riemannian diffeological space. Its relation to Kuribayashi, Sakai, and Shiobara’s notion
remains to be explored.
The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we introduce orbifolds as diffeological spaces and as
stacks. This includes a review diffeology, Lie groupoids and stacks. In Section 3, we recall the definitions
of a Riemannian metric on a diffeological space and on a stack. In Section 4, we formally define what
it means for a Riemannian metric on a stack to induce/descend to a weak Riemannian metric on the
corresponding diffeological orbit space, and establish our main results (a), (b), and (c) enumerated above.

2.1. Diffeological orbifolds. We begin with some diffeological prerequisites. The standard textbook
for diffeology is [Igl13] and its reprint [Igl22]. Given a category C and objects c, c′ ∈C, we write C(c, c′)

spaces, i.e., all open subsets U ⊆Rn for all n ≥0. A morphism f ∈Eucl(U, V ) is a C∞-smooth map
f : U →V . A diffeological space is a set X equipped with an assignment D that associates to each
U ∈Eucl a subset DU ⊆Set(U, X) such that:
• (Concreteness) For each U and each x ∈X, the constant map r 7→x is in DU.

• (Sheaf) For each U and open cover {Uα}, if f : U →X is a map, and f|Uα : Uα →X is in DUα
for each α, then f ∈DU.

Theorem 4.22. Let X be a stack presented by a regular and proper Lie groupoid G ⇒M, and let [η2]

metric is definite with respect to {π|T : T →M/G | T ⊥F}.
Proof. This is a direct consequence of Proposition 4.19 and Proposition 4.10.

metric η on M/G induced by η2 makes (M/G, η) a Riemannian orbifold.

4.3. Beyond orbifolds. A necessary condition for a 0-metric on G ⇒M to induce a metric on M/G
is that G is regular. A sufficient condition is that G is regular and proper. To close our discussion,
we give another class of groupoids whose 0-metrics descend to the orbit space. We adopt the following
terminology from [Ahm24, Definition 5.4].
Definition 4.26. A diffeologically ´etale map is a smooth map π: X →Y which satisfies the following
lifting condition: for every plot p: U →Y , and every r ∈U and x ∈X with p(r) = π(x), there exists a
local lift q: U 99K X of p along π sending r to x, and moreover the germ of such a lift is unique.
⋄
Remark 4.27. If M is a manifold, the diffeologically ´etale maps π: M →Y were introduced earlier under
the name “Q-charts” in [Bar73].
◁
Example 4.28. If Γ is a finite group acting smoothly and freely on a diffeological space X, then the
quotient map π: X →X/Γ is diffeologically ´etale. For a proof, see [Miy24, Example 2.7] (this proof
is for the case X = R and Γ = Z + αZ, for α irrational, but generalizes mutatis mutandis). The key
lemma is Serpinski’s theorem [Eng89, Theorem 6.1.27], which states that a partition of a connected and
compact topological space into countably many disjoint subsets must have exactly one component.

Proposition 4.29. Suppose that G ⇒M is an ´etale Lie groupoid whose orbit map π: M →M/G is
diffeologically ´etale. Then every 0-metric on G induces a Riemannian metric on M/G. Conversely, a
weak Riemannian metric g on M/G that is definite with respect to π: M →M/G pulls back to a 0-metric
π∗η on G, which induces a metric on X that induces g.
Proof. By Proposition 4.10 and Proposition 4.13, it suffices to show that if (p, q): U →M ×G M is a
plot, then so is (T2p, T2q): T2U →T2M ×T2G T2M, so take such a plot (p, q). Exactly as in the first part
of the proof of Proposition 4.19 (which used only the fact that G is a Lie groupoid), we may work locally
near r ∈U, and assume that p(r) = q(r) = x.
Our assumption on (p, q) is equivalent to the equality π ◦p = π ◦q. Since π is diffeologically ´etale,
this means that the germs of p and q are identical at r. But then, at least for v, w ∈TrU, we must have
T2p(v, w) = T2q(v, w). In other words, (T2p, T2q) maps TrU ×TrU into the diagonal in T2M ×T2M, and
in particular into T2M ×T2G T2M. Since this holds for each r, the proof is complete.
The converse holds for the exact same reason it holds in Theorem 4.24.
□
Example 4.30. Take Γα to be the subgroup Z+αZ of R, for α irrational. The action groupoid R⋊Γα ⇒
R satisfies the preconditions of Proposition 4.29.
Therefore any 0-metric on R ⋊Γα descends to a
Riemannian metric on Tα = R/Γα. Since the orbits of Γα are dense in R, and Γα acts by translation, the
only 0-metric on R (up to scaling) is the standard Euclidean metric. By the converse to Proposition 4.29,
the only weak Riemannian metric on Tα (up to scaling) is the one induced by the standard Euclidean
metric.
