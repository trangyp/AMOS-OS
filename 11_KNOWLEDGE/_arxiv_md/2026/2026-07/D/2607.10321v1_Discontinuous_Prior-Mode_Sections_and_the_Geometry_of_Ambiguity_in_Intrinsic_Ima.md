Discontinuous Prior-Mode Sections and the Geometry of
Ambiguity in Intrinsic Image Decomposition

Abstract
The viral 2015 photograph known as The Dress divides observers into two camps because it is
ambiguous: the same image colors can be explained either as a blue–black surface under one illuminant
or as a white–gold surface under another. We propose a geometric account in which the ambiguity arises
from a singularity in intrinsic image decomposition, the inverse problem of separating an observed image
into reflectance and illumination. Our central claim is that the prior-mode section—the prior-preferred
decomposition—switches across an ambiguity boundary in image space, and that any smooth learned
model can only approximate this discontinuous switch by forming a thin transition layer. This predicts
two observable signatures, where ∆is the jump between branches of the prior-mode section and λ is
the regularization strength: for inverse decomposers, an albedo Jacobian scaling as |∆|/
√

forward encoders, the Fernet curvature that blows up on a scale of 1/

Introduction
The 2015 photograph known as The Dress divides observers into two camps of roughly equal size: one
perceives the garment as blue-and-black, the other as white-and-gold [Lafer-Sousa et al., 2015, Cacoub et al.,
2011, Witzel et al., 2017]. This split is not measurement noise: both readings are physically consistent, each
assuming a different color temperature for the ambient light and yielding a reflectance map that re-renders to
the same pixels. Existing Bayesian accounts [Witzel et al., 2017] locate the ambiguity in a bimodal posterior
over illuminant priors. We adopt a complementary, sharper view: the relevant object is not the bimodality of
the posterior but the discontinuity of its mode.
We reframe the perception of The Dress as an intrinsic decomposition task and, simultaneously, an
approximation problem in the language of differential topology. Let π: E →B denote the rendering map, the
pixel-wise product taking (reflectance, illumination) pairs to images; it is non-injective, since all equal-product
pairs render to the same pixel. To fix a percept, the visual system effectively selects a single point on the
fiber π−1(c) as the mode of a prior p over (R, I); we call this map the prior-mode section g(c) (definition 1).
We argue that this singular behavior reflects g being single-valued and smooth on an open dense subset of
B but multi-valued on a codimension-one ambiguity locus Σ ⊂B. Generic images sit off Σ and admit an

The two propositions describe different observables for the same geometric event. Inverse decomposers
expose the ambiguity boundary through a large albedo Jacobian, while forward encoders expose it through
curvature of a latent path when the competing latent branches meet with different tangent directions
(numerically verified in section 4). Detailed proofs are provided in Appendix B.
The regularization term in the Tikhonov problem plays the role of a weight penalty in neural-network
training. For shallow feed-forward neural networks, Parhi and Nowak [2021] constructs a family of semi-norms
in the second-order Radon-domain bounded-variation space, closely related to the Sobolev norm H1 we
impose in the Tikhonov model problem. For deep networks, Rahaman et al. [2019] shows an analogous
implicit spectral bias that suppresses high-frequency components and plays the role of λ in eq. (2). We defer
the connection between the neural-network training loss and the Tikhonov problem to Appendix A.
3
Σ in Intrinsic Decomposers: the Albedo Jacobian
This section provides the main empirical support for section 2. We probe Σ in the intrinsic decomposition
setting via the per-pixel albedo Jacobian under illuminant perturbation, and confirm that the pattern survives
an architecture change.
Dataset and models.
We use CGIntrinsics [Li and Snavely, 2018], a large-scale rendered dataset that
supplies ground-truth albedo (reflectance R) and shading I for indoor scenes under physically-based illu-
mination. Our anchor model is the feed-forward DPT-style U-Net of Careaga and Aksoy [2023], trained
with an MSE-family loss on ground-truth albedo: this is the closest empirical analogue to the Tikhonov
model problem of section 2, with MSE matching the quadratic fidelity and weight decay acting as an implicit
Sobolev penalty. For an architecture contrast we include Intrinsic Anything (IA) of Chen et al. [2024], a
latent-diffusion model.
Setup.
For each image c in CGIntrinsics we construct three families of perturbed inputs and approximate
the Jacobian by central differences:
• CCT: white-balance shift [Kang, 2006] at four reference color temperatures T ∈{3500, 5000, 6500, 8000} K,
each probed at T ± 500 K (δ = 500 K); this is the illuminant-prior axis aligned with Σ.
• Brightness: uniform linear-RGB scaling c 7→αc at α ∈{0.70, 0.85, 1.00, 1.15}, δ = 0.10.
• Saturation: HSV saturation scaling at the same four reference scales, δ = 0.10.
We run Careaga on each perturbed image, compute the per-pixel central-difference Jacobian norm ∥∂R/∂(perturb)∥2
at each reference point, and take the pixel-wise maximum across the four reference points as a single sensitivity
map per axis. Albedo error e := ∥Rpred −RGT∥2 is computed per pixel on the unperturbed image. The
per-image scalars rT , rbright, rsat are Spearman correlations between each sensitivity map and e, restricted
to foreground pixels. Of 2000 sampled images, N = 1998 pass the foreground-mask threshold; the pooled
analysis uses n = 2 × 107 pixels.
Marginal vs. partial correlation.
The three perturbation axes are mutually correlated since a white-
balance shift changes chromaticity but also perturbs apparent brightness and saturation. A raw (marginal)
Spearman correlation between CCT-sensitivity and error is therefore inflated by whatever CCT shares with
the brightness and saturation channels. The partial Spearman correlation rT · bright,sat removes this confound
by regressing out the other two sensitivity channels from both and thus isolates the residual association.
Results.
Table 1 reports marginal and partial Spearman correlations between per-image sensitivity and
per-image error. CCT dominates in both the marginal and partial analyses. Once CCT is controlled,
brightness and saturation carry essentially no residual signal (r = 0.087 and 0.021), identifying illuminant
chromaticity as the operative axis. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests give z = 11.0 for rT > rbright (mean
difference ∆¯r = 0.060) and z = 13.2 for rT > rsat (∆¯r = 0.098). Per Proposition 1, error and |Dfλ| both
blow up near Σ, parameterized by illuminant chromaticity. The CCT-dominant partial-r is much stronger

Σ in CLIP Latent Space: Curvature at The Dress
Sections 2 and 3 established the Jacobian observable on an inverse model (image →reflectance). We now turn
to the curvature observable on a forward encoder and show that the same ambiguity locus Σ is detectable
there.
Setup.
We use CLIP ViT-L/14 (OpenAI weights, Radford et al. [2021]) as the encoder. The image space
B is parameterized by color temperature T ∈[3000, 10000] K: for each T we apply a Bradford chromatic-
adaptation transform (CAT, Hunt [1997]) from D65 (6504 K) to T, embed the adapted image with CLIP,

5
Discussion and Conclusion
Σ and the geometric picture.
The locus Σ is determined by the training prior p(R, I), not architecture:
for both decomposers it lies along the warm-to-daylight illuminant boundary (section 3); for CLIP the
curvature peak at D65 (section 4) reflects a bimodal illuminant prior. That the same locus surfaces across
architectures and observables is the central empirical finding. Recasting The Dress as a singularity of g makes
it observable in any learned system, and the 1/
√
λ, 1/λ scalings of section 2 promote it from qualitative
failure mode to quantitative signature.
Conclusion and implication for mode-aware training.
The bistable percept is the human-visible
tip of a codimension-one discontinuity that any approximation fλ inherits as a Jacobian or curvature spike,
verified here on two unrelated decomposers and a frozen CLIP encoder at the predicted locus. Because the
albedo Jacobian proxies prediction error, downweighting high-Jacobian samples under CCT perturbation
provides a principled, mode-aware alternative to uniform regularization.

From Training Losses to Prior-Mode Section
This appendix supports section 2.2 by showing that standard training objectives recover the prior-mode
section g, and that empirical risk minimisation (ERM) on finite data preserves this conclusion.
8


## Page 9


Population minimiser
When the label yi = g(ci) is deterministic and noise-free, the population minimiser
of any proper scoring rule (including MSE and cross-entropy) is f ∗= g a.e. . Standard ERM consistency
(e.g. via Rademacher complexity bounds; Bartlett and Mendelson 2002) ensures that with sufficient data the
empirical minimiser targets g. The crucial corollary is that f ∗is the prior mode rather than the posterior
mean. The posterior is bimodal near Σ and the posterior mean lies between the two modes (typically off the
fibre) among which f ∗= g selects one of them.
CLIP (InfoNCE)
CLIP’s InfoNCE loss lower-bounds mutual information under a Gaussian mixture model
for the embeddings. Under that prior, log p(R, I | c) is quadratic and g(c) = arg max p is the nearest mixture
centre. InfoNCE pushes each embedding toward its conditional MAP centre; the limiting encoder therefore
approximates the prior-mode section for the Gaussian-mixture prior.
Careaga (Ordinal shading MSE)
Careaga and Aksoy [2023] train with a direct MSE on the ordinal
ground-truth shading and the albedo is an immediate consequence by dividing the observed image pixels
with the recovered shading map. Thus, the model problem can be readily applied to the shading prediction
and thus the singularity at the ambiguous locus translates to the reflectance prediction.

Each objective in Appendix A is a proper loss whose population minimiser is the prior-mode section
g (section A), so each encoder approximates g, and the implicit smoothing of training (weight decay,
augmentation, finite capacity) plays the role of the regulariser λ. Proposition 1 then predicts the same

that spikes rather than the bounded curvature seen off Σ.

the chromatic prior mode, gives the flattest response of the four. The curvature blow-up therefore tracks how
strongly an objective commits to the prior-mode section, exactly as the theory anticipates.
