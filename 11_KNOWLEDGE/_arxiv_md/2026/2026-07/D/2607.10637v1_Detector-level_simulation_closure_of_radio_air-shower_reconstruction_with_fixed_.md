Abstract: Autonomous radio arrays reconstruct air showers from trigger-selected ADC
traces rather than from ideal electric-field footprints. We present a detector-level simulation-
closure test that connects RF-chain-triggered ZHAireS event packages to direction, energy
and quality labels. Geometry is reconstructed from voltage amplitudes and station timing.
The ADC traces are then inverted through the RF chain, restricted to 50–200 MHz and
reduced to the geomagnetic peak amplitude |Exπ| in a radio-core-recentered shower plane.
Energy is obtained by fitting a fixed equal-prior mean of locked iron and proton template
families. The estimator uses neither the simulated primary label nor an empirical amplitude
or zenith scale. Of 972 event packages, 711 pass a common reconstructed-quality gate in the
raw-noisy weighted, raw-hard-denoised equal-weight and clean-reference branches. Their
mean fractional energy residuals are +1.20%, +0.55% and −0.10%, with event-to-event
scatters of 14.51%, 14.40% and 14.25%, respectively.
The median truth-level opening-
angle residual is 0.050◦in this selected simulated sample. These aggregate values conceal
a zenith-dependent energy residual, from about −8% at 60◦–70◦to +11% at 80◦–85◦,
with substantially larger biases in sparsely populated edge bins. The results establish an
internally consistent reconstruction baseline for selected simulated event packages, not a
deployed-array resolution or exposure.
Keywords: ultra-high-energy cosmic rays, extensive air showers, radio detection, event
reconstruction, RF chain, trigger

Introduction
Radio measurements provide a calorimetric view of extensive air showers and can constrain
arrival direction, primary energy and shower development [1–3]. Measurements by AERA,
LOFAR and Tunka-Rex have established reconstruction from lateral distributions, two-
dimensional footprints, pulse templates and radiation energy [4–11]. Sparse autonomous
arrays, including concepts developed for GRAND, face an additional inverse problem [12].
Their offline input is a trigger-selected collection of ADC traces shaped by the antenna,
– 1 –


## Page 3


electronics, filtering, noise and station-level pulse selection, rather than an ideal electric-
field footprint.
Microscopic codes such as ZHAireS and CoREAS remain the reference for radio-
emission simulations [13, 14]. Fast forward and efficiency models, including Radio Mor-
phing, template-synthesis methods, probabilistic array-efficiency models and FARSim, re-
duce the cost of detector-layout and trigger studies [15–18]. FARSim specifically reuses
ZHAireS-derived shower-plane footprints and combines geomagnetic scaling, projection and
RF-chain-aware trigger screening. The present work addresses a different task: the inverse
reconstruction that starts after the RF-chain trigger has produced an event package. Un-
like FARSim’s forward trigger-footprint prediction, this analysis asks whether detector-level
outputs retain sufficient information for stable event reconstruction.
This distinction also separates the present work from ideal-field reconstruction stud-
ies. Noise, RF-chain inversion and pulse-window choices can change station amplitudes,
while trigger selection changes the event population. Detector studies further show that
real autonomous radio units must handle Galactic and receiver noise, radio-frequency inter-
ference (RFI), station-dependent response and ADC-level calibration [19, 20]. A credible
system-level closure test must therefore define the input event object, process noisy and
clean branches under a common reconstruction contract, isolate truth information from
production decisions and state where the resulting numbers cease to apply.
We implement such a contract for simulated GP80-like event packages. Direction is
reconstructed in the voltage domain with a free-ring angular-distribution function (ADF)
and timing fit. The RF chain is inverted to a 50–200 MHz electric field, and energy is fitted
from the geomagnetic shower-plane amplitude |Exπ|. The template is a pointwise equal-
prior mean of locked iron and proton families, fixed before event fitting. Simulated primary
labels and true energies are used only after reconstruction to calculate closure residuals. No
composition-dependent, event-dependent or zenith-dependent amplitude scale is permitted.
The main result is a three-branch system comparison on one common selected event
set: raw-noisy traces with noise-weighted inversion, raw-hard-denoised traces with equal-
weight inversion, and a clean equal-weight reference. This construction tests whether the
two detector-level branches reproduce the same closure behavior as the clean loop without
allowing selection differences to masquerade as waveform improvements.
The resulting
energy scatter is approximately 14.3–14.5% in all three branches. The remaining limitation
is not a branch-specific noise effect but a strong zenith dependence, particularly near the
boundaries of the simulated inclination range.
The scope is intentionally limited.
The quoted energy and direction residuals are
conditional on selected simulated event packages under one site, RF-chain and template
configuration.
They do not include deployed-array GPS timing and positioning errors,
station-to-station gain and noise differences, group-delay calibration uncertainty, long-term
drift, RFI pulse misidentification or accidental triggers.
They are neither an exposure
calculation nor an independently calibrated detector energy scale.

Simulation and detector-level event sample
2.1
Air-shower and site configuration
The validation library contains 2000 ZHAireS showers, with 1000 proton and 1000 iron
primaries. Generated energies span 0.001–4 EeV, zenith angles span 45◦–89◦, and azimuth
angles span 0◦–359◦. The simulations use QGSJET-II-04, a site-specific GDAS atmosphere,
a relative thinning level of 10−5, a thinning weight factor of 0.06 and 3 MeV electron/photon
cuts. Electric-field traces are sampled at 2 ns.
The site configuration is XiaoDuShan at 40.90◦N, 94.05◦E and 1300 m altitude.
The geomagnetic-field magnitude is 56.568 µT, with inclination 61.907◦and declination
−0.058◦. All event construction and reconstruction stages use the same fixed 148-station
layout shown in figure 1.
2.2

Three-polarization ZHAireS electric fields are propagated through antenna effective length,
front-end response and ADC conversion using the same RF-chain implementation for all
branches. The station trigger is evaluated on the filtered, median-subtracted ADC voltage.

This trigger defines the reconstruction input; no
noise-only accidental rate or autonomous operating point is inferred [21, 22].
The trigger and event builder produce 972 packages. Each package contains triggered
station IDs and positions, three-channel ADC traces and trigger times. We reconstruct
three parallel waveform branches:
• Raw-noisy weighted: the full noisy ADC trace is retained. A quiet-window voltage
spectrum supplies channel-dependent weights for RF-chain inversion.
• Raw-hard denoised equal-weight: a zero-phase 50–200 MHz envelope locates the pulse,
but the retained samples are copied from the raw noisy trace. The gate contains 32
samples before and 64 samples after the pulse center, or 192 ns at 2 ns sampling.
Samples outside the gate are zeroed, and equal inversion weights are used.
• Clean equal-weight reference: no stochastic voltage noise is added; equal inversion
weights provide the closed-loop reference.
The raw-noisy and raw-hard-denoised branches are parallel methods.
The latter is not
a post-correction of the former. Preserving the raw waveform within the denoising gate
is essential because replacing it with the bandpassed trace suppresses the reconstructed
amplitude.

3.1
Workflow and truth isolation
Figure 2 summarizes the reconstruction. Voltage amplitudes and trigger times determine
the event geometry.
RF-chain inversion then reconstructs station electric fields, which
are projected into a radio-core-recentered shower plane. A fixed one-parameter template
fit provides the energy estimate. Only reconstructed quantities enter these stages. Truth
direction, energy and primary are joined after reconstruction for validation plots and tables.

3.3
RF-chain inversion and station observable
The ADC traces are inverted through the recorded antenna and electronics response. The
reconstructed frequency-domain field is restricted to 50–200 MHz and transformed back to
the time domain. At each station, the peak sample is selected from the envelope of the full
three-component electric-field vector. The signed vector at that sample is projected into
the shower-plane basis. The xπ axis is parallel to ⃗v × ⃗B, and the fitted station observable is
Ei = |Exπ,i|.
(3.3)
Selecting the time from the vector envelope avoids assigning the pulse time from one po-
larization while retaining a geomagnetic amplitude for the template fit.
We use peak field rather than energy fluence in the production estimator. Fluence and
radiation-energy methods are physically well motivated for calibrated electric-field analy-
ses [4, 5]. In this detector-level setting, however, a squared-field time integral also integrates
residual RF-chain noise, gate leakage and bandpass ringing. It therefore requires a sepa-
rately validated signal-window, noise-window subtraction and calibration convention. The
localized peak observable provides the better-controlled baseline for the present system-
closure test.

The intersection is a comparison device, not an unbiased population estimator. The
generated library extends below the energies that produce event packages in the fixed trigger
configuration, and only six selected events lie in the 45◦–60◦bin. An exposure calculation
would require thrown-core distributions, livetime, noise-only accidental rates and energy-

The low-zenith deficit has a direct footprint-sampling component. The radial maximum
of the locked equal-prior template increases from approximately 113 m at 45◦to 206 m at
60◦, 347 m at 70◦and 466 m at 75◦(figure 3c).
By comparison, for a radio core at the central station, the nearest noncentral station
ring is at 577 m and the next ring is at 1000 m. The central station then samples inside the
Cherenkov-like maximum, while the nearest ring is already on the falling flank for zenith
angles up to about 75◦; at 45◦–50◦, it is even beyond the radial support shared by the two
locked templates. Offset cores can bring individual stations through the compact footprint,

Consistently, only 8 of 690 generated 45◦–60◦showers form event packages, and the six final
events have a median of 6.5 fitted stations. The corresponding event-package fractions are
71.7% and 90.3% in the 60◦–70◦and 70◦–75◦bins, where the selected median Nfit rises to 11
and 25. This trend follows the established growth of the radio-illuminated area with zenith
angle and explains why kilometre-scale sparse arrays are primarily effective for inclined
showers [25, 26]. The low-zenith residuals therefore combine a compact-footprint sampling
limitation with a strongly selected event population; they should not be interpreted as an
intrinsic LDF-model bias alone.
4.2
Energy closure in the three waveform branches
The three branches give similar energy closure on the 711-event common set (figure 4 and ta-
ble 3). The raw-noisy weighted branch has mean residual +1.20% and scatter 14.51%. The
raw-hard-denoised equal-weight branch gives +0.55% and 14.40%, and the clean reference
gives −0.10% and 14.25%. Their 95% bootstrap intervals overlap. The result supports
the use of raw noisy traces with inversion weights and raw-hard-gated traces with equal
inversion weights as two parallel detector-level methods.
The paired differences are substantially narrower than the full event-to-event residu-
als. Relative to clean, the raw-noisy branch differs by +1.29% on average with a 2.64%
scatter, while the raw-hard-denoised branch differs by +0.65% with a 1.56% scatter. Thus
most of the approximately 14% spread is common to all waveform branches and originates
downstream of, or independently from, stochastic voltage noise.
Representative high-R2 LDF fits are shown in figure 6. The triggered-station ampli-
tudes follow the equal-prior template well in the central zenith range, while the footprint
broadens and the one-dimensional collapse becomes less complete for the most inclined
example. The fitted amplitude uses the interpolated template values at station radii; the
continuous line is drawn only to display the underlying locked profile. Events below 70◦
are omitted from this visual-fit panel because the selected low-zenith sample has too few
fitted stations for a comparably informative LDF-shape display; their statistics remain
documented in table 5.
4.3
Residual structure and estimator boundary
The near-zero aggregate mean results from averaging over an angle-dependent residual
(figure 7 and table 5). In the raw-hard-denoised branch, the mean changes from −8.1% at
60◦–70◦to +11.0% at 80◦–85◦. The 45◦–60◦and 85◦–90◦bins reach −23.8% and +31.0%,
respectively, but contain only 6 and 23 events. Similar trends occur in the raw-noisy and
clean branches, indicating that the dominant residual is not caused by denoising.
The truth-primary split gives an additional validation boundary.
In the raw-hard-
denoised branch, true iron events have mean −5.89% and scatter 9.18%, while true proton
events have mean +6.32% and scatter 15.73%. Because the same equal-prior template is
used for both subsets, these opposite residuals quantify an unresolved template-family and
shower-development dependence. They do not constitute a composition classifier.

Truth-free template and quality-gate audits
Figure 9 compares the fixed equal-prior template with three truth-free alternatives on the
same 711 events. A minimum-log-mismatch choice does not improve the result: its scatter
is about 18.6–18.7%. A fixed iron template produces a positive mean and about 18.5–18.8%
scatter. A fixed proton template gives the smallest scatter, about 11.7–12.0%, but carries a
−6.3% to −7.4% mean bias. The equal-prior policy is used as the symmetric, coefficient-free
endpoint mean: it is fixed for every event, remains close to zero mean in all branches and
stays below 15% scatter without empirical calibration. Because all policies are audited on
the same catalog, this comparison is a diagnostic rather than an independent demonstration
that the equal-prior policy is optimal.
The quality-cut scan in figure 10 shows why the baseline keeps Nfit ≥5. Requiring 8
or 10 fitted stations lowers the scatter by less than one percentage point but removes 49
or 87 events and shifts the mean positive. Tightening R2 reduces the catalog to 555 events
with only a modest scatter change. Loosening it increases the catalog to 801 events and
broadens the scatter to about 15.7–15.8%. The baseline is therefore a balanced analysis
domain rather than the threshold combination that minimizes scatter post hoc.

5
Discussion
The principal result is the closure of a complete detector-level reconstruction contract, not
a new microscopic emission model. Trigger-selected ADC traces are converted to geometry,
electric fields, energy and quality labels without event-primary truth or an empirical am-
– 14 –


## Page 16


plitude scale. Raw-noisy weighted inversion and raw-hard-denoised equal-weight inversion
give nearly the same event-level result as the clean loop. Their small paired differences
show that the dominant 14% scatter is shared by all branches rather than introduced by
stochastic voltage noise alone.
The fixed equal-prior template is central to this interpretation. It removes the truth-
primary leakage that would arise from selecting the template family from the simulated file-
name. It also avoids using minimum station-level mismatch as an implicit event-dependent
family selector. The remaining iron/proton residual split is therefore visible rather than
calibrated away. This is scientifically preferable for a closure paper: the aggregate mean
is close to zero, but the validation plots expose the unresolved shower-development depen-
dence and the zenith region where the estimator is not yet reliable. The same-sample policy
audit and the non-identical detailed bandpass shapes mean that the near-zero aggregate
bias should not be read as an independently calibrated unbiasedness claim; that requires a
disjoint template-validation sample and a harmonized transfer-function test.
The angular trend identifies the most important next methodological step.
A one-
dimensional Egeo(ρ) profile cannot fully describe a footprint whose Cherenkov ring, charge-
excess interference, early-late asymmetry and radio-core projection vary across the shower
plane. Models developed for inclined showers support moving toward physically corrected
LDFs or two-dimensional footprint fits, including density, geomagnetic-angle, coherence and
Cherenkov-radius dependence [26, 27]. Such an extension should be tested under the same
no-scale rule and on an independent template-validation split. It should not be introduced
as a zenith-dependent multiplicative correction to the present catalog.
Peak field is retained as the production observable because it localizes the measurement
around the reconstructed pulse and limits integrated noise. A fluence or radiation-energy
estimator may ultimately be more stable against phase and peak-definition effects, but only
after the RF-chain inversion, signal window and noise subtraction are calibrated jointly. In
threshold-level detector traces, squaring and integrating a residual noise floor can create
a positive contribution even when the pulse itself is unbiased.
The correct comparison
is therefore a separate detector-level estimator with fixed noise subtraction, not a direct
replacement of peak amplitude by an uncorrected integral.
Several limitations prevent direct field-performance claims. First, the templates and
validation events share the ZHAireS/site framework; no cross-generator or leave-one-shower-
out template test is claimed. Second, the template library contains only proton and iron
endpoints at one reference energy and fixed reference azimuth before geometrical transfor-
mations. Intermediate masses and Xmax-resolved fluctuations are not explicitly modeled.
Third, trigger thresholds define the event packages but are not calibrated to a noise-only
accidental rate. Fourth, no deployed-event sample is analyzed. Finally, the radio-core shift
is a refractive coordinate proxy, and no standalone particle-core or radio-core resolution is
reported.
The quoted numbers must remain attached to these boundaries. The approximately
14.3–14.5% energy scatter is selected-sample simulation closure, not detector energy reso-
lution. The 0.050◦median opening residual excludes timing, positioning and calibration
systematics. The 85◦–90◦result is a diagnostic edge bin with 23 events, not a validated
– 15 –


## Page 17


precision regime. These qualifications are not secondary caveats; they define what has and
has not been demonstrated.
6
Conclusions
We have constructed and tested a truth-isolated reconstruction pipeline for RF-chain-
triggered radio air-shower simulations. Voltage-domain geometry, RF-chain inversion, a
50–200 MHz geomagnetic peak observable and a fixed equal-prior radio-core-recentered
template produce consistent energy closure in raw-noisy weighted, raw-hard-denoised equal-
weight and clean-reference branches. On the common 711-event quality-selected catalog,
the three branches have mean residuals between −0.10% and +1.20% and scatters between
14.25% and 14.51%, without empirical amplitude scaling.
The global closure does not remove the estimator boundary. Energy residuals evolve
from negative at lower zenith to positive at high zenith, and true proton events remain
broader than true iron events under the common template. The next improvement should
therefore target the physical footprint model, preferably with a corrected or two-dimensional
template and an independent validation sample, while preserving the fixed-normalization
and truth-isolation requirements used here.
The present result provides a reproducible
detector-level baseline against which such extensions can be judged.

The submission package accompanying this manuscript contains the 711-event truth-free
