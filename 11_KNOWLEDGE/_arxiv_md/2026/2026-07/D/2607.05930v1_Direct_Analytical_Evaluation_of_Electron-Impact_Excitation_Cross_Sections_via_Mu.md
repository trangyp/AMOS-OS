Direct Analytical Evaluation of Electron-Impact Excitation Cross Sections via
Multiconfigurational Binary Encounter Approach: Applications to Benzene and
Naphthalene
Kaoru Yamazakia)
Materials DX Research Center, National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST), 1-1-1 Umezono, Tsukuba,
Ibaraki 305-8568, Japan
We present a multiconfigurational binary-encounter (MC-BE) framework for direct analytical evaluation of electron-
impact electronic-excitation cross sections from ab initio excited-state data for dipole-allowed transitions. The method
combines the threshold-modified Mott–Massey (TMMM) approximation with binary-encounter (BE/BE 𝑓) scaling. In
this multiconfigurational extension, the effective binding energy entering the BE/BE 𝑓prefactor is evaluated from
amplitude-weighted occupied-orbital contributions computed by linear-response time-dependent density functional
theory (LR-TDDFT), without system-specific fitting parameters. For benzene, MC-BE/TMMM cross sections for the
dominant 1 1𝐸1u (𝜋→𝜋∗) band agree well with experiment over the 10–20 eV range and, for this band and range, show
better agreement with experiment than the SMC/TCIS results of Falkowski et al. [J. Chem. Phys. 159, 194301 (2023)].
For naphthalene, the calculated total excitation cross section reproduces the onset and principal maximum of the gas-
phase apparent fluorescence cross section, used as an emission-based proxy under dipole-dominated conditions, without
empirical energy shifts or intensity scaling. Analytic peak-position and peak-height expressions, parameterized by
𝑟= ⟨𝐵⟩/Δ𝐸, where Δ𝐸is the vertical excitation energy, indicate that typical valence excitations peak at about 1.5–1.6 Δ𝐸
with substantial BE attenuation, providing a practical diagnostic for relating measured cross-section profiles to excitation
energies. Although illustrated with LR-TDDFT, the framework can be formally extended to other wave-function-based
excited-state theories, provided that compatible amplitudes and well-defined orbital energies are available for the chosen
reference function. Together, the results support MC-BE/TMMM as a practical, computationally inexpensive route for
modeling electron-impact excitation of polyatomic molecules for transitions carrying finite oscillator strength.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Electron-impact electronic-excitation cross sections are
fundamental quantities in gas-phase scattering1–3, plasma
kinetics4, and radiation chemistry5. Rigorous scattering ap-
proaches such as the 𝑅-matrix method6,7, the Schwinger multi-
channel (SMC) method8,9, and the molecular convergent close-
coupling method10 can, in principle, provide accurate cross
sections across the full energy range; however, their compu-
tational cost scales steeply with the number of electrons and
target states, limiting practical applications to atoms and small
molecules4,6,7,9,10. For medium-to-large polyatomic systems,
computationally inexpensive analytical formulations are there-
fore essential.
Analytical approaches rooted in the first Born picture—
such as the dipole-Born approximation (DBA)11—yield com-
pact expressions that are accurate at high incident energies
but require threshold corrections and empirical adjustments to
perform reliably near the onset of excitation. The threshold-
modified Mott–Massey (TMMM) approximation4,12 enforces
the correct threshold behavior and retains the proper high-
energy asymptote; nevertheless, it systematically overesti-
mates integral cross sections at low incident energies, mo-
tivating the introduction of binary-encounter (BE) scaling and
its oscillator-strength variant (BE 𝑓)1,13. Yet direct application
of these models to ab initio excited-state data has remained im-
practical: standard binary-encounter (BE) formulations treat
each transition as originating from a single occupied orbital

and therefore cannot account for the multiconfigurational char-
acter of realistic electronic excitations1,13. In particular, no
principled procedure has existed to determine the effective
binding energy1,13—the key quantity that governs the BE/BE 𝑓
scaling—when multiple occupied orbitals contribute to a given
transition.
In this work, we remove this limitation by developing a
multiconfigurational binary-encounter (MC-BE) framework
in which the effective binding energy is evaluated from
amplitude-weighted occupied-orbital contributions obtained
via linear-response time-dependent density functional theory
(LR-TDDFT)14.
Together with excitation energies and os-
cillator strengths computed at the same level of theory, this
enables ab initio excited-state data to feed directly into analyt-
ical cross-section formulas for dipole-allowed transitions with-
out system-specific fitting. In the resulting MC-BE formula-
tion, the TMMM expression provides the threshold-corrected
Born-type baseline, while the BE/BE 𝑓prefactor uses a mul-
ticonfigurational effective binding energy evaluated from the
excited-state amplitudes.
We validate the approach through benchmark calculations
for benzene and naphthalene, two prototypical aromatic hy-
drocarbons for which reliable experimental electron energy-
loss spectra (EELS) in the optical limit and excitation cross-
section data in the gas phase are available15–18.

III.
COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
Equilibrium geometries of benzene and naphthalene were
optimized at the B3LYP/def2-TZVP level of density functional
theory31,32.
Normal-mode analyses were performed at the
same level to confirm convergence to minima.
Singlet excited-state wave functions, excitation energies,
and oscillator strengths for 3000 low-lying states were com-
puted by LR-TDDFT using the Casida formalism14,19.
We
employed the long-range corrected hybrid functional 𝜔B97X-
D33 to describe both valence and Rydberg excitations and to
provide reliable orbital energies and ionization potentials (IPs),
which are essential for evaluating ⟨𝐵𝑛⟩via Eq. (4), as sum-
marized in Table IV in the Appendix D. To compute ⟨𝐵𝑛⟩, we
included excitation configurations satisfying 10−4 ≤|𝑋𝑛
𝑖𝑎| ≤1
and de-excitation configurations satisfying 10−4 ≤|𝑌𝑛
𝑖𝑎| ≤1.
For C, we used the Sapporo-2012-TZP34 basis set aug-
mented with diffuse functions (1𝑠, 1𝑝, 1𝑑, 1 𝑓) and fur-
ther augmented by the Dunning–Hay double-Rydberg set
(2𝑠, 2𝑝, 2𝑑)35 to balance valence and Rydberg character. For
H, Sapporo-2012-DZP34 was employed.
The IP was evaluated by the ΔSCF method at the same level
of theory as the LR-TDDFT calculations.
All quantum-chemistry calculations were carried out with
Gaussian 16 Rev. C.0236. Basis sets were obtained from the
Basis Set Exchange37–39.
IV.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
To validate the MC-BE/TMMM framework developed in
Sec. II, we apply it to the electron-impact excitation of two
prototypical aromatic hydrocarbons: benzene and naphtha-
lene. These molecules were chosen because (i) reliable gas-
phase experimental data are available—an electron energy-loss
spectrum (EELS)15 and an integral excitation cross section16
for benzene, and an EELS17 and a fluorescence cross section18
for naphthalene—and (ii) progression from a monocyclic to
a fused polycyclic system probes the ability of the method to
handle an increasingly dense manifold of low-lying excited
states. For each molecule, we first assess the quality of the
TD-𝜔B97X-D excitation energies and oscillator strengths by
comparing the simulated EELS with experiment, thereby con-
firming that the ab initio input to the MC-BE formulas is phys-
ically reasonable. We then evaluate the MC-BE/TMMM cross
sections against the available experimental cross-section data
and identify the dominant contributing excited states through
state-resolved analysis.

Benzene

1.
Overview of benzene excitation features in EELS
Figure 1(a) displays the experimental EELS spectrum of
benzene recorded at an incident electron energy of 𝑇= 100 eV
and a scattering angle of 𝜃= 5◦.15 The spectrum exhibits
a prominent dipole-allowed feature, hereafter labeled B1
(∼6.9 eV), which encompasses contributions from one or more
singlet excited states.
To facilitate comparison, a simulated spectrum was con-
structed from the TD-𝜔B97X-D vertical excitation energies
(Sec. III) by convolving each transition with a Gaussian line
shape of FWHM = 0.30 and 0.60 eV for singlet excited states
below and above the ΔSCF ionization potential (9.27 eV), re-
spectively. A uniform energy shift of −0.13 eV was applied
to align the calculated envelope with the experimental spec-
trum. The resulting stick spectrum and the broadened profile
are superimposed on the experimental EELS trace in Fig. 1(a).
On the basis of both the present TD-𝜔B97X-D results and
previous calculations at the CASPT240, EOM-CC341, and
FC/SSE(SAC–CI, ESF)42 levels of theory (Table II), the B1
band is attributed to the 11𝐸1u, 21𝐸1u, and 11𝐴2u states. The
four theoretical methods agree with one another and with ex-
periment to within ∼0.2 eV for the band positions (Table II).
The vertical excitation energies and oscillator strengths there-
fore provide physically reasonable input for the subsequent
electron-impact excitation cross-section calculations within
the MC-BE formalism. The ionization potential calculated
from the orbital energy of the HOMO (9.07 eV) quantitatively
agrees with that from ΔSCF (9.27 eV) and with experiment
(9.24 eV43) as summarized in Table IV in the Appendix D.
This indicates that the calculated orbital energies are reason-
able input for evaluating the effective binding energy ⟨𝐵𝑛⟩in
the MC-BE formalism.

2.
Integral excitation cross section
The gas-phase integral excitation cross section for the B1
band serves as an experimental reference for validating the cal-
culated cross sections. For a typical electronic excitation with
𝑟∼1–10, the cross-section maximum for a dipole-allowed
transition is expected to appear at an incident energy of ap-
proximately 1.5–1.6 Δ𝐸(Table I). On this basis, the main fea-
ture of the experimentally observed cross section at 12.5 eV is
assigned to the dipole-allowed 11𝐸1u, 21𝐸1u, and 11𝐴2u states.
The calculated total excitation cross section is compared
with the experimental excitation cross section in Fig. 1(b).
The overall agreement is satisfactory: the theoretical profile
reproduces the behavior in the range 𝑇= 10–20 eV. Further-
more, for this B1-band benchmark over the 10–20 eV range,
the MC-BE/TMMM results show better agreement with ex-
periment than the SMC/TCIS results of Ref.16. For the ben-
zene B1-band benchmark examined here, the calculated cross
section is weakly sensitive to the choice between the two long-
range corrected exchange–correlation functionals considered
in Appendix E (Fig. 7 and Table V).
Analysis of the state-resolved cross sections (Fig. 2) con-
firms that the dipole-allowed 1 1𝐸1u (𝜋→𝜋∗) transition is the

(Fig. 4) confirms that the dipole-allowed 2 1𝐵3u (𝜋→𝜋∗) tran-
sition is the dominant state-resolved contributor, accounting
for 74% of the total excitation cross section at its peak posi-
tion (𝑇= 9.07 eV). This percentage should be viewed as a
calculation-specific state-resolved indicator; the total excita-
tion cross section is the more relevant quantity for comparison
with experiment. The threshold of the 1 1𝐵2u (𝜋→𝜋∗) state is

cross section appears at 𝑇= 8.90 eV, overestimating the ex-
perimental fluorescence maximum by ∼1.1 eV. This deviation
may correspond to an overestimation of the excitation energy
by ∼0.7 eV, which lies within the combined uncertainties of the
TD-𝜔B97X-D calculation (≲0.3 eV) and the vertical approx-
imation. The 2 1𝐵2u (𝜋→𝜋∗) state exhibits a broad plateau in
its calculated cross section over the 9–13 eV range, supporting
this tentative assignment.
The remaining discrepancies between the calculated and ex-
perimental profiles can be attributed to two principal sources:
(i) the intrinsic accuracy of the LR-TDDFT excitation energies
(≲0.3 eV, Table III) and (ii) the neglect of vibronic struc-
ture within the vertical approximation20–22,49. Extension of
the present MC-BE framework to incorporate vibronic effects
constitutes a natural direction for future work.

The methodology is not restricted to LR-TDDFT. Because
the effective binding energy ⟨𝐵𝑛⟩is defined through amplitude-
weighted occupied-orbital contributions, the framework ad-
mits a formal extension to other wave-function-based excited-
state theories, provided that compatible amplitudes and well-
defined orbital energies are available for the chosen refer-

BE/TMMM result is weakly sensitive to the choice between
the two long-range corrected functionals examined here, sup-
porting the robustness of the total cross section for this test
case.
Two principal directions for future work emerge from the
present study. First, incorporation of Franck–Condon factors
and Herzberg–Teller vibronic coupling20–22 would account for
vibronic effects beyond the present vertical treatment and im-
prove band-shape predictions, while spin–orbit coupling23–26
would allow transitions that acquire finite oscillator strength
through spin–orbit mixing to be treated, particularly for transi-
tions such as the 1 1𝐵2u state of naphthalene, where the Franck–
Condon envelope significantly broadens the spectral profile49.
Second, systematic application to larger polycyclic aromatic
hydrocarbons and heteroaromatic systems46 would further es-
tablish the scope and predictive capability of the method across
diverse molecular classes.

To assess the sensitivity of the MC-BE/TMMM cross sec-
tions to the choice of exchange–correlation functional for the
benzene B1-band benchmark, we recomputed the benzene
cross sections with a second long-range corrected hybrid func-
tional, LC-𝜔HPBE51, using the same basis set and computa-
tional protocol as described in Sec. III.
Figure 7 compares the integral cross sections in the B1
band of benzene obtained with 𝜔B97X-D33 and LC-𝜔HPBE51.
The two profiles are nearly indistinguishable over the entire
7–21 eV range, confirming the qualitative robustness of the
MC-BE/TMMM results.

and total peak properties are summarized in Table V: the total
peak cross sections agree to within ∼5 % (3.83×10−16 cm2 for
