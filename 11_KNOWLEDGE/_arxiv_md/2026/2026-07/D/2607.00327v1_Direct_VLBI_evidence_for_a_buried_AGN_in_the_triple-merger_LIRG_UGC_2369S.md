ABSTRACT
Context. UGC 2369S is a luminous infrared galaxy (LIRG) undergoing a late-stage merger in a triple system, where the heavily
obscured northern core is suspected to host an active galactic nucleus (AGN). However, severe dust and gas obscuration makes
definitive confirmation challenging.
Aims. We aim to provide direct observational evidence for the buried AGN through high-resolution radio imaging, while investigating
the AGN accretion and feedback properties within this merger-driven gas-rich environment.
Methods. We analyzed archival European VLBI Network (1.6 GHz) and Very Long Baseline Array (1.7 and 5 GHz) data of
UGC 2369S. Through high-resolution imaging and visibility-domain Gaussian modeling, we characterized the morphology and in-
tensity of its milliarcsecond-scale radio emission.
Results. A compact radio component is detected at the northern core, exhibiting high brightness temperature (Tb > 107 K) and flat
radio spectrum (α ≈−0.45), which confirms the presence of an obscured AGN. The sub-Eddington accretion rate (λEdd ≈2.7 × 10−4)
indicates that it falls within the radiatively inefficient accretion flow (RIAF) state.
Conclusions. We provide direct imaging evidence for an AGN in the northern core of UGC 2369S, revealing a deeply buried,
jet-emitting low-luminosity AGN (LLAGN) enshrouded by a Compton-thick gas cocoon. This demonstrates that VLBI is a uniquely
effective tool for disentangling nuclear accretion and feedback processes within the heavily obscured environments of multiple-merger
systems.

1. Introduction
Galaxy mergers play a fundamental role in the co-evolution
of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) and their host galax-
ies (Sanders et al. 1988; Hopkins et al. 2008). During late-stage
mergers, strong gravitational torques effectively remove angu-
lar momentum from the gas, driving massive inflows into the
central regions (Barnes & Hernquist 1991, 1996). These in-
flows not only trigger intense starbursts (Barnes & Hernquist
1996) but also fuel the central engines (Di Matteo et al. 2005;
Treister et al. 2012), frequently forming dual or multiple ac-
tive galactic nuclei (AGNs) when two or more SMBHs are
simultaneously active (e.g., Koss et al. 2012). Among them,
triple-merger systems represent an exceptionally rare and ex-
treme regime, providing unique insights into AGN accretion
and feedback in complex environments (De Rosa et al. 2019;
Pfeifle et al. 2019). However, these merger-driven inflows cre-
ate remarkably dense environments, heavily obscuring the ac-
creting SMBHs (Ricci et al. 2017a; Koss et al. 2018). In the case

of jetted AGNs, very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) pro-
vides a highly effective approach to probe the exact interplay
among accretion triggering, nuclear obscuration, and jet feed-
back in multi-SMBH systems (e.g., Deane et al. 2014; An et al.
2018; Gabányi et al. 2019).
UGC 2369S, the southern irregular galaxy in the UGC 2369
interacting pair, is a late-stage luminous infrared galaxy (LIRG)
merger system comprising three distinct components separated
by ∼3 kpc: the northern (N), southeast (SE), and southwest
(SW) cores (see Figure A.1; Roper et al. 2010; Ding et al. 2026).
Recent comprehensive multi-wavelength observations character-
ized this complex triple-merger environment, indicating that a
buried AGN might reside within the northern core (Ding et al.
2026). However, definitive observational confirmation of the
central engine is still lacking, as the overall infrared emission can
also be fully explained by starburst activity (Vega et al. 2008). To
unambiguously identify the central engine, high-resolution radio
observations are crucial. While early VLBI snapshot detection
experiments inferred an AGN-like core with a high brightness
temperature (> 107 K) in UGC 2369S (Lonsdale et al. 1993)
and indirectly deduced its presence by ruling out extreme radio

Throughout this work, a flat ΛCDM cosmological model with
Ωm = 0.31, ΩΛ = 0.69, and H0 = 67.7 km s−1 Mpc−1 is adopted

z = 0.0318 (Ding et al. 2026), corresponding to a luminosity dis-
tance of DL = 144.2 Mpc, yielding a physical scale of ≈0.66 pc
per milliarcsecond (mas). To facilitate comparison with the liter-
ature, we report radio luminosities in SI units (W or W Hz−1) and
luminosities in X-ray and optical bands in CGS units (erg s−1).
2. Data reduction
In this study, we retrieved the publicly available archival VLBI
data for UGC 2369S from the European VLBI Network (EVN;
comprising eight European antennas) and the Very Long Base-
line Array (VLBA). The dataset comprises three projects:
EC020B (PI: J.E. Conway, mentioned in Polatidis et al. 2004),
BL029 (PI: C.J. Lonsdale), and BM190A (PI: R. Maiolino).
The total on-source time for the target UGC 2369S, observed in
phase-referencing mode, was approximately 100 minutes in each
project. Due to technical malfunctions and tape recording errors
at certain stations, a portion of the data from project EC020B was
irreversibly lost, resulting in a slight degradation of the overall
array sensitivity and (u, v)-coverage. Table 1 summarizes the ob-
servational details, while Figure A.1 displays the corresponding
fields of view (FoVs). Although these observations were con-
ducted more than 20 years ago with the primary goal of studying
elusive AGNs in starburst galaxies, to our knowledge, the data
have not been published until now.
Data calibration was performed using the US National Ra-
dio Astronomy Observatory (NRAO) Astronomical Image Pro-
cessing System (AIPS; Greisen 2003). For the VLBA data, the
calibration generally followed the standard procedures described
in the AIPS Cookbook1; the EVN data were calibrated follow-
ing the EVN Data Reduction Guide2. A priori amplitude cali-
bration was applied using the antenna system temperatures (TY)
and gain curves (GC) provided by the VLBI stations. Following
instrumental and bandpass corrections, global fringe-fitting so-
lutions derived from the phase calibrators were interpolated and
1 http://www.aips.nrao.edu/cook.html
2 https://www.evlbi.org/evn-data-reduction-guide
applied to the target source. For the 1996 observation (project
BL029), ionospheric correction was not performed because the
ionospheric calibration file was unavailable for this early epoch.
The Los Alamos (LA) and Effelsberg (EF) stations were chosen
as the reference antennas for the VLBA and EVN data calibra-
tion, respectively.
The calibrated visibility data were loaded into the DIFMAP
software package (Shepherd 1997) for imaging and brightness
distribution modeling. An initial inspection of the dirty maps re-
vealed a clear signal (> 8σ) near the northern core position in all
three observations. The positional stability of this signal under
different weighting schemes (i.e., uniform and natural weight-
ing) further demonstrates the robustness of the detection. Phase
and amplitude self-calibration was not performed given the faint-
ness of the detected core (S ν < 5 mJy), resulting in conservative
flux density estimates due to coherence loss (e.g., Mosoni et al.
2006; Martí-Vidal et al. 2010). To quantitatively describe the
source structure, we fitted a single circular Gaussian model to
the visibility data of each observation using the modelfit task,
deriving the component size, position, and flux density.
3. Results
Figure 1 shows the restored VLBI images after Gaussian mod-
eling. A bright radio component corresponding to the northern
core was clearly detected (> 18σ) in all three observations.
We applied an astrometric correction to the target coordinates
based on updated phase calibrator coordinates from the Astrogeo
RFC_2026a release (Petrov & Kovalev 2025; see Appendix B).
The corrected VLBI coordinates, along with the Gaia DR3 coun-
terpart (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016, 2023), are listed in Ta-
ble 2. Among them, the VLBA 5 GHz coordinates represent
the most precise position of the AGN jet base. The radio po-
sitional uncertainties account for statistical errors from thermal
noise, intrinsic uncertainties of the phase calibrator, and an ad-
ditional systematic error of ∼1 mas arising from unmodeled at-
mospheric phase residuals scaled by the target–calibrator sep-
aration (Pradel et al. 2006). Notably, the spatial offsets among
the different observations significantly exceed the positional un-
certainties, likely arising from resolution mismatches, intrinsic
source structure variations, and Gaia photocenter jitter within the
dust-obscured merger environment.

unambiguous evidence for a compact, non-thermal jet base. An
alternative origin, such as a nuclear starburst (e.g., Condon 1992;
Middelberg et al. 2004) or clustered radio supernovae, can be
ruled out given the unphysical source concentration required
(Smith et al. 1998), the decade-long stability of the high bright-
ness temperature (Pérez-Torres et al. 2010), and the insufficient
local star formation rate (∼14.7 M⊙yr−1; Ding et al. 2026) to
power the compact radio emission.
4. Discussion
Combining radio, optical, and X-ray diagnostics, we performed
a multi-wavelength analysis to characterize the accretion and
feedback properties of the northern core (see Appendix D).
By applying the black hole fundamental plane (FP) relation
for sub-Eddington objects (Plotkin et al. 2012) to the VLBA
5 GHz luminosity of LR = νLν = (2.61 ± 0.29) × 1038 erg s−1
and black hole mass of MBH
=
(4.73 ± 0.24) × 108M⊙
(Ding et al. 2026), we predict an intrinsic X-ray luminosity of
log(LX,FP/erg s−1) = 42.00 ± 0.41. The Chandra-observed X-ray
luminosity of log(L2−8 keV/erg s−1) = 40.12 ± 0.08 (Ding et al.
2026) is lower than the VLBI-predicted value by a factor of
∼76, implying either an intrinsically fuel-starved engine or se-
vere gas obscuration (e.g., Liu et al. 2013; Xu et al. 2024). Based
on standard X-ray photoelectric absorption modeling (see Ap-
pendix D.1), we estimate an extreme hydrogen column density
of NH ≳2.6 × 1024 cm−2. Given that simple absorption mod-
els typically underestimate the column density under such ex-
treme obscuration (NH ≳1024 cm−2; Murphy & Yaqoob 2009),
our result is consistent with the column density independently
derived from near-infrared integral field spectroscopy of the nu-
clear molecular gas (NH ≳1025 cm−2; Ding et al. 2026). This
deeply buried nature is further corroborated by the thickness
parameter (T = LX/Lint
[O III]; Bassani et al. 1999). Using the ob-
served X-ray and extinction-corrected [O III] luminosities, the
exceptionally low ratio of T ≈0.09 firmly places the northern
core in the Compton-thick regime (T < 1). Ultimately, the agree-
ment between the [O III]-derived and FP-predicted intrinsic X-
ray luminosities (see Figure D.1) confirms a powerful central
engine hidden behind a dense cocoon, effectively ruling out the
fuel-starved scenario.

On a larger scale extending to a radius of 1′′.8 (∼1.2 kpc),
Ding et al. (2026) reported massive gas outflows with a total ki-
netic power of ˙Ekin ∼3×1042 erg s−1, which falls well within the
maximum starburst energy injection rate (∼1.0 × 1043 erg s−1)
and is thus consistent with a starburst-driven origin. The AGN
jet mechanical power we derive (Lmech ≈2.07 × 1042 erg s−1) is
roughly a factor of five lower than this starburst budget, indicat-
ing that the jet alone is insufficient to power the outflow. AGN
feedback can also proceed radiatively (e.g., Fiore et al. 2017),
and the AGN bolometric luminosity (Lbol ≈1.60 × 1043 erg s−1)
is comparable to the starburst energy injection rate. However,
driving the outflow by radiation alone would require a coupling
efficiency of ˙Ekin/Lbol ≈0.19, above the 0.1%–10% range found
for AGN-driven winds (Fiore et al. 2017). Therefore, the nuclear
starburst most likely dominates the energy budget of the large-
scale outflows, while a contribution from the AGN cannot be
fully excluded.
The analysis of radio emission origins (see Appendix D.4)
indicates that the VLBI-detected emission is jet-dominated
rather than coronal. Of the total VLA 1.49 GHz emission
(Condon et al. 1990; Vardoulaki et al. 2015), the compact emis-
sion detected by EVN 1.6 GHz observations accounts for ∼11%,
while star formation contributes an estimated ∼22%, and the
residual ∼67% is associated with extended jets or outflows re-
solved out by VLBI.
For the southeast (SE) and southwest (SW) components
of the triple system UGC 2369S, no significant radio emis-
sion (> 6σ) was detected within a 1′′ radius of their Gaia
positions. Therefore, we only report 6σ upper limits for their
VLBA 5 GHz flux densities. The radio luminosity upper lim-
its and the corresponding maximum X-ray luminosities permit-
ted by the FP relation were also estimated (see Table D.1).
Based on the extinction-corrected [O III] luminosities and Equa-
tion D.3, the intrinsic X-ray luminosities were estimated to be
log(LX,[O III]/erg s−1) ≈42.24 and 41.79 for the SE and SW
cores, respectively. For the SE core, LX,[O III] > LX,FP, which
implies that if it hosted a typical AGN following standard scal-
ing relations, the expected radio emission would well exceed
our VLBA 5 GHz detection threshold. The non-detections in
both VLBI and Chandra observations strongly suggest that the
SE core is inherently radio-deficient, where jet activity is likely
suppressed by the central dense-gas environment typical of late-
stage mergers. In contrast, for the SW core, assuming the nom-
inal mass estimate (see Table D.1), the intrinsic X-ray luminos-
ity LX,[O III] falls below its corresponding FP upper limit LX,FP,
suggesting that the VLBI non-detection is likely due to insuf-
ficient sensitivity rather than an intrinsic deficit; thus, we can-
not rule out the presence of a radio-quiet AGN. Another possi-
bility is that the radio emission in both nuclei originates from
non-AGN processes (e.g., star formation), which would be com-
pletely resolved out by the long baselines, leading to the VLBI
non-detections.
5. Conclusion and summary
We have presented high-resolution imaging and analysis of
archival VLBI data to reveal the compact radio emission within
the triple-merger system UGC 2369S, and investigated the ac-
cretion and feedback properties of the buried AGN in a highly
gas-rich environment. The main results and conclusions of this
paper are summarized below.
1. Our imaging of the archival EVN and VLBA data reveals a
high-brightness-temperature (Tb > 107 K) and flat-spectrum
(α ≈−0.45) compact radio component corresponding to the
northern core, providing direct evidence for the presence of

a buried AGN in UGC 2369S. We also report its precise as-

2. The buried AGN exhibits a RIAF state with an Eddington

evated compared to typical LLAGNs, it remains well within
the sub-Eddington regime, naturally sustaining the powerful
compact jet within the central sub-kpc region.
3. The observed hard X-ray emission exhibits a significant
deficit (∼2 dex) relative to the [O III]-derived intrinsic lu-
minosity, indicating intense gas absorption. The corrected X-
ray luminosity agrees well with the FP prediction, confirm-
ing the presence of a Compton-thick gas cocoon surrounding
the deeply buried AGN.
4. The other two cores in UGC 2369S, SE and SW, remain
undetected by VLBI, implying they are either intrinsically
radio-silent, powered by non-AGN processes such as star
formation, or fall below the current sensitivity limit.

D.1. Fundamental plane relation
The fundamental plane (FP) of black hole activity connects the
radio luminosity, X-ray luminosity, and black hole mass across a
wide range of accreting systems, indicating a scale-invariant na-
ture of the disk–jet coupling (Merloni et al. 2003; Falcke et al.
2004). To evaluate the intrinsic X-ray luminosity from the ra-
dio emission, we adopt the Bayesian regression derived for sub-
Eddington objects by Plotkin et al. (2012):
log LX,FP = (1.45 ± 0.04) log LR −(0.88 ± 0.06) log MBH
−6.07 ± 1.10,

where LX,FP and LR are the intrinsic X-ray and radio luminosities
in units of erg s−1, respectively, and MBH is the black hole mass
in units of solar masses (M⊙). The core radio luminosity is de-
rived from the VLBA 5 GHz observations, yielding LR = νLν =
(2.61 ± 0.29) × 1031 W ≡(2.61 ± 0.29) × 1038 erg s−1. The black
hole mass used in the calculation is MBH = (4.73±0.24)×108 M⊙
(Ding et al. 2026), which was estimated from the stellar veloc-
ity dispersion (σ∗) via the MBH −σ∗relation (Kormendy & Ho
2013). The final predicted uncertainty was calculated by adding
the propagated observational errors in quadrature with the in-
trinsic scatter of the FP relation (σint ∼0.40 dex; Plotkin et al.
2012). To assess the degree of X-ray obscuration, the hydrogen
column density (NH) was estimated using standard photoelectric
absorption models via WebPIMMS3. For this calculation, we as-
sumed a primary power-law X-ray spectrum with a typical pho-
ton index of Γ = 1.8 (e.g., Tozzi et al. 2006; Ricci et al. 2017b).
