DUAL BANACH SPACES WITH THE BALL-COVERING PROPERTY
JOHANN LANGEMETS, EMMA M˜OTTUS, AND NATALIA SAEALLE
Abstract. We study ball-covering properties of dual Banach spaces and their connections with
the geometry of predual unit balls. One of our main results shows that, for every separable Ba-
nach space X, the unit ball BX is a slicely countably determined set if and only if bc(X∗) = 1,
where bc(·) is the ball-covering index introduced by A. J. Guirao, A. Lissitsin, and V. Mon-
tesinos. We obtain several sufficient conditions for the uniform ball-covering property in dual
spaces, including duals of spaces with a K-unconditional basis for K < 2, and duals of separable
spaces whose unit ball is the closed convex hull of a set of uniformly strongly exposed points.
The constant 2 is sharp: there is a space with a 2-unconditional basis whose dual fails the
ball-covering property. Applications are given to spaces of operators and to Lipschitz spaces. In
particular, L(Lp[0, 1]) has the uniform ball-covering property for every 1 < p < ∞, which an-
swers a question posed by Q. Bao, R. Liu, and J. Shen. As an application to Lipschitz spaces, we
prove that Lip0(M) has the uniform ball-covering property whenever M is a separable complete
ultrametric or H¨older metric space.

introduced by A. Avil´es, V. Kadets, M. Mart´ın, J. Mer´ı, and V. Shepelska in [3] and includes,

Theorem B. Let X be a separable Banach space. Assume that either of the following conditions

• X has a K-unconditional basis, where 1 ⩽K < 2;
• BX is the closed convex hull of a set of uniformly strongly exposed points.
Then X∗has the UBCP.
Our first source of examples of dual spaces with the UBCP comes from unconditional bases.
V. Kadets, M. Mart´ın, J. Mer´ı, and D. Werner proved that the unit ball is an SCD set for every
Banach space with a 1-unconditional basis [20, Theorem 3.1]. Recently, M. L˜oo and Y. Perreau
constructed a Banach space with a 2-unconditional basis whose unit ball is not SCD [24]. It
is an open question whether the unit ball of every Banach space with a K-unconditional basis,
where 1 < K < 2, is an SCD set [24]. Note that our dual uniform covering result is independent
of the SCD problem. Nevertheless, using the space in [24], we prove that the dual of this space
even fails the BCP (see Proposition 3.5), hence the constant 2 is optimal.
Our second source of UBCP examples is based on uniformly strongly exposed sets (see Def-
inition 2.3). J. Lindenstrauss introduced this notion in his work on norm-attaining operators
[22], where he showed that if BX is the closed convex hull of a set of uniformly strongly exposed
points, then X has what is now called Lindenstrauss property A. We prove that the same geo-
metric assumption implies that X∗has the UBCP and bc(X∗) = 1 (see Theorem 3.6). However,
Lindenstrauss property A alone does not imply any strong form of bounded ball-covering in
the dual. We construct a separable Banach space X with Lindenstrauss property A such that
bc(X∗) = 1, but X∗fails the SBCP (see Proposition 3.12).

The abstract dual criteria above have two main applications. The first concerns spaces of
operators. The ball-covering property in spaces of operators has been recently studied in several
papers [4, 5, 23]. In particular, it has been proven that L(c0) and L(ℓp), 1 ⩽p < ∞, have the
UBCP [23, Corollaries 3.1 and 3.4], and that L(Lp[0, 1]) has the UBCP whenever 3/2 < p < 3
[5, Theorem 3.3]. In [5, Question 3], it is asked whether L(Lp[0, 1]) has the BCP whenever
1 < p < ∞. Our main application is the following theorem (see Theorem 4.5), which answers
the aforementioned question positively and provides an alternative way to simultaneously prove
that L(ℓp) and L(Lp[0, 1]) have the UBCP whenever 1 < p < ∞.
Theorem C. Let X and Y be separable Banach spaces such that BX and BY are the closed
convex hulls of sets of uniformly strongly exposed points. Then L(X, Y ∗) and L(Y, X∗) both have
the UBCP, and bc(L(X, Y ∗)) = bc(L(Y, X∗)) = 1.
The second class of applications concerns Lipschitz spaces. If M is a pointed complete metric
space, then Lip0(M) is the dual of the Lipschitz-free space F(M). Hence the preceding duality
results naturally apply to Lip0(M). The known picture for infinite metric spaces is quite lim-
ited. On the positive side, if M is separable and complete and F(M) has the Radon–Nikod´ym
property, then bc(Lip0(M)) = 1; in particular this applies to complete purely 1-unrectifiable
metric spaces by [2]. On the negative side, if M is a length metric space, then Lip0(M) has the
Daugavet property [17] and fails the (−1)-BCP [14].
We add two new types of examples. First, for the unit circle T with the chordal metric, we
prove that Lip0(T) has the BCP, indeed bc(Lip0(T)) = 1, but fails the UBCP (see Theorem 5.2).
This gives a natural Lipschitz-space separation between the BCP and the UBCP. Secondly, in
Theorem 5.7, we prove the following result.
Theorem D. If M is a separable complete uniformly Gromov concave metric space, then
Lip0(M) has the UBCP and bc(Lip0(M)) = 1.
This theorem applies, in particular, to separable ultrametric spaces and to H¨older metric
spaces [13], using the known characterization of uniformly Gromov concavity in terms of uni-
formly strongly exposed molecules in F(M) [7].
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains notation and preliminary material on the
ball-covering properties and related notions. In Section 3, we prove the main dual results: the
SCD characterization of bc(X∗) = 1 (see Theorem 3.1), the K < 2 unconditional basis criterion
(see Proposition 3.4), the sharp 2-unconditional counterexample (see Proposition 3.5), and the

results to spaces of operators via projective tensor products, and the main results of this section

2.2. Slices and SCD sets. If C is a bounded convex subset of a Banach space X, by a slice
of C we will mean the non-empty intersection of an open half-space with the set C. Every slice
can be written in the form
S(C, f, δ) := {x ∈C : f(x) > sup f(C) −δ}
for suitable f ∈X∗and δ > 0.
The notion of a slicely countably determined set was first introduced by A. Avil´es, V. Kadets,
M. Mart´ın, J. Mer´ı, and V. Shepelska in [3, Definition 2.5]. A separable Banach space X is said
to be slicely countably determined if every convex bounded subset of X is an SCD set. These
spaces generalize simultaneously Banach spaces which are Asplund or have the Radon–Nikod´ym
property.
Definition 2.1 ([3]). Let X be a Banach space. A convex bounded subset A of X is said to be a
slicely countably determined set (for short, SCD set) if there is a sequence of slices {Sn : n ∈N}
of A such that for every sequence {bn}n∈N with bn ∈Sn, one has that A ⊆conv({bn : n ∈N}).
Definition 2.1 is equivalent to the following: there is a sequence of slices {Sn : n ∈N} of A
such that for every slice S of A, there is some n ∈N such that Sn ⊆S [3]. The unit ball of a
separable Banach space X is SCD, for example, in the following cases:
• X is Asplund;
• X has the Radon–Nikod´ym property;
• X has a 1-unconditional basis [20, Theorem 3.1];
• X is a separable Banach space with a locally uniformly rotund norm [3, Example 2.10].

In this section we study ball-covering properties of dual Banach spaces in terms of the geom-

if the unit ball of a separable Banach space is a closed convex hull of its strongly exposed points,
then bc(X∗) = 1. Our first main result (see Theorem 3.1) gives a precise dual characterization
of the optimal ball-covering index: if X is separable, then

Proof. Since M is a uniformly Gromov concave metric space, the set Mol (M) is a set of uniformly
strongly exposed points of BF(M) [13, Remark 3.2]. Therefore, BF(M) is the closed convex hull
of a uniformly strongly exposed set, and we can apply Theorem 3.6.
