Divergent Evolution of Radial Metallicity Gradients in the Thin and Thick Disks of the Milky Way
Weixiang Sun1,7, Gaohuan Long2,3, Hui Li1,7, Han Shen4,5, Shu Wang3, Xiaodian Chen3, Biwei Jiang2, Xiaowei Liu6,7, and Di Li1
Submitted to ApJL; Accepted July 6, 2026
ABSTRACT
Using 200,388 red clump stars from LAMOST and APOGEE, we investigate the radial metallicity gradients of
the Galactic disk as a function of vertical height and stellar age. The thin disk displays a pronounced negative
radial metallicity gradient near the Galactic mid-plane that progressively flattens with increasing |Z|, following
∆[Fe/H]/∆R = −0.0784 + 0.0776 (1 −exp (−|Z| / 1.42)). The thin disk also exhibits a clear age dependence
in radial metallicity gradients, evolving smoothly from a strong gradient regime for young stars to a weak
gradient regime for old stars, following ∆[Fe/H]/∆R = −0.0438 + 0.0233 tanh ((τ −11.29) / 4.21). The thick
disk shows weakly positive radial metallicity gradients that remain statistically invariant with respect to both
vertical height and stellar age, following respectively, ∆[Fe/H]/∆R = 0.0038 + 0.0009 |Z| and ∆[Fe/H]/∆R
= 0.0146 −0.0007 τ. These results indicate that the thin disk retains radial metallicity gradients shaped by
relatively ordered inside-out growth and long-term secular evolution processes. The thick disk exhibits spatially
and temporally homogeneous radial metallicity gradients, which are consistent with a formation environment
characterized by mergers of gas-rich systems and/or a turbulent ISM.
Keywords: Stars: abundance – Galaxy: formation and evolution – Galaxy: disk – Galaxy: structure
1. INTRODUCTION
Understanding the formation and evolution of the Galac-
tic disk is one of the central problems in Galactic astronomy
and galaxy formation theory (e.g., Matteucci 2012; Bland-
Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016). Radial metallicity gradients pro-
vide important observational constraints on these processes
because they preserve rich information about numerous com-
plex physical processes in the Milky Way, including: star for-
mation and chemical evolution (e.g., Matteucci & Francois
1989; Sch¨onrich & McMillan 2017; Grisoni et al. 2018), stel-
lar migration (e.g., Sellwood & Binney 2002; Lee et al. 2011;
Sun et al. 2024a), the infall and outflow of gas (e.g., Larson
1976; Pezzulli & Fraternali 2016; Andrews et al. 2017), the
accretions of dwarf galaxies (e.g., Quinn et al. 1993), as well
as the non-axisymmetric perturbations induced by the central
bar or spiral arms (e.g., Andrievsky et al. 2004; Scarano &
L´epine 2013).
The radial metallicity gradients have been widely character-
ized by various tracers, including Cepheids (e.g., Andrievsky
et al. 2002; Luck et al. 2006), open clusters (Chen et al. 2003;
Magrini et al. 2009), planetary nebulae (Costa et al. 2004;
Henry et al. 2010), OB stars (Daflon & Cunha 2004), HII
regions (Balser et al. 2011), FGK dwarfs (Katz et al. 2011;
Boeche et al. 2013), giant stars (Hayden et al. 2014; Yan et
al. 2019), red clump star (Huang et al. 2015), as well as main-
sequence turn-off and sub-giant (MSTO-SG) stars (Wang et

with the prediction of the classical “inside-out” star formation
scenario, which suggests that the establishment of negative ra-

growth of the Galactic disk (e.g., Chiappini 2009; Sch¨onrich

Several studies determined the radial [Fe/H] gradients of
mono-age stellar populations, and found that the radial gra-
dients show strong variations among different mono-age stel-
lar populations (e.g., Xiang et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2019).
The radial gradients are generally flat for the oldest popula-
tions, and become steeper with decreasing age, and then flat-
ten again as age decreases. These results reveal that subse-
quent secular disk evolution, including radial migration, dy-
namical heating, and disk mixing, can redistribute stars and
partially erase primordial metallicity gradients (e.g., Lee et al.
2011; Minchev et al. 2013; Mackereth et al. 2019). Disentan-
gling the relative contributions of these processes remains a
major challenge for understanding the chemo-dynamical evo-
lution of the Milky Way disk.
Large spectroscopic surveys in the new era have enabled
increasingly detailed measurements of the metallicity struc-
ture of the Galactic disk (e.g., Boeche et al. 2014; Hawkins
2023; Imig et al. 2023). Previous studies have shown that
radial metallicity gradients vary systematically with Galactic
position and stellar population (e.g., Yan et al. 2019; Han et
al. 2020; Sun et al. 2024a). In particular, the thin and thick
disks exhibit markedly different chemical and kinematic prop-
erties, suggesting distinct evolutionary histories (e.g., Lee et
al. 2011; Bensby, Feltzing & Oey 2014; Sun et al. 2024b). The
thin disk generally exhibits ordered chemical structures con-
sistent with long-term disk evolution, whereas the thick disk
likely formed during an earlier and dynamically hotter phase
of Galactic evolution (e.g., Brook et al. 2012; Wisnioski et al.
2015; Sun et al. 2024a, 2025).

2. DATA
In this paper, we mainly used a sample with 200,388 RC
stars selected from the APOGEE (Majewski et al. 2017) and
LAMOST (e.g., Cui et al. 2012; Deng et al. 2012) spectro-
scopic surveys following the RC selection methods of (Bovy
et al. 2014) and (Huang et al. 2020), selecting the parame-
ters with a large signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for the common
sources. The uncertainties of the effective temperature (Teff),
surface gravity (logg), line-of-sight velocity (Vr), [α/Fe] and
[Fe/H], of the whole sample stars, are typically better than,
100 K, 0.10 dex, 5 km s−1, 0.03−0.05 dex and 0.10−0.15 dex,
respectively (Bovy et al. 2014; Huang et al. 2020). Given the
distance from the standard-candle nature of RC stars, their
typical accuracies are generally better than 5%–10% (Bovy
et al. 2014; Huang et al. 2020). Stellar masses and ages are
determined from the LAMOST spectra using the kernel prin-
cipal component analysis (KPCA) method (e.g., Sch¨olkopf
et al. 1998), which is trained with thousands of red clump
stars in the LAMOST–Kepler fields with accurate asteroseis-
mic mass measurements. The typical uncertainties of the de-
rived stellar masses and ages are around 15% and 30%, re-
spectively. For the age-dependent analysis presented in this
work, the APOGEE RC stars were cross-matched with the
LAMOST KPCA age catalog, and only stars with available
KPCA age estimates were retained. This procedure ensures a
homogeneous age scale for both the LAMOST and APOGEE
samples used in the age analysis, thereby avoiding systematic
differences introduced by different age-determination meth-
ods.
The standard Galactocentric cylindrical Coordinate (R, ϕ,
Z) has been used for this study, with three velocity compo-
nents respectively VR, Vϕ and Vz. R is the projected Galac-
tocentric distance with increasing radial outwards, ϕ is the
Galactic disk rotational direction, and ϕ = 0◦points to the
Galactic anti-center direction, Z points to the North Galactic
Pole. To transform the heliocentric coordinates to Galacto-
centric coordinates, we set the local circular velocity as Vc,0
= 238 km s−1 (Reid & Brunthaler 2004; Sch¨onrich et al.
2010, 2012; Huang et al. 2016; Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard
2016), the Galactocentric distance of the Sun as R⊙= 8.34
kpc (Reid et al. 2014), and solar motions as (U⊙, V⊙, W⊙) =
(13.00, 12.24, 7.24) km s−1 (Sch¨onrich & Dehnen 2018).
To improve the accuracy of the chemical distribution calcu-
lations, we further use cuts with SNRs > 20 and the distance
uncertainty ≤10%. To exclude any possibility of the halo
stars, we further set stellar [Fe/H] ≥−1.0 dex and |Vz| ≤
120 km s−1 (Hayden et al. 2020; Sun et al. 2020), and finally,
168,344 RC stars were selected. The spatial and metallicity
distributions of the final selected stars are shown in Fig. 1.
Since the stellar parameters, such as [Fe/H] and [α/Fe], are
derived using different analysis pipelines in the APOGEE
and LAMOST surveys, a homogenization procedure is per-
formed before combining the datasets. Given that the LAM-
OST sample is significantly larger than the APOGEE sample,
the [Fe/H] and [α/Fe] measurements of the APOGEE datasets
are calibrated onto the LAMOST datasets based on the best-
fitting relations derived from the common targets of the two
surveys (see Appendix Fig. A1).
Given that the metallicity gradients are distinguished for
the thin and thick disk populations (e.g, Yan et al. 2019; Sun
et al. 2024b), we separate our RC sample stars into thin and
thick disks according to their locations on the [Fe/H]–[α/Fe]
plane (see Appendix Fig. B1). To obtain clean thin/thick disk
stars, two empirical cuts are used to separate the two disks
(e.g., Bensby et al. 2011; Lee et al. 2011; Han et al. 2020;
Sun et al. 2020), and this chemically based classification has

respectively (e.g., Bensby, Feltzing & Oey 2014; Imig et al.

To measure radial metallicity gradients, we divide the sam-
ple into bins of vertical height and stellar age. Within each
bin, we fit a linear relation between [Fe/H] and Galactocentric
radius R. The fitting was performed using the Python pack-
age emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013) to generate MCMC
samples of the posterior distributions of the fitting parameters.
Assuming Gaussian uncertainties, the negative log-likelihood
was defined using the least-squares statistic. We adopted 100
walkers and evolved each chain for 5000 steps, discarding the
initial 2000 steps as burn-in.
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The radial metallicity gradients (∆[Fe/H]/∆R) as a func-
tion of vertical height (|Z|) for the two disks are shown in
Fig. 2.
The thin disk exhibits a pronounced negative ∆[Fe/H]/∆R
near the Galactic mid-plane that systematically flattens with
increasing |Z|, following an approximately exponential flat-
tening trend from −0.0784 dex kpc −1 at |Z| ∼0.0 kpc to
−0.0075 dex kpc −1 at |Z| ∼3.5 kpc (see right panel of
Fig. 2). The thick disk displays an almost constant week pos-
itive ∆[Fe/H]/∆R (nearly +0.0050 ∼+0.0080 dex kpc −1)
over Galactic height (see right panel of Fig. 2).
Our results indicate that the thin disk exhibits ∆[Fe/H]/∆R
of −0.0658 ± 0.0004 dex kpc−1 at |Z| ∼0.25 kpc, and the
thick disk shows weak positive ∆[Fe/H]/∆R of +0.0050 ±
0.0014 dex kpc−1 at |Z| ∼2.25 kpc (see Fig. 2), which are in
good agreement with the A/F/G/K-type giant sample of Yan et
al. (2019). The thick disk displays a globally weakly positive
∆[Fe/H]/∆R (this positive gradient has also been observed in
other works; e.g., Vickers et al. 2021; Imig et al. 2023), and it
shows no obvious change with |Z|, which is slightly different
from the result of Yan et al. (2019), while it is broadly consis-
tent with the results of old/high-α populations determined by
the MSTO-SG sample of Wang et al. (2019), the RC sample
of Sun et al. (2024a), and the red giant sample of Imig et al.
(2023). Since the distances of giants are generally obtained
from Gaia parallaxes, the discrepancy between our results and
theirs is likely due to their larger distance uncertainties com-
pared to the more precise RC distances adopted in this work.

at larger |Z|.

These results indicate that the thin disk exhibits a clear evo-
lution of the ∆[Fe/H]/∆R with stellar age, consistent with
a relatively ordered phase of disk evolution, during which
radial metallicity gradients were gradually established and
maintained through “inside-out” disk growth (e.g., Chiappini
2009; Frankel et al. 2019) and long-term secular evolution
processes, particularly stellar radial migration (e.g., Sellwood
& Binney 2002; Sch¨onrich & Binney 2009; Minchev et al.
2013; Sun et al. 2025).
This interpretation is further sup-
ported by the observed ∆[Fe/H]/∆R–|Z| relation (Fig. 2).
The thick disk displays temporally invariant ∆[Fe/H]/∆R,
implying that its radial metallicity gradient was established
during an early phase when the disk was dynamically hot and
efficiently mixed and has remained unchanged over time. This
lack of temporal evolution disfavors secular evolution scenar-
ios involving “inside-out” and “upside-down” star formation
accompanied by long-term stellar migration. Our results are
therefore more consistent with the scenario in which thick
disk stars formed in an environment characterized by merg-
ers of gas-rich systems and/or a turbulent ISM (Brook et al.
2012; Wisnioski et al. 2015; Sun et al. 2024a).
It is worth emphasizing that precise stellar age deter-
minations remain challenging (Soderblom
2010), and the
associated uncertainties may affect the measurements of
∆[Fe/H]/∆R as a function of stellar age, thereby limiting
constraints on the detailed evolutionary history of the Galactic
disk (e.g., Sharma et al. 2021; Sun et al. 2025). Future mea-
surements with improved age precision will provide stronger
constraints on the temporal evolution of radial metallicity gra-
dients and the assembly history of the Milky Way disk.
