ABSTRACT
The first several years of JWST observations have yielded surprisingly large numbers of bright z > 10
galaxies, with follow-up spectroscopy of many of these sources implying extreme star formation activity
and/or AGN content. Here, we present a combination of two deep Cycle 3 NIRSpec G395M programs,
totaling over 19 hours of exposure time, plus MIRI/LRS observations for one such high-redshift source:
Maisie’s Galaxy. We provide an updated redshift measurement of z = 11.408 ± 0.005 for this source.
Measurements of the [O ii] doublet in these data yield an electron density (ne = 108.56+873.9
−35.37) and a
star-formation rate (SFR[O ii] = 1.3±0.35), placing it along the star-formation main sequence (SFMS)
and indicating that this is a much more typical, rather than extreme, source in the early Universe.
We also report fluxes for the [O iii]λ5008 and [Ne iii]λ3869 lines that provide us with a log(Ne3O2)
= −0.219 ± 0.145 and a log(O32) = 0.724 ± 0.191. We estimate the metallicity (Z/Z⊙= 0.17 ± 0.05)
and ionization parameter (log(U) = −2.26 ± 0.13) from the Ne3O2 ratio. We place this galaxy in
the context of other z > 10 sources with similar line detections and compare the results to those
obtained from SED fitting. The results suggest that we should go deeper with our observations to
better understand the average galaxy population at these early times.

1. INTRODUCTION
The study of galaxies in the early Universe was one of
the key science drivers behind JWST (e.g., V. Bromm &
N. Yoshida 2011; B. E. Robertson 2022), and the tele-
scope’s first science results exceeded expectations (A.
Adamo et al. 2025). Many early JWST/NIRCam (J. P.
Gardner et al. 2006, 2023; M. J. Rieke et al. 2023) sur-
veys yielded discoveries of galaxy candidates at z > 10,
those existing < 500Myr after the Big Bang (e.g. M.
Castellano et al. 2022; R. P. Naidu et al. 2022; S. L.
Finkelstein et al. 2022a; Y. Harikane et al. 2022; C. T.
Donnan et al. 2023; P. G. P´erez-Gonz´alez et al. 2023;
C. M. Casey et al. 2024; M. Franco et al. 2024). Sur-
prisingly, their number densities, especially at the bright
end of the ultraviolet luminosity function (UVLF), were
Email: rlarson@stsci.edu
higher than those expected from most pre-JWST models
(P. Arrabal Haro et al. 2023a; R. Bouwens et al. 2023;
S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2022b, 2024). These discover-
ies call for changes to our understanding of the physical
conditions in early galaxies (M. Boylan-Kolchin 2022;
L. Y. A. Yung et al. 2024).
Many scenarios have been proposed to explain this
excess of bright galaxies so early on in cosmic history.
These include such things as a top-heavy initial mass
function (IMF; A. Trinca et al. 2024; T. B. Jeong et al.
2025; L. Y. A. Yung et al. 2024), lack of dust due to
radiation-driven outflows (A. Ferrara et al. 2023), UV
variability (X. Shen et al. 2023; G. Sun et al. 2023), a
higher stellar-to-halo mass ratio and/or star-formation
efficiency (C. A. Mason et al. 2023; Y. Harikane et al.
2023), possible further boosting of the photon per stellar
baryon yield via rapid rotation (B. Liu et al. 2025), and
the contribution of early active galactic nuclei (AGN; N.
Cappelluti et al. 2022). To begin testing these theories,

The rest-frame UV emission lines have strong diagnos-
tic power but are often weak. Lyα can constrain the
source’s ionization, and at these early times, detection of
this feature implies that the source lies in already reion-
ized regions of the Universe (J. Witstok et al. 2025). The
carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen lines can be used to con-
strain chemical abundances, which tells us more about
the interstellar medium (ISM) of these early galaxies. C
and O are also predicted key drivers in the transition
of SF mode from top-heavy to bottom-heavy (“critical
metallicity”, Zcrit). (e.g., V. Bromm & A. Loeb 2003).
However, to detect these powerful diagnostic lines, we
have to go deep, and at these high redshifts, which re-
quires long exposure times.
Many of the z > 10 galaxies that have been studied
in detail with JWST spectroscopy appear to be some-
what extreme systems.
Several of these objects have
shown emission-line features that are not typically found
at lower redshifts.
For example, GNz11 at z = 10.6
shows evidence of a nitrogen abundance exceeding ex-
pectations given its low metallicity and young age (A. J.
Bunker et al. 2023; A. J. Cameron et al. 2023). This
could mean we are observing the formation of globular-
cluster progenitors (K. Bekki 2023; F. D’Antona et al.
2023; P. Senchyna et al. 2024; R. Marques-Chaves et al.
2024; K. Watanabe et al. 2024). Alternatively, a high
C/O ratio in a galaxy at z = 12.5, GSz12, may be the
result of ejecta from a previous generation of Population
III stars (B. Liu et al. 2021; F. D’Eugenio et al. 2024).
Additionally, a significant fraction of sources have shown
evidence of AGN activity even at this early epoch (A. D.
Goulding et al. 2023; ´A. Bogd´an et al. 2024; R. Maiolino
et al. 2024; A. Crespo G´omez et al. 2026), but they are
under debate when you observe only the rest-frame opti-
cal lines (J. ´Alvarez-M´arquez et al. 2025, 2026). It is im-
perative that we understand these galaxies in detail and
push our spectroscopic observations of z > 10 sources to
greater depths to determine whether these bright galax-
ies are typical of the galaxy population in the early Uni-
verse or represent extreme tip-of-the-iceberg sources.
Maisie’s Galaxy (CEERS 16943) was first discovered
in the Cosmic Evolution Early Release Science (CEERS;
ERS 1345, PI Finkelstein, M. B. Bagley et al. 2022; S. L.
Finkelstein et al. 2025) survey JWST/NIRCam observa-
tions (S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2022c), and was one of the
earliest JWST z > 10 discoveries (e.g. M. Castellano
et al. 2022; R. P. Naidu et al. 2022; C. T. Donnan et al.
2022). This object was originally measured to have a
photometric redshift (zphot) of ∼12 (S. L. Finkelstein
et al. 2022c). As the data pipelines and processing im-
proved, the measurement was modified to zphot = 11.5
(S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2022b) and later updated to
zphot = 11.05 ± 0.9 (P. Arrabal Haro et al. 2023b; Y.
Harikane et al. 2023).
Follow-up NIRSpec prism observations of this source
(MPTID 1) and a few other key early discoveries were
conducted through a Director’s Discretionary Time pro-
gram (DDT 2750; PI Arrabal Haro). They successfully
detected the Lyman break and an [O ii] emission line
feature, confirming the high-redshift nature and giv-
ing a spectroscopic redshift of z = 11.416 ± 0.005 (P.
Arrabal Haro et al. 2023b), making this object the first
early JWST discovery to be spectroscopically confirmed.
However, this data was at the lowest available resolu-
tion with NIRSpec and did not allow detailed studies
of the source’s physical properties beyond a simple red-
shift confirmation. The spectrum was largely feature-
less, with only an [O ii] emission line detection and no
rest-UV emission lines such as those found in several
of the other early JWST discoveries (i.e. A. J. Bunker
et al. 2023; M. Castellano et al. 2024; J. Witstok et al.
2025). This source, while one of the first z > 10 sources
discovered with JWST, appears less extreme than many
other discoveries in this era.
In this paper, we present follow-up observations of
this source with two JWST/NIRSpec (P. Jakobsen et al.
2022) Cycle 3 GO programs to obtain deep (∼19 hr)
spectroscopy of Maisie’s Galaxy at a higher spectral res-
olution. We also utilize JWST/MIRI LRS (G. H. Rieke
et al. 2015; G. S. Wright et al. 2023) observations of
this source. We describe the data in §2, the line mea-
surements and upper limits in §3, update the spectro-
photometric SED fitting measurements in §4, and dis-
cuss the derived physical properties of the source and
place it in context in §5. We assume a flat ΛCDM cos-
mology, in line with the Planck results ( Planck Collabo-
ration et al. 2020), of H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3,
and Ωb = 0.05 for all calculations in this paper.

3. MEASURED EMISSION LINES
Emission lines in the NIRSpec data were measured
using an automated line-finding routine developed orig-
inally for HST grism data (R. L. Larson et al. 2018) and
modified for JWST/NIRSpec data (R. L. Larson et al.
2023).
This code first searched the spectrum, fitting
Gaussians to each wavelength pixel, and then identified
any significant peaks. The strongest line (highest flux
and largest SNR) is then used to measure the redshift
of the spectroscopic redshift of the source based upon
the brightest expected line in this filter at the photo-
metric redshift. Once the redshift is measured, the code
fits the expected emission lines using single- or double-
Gaussian profiles, depending on the line type. Applying
this code to our NIRSpec data, we find three significant
(> 3σ) emission-line features in the stacked spectrum:
He iλ3889˚A, [Ne iii]λ3869˚A, and [O ii]λλ3727, 3729˚A,
shown in Figures 2 and 3. Emission-line measurements
are summarized in Table 1.

3.5. Upper Limits
Two other potentially strong emission-line features are
covered by the wavelength range of this data but are not
detected: Lyα and Hδ. Here, we set an upper limit by
forcing a Gaussian fit at that wavelength and using its
flux as the 1σ error. We then calculate a 3σ upper limit
for each emission line. For Maisie’s Galaxy we find that
Hδ has an upper limit measurement of 13.20 × 10−20
erg s−1 cm−2 from the ∼19 hours of stacked G395M
spectra.
This is consistent with the non-detection of
Hα in the MIRI/LRS data.
To measure the upper limit on Lyα we first assume
a zero velocity offset and fit a Gaussian at the systemic
redshift. Using the ∼13 hour G140M spectrum from
C3PO we find that Lyα has an upper limit of 8.30 ×
10−20 erg s−1 cm−2. There are no line detections within
200 km s−1 redward of the systemic redshift.

4. SPECTRO-PHOTOMETRIC SED FITTING
We use the BAGPIPES (v1.3.2; A. C. Carnall et al.
2018, 2019) software to perform spectral energy distribu-
tion (SED) fitting for this source, incorporating both the
photometry from JWST/NIRCam and the spectroscopy
from NIRSpec. Here, we use the C3PO G395M spec-
trum in conjunction with CEERS imaging (S. L. Finkel-
stein et al. 2025) and the UNICORN photometric cat-
alog (S. Finkelstein et al., in prep.), which emphasizes
accurate colors and total fluxes.
The combined spec-
trophotometric fits are shown in Figure 5.
The BAGPIPES fitting generally follows the method
of C. Papovich et al. (2026), which we briefly detail here.
We use the Binary Population and Spectral Synthesis
(BPASS, v2.2.1) models from E. R. Stanway & J. J.
Eldridge (2018), assuming a Chabrier IMF (G. Chabrier
2003) with an upper stellar mass cutoff of 300 M⊙. We
also adopt star formation histories (SFHs) parameter-
ized by Gaussian processes (K. G. Iyer et al. 2019), as
they offer the flexibility to model bursts and quench-
ing events without imposing a prior on the widths of

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. Lack of Ionized Bubble
The non-detection of Lyα implies that Maisie’s Galaxy
does not reside in an ionized bubble such that the Lyα
would resonantly scatter out of the surrounding neu-
tral hydrogen in the intergalactic medium (IGM) and be
detectable (e.g., S. Malhotra & J. E. Rhoads 2004; A.
Smith et al. 2015). This is different than JADES GSz13-
1, a z = 13 galaxy (J. Witstok et al. 2025), with detected
significant Lyα emission. The lack of other sources at
this redshift in CEERS (S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2022b)
indicates that it is not in an overdense region and there-
fore does not benefit from the combined ionizing power
of multiple sources.

5.3. Electron Density
Our coadded spectrum yields a line ratio measurement

−60.78 .

5.4. Metallicity
The redshift of Maisie’s Galaxy is high enough such
that the [O iii] and Hβ lines are shifted out of the
NIRSpec G395M coverage. As we have MIRI/LRS ob-
servations that detect the [O iii] doublet, we can use
the [O iii]/[O ii] (O32) ratio to measure the metallicity
of this galaxy.
Using the relation derived from R. L.
Sanders et al. (2025), we can calculate the metallicity as
log(O32) = 0.697 −1.245x −0.869x2
where x = 12 + log(O/H) - 8.0 and find this relationship
is valid for values of Z = 12 + log (O/H) between 7.3
and 8.6 (with Z/Z⊙= 8.69 ). For Maisie’s Galaxy, we
calculate a log (O32) = 0.724 ± 0.191 which gives us a
12 + log (O/H) = 7.98 ± 0.16 or Z/Z⊙= 0.19 ± 0.08.
Often, the O32 ratio is used to determine metallicity,
but when [O iii] emission is absent due to lack of MIRI
coverage at these redshifts, we can use alternative line
ratios. Using more than 100 high-redshift sources, R. L.
Sanders et al. (2025) determine the relationship between
the [Ne iii]/[O ii] (Ne3O2) ratio and metallicity as
log(Ne3O2) = −0.333 −1.459x −1.127x2
where x = 12 + log(O/H) - 8.0 and find this relationship
is valid for values of Z = 12 + log (O/H) between 7.4 and
8.6 (with Z/Z⊙= 8.69 ). Using our measured line ratio,
log (Ne3O2) = -0.219± 0.145, we infer a metallicity of 12
+ log (O/H) = 7.92 ± 0.12 or Z/Z⊙= 0.17 ± 0.05. This
relationship is also discussed in F. Bian et al. (2018); T.
Jones et al. (2015); R. Maiolino et al. (2008).
Using
those equations, we infer metallicities that agree with
the value reported above. Our SED fitting of this source
yielded Z/Z⊙= 0.13+0.08
−0.04 for this galaxy, but using these
line diagnostics yields a tighter constraint on this value.

We note that the range of log(U) values from SED fitting

those measured from the line ratios all fall within the
log(U) ∼2 −2.5 range, with no apparent dependence
on βUV . In most cases, the line-ratio-derived log(U) is
lower than that measured by SED fitting methods. This
implies that either SED fitting processes are not accu-
rately accounting for line fluxes and ratios, or that we
need more diagnostic data for galaxies at these redshifts
to inform our modeling.
6. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we present combined observations
from two JWST/NIRSpec programs (GO#5507 and
GO #5943) to achieve > 19 hour depth on Maisie’s
Galaxy, an early-identified high-redshift galaxy in the
first JWST data (S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2022c).
We
detect three emission lines, [O ii], [Ne iii], and He i,
and report an updated spectroscopic redshift of z =
11.4082 ± 0.0057 for this source, consistent with the
value measured from the lower-resolution prism data in
P. Arrabal Haro et al. (2023b). The deep (> 13 hour)
G140M data covers the rest-frame UV of the source, but
there are no detections of Lyα or either Carbon line, un-
like in some of the other extreme z > 10 sources in the
literature (e.g. J. Witstok et al. 2025; A. J. Bunker et al.
2023). We also present the 9-hour MIRI/LRS observa-
tion of this source from GO#3703 and the successful
detection of the [O iii] doublet.
We use the measured [O ii] emission line feature to de-
termine a star-formation rate of SFR[O ii] = 1.29±0.36
M⊙yr−1, comparatively low for the other z > 10 sources
in the literature with [O ii] line detections. We also find
that the SFR calculated from [O ii] is lower than that re-
ported by SED fitting, which could indicate that [O ii] is
not a reliable SFR indicator at these epochs, or that our
modeling needs improvement. We also find that these
z > 10 galaxies all fall along the star-formation main se-

Using the Ne3O2 emission line ratio, we can infer the
ionization parameter, log(U), for this source based on
the relation between Ne3O2 and O32 at high O32, as
expected and measured in the early Universe (J. Witstok
et al. 2021). For Maisie’s Galaxy, we find that log(U)
= −2.26 ± 0.13 when calculated using the Ne3O2 ratio,
which is lower than that inferred from SED fitting. We
compare this to the value we measure using the O32
ratio, log(U) = −2.38 ± 0.15, and find that it agrees
with that derived from the Ne3O2 ratio. In fact, many
of the log(U) measurements calculated using Ne3O2 or
O32 for sources in the literature with both lines detected
are lower than those obtained from SED fitting.
We
also find that there is little to no dependence on βUV as
might be implied by SED fitting routines. Clearly, more
data and more robust detections of diagnostic lines at
these redshifts are required to determine the properties
of these z > 10 galaxies.
The status of Maisie’s Galaxy as one of the first “nor-
mal” galaxies, with a moderate SFR and metallicity,
implies an already advanced evolutionary state, pushing
its assembly history to even higher redshifts. A related
question then is: How could such a relatively mature
galaxy have emerged so early in cosmic history? Ap-
proximately, one can estimate that 100 Myr were needed
to produce the ∼108 M⊙in stars, and ∼105 M⊙in met-
als, assuming standard nucleosynthetic yields.
This
would push the true birth of Maisie’s Galaxy to z ∼14.5,
allowing a multi-generational cycle of star formation and
supernova-driven chemical enrichment that was able to
establish a high degree of complexity already at the
very beginning of galaxy formation (e.g., M. Jeon & V.
Bromm 2019; H. Yajima et al. 2023). A more complete
census of the mix of first-galaxy types is needed to fully
assess such questions of ultimate galactic origins, but
these types are now clearly within reach.
Maisie’s Galaxy is a more typical galaxy in the early
Universe, lacking extreme emission-line detections or in-
ferred physical properties. It is imperative to go deeper
with our observations if we want to study the more typ-
ical population of galaxies in this epoch. JWST/MIRI
observations to detect the [O iii] and Hα emission lines
are the best next step to understanding the physical
properties of galaxies in the first 500 Myr.
