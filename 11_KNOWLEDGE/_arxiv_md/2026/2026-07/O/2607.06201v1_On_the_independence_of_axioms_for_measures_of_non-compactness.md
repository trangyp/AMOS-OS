ON THE INDEPENDENCE OF AXIOMS FOR MEASURES OF
NON-COMPACTNESS
JERZY GRZYBOWSKI, PIOTR KASPRZAK, AND PIOTR MA ´CKOWIAK
Abstract. We investigate the independence of the axioms in the Bana´s–Goebel approach to
the theory of measures of non-compactness. We present a complete answer for four of the
five axioms. For the remaining axiom, we provide a full solution in finite-dimensional spaces
and a partial one in the infinite-dimensional case.
1. Introduction
The notion of a measure of non-compactness was introduced by Kuratowski in 1930
(see [23, p. 303]). For several decades, it remained largely unnoticed. It re-emerged in
1950s and 1960s, when it became an important tool in operator theory and fixed point
theory, particularly in the works of Darbo, Sadovskii, and others (see, for example, the
classical papers [18–20, 27]). This period also marked the beginning of efforts to place
various notions of non-compactness within a unified framework.
One of the earliest axiomatic approaches in this direction was due to Sadovskii (see [28,
29], cf. also [2, Definition 1.2.1]). In that setting, a set function ν, defined on the family
of all non-empty bounded subsets of a locally convex space, is called a measure of non-
compactness if it is invariant under passage to the closed convex hull, that is,
ν(conv A) = ν(A) for every bounded set A.
A different axiomatic approach in metric spaces was later proposed by Martin´on in 1990
(see [26]).
Another development in this direction is the notion of a quasimeasure of non-compactness
introduced by Krukowski in 2016 (see [21,22]). In this approach, the set function is not
required to be invariant under taking convex hulls. This results in a framework that seems
too broad to be used directly in classical Darbo- or Sadovskii-type fixed point theorems.
Nevertheless, certain fixed point results can still be obtained by combining quasimeasures
with an additional control of non-convexity (see [22, Theorem 24] and [10, Theorem 4]). A
concrete examples of quasimeasures were provided, for example, by Krukowski in [21]
in the case of the space of bounded and continuous mappings, and by Bugajewski and
Gulgowski in [10] in the case of the space of functions of bounded Jordan variation.
Among the various axiomatic approaches that have been proposed, the framework
introduced by Bana´s and Goebel in their 1980 monograph (see [5]) has proved particularly
influential and is nowadays regarded as one of the standard approaches to the theory.

Measures of non-compactness, especially within the Bana´s–Goebel framework, play
an important role in fixed point theory as well as in the study of integral and differential
equations, evolution equations, differential inclusions, semigroup theory, and dynamical
systems. For a detailed overview and applications, we refer the reader to the monographs [2,
4,7,8].
While the classical literature is largely concerned with applications of measures of
non-compactness, more recent work has focused on structural and qualitative aspects
of the theory. This includes general representation results for abstract measures of non-
compactness on Banach spaces (see [12,15]), as well as investigations into the existence of
inequivalent measures (see [1,6,24,25]). Other directions include questions of countable
determination of the Kuratowski measure [11] and the study of minimal sets associated
with measures of non-compactness (see [17] and [4, Chapter III]). For a broader discussion
of these topics, we refer to the recent survey [13].
One aspect of the theory of axiomatic measures of non-compactness that is still not
fully understood concerns the independence of the defining axioms. This issue is important
from both theoretical and practical perspectives, as it clarifies whether the axiom system
is minimal and, consequently, whether the assumptions used in applications are in some
sense optimal.
In this paper we investigate the independence of the axioms in the Bana´s–Goebel
framework. We present a complete answer for four of the five axioms. In the case of the
generalized Cantor intersection property, we restate a general result originally published
in Chinese in [14] (see also Section 4.5 for further details) and present an example that
is essentially different from the one given therein. For the remaining axiom, we solve
the problem completely in finite-dimensional spaces and obtain only partial results in the
infinite-dimensional setting.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect notation and recall the
definition of an axiomatic measure of non-compactness due to Bana´s and Goebel. Section 3
reviews known properties of axiomatic measures of non-compactness and related set
functions, and develops several new refinements, extensions, and equivalent reformulations
of the axioms. Section 4 is devoted to the independence of the axioms. Finally, in Section 5
we present equivalent reformulations of the definition of an axiomatic measure of non-
compactness in both finite- and infinite-dimensional settings, based on the results obtained
in the previous sections. The paper concludes with an open question.

axiomatic measures of non-compactness are continuous with respect to the Hausdorff

functions.

Remark 3. One immediate consequence of Proposition 2 is that every axiomatic measure of
non-compactness is invariant under taking closures, that is, µ(A) = µ(A) for all A ∈BE.
Although this fact was already observed by Bana´s and Goebel (see [5, p. 13]), many authors
include it explicitly among the axioms for the sake of simplicity (see, for example, [7,8]).
Now that we have discussed a property of axiomatic measures of non-compactness
which is not explicitly included among the axioms in Definition 1, we return to the natural
order in which the axioms are introduced.
In all works on axiomatic measures of non-compactness known to the authors, the
kernel of the measure µ in axiom (A1) is always assumed to be a subset of KE. Surprisingly,
this property already follows from axioms (A2) and (A5).
Proposition 4. Let E be a Banach space, and let ν: BE →[0, +∞) be a set function that satisfies
axioms (A2) and (A5). Then, ker ν ⊆KE.
Proof. If the Banach space E is finite-dimensional, the claim is immediate, since in this case
BE = KE. Thus, we may assume that E is infinite-dimensional. Suppose, on the contrary,
that there exists a set A ∈BE \ KE such that ν(A) = 0. Since A is not relatively compact, we
can choose a sequence (xn)n∈N of points in A with the property that none of its subsequences
converges in E. For each n ∈N, let An := {xn, xn+1, . . .}. Clearly, these sets form a non-
increasing sequence. They are also closed, since otherwise the sequence (xn)n∈N would
admit a convergent subsequence. By axiom (A2), we further obtain 0 ⩽ν(An) ⩽ν(A) = 0
for all n ∈N. Hence, axiom (A5) ensures that the intersection T∞
n=1 An is non-empty.
However, any point in this intersection would serve as a limit of a subsequence of (xn)n∈N,
contradicting our assumption that the sequence has no convergent subsequences. Therefore,
ker ν ⊆KE.

4. Independence of the axioms
We say that an axiom is independent of the others if in every Banach space there exists
a set function which satisfies all the axioms in Definition 1, except for that particular one.
4.1. Axiom (A1). Axiom (A1) is independent of the others. Indeed, consider the constant
set function ν: BE →[0, +∞) defined by ν(A) := 1 for all A ∈BE; it never vanishes on
a non-empty bounded set, yet all other axioms are satisfied, with axiom (A5) holding
vacuously.
A brief comment is in order. Formally, axiom (A1) is the conjunction of two conditions,
which might suggest the need for separate examples addressing each condition individually.
However, in view of Proposition 4, a single example is sufficient.

5. Concluding remarks
The discussion carried out in this paper allows us to restate the definition of an ax-
iomatic measure of non-compactness in a simpler but equivalent form. In the case of finite-
dimensional spaces, Definition 1 can be reformulated as follows.
Definition 35. Let E be a finite-dimensional Banach space. A function µ: BE →[0, +∞) is
called an axiomatic measure of non-compactness, if
(a1) the family ker µ := {A ∈BE | µ(A) = 0} is non-empty,
(a2) for any A, B ∈BE if A ⊆B, then µ(A) ⩽µ(B),
(a3) there exists λ ∈(0, 1) such that µ(λA + (1 −λ)B) ⩽λµ(A) + (1 −λ)µ(B) for all
A, B ∈BE.
In the case of infinite-dimensional spaces, Definition 1 is equivalent to the following
formulation.
Definition 36. Let E be an infinite-dimensional Banach space. A function µ: BE →[0, +∞)
is called an axiomatic measure of non-compactness, if
(a1) the family ker µ := {A ∈BE | µ(A) = 0} is non-empty,
(a2) for any A, B ∈BE if A ⊆B, then µ(A) ⩽µ(B),
(a3) µ(conv A) = µ(A) for any A ∈BE,
(a4) there exists λ ∈(0, 1) such that µ(λA + (1 −λ)B) ⩽λµ(A) + (1 −λ)µ(B) for all
A, B ∈BE,
(a5) if the sets An are closed and An+1 ⊆An for n ∈N with µ(An) →0, then their
intersection T∞
n=1 An is non-empty.
We end the paper with an open question concerning the independence of axiom (A4).
Open problem. Does every infinite-dimensional Banach space E admit a set function
ν: BE →[0, +∞) that satisfies all the axioms of Definition 1 except axiom (A3)?
