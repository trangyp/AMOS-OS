By analyzing (2712.4 ± 14.3) × 106 ψ(3686) events collected with the BESIII detector operating
at the BEPCII collider, the decays χcJ →pK−¯Λη + c.c. (J = 0, 1, 2) are observed for the first time,
with statistical significances exceeding 5σ for all three χcJ states. The measured branching fractions
are B(χc0 →pK−¯Λη + c.c.) = (5.3 ± 0.7 ± 0.5) × 10−5, B(χc1 →pK−¯Λη + c.c.) = (9.8 ± 0.6 ±
0.6) × 10−5, and B(χc2 →pK−¯Λη + c.c.) = (9.3 ± 0.6 ± 0.6) × 10−5, where the first uncertainties are
statistical and the second are systematic. Structures consistent with the known hyperon resonances
Λ(1520) and ¯Λ(1690) are seen in the pK−and ¯Λη invariant mass spectra, respectively. The reported
branching fractions include both resonant and non-resonant contributions. These results provide
new experimental information on hadronic decays of P-wave charmonium states and contribute to
the understanding of baryon production and hadronization dynamics in the nonperturbative QCD
regime.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In the quark model, the χcJ states (J = 0, 1, 2) cor-
respond to the 3PJ charmonium states.
Their quan-
tum numbers are incompatible with those of a single
virtual photon, and therefore they cannot be produced
through single-photon exchange in e+e−annihilation [1].
The χcJ states are efficiently produced via the radiative
transitions ψ(3686) →γχcJ, whose branching fractions
are about 9% for each χcJ state [2]. These transitions
make ψ(3686) decays an excellent laboratory for study-
ing the hadronic decay properties of χcJ states and for
testing the predictions of nonperturbative quantum chro-
modynamics (QCD) [3]. Hadronic decays of χcJ proceed
predominantly through the annihilation of a c¯c pair into
gluons, followed by hadronization into multi-hadron fi-
nal states. Such processes are sensitive to intermediate
resonant structures and final-state interactions, thereby
providing valuable information on hadronization dynam-
ics and baryon production mechanisms [4].
In the previous studies at the BES and BESIII experi-
ments, a near-threshold enhancement in the p¯Λ invariant-
mass spectrum was observed in decay channels of J/ψ
and ψ(3686) [5], and similar behavior was also reported
in χcJ →pK−¯Λ decays [6].
This structure has at-
tracted considerable interest in hadron spectroscopy, and
has been interpreted in terms of possible baryonium-like


## Page 6


6
states [7], strong final-state interactions [8], or interfer-
ence effects from excited kaon contributions [9]. Extend-
ing such investigations to more χcJ decay modes can help
further clarify the role of similar effects in P-wave char-
monium transitions, where the gluon hadronization dy-
namics may differ from those in S-wave decays.
Given the considerations discussed above, the decays
χcJ →pK−¯Λη provide a unique opportunity to study
baryon–antibaryon threshold behavior and hyperon exci-
tation dynamics. The final state contains both a baryon–
antibaryon pair and pseudoscalar mesons, making it par-
ticularly sensitive to potential excited Λ hyperon contri-
butions in the pK−and ¯Λη subsystems. In this paper,
by analyzing (2712.4 ± 14.3) × 106 ψ(3686) events col-
lected with the BESIII detector [10], we present the first
observation and of the decays χcJ →pK−¯Λη and mea-
sure their corresponding branching fractions.
Charge-
conjugate modes are implied throughout this paper.
II.
BESIII DETECTOR AND MONTE CARLO
SIMULATION
The BESIII detector [11] records symmetric e+e−col-
lisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring [12] in the
center-of-mass energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV, with
a peak luminosity of 1.1 × 1033 cm−2s−1 achieved at
√s = 3.773 GeV.
The cylindrical core of the BESIII
detector covers 93% of the full solid angle and consists
of a helium-based multilayer drift chamber (MDC), a
plastic scintillator time-of-flight system (TOF), and a
CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), which are
all enclosed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet pro-
viding a 1.0 T magnetic field, except for the data taken
in 2012, for which the magnetic field was 0.9 T. The
solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-return yoke
with resistive plate counter muon identification modules
interleaved with steel. The charged-particle momentum
resolution at 1 GeV/c is 0.5%, and the dE/dx resolution
is 6% for electrons from Bhabha scattering. The EMC
measures photon energies with a resolution of 2.5% (5%)
at 1 GeV in the barrel (endcap) region. The time reso-
lution in the TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while that in the
endcap region was 110 ps. The endcap TOF system was
upgraded in 2015 using multi-gap resistive plate chamber
technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps [13–15].
Around 83% of the data used in this analysis benefits
from this upgrade.
Simulated data samples produced with a geant4-
based [16] Monte Carlo (MC) package, which includes the
geometric description [17–20] of the BESIII detector and
the detector response, are used to determine detection
efficiencies and to estimate backgrounds. The simulation
models the beam energy spread and initial-state radia-
tion (ISR) in the e+e−annihilations with the generator
kkmc [21]. An inclusive MC sample containing 2.7×109
generic ψ(3686) events is used to study potential back-
grounds. The inclusive MC sample includes the produc-
tion of the ψ(3686) resonance. Known particle decays are
modeled with evtgen [22, 23] using branching fractions
taken from the Particle Data Group (PDG) [2], while un-
known decays are modeled with lundcharm [24]. Final-
state radiation from charged final state particles is incor-
porated using the photos package [25].
To account for the effect of intermediate resonance
structures on the detection efficiency, each of the χc0,1,2
decays is modeled using 5.04 million mixed signal MC
samples. The dominant decay modes include intermedi-
ate resonances such as Λ(1520) and ¯Λ(1690), correspond-
ing to the pK−and ¯Λη systems, respectively. These com-
ponents are generated using a phase-space (PHSP) model
employed in evtgen. These samples are then mixed with
four-body PHSP signal MC samples. The mixing ratios
are determined by examining the corresponding invariant
mass spectra, as discussed in Sec. V.
III.
EVENT SELECTION
We study the process ψ(3686) →γχcJ followed by
χcJ →pK−¯Λη, with ¯Λ →¯pπ+ and η →γγ. Charged
tracks (p, K−, ¯p, and π+) and photons are reconstructed
with the standard BESIII algorithms. Candidate events
are required to have at least four charged tracks with
zero net charge. Tracks reconstructed in the MDC must
satisfy |cos θ| < 0.93, where θ is the polar angle with re-
spect to the MDC z axis. For charged tracks originating
directly from the χcJ decay, the distances of closest ap-
proach to the interaction point (IP) are required to satisfy
|Vz| < 10 cm and |Vxy| < 1 cm, where Vz and Vxy denote
the distances along the beam and transverse directions,
respectively. No IP-vertex requirement is imposed on the
daughter tracks from the ¯Λ decay.
The PID information combines the dE/dx measure-
ments in the MDC and the TOF information to form
likelihoods L(h) for h ∈{p, K, π}. A track is identified
as a proton if L(p) > L(K) and L(p) > L(π), and as a
kaon if L(K) > L(p) and L(K) > L(π). Tracks failing
both criteria are treated as pion candidates.
Photon candidates are reconstructed from isolated
EMC clusters not associated to any charged track. At
least three good photon candidates are required.
The
deposited energy must exceed 25 MeV in the barrel re-
gion (|cos θ| < 0.80) and 50 MeV in the endcap region
(0.86 < |cos θ| < 0.92). The EMC time is required to be
within [0, 700] ns relative to the event start time to sup-
press noise and beam backgrounds. To reduce contam-
ination from hadronic interactions or bremsstrahlung,
clusters within a cone of 20◦around the extrapolated
EMC position of a ¯p track and within 10◦of any other
charged track are rejected.
The ¯Λ candidates are reconstructed from pairs of op-
positely charged tracks excluding the selected proton and
kaon candidates, under the ¯pπ+ mass hypothesis, with-
out applying PID requirements to the daughter tracks.

IV.
BACKGROUND ANALYSIS
Potential background contributions surviving the event
selection are investigated using the inclusive ψ(3686)
MC sample.
The decay chains of the candidate back-
ground events are identified with the TopoAna pack-
age [26]. The dominant decay chains can be categorized
into three classes: (i) ψ(3686) →ηJ/ψ with η →γγ, (ii)
ψ(3686) →γχcJ followed by χcJ →γJ/ψ or hadronic
decays, and (iii) multi-body direct decays possibly in-
volving K∗(892), Λ, or Σ∗(1385) resonances.
For the selected inclusive MC sample, the MpK−¯Λη
distribution exhibits a broad, non-peaking structure, in-
dicating that the background predominantly originates
from nonresonant processes and combinatorial contribu-
tions. A mild enhancement is found near 3.5 GeV/c2,
which is mainly attributed to cascade decays such as
ψ(3686) →γχc1 followed by χc1 →γJ/ψ with J/ψ →
pK∗−(892)¯Λ or J/ψ →pK−¯Σ∗0(1385).
Dedicated ex-
clusive MC studies show that the expected residual con-
tributions from these two processes are 1 and 3 events,
respectively. Therefore, their contributions are negligible
and are not included in the final fit.
The possible quantum electrodynamics (QED) contri-
bution from the process e+e−→γχcJ is evaluated with
the continuum data at √s = 3.650 GeV, corresponding
to an integrated luminosity of 401.0 pb−1 [10]. Applying
the same event selection criteria as for the ψ(3686) data,
no surviving event is found. Hence, the QED background
is negligible in this analysis.

SYSTEMATIC UNCERTAINTY
The systematic uncertainties on the branching frac-
tion measurements arise from multiple sources.
These
include the uncertainties associated with the total num-
ber of ψ(3686) events, tracking and PID efficiencies, pho-
ton detection, η mass-window requirement, ¯Λ reconstruc-
tion, the π0, J/ψ, and Σ0 vetoes, the modelling of combi-
natorial background shapes, the choices of fit ranges and
sideband regions, the 4C kinematic fit, and the limited
signal-MC statistics. In addition, the uncertainties of the
external branching fractions quoted from the PDG are
included. Possible contributions from the Λ(1670) and
N(1535) structures are also evaluated and incorporated
as additional systematic uncertainties. All these sources
are treated as independent and summarized in Table II,
with their individual evaluations described in the follow-
ing paragraphs. Here, a dash in Table II denotes that the
corresponding source is not applicable to the given χcJ
mode.

VII.
SUMMARY
Using a sample of (2712.4±14.3)×106 ψ(3686) events
collected with the BESIII detector, the decays χcJ →
pK−¯Λη (J = 0, 1, 2) are observed for the first time. The
statistical significances of the χc0, χc1, and χc2 signals
all exceed 5σ. The measured branching fractions are
B(χc0 →pK−¯Λη) = (5.3 ± 0.7 ± 0.5) × 10−5,
B(χc1 →pK−¯Λη) = (9.8 ± 0.6 ± 0.6) × 10−5,
B(χc2 →pK−¯Λη) = (9.3 ± 0.6 ± 0.6) × 10−5,
where the first and second uncertainties are statistical
and systematic, respectively. To improve the determina-
tion of the detection efficiencies and account for possi-
ble hadronic substructures, a mixed signal MC sample is
