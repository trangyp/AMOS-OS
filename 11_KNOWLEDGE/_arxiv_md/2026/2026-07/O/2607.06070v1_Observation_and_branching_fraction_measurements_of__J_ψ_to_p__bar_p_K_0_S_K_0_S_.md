I.
INTRODUCTION
Investigations of various decays of charmonia are im-
portant for the understanding of Quantum Chromody-
namics (QCD) [1]. In perturbative QCD (pQCD), the
hadronic decays of both J/ψ and ψ(3686) are thought
to occur mainly via c¯c pair annihilation into three glu-
ons, with the branching fractions (BFs) proportional to
the squares of the charmonium wave functions at the ori-
gin [2]. This leads to a ratio of BFs of ψ(3686) and J/ψ
decays to light hadrons:
Qh = Bψ(3686)→h
BJ/ψ→h
= Bψ(3686)→e+e−
BJ/ψ→e+e−
= 12.7%,
which is known as the pQCD 12% rule. A violation of
this rule was first reported by the Mark-II experiment
in ψ →ρπ decay in 1984 [3], and subsequently found in
many other decay channels. The discrepancy between the
pQCD predictions and the experimental measurements
is known as the ρ-π puzzle. Different mechanisms have
been proposed to explain this puzzle. However, no model
provides a self-consistent explanation for all observed ex-
perimental results. See Ref. [4] for a review.
In recent years, much progress has been made in the
experimental studies of multi-body decays of J/ψ and
ψ(3686).
However, knowledge of their decays into the
p¯pK ¯K final states is limited. So far, only a few mea-
surements of J/ψ or ψ(3686) decays into p¯pK+K−or
p¯pϕ(→K+K−), have been reported [5–8], yielding BFs
around 10−6 to 10−5.
Assuming isospin symmetry,
the BFs of J/ψ →p¯pK0
SK0

SK0
S
would be one-fourth of those for J/ψ →p¯pK+K−and
ψ(3686) →p¯pK+K−. To date, no experimental study of

SK0

has been reported. The measurements of the BFs of these

II.
BESIII DETECTOR AND MONTE CARLO
SIMULATION
The BESIII detector [16] records symmetric e+e−col-
lisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring [17] in the
center-of-mass energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV, with
a peak luminosity of 1.1 × 1033 cm−2s−1 achieved at
√s = 3.773 GeV.
The cylindrical core of the BESIII detector covers 93%
of the full solid angle and consists of a helium-based mul-
tilayer drift chamber (MDC), a plastic scintillator time-
of-flight system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl) electromagnetic
calorimeter (EMC), which are all enclosed in a supercon-
ducting solenoidal magnet providing a 1.0 T magnetic
field. The solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-
return yoke with resistive plate counter muon identifica-
tion modules interleaved with steel. The charged-particle
momentum resolution at 1 GeV/c is 0.5%, and the dE/dx
resolution is 6% for electrons from Bhabha scattering.
The EMC measures photon energies with a resolution of
2.5% (5%) at 1 GeV in the barrel (end cap) region. The
time resolution in the TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while
that in the end cap region was 110 ps. The end-cap TOF
system was upgraded in 2015 using multi-gap resistive
plate chamber technology, providing a time resolution of
60 ps [18–20]. This benefit 86% of the J/ψ sample and
85% of the ψ(3686) sample used in the analysis.
Simulated
data

produced
with
geant4-

the geometric description of the BESIII detector and the
detector response, are used to determine detection eﬀi-
ciencies and to estimate background contributions. The
simulation models the beam energy spread and initial
state radiation (ISR) in the e+e−annihilations with the
generator kkmc [22]. Inclusive MC samples containing
about 1010 J/ψ events and about 2.7×109 ψ(3686) events
include the production of the J/ψ or ψ(3686) resonance,
the J/ψ ISR production, and the continuum processes
incorporated in kkmc [22]. All particle decays are mod-
eled with evtgen [23] using BFs either taken from the
Particle Data Group (PDG) [24], when available, or oth-
erwise estimated with lundcharm [25]. Final state ra-

using photos [26]. Exclusive MC samples of 5×105 J/ψ
and ψ(3686) →p¯pK0
SK0

K0
