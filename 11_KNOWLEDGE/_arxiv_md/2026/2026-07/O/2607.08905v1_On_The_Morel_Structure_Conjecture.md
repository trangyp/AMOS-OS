1.
Introduction
The classical Postnikov tower decomposes a spectrum into layers controlled by its homotopy groups. By
Bott-periodicity, the spectrum representing topological real K-theory KO is eight-periodic with homotopy
groups given by Z, Z/2, Z/2, 0, Z, 0, 0, and 0.
In motivic homotopy theory, the presence of two possible candidates for a circle, the Tate circle Gm and
the simplicial circle S1, gives rise to at least two distinct Postnikov-type filtrations called the (effective) slice
filtration [Voe02] (Gm-Postnikov type filtration) and the generalized (or very effective) slice filtration [RØ16]
(a P1-Postnikov type filtration). We are interested in studying the motivic analogue of KO; this motivic
spectrum is called the Hermitian1 K-theory motivic spectrum KQ. It was first constructed in [Hor05] over
fields of characteristic not equal to 2, and in [CHN25] over any base scheme without assuming that 2 is
invertible. Motivic Bott-periodicity holds for KQ [CHN25, Prop. 1.0.4], which is likewise four-periodic2, i.e.,
KQ ≃KQ ⊗(P1)⊗4. However, the slice filtration for KQ does not closely reflect the topological behavior of
KO, in the sense that effective slices are not simply given by motivic cohomology HZ (which can be defined
as the zeroth slice of the sphere spectrum, the tensor-unit in SH(k).) and HZ /2 up to twist. The effective
slices of KQ have been computed by Röndigs-Østavær [RØ16, Thm. 4.18] and by Kolderup-Röndigs-Østavær
[KRØ25, Cor. 4.4]. Rather than exhibiting four-periodicity, the slices have a two-periodic description, that,
however, involves both twisting and shifting of some components, and the addition of extra components
every period. Better behavior is expressed by the generalized slices, as proved by Bachmann [Bac17] over
a field of characteristic not equal to 2, and by Kolderup-Röndigs-Østavær [KRØ25] over essentially smooth
schemes over a Dedekind scheme. The generalized effective slices of KQ are four-periodic up to twists. For
i ≡1, 2, 3 (mod 4), the ˜si(KQ) are simply given by some ordinary zero-slices, reflecting the vanishing of
πiKO for i = 3, 5, 7; furthermore, these zero-slices can be described in terms of motivic cohomology. Instead,
˜s0(KQ) is an extension of two objects, mirroring the fact that both π0KO and π1KO are nonzero.

that HKW is effective.
