ABSTRACT
We study the accretivity and m-accretivity of Laplacian and porous medium-type operators on
weighted graphs. In particular, we give several conditions that imply these properties for maximal
operators and investigate when these operators agree with various restrictions. For porous medium-
type operators on ℓ1 and for Laplacians on ℓp for p ∈[1, ∞), we show that there always exists a
dense subset of the domain on which the maximal operator is m-accretive. As a consequence, we
establish that accretivity, m-accretivity and injectivity of the shifted operator are all equivalent for
these maximal operators. Under additional conditions on the graph, we then prove that the maximal
operators are m-accretive on the entire domain, not just a dense subset.
We also investigate minimal operators and show that they are m-accretive if and only if the minimal
and maximal operators agree and the maximal operator is accretive. We then give some conditions
that imply this agreement. Furthermore, for the minimal Laplacian on ℓp, we show that accretivity
and m-accretivity are not equivalent. For the ℓ2 case, we give connections to Markov uniqueness
and essential self-adjointness. For the ℓ∞case, we establish the equivalence of stochastic complete-
ness at infinity, m-accretivity for the maximal Laplacian on ℓ∞, and m-accretivity of the minimal
Laplacian on ℓ1.
Keywords. weighted graphs · Laplacian and porous medium-type operators · accretivity · m-accretivity ·
minimal and maximal operators · stochastic completeness

discretization converge to a mild solution within certain prescribed bounds [6, 11, 52].

In [7], the existence and uniqueness of mild solutions to the Cauchy problem for the generalized porous medium
equation on a graph with ℓ1(X, µ) initial data were established by using a tailored sequence of approximating solutions.
A key step was to prove that L = L(1), which can be considered as an operator with a maximal domain on ℓ1, is m-
accretive when restricted to a suitable dense subset of its domain under additional assumptions on the graph. In this
paper, we remove all restrictions on the graph and show that there always exists such a dense subset, see Theorem 3.11.
We then give sufficient conditions to guarantee that L is m-accretive on the entire domain and not just a dense subset.
Our main results show that this is the case when we assume infinite measure of infinite paths which holds, in particular,
if there is a uniform lower bound on the vertex measure, see Theorem 3.16; or when we uniformly bound the edge
degree and assume that Φ preserves ℓ1, see Theorem 3.17. A key observation is that injectivity of the shifted operator
is already sufficient for the m-accretivity of L, see Theorem 3.14.
As mentioned above, accretivity has connections to uniqueness of operators. In this paper, we also consider the
operator obtained by restricting the maximal L to the finitely supported functions and taking the closure when this
restriction is closable. We denote the resulting operator by Lmin and think of Lmin as a minimal restriction of L when
L is closed. It turns out, when L is accretive, that the m-accretivity of Lmin is equivalent to the fact that these two
operators agree, see Lemma 3.19. In this context, our ultimate results give conditions for L = Lmin in the case of
the porous medium operator, see Theorem 3.29 and Corollary 3.30. One of the assumptions introduces a notion for
a metric to be intrinsic that is adapted to functions in ℓ1. Here, completeness properties with respect to such a metric
imply uniqueness of operators in the spirit of the use of such metrics to conclude essential self-adjointness and Markov
uniqueness on ℓ2, see [26, 31, 40, 55].

We also consider the m-accretivity of the maximal Laplacian on ℓp. We first study the closability of the Laplacian on
ℓp, see Lemma 2.6, and give conditions for the maximal Laplacian on ℓ1 to be equal to the minimal restriction that
are weaker than those required for the corresponding result for L, see Theorem 3.27. In parallel to L on ℓ1, we then
show that there always exists a dense subset of ℓp so that the restriction of the maximal Laplacian to this subset is
m-accretive, see Theorem 4.5. As a consequence, as for L, we show that accretivity, m-accretivity and injectivity of
the shifted operator are all equivalent for the maximal Laplacian on ℓp, see Theorem 4.7. We also show that metric
completeness with respect to an ℓ2-intrinsic metric implies m-accretivity of the maximal Laplacian on all ℓp spaces for
p ∈(1, ∞) and extend the criteria for the m-accretivity of L on ℓ1 to various ranges of ℓp spaces, see Theorem 4.16.
For the minimal Laplacian on ℓp, we show that this operator is accretive whenever it is defined, see Lemma 2.18. We
then explore when the maximal Laplacian is equal to the minimal restriction, see Propositions 4.18 and 4.20 as well as
Corollaries 5.5 and 6.6. This equivalence implies the m-accretivity of the minimal restriction. On the other hand, we
give conditions under which the minimal Laplacian on ℓp is not m-accretive for all p ∈[1, ∞], see Theorem 4.23. This
shows that the phenomenon of the equivalence of accretivity and m-accretivity which was observed for the maximal
operators, does not hold for the minimal operators.
By using general theory, m-accretivity of the maximal Laplacian on ℓ2 implies both form (equivalently, Markov)
uniqueness and essential self-adjointness, which is furthermore equivalent to the fact that the minimal and maximal
Laplacians on ℓ2 are equal, see Propositions 5.1 and 5.4. On the other hand, failure of form uniqueness implies that
the maximal Laplacian is not accretive on all ℓp spaces and thus the maximal Laplacian is not equal to the minimal
restriction, see Theorem 5.6. For the case of p = ∞, we show that m-accretivity of the maximal Laplacian on ℓ∞is
equivalent to stochastic completeness at infinity which is also equivalent to the m-accretivity of the minimal Laplacian
on ℓ1, see Theorem 6.3. As a consequence, when a graph is stochastically complete at infinity and the measure is
uniformly bounded from below, the maximal Laplacians on ℓp are m-accretive for all p ∈[1, ∞] and are equal to the
minimal Laplacians for p ∈[1, ∞), see Corollary 6.6.
To the best of our knowledge, m-accretivity of (Schr¨odinger operators driven by) the graph Laplacian was studied
for the first time in [50] for the case of vector bundles over graphs. Furthermore, [50] gives some criteria for the
equality of the minimal and maximal operators. For the case of non-symmetric graph Laplacians, see [1]. On the
other hand, form uniqueness, essential self-adjointness, and stochastic completeness are all well-studied properties for
graph Laplacians; see, e.g., [9, 12, 19, 23, 25, 26, 28, 31, 32, 33, 34, 38, 40, 43, 49, 55, 57, 59, 61, 62, 63] among
other works. Furthermore, there is a rapidly growing literature on nonlinear equations on graphs; we mention [4, 5,
20, 21, 22, 29, 30, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 53] as a representative sample.
We structure the paper as follows. In Section 2, we introduce basic definitions and give some consequences of ad-
ditional assumptions on the graph. We also prove a comparison principle which will be useful for subsequent con-
siderations and discuss the accretivity of formal operators on finitely supported functions. Section 3 is the heart of
the paper. There, we discuss the accretivity and m-accretivity of the maximal operator L as well as when it agrees
with some of its restrictions. In Section 4, we focus on the maximal Laplacian on ℓp as well as restrictions, extending
many results for L on ℓ1 to Laplacians on ℓp. In Section 5, we discuss connections between form uniqueness, essential
self-adjointness and accretivity for the Laplacian on ℓ2 and show how the minimal operator may not be m-accretive.
In Section 6, we give connections between stochastic completeness and accretivity on ℓ∞and ℓ1. Along the way,
we strengthen one of the main results in [7]. We give additional details on the connection to this previous result in
Appendix A.

Notation and preliminaries
In this section we introduce basic notations and the main players. In particular, we first introduce graphs as well as
Laplacian and porous medium-type operators. We then prove a comparison principle which gives uniqueness of solu-
tions for equations involving porous medium-type operators. This uniqueness will be crucial for future considerations.
We then introduce some additional assumptions on graphs which will be used when we establish our main results
as well as introduce the notions of accretivity and m-accretivity for general operators on Banach spaces. Finally, we
discuss the accretivity of formal operators on finitely supported functions.

We now give a general definition for accretive and m-accretive operators. We also give an easy consequence of these
properties which will be useful later. Finally, we introduce the bracket which allows us to analyze accretivity on ℓp

Given a linear or nonlinear operator T with domain dom(T) ⊆E where E is a Banach space and λ ∈R we write
Sλ = id +λT
for the operator acting on dom(T) where id is the identity operator. We refer to Sλ as the shifted operator.
Definition 2.9 (Accretive and m-accretive operators). If E = (E, ∥·∥) is a real Banach space and T : dom(T) ⊆E →
E is an operator, then T is said to be accretive if
∥Sλu −Sλv∥≥∥u −v∥
for all u, v ∈dom(T) and every λ > 0. An accretive operator T is called m-accretive if Sλ is surjective for every
λ > 0.
Note that accretivity implies the injectivity of Sλ for every λ > 0.
Remark 2.10 (For some/all λ). The norm inequality in Definition 2.9 need not be checked for every λ > 0. Indeed, if
it holds for every λ ∈(0, λ0] with some fixed λ0 > 0, then it automatically holds for every λ > 0. This follows from
the equivalent characterization of accretivity in terms of the bracket [ · , · ], see [3, II. Proposition 2.5] and Remark 2.12
directly below. However, we will see that, for the maximal porous medium-type operators on ℓ1 and maximal Lapla-
cians on ℓp, if the accretivity norm inequality holds for one λ > 0, then it holds for all λ > 0. Furthermore, for
general operators, the surjectivity in Definition 2.9 need not be checked for every λ > 0 either as an accretive operator
is m-accretive as soon as Sλ is surjective for some λ > 0, see [2, Proposition 3.3].

Accretivity and m-accretivity of porous medium-type operators on ℓ1
In this section we provide sufficient conditions under which porous medium-type operators are m-accretive on ℓ1.
In the case of finite graphs, we have just discussed how the Laplacian ∆(p) is accretive for every p ∈[1, ∞], see
Corollary 2.16 directly above. However, for general porous medium-type operators, accretivity may not hold even for
finite graphs. For instance, accretivity already fails for the porous medium operator L(2) with ϕ(s) = s|s|3 in the
case of a graph with only four vertices, see [7, Example following Proposition 2.5]. On the other hand, L is always
accretive on finite graphs by Corollary 2.14 above. Therefore, we will focus on the case of L and infinite graphs.
We will first show that L is accretive under conditions (B) and (C). Furthermore, there always exists a dense subset
Ω⊆ℓ1(X, µ) such that L|Ωis m-accretive. We will then give conditions under which L = L|Ωwhich yields m-
accretivity of L. A key insight is that accretivity of L already suffices for m-accretivity, in fact, it suffices to show that
the shifted operator Sλ = id +λL is injective for λ > 0.
Finally, we will introduce the minimal operator Lmin which is the closure of the restriction of L to the finitely supported
functions, and give conditions under which L and Lmin coincide. We point out that Lmin is always accretive when it
is defined, thus, when these operators agree, they are automatically m-accretive. Along the way, we also investigate
these questions for the Laplacian on ℓ1 where we show that the minimal and maximal operators agree under some
weaker assumptions.
