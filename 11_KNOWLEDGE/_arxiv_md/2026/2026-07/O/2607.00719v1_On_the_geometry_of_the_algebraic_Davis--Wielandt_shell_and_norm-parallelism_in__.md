Abstract. This article is devoted to the study of the Davis–Wielandt shell and the Davis–Wielandt
radii of elements in a C∗-algebra. Utilizing a state-space approach, several geometric properties of
the algebraic Davis–Wielandt shell are established. Upper and lower bounds for the algebraic Davis–
Wielandt radii are obtained including the Davis–Wielandt radius of the sum of k elements. We also
explore the relationship between norm-parallelism and the Davis–Wielandt radii of elements.
1. Introduction
The study of numerical ranges and their corresponding radii forms a cornerstone of modern
operator theory and functional analysis. For a bounded linear operator T acting on a complex
Hilbert space H , the classical spatial numerical range W(T) and the numerical radius w(T) provide
invaluable insight into the spectral, geometric, and structural properties of T.
Over the decades, this framework has been elegantly extended to an abstract setting. For a unital
C∗-algebra A with the unit 1, the algebraic numerical range and the algebraic numerical radius of
an element a ∈A, are defined respectively as:
WA(a) = {f(a) : f ∈S(A)},
wA(a) = sup{|z| : z ∈WA(a)},
where S(A) denotes the state space of A, defined as the set of all positive linear functionals f ∈A∗
such that ∥f∥= f(1) = 1.
It is a standard result in operator algebra theory that S(A) is a
weak∗-compact convex subset of the dual space A∗[28, Proposition 13.8].
The algebraic numerical range of a ∈A is a nonempty compact convex subset of C (see [14]).
Considering B(H ), the algebra of all bounded linear operators in H , we have WA(T) = W(T).

4. Norm-Parallelism and the Algebraic Davis–Wielandt Radius
In this section, we investigate the relationship between norm-parallelism and the Davis–Wielandt
radius within the structural framework of unital C∗-algebras. The algebraic Davis–Wielandt shell
DWA(a) encodes refined geometric and structural information about an element a ∈A that extends
well beyond the classical numerical range. Utilizing the baseline inequalities of Section 3, we show
that norm-parallelism to the identity element 1 can be completely characterized by an elegant,
explicit formula for dwA(a). Along the way, we demonstrate that this radius criteria serves as a
bridge to characterizing normaloid elements, effectively linking spatial norm-attainment with state-
space properties on the powers of the elements.
We begin by recalling the formal definition of
norm-parallelism and reviewing some of its fundamental properties.
Definition 4.1. Let A be a unital C∗-algebra. Two elements a, b ∈A are said to be norm-parallel,
denoted by a ∥b, if there exists a scalar λ ∈T such that
∥a + λb∥= ∥a∥+ ∥b∥.
In this context, the Daugavet equation ∥a + 1∥= ∥a∥+ 1 arises as a special case of norm-
parallelism. It is worth noting that norm-parallelism is a symmetric and homogeneous relation;
however, it does not satisfy transitivity relation in general (see [24]). Although linear dependence
clearly implies norm-parallelism, the converse does not hold. Characterizations of norm-parallelism
in the broader setting of Hilbert C∗-modules can be found in [23,24].

results, we shall frequently utilize [23, Corollary 4.4], which we restate below for completeness.

5. Conclusion
In conclusion, this paper establishes a rigorous state-space framework for the algebraic Davis–
Wielandt shell and radius in unital C∗-algebras, successfully bypassing classical spatial non-convexity
irregularities. By characterizing the shell’s boundary geometry, deriving sharp metric inequalities
for sums of elements, and clarifying the structural interplay with norm-parallelism, these results
significantly advance numerical radius theory.
