Abstract. In this article, we investigate the arithmeticity of the one-cusped Dehn fillings of the
(−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement and of the Berge manifold, which respectively are the sisters
of the Whitehead and 62
2 link complements. We show that for each such one-cusped hyperbolic
Dehn filling, the cusp field, the trace field and the invariant trace field coincide. Moreover, we
establish that no one-cusped hyperbolic Dehn filling of the Berge manifold is arithmetic and that
the only arithmetic one-cusped hyperbolic Dehn filling of the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement
is the sister of the figure eight knot complement. The techniques used to prove these results
further show that each knot complement covering a one-cusped hyperbolic Dehn filling of either
of these two sisters manifolds admits no hidden symmetries, effectively generalizing already
known results in this regard.
1. Introduction
This paper is devoted to the study of arithmetic invariants of hyperbolic 3-manifolds and their
Dehn fillings.
We here direct our attention to the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement and the
Berge manifold. These are two of the four 2-cusped orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds of lowest
complexity 4 (in the sense of Matveev [18]) and lowest known volume, as observed by Martelli and
Petronio [17] from the work of Callahan, Hildebrand and Weeks [5, Appendix A], the other two
being their “siblings” the Whitehead link and 62
2 link complements respectively. Agol [2, Theorem
3.6] in fact proved that the complements of the Whitehead link and the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link
do have minimal volume among all 2-cusped such manifolds. All four of these manifolds are also
arithmetic, the Whitehead link and (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complements covering H3/PSL2(O1) and
the Berge manifold and 62
2 link complement covering H3/PSL2(O3).
The non-hyperbolic Dehn fillings of the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement and the Berge man-
ifold were classified in [17].1 We here concentrate on the hyperbolic Dehn fillings of the (−2, 3, 8)-
pretzel link complement and the Berge manifold, more specifically the one-cusped hyperbolic Dehn
fillings. Our approach in this venture is three-fold:
• we relate them with the notion of hidden symmetries,
• we classify which such ones are arithmetic, and
• we correlate their associated number fields.
Recall that hidden symmetries are symmetries between finite covers which are not lifts of actual
symmetries. Here, we establish the following result.
Theorem 9.1. No hyperbolic knot complement covering a one-cusped Dehn filling of either the
Berge manifold or the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement has hidden symmetries.
The above result effectivizes [13, Proof of Theorem 1.1] and [7, Example 5.2, Example 5.3] (and
generalizes [14, Proof of Theorem 6.1] for orbifold fillings). Its analog was proved for the Whitehead
link complement in [20] and for the 62
2 link complement in [6].

Berge manifold and the pertinent number theoretic properties of their associated algebraic varieties

To prove Theorem 6.4 and 8.5, we use the characterization of non-compact arithmetic Kleinian
group of finite co-volume in [20, Proposition 4.4], which together with [20, Proposition 2.7] would
require the cusp field of a one-cusped arithmetic hyperbolic orbifold to be quadratic imaginary. We
utilize the ideal triangulations of our two manifolds and the fact that their one-cusped hyperbolic
Dehn fillings can be realized as points in a co-dimension one subvariety V0 of the gluing variety
V associated with these triangulations. It is important to bear in mind that [7, Proposition 1.6]
implies that the cusp field of such a one-cusped Dehn filling is generated over Q by the value of a
rational function on V at the corresponding point in V0.
We point out that both the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement and the Berge manifold have
non-trivial self-isometries, which can be checked via SnapPy [8].
This helps us conclude that
analyzing just the one-cusped (p, q)-Dehn fillings, where both p and q are non-negative integers,
is enough. We first analyze such one-cusped Dehn fillings for which the computed cusp moduli is
a unit non-rational algebraic integer. For the Berge manifold, all of them satisfy this (Proposition
6.3) and for the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement it is true when the filling coefficients satisfy
p ̸= 2q (Proposition 8.3). We show that the cusp fields of the (2q, q) one-cusped fillings of the
(−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement are not quadratic imaginary. As observed in [6] (recorded as
Fact 3.1 in Section 3.2), there are only three quadratic imaginary number fields which have a
unit non-rational algebraic integer. We argue that none of these could be the cusp fields of the
respective one-cusped Dehn fillings of the Berge manifold with unit algebraic integral cusp moduli
and the sister m003 of the figure eight knot complement is the only one-cusped Dehn filling of the
(−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement with such a cusp field.
Theorem 9.1 can derived as a consequence of [20, Proposition 9.1] and the fact that the sister
of the figure eight knot complement is the only one amongst the considered one-cusped hyperbolic
Dehn fillings which has a quadratic imaginary cusp field (proof of Theorem 6.4 and 8.5). This
method of using degree bound for the cusp field gives us stronger control on understanding which
one-cusped Dehn fillings can cover rigid cusped orbifolds2 than the argument presented the proof of
[13, Theorem 1.1], which involves the limiting behavior of the sequence of the orbifold fundamental

basic notations that we will use throughout the paper.
2.1. Cusps of hyperbolic 3-orbifolds. Unless otherwise specified, by a hyperbolic 3-orbifold,
we will mean an orbifold H3/Γ where Γ is a discrete subgroup of Isom+(H3) = PSL2(C) of finite
co-volume.
In particular, H3/Γ whose (orbifold) fundamental group is Γ inherits a complete
hyperbolic metric from H3. Denote the hyperbolic 3-orbifold H3/Γ by MΓ. The Γ-orbits of the
parabolic fixed points of Γ on the extended complex plane bC are referred to as the cusps of MΓ.
For a parabolic fixed point x of Γ, let c = Γx denote the corresponding cusp of MΓ. Let Λx be
the stabilizer of x in Γ. If Bx is a horoball centered at x, then Λx acts as an oriented wallpaper
group of Euclidean isometries on the horosphere ∂Bx ∼= R2. It follows from [10, Corollary 2.2] that
Bx/Λx embeds into MΓ and is referred to as a cusp neighborhood of cusp c (also see [26, Corollary
5.10.2]). We say that ∂Bx/Λx is a cusp cross-section of cusp c. This cusp cross-section is one of
the five oriented Euclidean 2-orbifolds: T2, S2(2, 2, 2, 2), S2(2, 3, 6), S2(3, 3, 3), S2(2, 4, 4). When
Λx is Z2, this cusp cross-section is a Euclidean torus. In this case, we say that c is a torus cusp.
When Γ does not have any torsion elements, MΓ is a manifold and all of its cusps are torus cusps.

9. Hidden symmetries and Effectivization
There have been a wide variety of studies on which one-cusped Dehn fillings of the Berge
manifold can admit hidden symmetries. The earliest such trace back to Hoffman [13, Proof of
Theorem 1.1], where he showed that for integers m and n such that n > 0 and gcd(n, 7) = 1,
all but finitely many (n, m)-Dehn filling with respect to a standard framing4 on the unknotted
cusp of the Berge manifold lack hidden symmetries. He strengthened this result further in [14,
Theorem 6.1] by showing that when both gcd(n, 7) and gcd(n, m) are 1, none of such hyperbolic
(n, m)-Dehn fillings on the unknotted cusp of the Berge manifold admit hidden symmetries. The
proof technique in [13, Theorem 1.1] uses a limiting argument concerning orbifold fundamental
groups whereas the proof of [14, Theorem 6.1] uses degree bounds for covers of rigid one-cusped
orbifolds. More recently, using separate techniques, [7, Example 5.3] and [19, Theorem 8.4] imply
that at-most finitely many Dehn fillings on the unknotted cusp of the Berge manifold can have
hidden symmetries. The work in [7, Example 5.3] centers around the non-geometric isolation of
cusps and and that in [19, Theorem 8.4] upon the study of certain order 3 symmetries in relevant
horoball packings of H3.
On the other hand, using the findings from [1], it was shown in [7, Example 5.2] (by virtue
of non-geometric isolation of cusps) that all but finitely many knot complements obtained by
one-cusped Dehn filling of the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement lack hidden symmetries.
Here, we can give an effective statement on the relation of such Dehn fillings with hidden
symmetries. Observe that the proof of Theorem 6.4 shows that the cusp field of a one-cusped
Dehn filling of the Berge manifold can’t be quadratic imaginary. On the other hand, Theorem 8.5
shows that the cusp field of a one-cusped Dehn filling of the (−2, 3, 8)-pretzel link complement is
quadratic imaginary if and only if it is the tetrahedral manifold m003. However, the only knot
