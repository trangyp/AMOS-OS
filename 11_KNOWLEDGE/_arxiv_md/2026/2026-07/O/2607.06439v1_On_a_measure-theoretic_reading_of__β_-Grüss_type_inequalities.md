ON A MEASURE-THEORETIC READING OF β-GRÜSS TYPE
INEQUALITIES
K. CASTILLO AND Â. MACEDO
Abstract. We show that the principal β-Grüss inequalities for the positive integral
can be obtained naturally from elementary measure theory. Once the positive β-integral
is recognised as integration with respect to a finite positive purely atomic measure, and
this measure is normalised to a probability measure, the associated Chebyshev functional
becomes simply a covariance. The corresponding inequalities then follow from standard
facts valid on arbitrary probability spaces: Korkine’s identity, Hölder’s inequality on the
product space, Cauchy’s inequality for covariance, and the elementary variance bound
for bounded functions. The Riemann–Stieltjes β-estimates follow, in the signed case,
by domination with respect to the total variation measure. Thus, rather than adding
another member to this family of β-Grüss inequalities, this note identifies the elementary
measure-theoretic mechanism that accounts for the family itself.
1. Introduction
Grüss type inequalities originate in the classical estimate of Grüss for the Chebyshev
functional associated with the ordinary integral [5]. Since then, Grüss type estimates have
been formulated in many different settings, including inner product spaces, positive linear
functionals and Riemann–Stieltjes integrals; see, for instance, [6, 7]. Grüss-type inequalities
for the β-integral and for a related Riemann–Stieltjes β-integral were studied in [2], which
provides a recent and comprehensive account of the subject and of the relevant literature.
The aim of the present note is not to add another proof based on the defining series
of the β-integral. Rather, it is to point out that, in the positive case, the β-integral is
already integration with respect to a finite positive purely atomic measure, with at most
countable support, concentrated on the corresponding β-interval. After normalisation by
its total mass, this measure becomes a probability measure, and the Chebyshev functional
associated with the β-integral becomes exactly the covariance with respect to that prob-
ability measure. This simple observation places the main β-Grüss inequalities in a more
general framework. Korkine’s identity, the product-space Hölder estimate, Cauchy’s in-
equality for covariance, the variance estimate and the pre-Grüss inequality all follow from
the elementary probability framework recalled below. Thus, Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.3,
Lemma 3.4, Propositions 3.5 and 3.6, Corollary 3.7, and Theorem 3.8 of [2] are recovered
from general facts valid on every probability space. In this sense, the interested reader is
referred to the broader literature on the subject, where the same measure-theoretic ideas
apply, mutatis mutandis.
The same distinction is useful for the Riemann–Stieltjes β-integral. When the integrator
induces a positive measure, the preceding covariance argument applies verbatim. When the
integrator induces a signed measure, the corresponding estimates are no longer covariance
estimates; they follow instead from domination by the total variation measure. In this
way, the principal Riemann–Stieltjes β-Grüss estimates of [2], including the mechanism
behind their Theorem 4.5, can also be placed within standard measure-theoretic principles.
Consequently, the role of the β-integral in these inequalities is to provide a particular finite
positive purely atomic measure. Once that measure is identified, the inequalities can be

6. Conclusions
The same point also explains, at a structural level, the results obtained for the spaces
Lp
β[a, b] associated with certain operators, see [3] and [4]. Once the β-integral is written
as integration with respect to its associated finite positive purely atomic measure, the
Hölder and Minkowski inequalities are the usual inequalities for Lp-spaces over a positive
measure. Likewise, after quotienting by equality µβ-almost everywhere, the space Lp
β[a, b]
is naturally identified with the Lp-space of a purely atomic finite measure, equivalently with
the product of the two weighted sequence spaces associated with the two β-orbits issuing
from a and b, with points of zero µβ-mass playing no role. Its completeness, separability for
1 ≤p < ∞, reflexivity for 1 < p < ∞, and the Hilbert structure of L2
β[a, b] can therefore
be understood as consequences of the elementary structure of atomic Lp-spaces.
It is worth stressing that this point is not specific to the β-calculus. The same reasoning
applies, mutatis mutandis, to other discrete positive calculi, such as the usual q-calculus,
whenever the corresponding integral can be represented as integration with respect to a

itivity, Hölder-type estimates, power-mean arguments, or normalisation of the underlying
discrete integral are governed by the same measure-theoretic mechanism. The particular
form of the nodes, weights and auxiliary identities may vary from one calculus to another,
but the underlying principle remains the elementary structure of integration with respect
to a positive atomic measure.
The references collected in [3, 4, 2] show that these constructions have generated a sub-
stantial body of work. The papers just cited are among its more significant contributions.
From the present viewpoint, however, a substantial part of this circle of problems can be
traced back to the same elementary measure-theoretic mechanism: the identification of
the relevant β-integral with integration against a finite positive purely atomic measure
with at most countable support. This does not diminish the usefulness of the β-formalism,
nor of analogous formalisms such as the usual q-calculus. It indicates, rather, that for
the material discussed in this note, and for a significant part of the surrounding work, the
essential mechanism is not a special feature of any one discrete calculus but the elementary
structure of integration with respect to an atomic finite measure.
