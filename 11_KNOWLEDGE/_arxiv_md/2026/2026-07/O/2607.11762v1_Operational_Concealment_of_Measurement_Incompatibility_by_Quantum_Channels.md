Measurement incompatibility can remain intact at the operator level yet become operationally
inaccessible when observations are restricted to the output of a quantum channel; we refer to this
phenomenon as operational concealment. We develop a systematic adjoint-kernel framework for
operational concealment in which observables are organized into operational equivalence classes
determined by the kernel of the adjoint channel. This framework yields a structural classification of
channels via kernel equivalence and monotonicity, together with a concealment robustness measure
admitting explicit SDP formulations. It also yields an approximate concealment framework and a
geometric characterization of concealment for unbiased binary qubit POVMs under rank-2 unital qubit
channels. We show that concealment robustness coincides with standard incompatibility robustness
for injective channels but can be strictly smaller for non-injective channels, as demonstrated by explicit
analytical families. These results provide a systematic characterization and quantitative treatment
of operationally inaccessible measurement incompatibility, with implications for restricted-access
quantum information and semi-device-independent certification.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Measurement incompatibility—the impossibility of
jointly measuring certain quantum observables—is a fun-
damental nonclassical feature that underpins numerous
quantum information tasks [1–5]. While incompatibil-
ity has recently been generalized from observables to
channels themselves [6], here we investigate the comple-
mentary question of how a quantum channel modifies
the operational accessibility of measurement incompatibil-
ity. Motivated by restricted-access scenarios, which also
arise in semi-device-independent certification settings [7],
we identify a subtle phenomenon: measurement incom-
patibility may remain intact at the operator level while
becoming operationally inaccessible when observations
are restricted to the output of a quantum channel.
The key observation is that two observables are opera-
tionally indistinguishable precisely when their difference
lies in ker(E†). This defines an equivalence relation on the
Hermitian operator space, partitioning observables into
operational equivalence classes. Our central result is that
operational concealment admits a complete characteriza-
tion in terms of the adjoint kernel of the channel. Under
the operational assumptions adopted throughout, the
adjoint kernel determines precisely which observable di-
rections remain operationally accessible. More specifically,
all operational predictions depend only on equivalence
classes in the quotient space
Herm(Hout)/ ker(E†),

Within this quotient space, measurement incompatibility
is determined by operational equivalence classes: if the
classes of two incompatible measurements admit compat-
ible representatives, then the original incompatibility is
operationally concealed.
Operational concealment is closely related to the no-
tion of compatibility relative to a restricted set of states
introduced by Heinosaari et al. [8], where the accessible
state set is given by the image of the channel.
A single input state is generally insufficient to certify in-
compatibility: for any fixed input state one can construct
compatible POVMs reproducing the statistics of a given
measurement pair on that state alone (see Appendix A).
This motivates the assumption of tomographically com-
plete input states adopted throughout.
We distinguish three related notions. Operational con-
cealment and effective compatibility are properties of a
measurement-channel pair. The former concerns the ex-
istence of compatible representatives within operational
equivalence classes, whereas the latter concerns the com-
patibility of the effective measurements themselves. Op-
erational concealment always implies effective compat-
ibility, but the converse fails in general.
In contrast,
2-incompatibility breaking is a property of the channel,
requiring effective compatibility for every measurement
pair.
Since measurement statistics depend linearly on observ-
ables through the Heisenberg action of the channel, the
adjoint channel—not the Schr¨odinger picture—provides
the natural object for characterizing operational indistin-
guishability. This observation underlies the entire frame-
work developed below.

to complete dephasing. Under complete dephasing, the

The operational equivalence relation induces the quo-

Proposition 5 (No-go for certification under conceal-

V.
OPERATIONAL CONCEALMENT VERSUS
INCOMPATIBILITY-BREAKING CHANNELS
We compare operational concealment with the estab-
lished notion of incompatibility-breaking channels.
Recall that a quantum channel E is 2-incompatibility-
breaking (2-IBC) if for every pair of POVMs {Ma}, {Nb}
on the output space, the effective measurements {E†(Ma)}
and {E†(Nb)} are jointly measurable [2, 13]. (Full incom-
patibility breaking concerns arbitrary finite collections;
we follow the pairwise convention here.)
Operational concealment and incompatibility breaking
are related but distinct notions.
A 2-incompatibility-
breaking channel guarantees that, for every pair of
POVMs, the corresponding effective measurements are
jointly measurable. By contrast, operational concealment
concerns whether a given measurement pair admits com-
patible representatives within its operational equivalence
classes. Thus incompatibility breaking is a channel-level
property, whereas concealment is a pair-level property.
Moreover, Proposition 6 establishes that concealment al-
ways implies effective compatibility, whereas the converse
fails in general. The depolarizing channel of Theorem 5
provides an explicit example in which the effective mea-
surements become compatible even though concealment
remains impossible.
The complete dephasing channel provides a simple ex-
ample of a 2-incompatibility-breaking channel because
every effective POVM is diagonal in the dephasing basis.
Consequently, every effective POVM belongs to a commu-
tative operator algebra, and any family of such POVMs
is jointly measurable [2, 9].

VIII.
RELATION TO EXISTING FRAMEWORKS
Using the adjoint characterization, we show that the op-
erational concealment condition is equivalent to restricted-
state compatibility on the channel-accessible state set SE
introduced by Heinosaari et al. [8]. Building on this equiv-
alence, we introduce a systematic adjoint-kernel frame-
work for operational concealment that yields structural
channel classifications, robustness measures, and geomet-
ric characterizations within the adjoint-kernel framework.
a.
Restricted-state and dimensional compatibility.
Operational concealment is precisely the specialization
of restricted-state compatibility to the channel-accessible
state set SE [8]. This is directly related to recent studies
of compatibility under restricted testing scenarios and
limited state information, as well as to the notion of com-
patibility dimension, where measurements are tested only
on subspaces of the full Hilbert space [21]. In particular,
the projection criterion for restricted-state compatibil-
ity of unbiased qubit observables established in Ref. [14]
is obtained by restricting the general projection crite-
rion to the operationally accessible subspace ker(T T
E )⊥.
The distinguishing feature of the present work is the
systematic Heisenberg-picture adjoint-kernel framework,
which yields a geometric characterization of concealment,
channel-dependent robustness measures, and a kernel-
induced ordering of channels.
b.
Incompatibility-breaking channels.
Incompatibil-
ity breaking is a channel-level property: every pair of
measurements becomes effectively compatible after the
channel [13]. Operational concealment, by contrast, is
a pair-level property. Moreover, incompatibility break-
ing does not imply concealment: effective compatibility
of all measurement pairs does not guarantee that the
operational equivalence classes of every pair contain com-
patible representatives. This distinction underscores the
difference between channel-level and pair-level notions of
incompatibility under restricted access.
c.
Robustness measures.
The concealment robust-
ness Rc is a generalized robustness measure relative to
the convex set of concealed pairs. This aligns with the
standard approach in which robustness quantifies the dis-
tance to a free set [19, 20, 22, 23]. It does not, however,
constitute a full resource theory; we do not define free
operations or prove monotonicity under them.
d.
Hierarchies of incompatibility.
Recent work has
shown that classical pre- and post-processing operations
can induce finer operational classifications of measure-
ment incompatibility beyond standard joint measurability
[24]. The present work complements this by showing that
channel-mediated access yields another class of opera-
tional refinements, with a clear geometric characterization
via adjoint kernels.
IX.
DISCUSSION
The principal contribution of this work is the identifi-
cation of operational concealment as a phenomenon char-
acterized by compatibility within the operational equiva-
lence classes induced by the adjoint kernel of a quantum
channel. The central insight is that measurement incom-
patibility under restricted channel access is determined
not by individual observables but by their operational
equivalence classes. Concealment occurs precisely when
these equivalence classes admit compatible representa-
tives. This viewpoint unifies the adjoint characterization,
the channel-specialized geometric results, and the quan-
titative measures developed here. More broadly, the op-
erational observable space provides a channel-dependent
description of how quantum resources may persist at the
operator level while becoming operationally inaccessible.
For rank-2 unital qubit channels, we obtain an explicit
adjoint-kernel formulation and geometric characterization
of concealment, providing the channel-specialized version


## Page 13


13
of the restricted-state projection criterion of Ref. [14].
The characterization is expressed in terms of the projec-
tion of measurement Bloch vectors onto the operationally
accessible subspace, illustrating how the adjoint kernel
determines which observable components remain opera-
tionally accessible and therefore influence measurement
incompatibility.
By Proposition 6, operational concealment implies ef-
fective compatibility, but the converse fails in general. Op-
erationally, both preclude steering certification through
channel-accessible measurements. However, effective com-
patibility concerns whether the effective measurements are
jointly measurable, whereas operational concealment con-
cerns the existence of compatible representatives within
the corresponding operational equivalence classes. Quan-
titatively, the concealment robustness satisfies Rc ≤Rinc,
with equality for injective channels and strict inequal-
ity for non-injective ones. This shows that operational
restrictions can reduce the amount of noise required to
operationally conceal incompatibility, yielding a genuinely
channel-dependent quantifier. More generally, this con-
nects operational concealment with the broader program
of certifying quantum properties through restricted mea-
surements.
Several directions remain open for future investigation.
Extending the framework to higher-dimensional and non-
unital channels is a natural next step. It would be inter-
esting to investigate whether the projection-based charac-
terization generalizes beyond rank-2 unital qubit channels.
Another important question is whether ancillary-assisted,
adaptive, or collective measurement strategies can recover
operational signatures of otherwise concealed incompat-
ibility. More broadly, the operational observable space
perspective suggests that the observability of quantum
resources depends not only on the resources themselves
but also on the accessible observable structure induced by
the surrounding dynamics. This viewpoint suggests that
quotient-space descriptions may also prove useful in other
operational resource theories, including applications to ap-
proximate tomography and multipartite scenarios where
restricted access to subsystems may conceal nonclassical
correlations.
