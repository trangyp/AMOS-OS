ON RANK TWO KLEINIAN GROUPS
WITH THREE PARABOLICS
ALEX ELZENAAR
ABSTRACT. The free group of rank 2 is the fundamental group of the genus 2 handle-
body H . We study discrete representations of this group into PSL(2,C) so that
three disjoint simple closed curves on the conformal boundary ∂∞H are sent to
parabolic elements. We show that the only infinite covolume groups of this form
are maximal cusp groups on the boundary of genus 2 Schottky space. We also ex-
hibit hitherto unexpected finite covolume groups which do not arise from Heegaard
splitting presentations of tunnel number 1 links.
1. INTRODUCTION
The classification of discrete subgroups of PSL(2,C) (called Kleinian groups) is
an old problem dating back in its modern form to the 1970s [22]. In recent decades
most interest in this area has been directed towards the theory of ending laminations
proposed by Thurston [34]. The ending lamination theorem, proved by Brock, Canary,
and Minsky [4, 25] but building on work of many others, gives a complete set of
topological invariants that can be used to classify Kleinian groups. It is very useful
for answering theoretical questions, but cannot easily be used to solve concrete
classification problems. The prototypical such problem is:
The Identification Problem. Suppose A1,..., Ak ∈PSL(2,C) are given such that
G = 〈A1,..., Ak〉is discrete. Identify the hyperbolic isometry class of H3/G.
Checking that a group given in terms of generating matrices is discrete is also a
very hard problem. Often with examples of matrix groups that arise in nature it is
possible to make an educated guess about discreteness by plotting the limit set, and
then generally one will try to validate this guess by applying various methods to solve
the identification problem, or by attempting to find a 2-generated indiscrete subgroup
using Jørgensen’s inequality [24, Theorem 2.17].
The most sophisticated techniques for attacking the identification problem for
PSL(2,C) exist in the special case that G = 〈A1, A2〉where A1 and A2 are both
parabolic matrices. Work in this area dates back to Riley’s study of two-bridge
link groups [27, 28, 30]. The general picture was completed by Keen, Komori, and
Series [19, 21] who described the deformation space of free Kleinian groups gener-
ated by two parabolic elements, and by Akiyoshi, Ohshika, Parker, Sakuma, and
Yoshida [1] who gave a complete description of all non-free Kleinian groups generated

For more complicated groups there are only a few substantial computationally

In this paper, we study the case of two generators X,Y ∈PSL(2,C). Since the free
group on two generators is the fundamental group of the genus two handlebody H ,
we can interpret words in X and Y as closed curves on the genus two surface Σ = ∂H .
In the following definition, recall that if f ∈PSL(2,C) satisfies tr2 f = 4 then either f
is trivial or f is parabolic (has a unique fixed point on the Riemann sphere P1(C)).
Definition 1.1. Let X be the character variety of representations F{X,Y } →PSL(2,C),
where F{X,Y } is the free group on the symbols X and Y. Suppose that α, β, and γ
are three distinct (up to homotopy) simple closed curves, all mutually disjoint, on Σ;
in other words, {α,β,γ} is a 2-simplex in the curve complex of Σ. Their parabolic locus
is the set
Par(α,β,γ) = {ρ ∈X : tr2 ρ(α) = tr2 ρ(β) = tr2 ρ(γ) = 4}.
The fundamental problem with which we are concerned can now be stated. It is a
special case of the identification problem for PSL(2,C) where the matrix generators
are drawn from a subvariety of the matrix group.
Problem 1.2. If {α,β,γ} is a 2-simplex in the curve complex of Σ so that none of the
three curves are homotopically trivial in H , classify the discrete, non-elementary,
non-Fuchsian representations in the parabolic locus Par(α,β,γ).
In this note we discuss Problem 1.2 in the special case that all three curves are
sent to parabolic elements rather than the identity. Our work is directed by the
interplay between tunnel number 1 links and the system of equations defining the
parabolic locus, so in Section 1.1 we will spend some time explaining the groups
and other geometric objects that arise. We then summarise our main results in
Section 1.2, which consist of (i) a complete classification of all such groups which act
discontinuously on an open subset of the Riemann sphere and (ii) an exhibition of
examples of such groups which act ergodically on the sphere. These examples show
that some results from the two parabolic generators setting do not transfer cleanly
to the general rank two case. In particular, we previously believed that if G is the
image of a discrete representation where all three curves become parabolic then the
domain of discontinuity of G is non-empty and G is a maximal cusp group—this is
true in particular when α and β generate the group. We show that this is not true in
general: if the domain of discontinuity of G is non-empty then G is a maximal cusp
(Theorem 2.5), but there are discrete representations with lattice images where the
three curves become parabolic. In fact, these lattices do not even arise naturally from
tunnel number 1 links. This shows that the structure theory for the parabolic locus
must be more complicated than previously expected.

1.2. Summary of results. The work in the case of two parabolic generators can
be interpreted as giving a complete solution to Problem 1.2 in the case that the two
words α and β are the handle cores X and Y of H . In this case, the only discrete,
non-elementary, non-Fuchsian representations in Par(α,β,γ) are:
(P1) Infinite covolume: the maximal cusp group on the boundary of Schottky space
corresponding to pinching all three curves to parabolics, and
(P2) Finite covolume: the holonomy group of S3 \b for a two-bridge link b ⊂S3
obtained by gluing a 2-handle to H along γ, or the holonomy group of a
quotient of S3 \b by some subgroup of Aut(b).
Here, we consider the discrete, non-elementary, non-Fuchsian representations
ρ ∈Par(α,β,γ) such that ρ(α), ρ(β), and ρ(γ) are all parabolic. We first show that the
expected generalisation of (P1) holds in the full rank 2 setting.
Theorem 2.5. Suppose that ρ ∈Par(α,β,γ) is discrete, non-elementary, and non-
Fuchsian, and let Γ = ρ(π1(H )). If the domain of discontinuity Ω(Γ) ⊂P1(C) is non-
empty, and all of ρ(α), ρ(β), and ρ(γ) are parabolic, then Γ is a maximally cusped
group on the boundary of genus 2 Schottky space.
Experiments with simple examples, like those above in Section 1.1, might suggest
that the parabolic locus of a triplet of curves always consists of exactly one maximal
cusp group up to conjugacy, and at most one additional family of finite covolume groups
that come from the finite quotients of a tunnel number 1 link (generalising the two-
bridge link case). In particular, one might conjecture that every finite covolume group
is the holonomy group of an orbifold obtained by gluing a 2-handle onto the genus 2
surface along one of the three curves (so that the curve will lie in the kernel of the
representation) and then taking a quotient by the automorphism group of the result,
as in (P2) of the parabolic setting. Indeed, our conversations with other researchers
suggest that the following disproof of this conjecture is genuinely surprising.
Theorem 3.6. There exists a triplet of curves {α,β,γ} on the genus two surface such
that Par(α,β,γ) contains a finite covolume representation which sends all of α, β, and
γ to parabolic elements and which is not virtually a tunnel number one link group.
Actually, our main example is a 3-fold quotient of the Borromean rings, and is
striking because the system of curves involved has no order three symmetry—the
system arises from an embedding of the 85 knot onto the genus two surface and has
symmetry group Z/2Z⊕Z/2Z.
At the end of the paper we also include an example (Theorem 3.8) to show that there
can exist discrete groups which lie in intersections of the parabolic loci corresponding
to distinct triplets of curves. This is also a phenomenon that cannot occur in the
setting of groups generated by two parabolics (the extensions of the Keen–Series
pleating rays do not collide) but unlike Theorem 3.6 it was expected that this could
occur, even though no example seems to appear in the literature.
2. INFINITE COVOLUME GROUPS ARE MAXIMAL CUSPS
We will require three useful topological facts. We first introduce all the language
which will appear in the statements of these facts.

We may prove our first main theorem:
Theorem 2.5. Suppose that ρ ∈Par(α,β,γ) is discrete, non-elementary, non-Fuchsian,
and let Γ = ρ(π1(H )). If Ω(Γ) ̸= ;, and all of ρ(α), ρ(β), and ρ(γ) are parabolic, then Γ
is a maximally cusped group on the boundary of genus 2 Schottky space.
Proof. We will split into cases. Set M = H3/Γ.
(1) Γ has a rank 2 parabolic subgroup. Since Ω(Γ) ̸= ;, K has at least 2 ends so is
not a handlebody. By Lemma 2.4 we see that it has exactly 2 ends. Let g be
the genus of the end which is not the known torus end. Then by Lemma 2.2 we
know −χ(∂M) = 2g−2 < 2. This means g = 0 or g = 1. Both of these situations
would contradict Ω(Γ) ̸= ;.
(2) We can therefore assume that Γ has only rank 1 parabolic subgroups and
split into two subcases depending on whether or not the three parabolics ρ(α),
ρ(β), and ρ(γ) lie in 3 distinct maximal parabolic subgroups or not.
(a) The three parabolics lie in distinct subgroups. By Lemma 2.3, there is no
elliptic locus. Hence we are in the setting of Theorem III of Keen, Maskit,
and Series [18] and Γ is a maximally cusped group.
(b) The three parabolics do not lie in distinct subgroups. Without loss of
generality, suppose that ρ(α) and ρ(β) lie in the same rank 1 parabolic
subgroup, say generated by ρ(η) where η ∈F{X,Y }. Since α and β are
simple closed curves, they define primitive elements of H1(Σ) (i.e. have
coprime coordinates in the usual basis). Since Ab(F{X,Y }) = H1(H ) is a
submodule of H1(Σ), the images [α] and [β] are also primitive in H1(H ).
Since they are linearly independent, they form a basis. But the map
Ab(F{X,Y }) →Ab(Γ) sends these two linearly independent elements into
the same rank one subspace Z[ρ(η)]. Hence r(AbΓ) < r(AbF{X,Y }) = 2,
so there is a strict inequality in Lemma 2.2: −χ(∂K) < 2. But since
Ω(Γ) ̸= ;, K has at least one boundary component of genus at least 2 and
so −χ(∂K) ≥2, giving a contradiction.

Theorem 3.8. The group G defined by the trace coordinates (4) is the holonomy group
of a tunnel number 1 link group.
Proof. One first sees that the relation
(6)
(X yyX X X)(yyX y) = 1
holds in G. Here yyX y is a conjugate of β, and X yyX X X is a parabolic in G which
is distinct from any of the three parabolics α,β,γ even up to conjugacy and taking
inverses. These new parabolic words yyX y and X yyX X X and the relator word (6)
represent a triplet of distinct simple closed curves on the genus two surface, as shown
in Figure 10. Gluing a two-handle to the handlebody H along the word (6) produces
a manifold M which is the complement of a tunnel number 1 link in some closed
3-manifold, and by the Siefert–van Kampen theorem we see that
(7)
π1(M) = 〈X,Y : X yyX X X yyX y = 1〉.
We can identify M as the manifold m003(0,0) in the SNAPPY census of orientable
cusped 3-manifolds [5, 6, 12, 17]: the census manifold has fundamental group 〈A,B :
abAAbabbb〉which agrees with (7) after the change of variables A = X and B =
Y x (so X = A, Y = BA, XY = ABA), so the two are isometric by Mostow–Prasad
rigidity [24, Theorem 3.32]. Further, the holonomy representation of the census
manifold produced by SNAPPY satisfies tr A = −0.5−0.8660i, trBA = 0.5−0.8660i,
and tr ABA = −2.5+0.8660i, agreeing with (4). Since rank two subgroups of PSL(2,C)
are determined by the set of traces of two generators and their product, this shows
that G is conjugate to the census manifold holonomy group, completing the proof of
the theorem.
Remark 3.9. The manifold m003(0,0) is a knot complement in a lens space, see [20,
Example 4.4].
