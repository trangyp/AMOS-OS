On Two Algebraic Realizations of Schubert Calculus
Andr´e Contiero, Letterio Gatto, Parham Salehyan

Abstract
Schubert calculus on complex Grassmannians can be played by means of differential operators
acting on Schur polynomials or Vertex Operators acting on exterior algebras. In this paper we
develop this point of view systematically and complement it with a parallel exterior-algebra
formalism, leading to what we call the bosonic and the fermionic Schubert calculi. The two alluded
realizations are related by (a finite type version of) the boson–fermion correspondence, thereby
providing a unified framework connecting Schubert calculus and integrals on the Grassmannian,
symmetric functions, exterior algebras and the representation theory of symmetric groups.

Inspired by previous contributions to the subject within the same spirit, such as in [5, 12, 8, 20,
11], and [6] in the more general framework of semi-algebras, the purpose of this paper is to revisit
Schubert calculus from two complementary viewpoints, which we shall call the bosonic Schubert
calculus and the fermionic Schubert calculus. The terminology is motivated by the fact that the ring B
carries the standard bosonic action of a Weyl algebra through creation and annihilation operators,
whereas the exterior algebra carries the corresponding fermionic action through Schubert deriva-
tions (as in [9]). Although the two formalisms are ultimately equivalent, they emphasize different
aspects of the theory.
The bosonic Schubert calculus is developed on the polynomial ring B = Q[x1, x2, . . .], (a repre-
sentation space for many relevant infinite dimensional Lie algebras), endowed with its Schur basis
{Sλ(x)}λ∈P., parametrized by the set P of all of the integer partitions . We regard B as a universal
receptacle containing the homologies of all Grassmannians at once. More precisely, for each pair
(r, n) there is a natural projection πr,n : B −→H∗(G(r, n), Q), sending the Schur polynomial Sλ(x)
to the corresponding Schubert cycle.
A key role throughout the paper is played by a simple (and known!) determinantal identity,
stated in Lemma 2.5. It provides a common source for the Giambelli- and Jacobi–Trudi-type formu-
las appearing in the paper and yields Pieri-type identities in a uniform way. In this sense, several
classical formulas of Schubert calculus and symmetric function theory arise from one elementary
mechanism, in our opinion not very exploited in literature.

2.2 One denotes by Pr the sub-monoid of P whose elements are the partitions of length at most r:

2.3 If λ ∈Pr, its Young diagram is an array of r left-aligned rows of boxes, where the i-th row
contains λi boxes. A Young tableau of shape λ is a filling of the boxes of the Young diagram with
integers. It is called a standard Young tableau if the entries are the integers 1, 2, . . . , |λ|, each appear-
ing exactly once, and increasing along each row (from left to right) and along each column (from
top to bottom). The important combinatorial and representation theoretical interpretation of the
number of Young tableaux of shape λ is that it coincides with the dimension of the Specht module
Sλ, which is the irreducible representation of the symmetric group on d = |λ| elements associated
to the partition λ (see [2]). Such a dimension can be computed by using the celebrated hook lenght
fromula by Frame-Robinson and Thrall [1]. We will look at the dimension of Sλ rather as the degree
ωλ of the Schubert variety in a Grassmannian G(r, n) (r ≥ℓ(λ)) of dimension |λ| associated to the
partition λ.

4.1 Doing Schubert Calculus by means of partial derivatives acting on B amounts to identify
the latter with the universal container of all the homologies of the Grassmannians at once. Then
