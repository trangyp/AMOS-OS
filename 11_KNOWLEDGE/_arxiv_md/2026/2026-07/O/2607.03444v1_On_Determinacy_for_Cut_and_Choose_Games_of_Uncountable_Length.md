1. Introduction
Ulam introduced the cut and choose game in order to explore the connections
between abstract set theory and infinite constructions in physical theories [Ula64].
The game starts with a set X and two players: cut and choose in modern termi-
nology. First, cut divides X into two disjoint sets, and then choose selects one,
and then cut divides the chosen set. This goes on for countably many steps, and
in the end, if the sets that choose has selected have an intersection containing at
least two points, then choose wins; otherwise cut wins.1 The question that Ulam
raised is whether choose might have a winning strategy. Mycielski and Steinhaus
had introduced the axiom of determinacy two years beforehand [MS62], so it was
natural to consider the existence of winning strategies. The question was intended
as an example of a problem that is, “rather easy to state” while embodying “the
core properties of abstract sets” [Ula64, page 347]. Ulam speculated that the an-
swers to such questions may be independent, citing the work of Paul Cohen which
at the time was yet unpublished.
The context for this paper is found in work of Jech, who studied the cut and
choose game and some of its variants for complete Boolean algebras [Jec84] (see
also [Jec78]). He found an interplay between winning strategies and distributivity
properties of the Boolean algebras and formulated the basic results, for example
showing that a complete Boolean algebra adds reals if and only if cut has a winning
strategy in the cut and choose game of length ω.
He asked whether there is a
Boolean algebra for which the cut and choose game of length ω is undetermined,
and was answered by Zapletal [Zap95], who obtained a number of other crucial
results.

Zapletal’s result on the existence of an undetermined game left the natural ques-
tion for cut and choose games of uncountable length. Questions of this form arise
in other contexts, including the Mycielski games from which the axiom of determi-
nacy is defined. Typically, the questions are more tractable for games of countable
length, and less tractable for games of uncountable length (see [Nee08]). In Sec-
tion 2, we obtain a model in which a version of the cut and choose game of length
λ but without a λ’th round is determined of all successors λ. This was originally
motivated by a question of Zapletal [Zap95, Question 2], the answer to which is
implied by the theorem.
In Section 3, we consider variants of the cut and choose game of length λ which
end with a final round. During the 2000’s, Dobrinen obtained extensive results
giving hypotheses that imply that uncountable length cut and choose games can
be undetermined [Dob03, Dob07, CD07, Dob08]. In Section 3, we obtain results
from weaker hypotheses on models in which uncountable cut and choose games are

2.1. Two-Parameter Strong Distributivity and the Existence of Winning
Strategies. For this section we want to establish an approach for studying the
existence or non-existence of winning strategies in the cut and choose game.
For purposes of discussion and later use, we recall the definition of strategic
closure for posets (see [Cum10, Section 6]).
Definition 2.1. Fix a poset P. We define the Banach-Mazur game of length λ on
P.
The game is a game of perfect information between two players. The play of the
game is a decreasing sequence of conditions ⟨pξ | ξ < η⟩in which Player II chooses
conditions pξ at limits ξ as well as even successor ordinals (successors of the form
ξ + n where ξ is a limit and n is even), and Player I chooses conditions at odd
successor ordinals. Player II wins a play ⟨pξ | ξ < η⟩of length η if it is possible for
the players to make a move at every step of the play, i.e. there is a lower bound of
the sequence ⟨pξ | ξ < ξ′⟩for all ξ′ < η.
We say that P is η-weakly strategically closed, or simply that P is η-strategically
closed, if Player II has a winning strategy for all games of length η. In other words,
P is η-strategically closed if the there is a function σ : <ηP →P such that for all
ξ < η, if pξ = σ(⟨pζ | ζ < ξ⟩) for all ξ < η such that ξ is either a limit or an even
successor, then ⟨pζ | ζ < ξ⟩has a lower bound.
The notion of strong distributivity was introduced by Jakob [Jak25, Definition
3.1], who observed that it characterizes existence of a winning strategy for the

Jakob noted that this characterization can be generalized and found a number
of applications (see also [Jak26]). We define the natural two- and three-parameter
versions of strong distributivity.
