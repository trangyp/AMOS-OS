OAM-Enabled Holographic MIMO Communications
with Stacked Intelligent Metasurfaces
G. Torcolacci, Member, IEEE, and D. Dardari, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—This study investigates orbital angular momen-
tum (OAM)-based holographic multiple-input multiple-output
(HMIMO) links enabled by stacked intelligent metasurfaces
(SIM) in the radiative near-field. By using multilayer pro-
grammable metasurfaces at both link ends, SIMs enable analog
electromagnetic domain wave processing for low-complexity and
energy-efficient flexible wavefront synthesis. We analyzed OAM
mode generation and reception with SIM-based transceivers and
quantified their ability to synthesize near-orthogonal modes in
practical discrete HMIMO architectures. We further developed
a correlation-driven optimization algorithm that maximizes re-
construction accuracy of OAM beams. Numerical evaluations
revealed a fundamental decoupling between the required antenna
aperture, which limits the supported mode orders, and the SIM
layer depth, which governs crosstalk suppression. The results
confirm that properly dimensioned SIM architectures provide
robust near-field spatial multiplexing, nearly balanced per-mode
capacities, and graceful degradation across link distances without
requiring continuous phase re-optimization, thereby supporting
scalable and low-overhead HMIMO communications.

degrees of freedom (DOF), beam focusing
I. INTRODUCTION
T
HE growing demand for high-throughput, low-latency
wireless services is pushing current radio networks to-
ward their physical limits, a trend expected to intensify with
6th Generation (6G) systems, which will rely on higher carrier
frequencies and larger, denser antenna apertures. Under these
conditions, transmitter-receiver interactions often occur in the
radiating near-field, where the plane-wave approximation no
longer holds, and spherical wave propagation must be ex-
plicitly modeled. In this context, holographic multiple-input
multiple-output (HMIMO) has emerged as a framework for
shaping emitted and received electromagnetic (EM) fields to
fully exploit the channel degrees of freedom (DOF). Prior
studies have demonstrated substantial capacity gains even
under line-of-sight (LOS) propagation [1]. However, practi-
cal HMIMO hardware technologies are largely underdevel-
oped. Existing implementations have explored large intelli-
gent surfaces (LISs) [2], [3], dynamic metasurface anten-
nas (DMAs) [4], and extremely large-scale multiple-input
multiple-output (XL-MIMO) arrays [5] for communication
and imaging/sensing, highlighting the potential of HMIMO for
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) applications.
However, fully digital HMIMO transceivers entail pro-
hibitive hardware complexity and power consumption, ex-
acerbating the digital bottleneck. The electromagnetic signal
processing (ESP) paradigm [6] offers an alternative path by
shifting signal processing operations from the digital baseband
to the EM domain, thereby enabling direct wave manipulation
during propagation, while drastically reducing the number of
active components and energy-hungry radio-frequency (RF)
chains. Within this framework, stacked intelligent metasur-
faces (SIMs) have emerged as a compelling hardware platform
to realize ESP, as their cascaded programmable metasurface
layers can synthesize complex wavefront transformations en-
tirely in the wave domain.
Recently, SIMs have attracted significant attention owing
to their broad applicability and promising wave-domain pro-
cessing capabilities [7]–[9]. A typical SIM includes multiple
stacked metasurface layers, each composed of hundreds of re-
configurable cells (meta-atoms), controlled by programmable
electronics (e.g., field programmable gate array (FPGA)). Each
meta-atom re-radiates toward subsequent layers following the
Huygens-Fresnel principle, enabling dense EM-domain inter-
action. As a result, SIMs can emit and receive information-
bearing EM fields over almost the entire aperture, making
them strong candidates for practical HMIMO implementation.
Compared to alternative HMIMO technologies, SIMs offer
higher spatial-domain gain [10], finer waveform control [11],
[12], and improved spatial multiplexing [11], [13]. Unlike
conventional reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs), which
are typically deployed in the environment to improve cover-
age, SIMs are integrated directly into transmitting (TX) and
receiving (RX) devices. This integration can improve the link
performance while reducing hardware complexity by limiting
the number of active RF chains and digital-to-analog converter
(DAC)/analog-to-digital converter (ADC) units [14], [15].
Moreover, native wave-domain processing enables ultrafast,
massive parallel operations, which are attractive for massive
communications, sensing, and physical-layer security [9], [16],
[17].
In parallel, increasing attention has been devoted to the
orbital angular momentum (OAM) modes for HMIMO trans-
missions [18]–[21]. OAM bases provide a low-complexity,
structured mode set capable of supporting near-field LOS
spatial multiplexing, with limited performance loss relative to
optimal designs based on circular prolate spheroidal functions
(CPSFs). Specifically, whereas CPSF-based optimal designs
mandate computationally intensive eigenfunction solutions and
accurate transmitter channel state information (CSI), OAM
schemes significantly simplify the transceiver architecture by
eliminating the requirement for transmitter-side CSI while
retaining a significant number of orthogonal spatial channels
[22]. However, conventional OAM generation often relies on
hardware-specific devices, such as spiral phase plates (SPPs)
and uniform circular arrays (UCAs), which are typically
static, costly, or weakly reconfigurable [23]. The multilayer

A. State of the Art
Research on SIMs has rapidly evolved across theories,
modeling, and optimization. Surveys such as [8] have high-
lighted their potential for wave-domain processing in sensing,
communications, and computing. The foundational work in
[7] established SIM-enabled HMIMO communications via
wave-domain precoding/combining, showing notable capac-
ity gains over conventional multiple-input multiple-output
(MIMO) transmissions. Low-complexity architectures have
also been proposed, such as meta-fiber designs, as in [24].
Nevertheless, the optimization of SIM configurations re-
mains challenging owing to strong non-convexity. Alternating
normalized mean squared error (NMSE)-based methods [7]
enable effective channel fitting but decouple amplitude and
phase, potentially disrupting the phase correlations required
by structured modes such as OAM. For large-scale systems
with statistical CSI, joint transmit-covariance and SIM-phase
optimization have been proposed in [25], whereas closed-
form alternating updates for meta-fiber architectures reduce
complexity [24]. Recently, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven
methods have emerged as an alternative. Deep reinforce-
ment learning has been applied to joint SIM phase-shift and
power allocation optimization in multiuser (MU)-multiple-
input single-output (MISO) systems [13], and diffractive
neural processing enables flexible amplitude-phase control
[26]. However, despite achieving significant gains in spectral
efficiency, energy efficiency, or mean squared error (MSE)
reduction, both conventional and AI-driven approaches remain
strictly anchored to standard performance metrics. Crucially,
they do not explicitly enforce the helical phase structure
required by OAM, thereby failing to preserve the modal profile
and spatial orthogonality that are essential for OAM-based
communications.
Within this context, OAM integration into HMIMO- and
SIM-based transmissions remains limited. Optical implemen-
tations have demonstrated OAM multiplexing capabilities via
metasurfaces [27], but their extension to the RF domain is
hindered by discretization effects that can degrade modes’
orthogonality. At RF, continuous-aperture analyses have es-
tablished the potential of near-field OAM bases and hybrid
Walsh-OAM modes [21], [22], but do not address discrete SIM
implementations and their optimization.
B. Motivation and Contributions
The above overview highlights that existing SIM optimiza-
tion frameworks, although effective for channel fitting or rate
maximization, do not explicitly address the preservation of
helical phase continuity and modal orthogonality and are thus
not directly suited for OAM-based systems. In particular,
current methods typically decouple the amplitude and phase
under NMSE- or rate-based objectives [7], [25], failing to
preserve the helical phase correlations required for OAM
orthogonality. Moreover, existing frameworks lack dedicated
metrics to quantify mode fidelity with respect to continuous-
space references and assess orthogonality under discrete im-
plementations. Finally, both gradient-based and learning-based
approaches [7], [13] remain sensitive to initialization and local
minima in highly non-convex phase landscapes, and do not
provide a unified decoupled transmit/receive design strategy.
To address these limitations, we propose a correlation-driven
optimization framework for OAM-enabled SIM systems. The
main contributions of this study are as follows:
• We introduce a correlation-based objective that align
generated EM fields with ideal continuous-space OAM
references. Unlike NMSE-based formulations, it enforces
amplitude-weighted phase consistency and preserves the
helical structure via complex-domain similarity, thereby
improving robustness to low-amplitude regions. We fur-
ther define dedicated metrics for the OAM mode fidelity
and inter-mode orthogonality in discrete SIM architec-
tures.
• We develop a robust decoupled transmit/receive optimiza-
tion strategy that combines multi-start gradient descent,
adaptive step sizing, and layer-wise updates. The algo-
rithm alternates exploration and refinement to mitigate
local minima effects and enables end-to-end mode align-
ment without requiring full CSI. Both fully optimized
and hybrid configurations (i.e., one side ideal, one opti-
mized) are considered to highlight the TX and RX side
asymmetries induced by discretization.
• We derive practical design rules from extensive simula-
tions, linking metasurfaces’ meta-atom density, number
of layers, and link geometry to OAM mode fidelity and
throughput. The results show that denser apertures signifi-
cantly improve the per-mode correlation, additional layers
enhance robustness to discretization but with diminish-
ing returns beyond moderate depth, and transmit/receive
asymmetries critically impact which side should be opti-
mized in practice.

II. OAM-BASED HMIMO COMMUNICATIONS
From a physical standpoint, EM waves carrying non-zero
OAM exhibit helical wavefronts twisted around propagation
axis1. The helical structure is mathematically described by
the exponential phase factor eȷℓφ, where ℓ∈Z denotes
the topological charge and φ the transverse azimuthal angle
defining the angular position on a plane orthogonal to the
propagation direction. Each vortex is characterized by ℓ, with
|ℓ| indicating the number of phase twists per wavelength and
the sign determining the twist chirality. The resulting vortex
states associated with a given ℓdefine the OAM modes.
Owing to their inherent orthogonality, OAM modes enable
spatial multiplexing, allowing multiple data streams to be
simultaneously conveyed through distinct helical beams.

III. SIM-BASED HMIMO SYSTEM
The framework above is specialized for a specific HMIMO
antenna realization based on SIMs, which provides a practical
implementation for manipulating EM waves in the EM wave
domain. This enables the generation and shaping of structured
wavefronts, including OAM modes, directly over a wireless
channel. Based on this perspective, the following section
introduces the detailed SIM-aided transceiver model for the
considered LOS HMIMO system.
A. System Model
Consider the SIM-aided HMIMO system shown in Fig. 2,
where two identical SIM-based transceivers operate in LOS
and paraxial configuration. The transmit and receive units are
denoted by TX-SIM and RX-SIM, respectively. The TX-SIM
is illuminated by a uniform linear array (ULA) with S antenna
elements, whereas the RX-SIM is followed by a receiving
ULA with S elements. The same S denotes the number of
parallel data streams, that is, the orthogonal channels created at
the EM level.2 Let L and K denote the number of metasurface

The correlation-based objectives in (22) and (26) depart
from the conventional NMSE-based formulations by directly
maximizing the normalized complex correlation [33]. Unlike
NMSE, this criterion enables amplitude-weighted phase align-
ment, naturally preserving the helical phase structure of the
OAM modes even in the presence of amplitude nulls and
phase singularities. In our preliminary evaluations, replacing
the proposed objective with an NMSE criterion consistently
led to poor convergence, yielding low-signal-to-interference
plus noise ratio (SINR) solutions with reduced inter-mode
orthogonality and phase distributions that no longer matched
the desired OAM profiles. This behavior is consistent with
the known limitations of NMSE-based objectives for phase-
sensitive wavefront synthesis [7].
The resulting optimization problems are highly non-convex.
To address this, we adopt a multi-start gradient-based strategy
using adaptive moment estimation (Adam), followed by a
refinement stage to improve convergence stability and mitigate
poor local minima, thus alternating exploration and fine-
tuning. Details are presented in Algorithm 1. Notably, because
PT and PR share the same structural form and are solved
independently, the proposed framework enables decoupled
TX/RX optimization without requiring explicit CSI exchange
or joint transceiver coordination.
Particularly, Nruns independent phase configurations Θ are
drawn uniformly over [0, 2π) (multi-start initialization). For
each run, Nscr gradient-based iterations are performed. At
each iteration, a forward pass constructs the SIM configuration
matrix P by cascading the intra-layer propagation matrices
{W(l)} and phase-shift operators {Φ(l)}, and computes the
effective precoder AT = PHT. The loss LT is then evaluated
using the normalized correlation in (22).
The gradients with respect to the phase variables {θ(l)}
are computed via backpropagation using Wirtinger calculus,
accounting for the cascaded structure of the metasurface
layers. The resulting gradients are used to update the phases
through the Adam optimizer, followed by projection onto the
[0, 2π) range.
After the screening phase, the best initialization is selected
and refined through Nphases additional phase optimization
stages. Each stage consists of Nref iterations with a progres-
sively decreasing learning rate following a cosine annealing
schedule [34], improving the convergence toward a local
minimum. Early stopping is applied based on the relative loss
improvement.
Lastly, the optimization algorithm returns the set of phase
shifts Θ⋆, the corresponding TX-SIM transformation P⋆,
and the achieved loss L⋆. As a consequence, the resulting
transmitter-side processing is described by the effective wave-

E. Impact of Link Distance
Fig. 8 illustrates the sum-rate Γ versus the normalized
HMIMO link distance dlink/λ for L = {3, 9, 15}. In this case,
both the TX and RX SIMs are optimized to support OAM-
based spatial multiplexing at a distance dlink = dLR.
Three distinct operating regimes are identified in the figure.
For dlink ≤dSR, the near-field HMIMO channel supports a
large number of orthogonal communication modes, thereby
enabling full multiplexing with well-coupled OAM modes;
deeper SIM architectures yield substantially higher capac-
ity owing to superior inter-mode interference suppression.
In the transition region (dSR ≤dlink ≤dLR), Γ degrades
monotonically as the channel rank decreases with increasing
distance, with the steepest drop observed for large L due to the
higher multiplexing gain being progressively lost. Beyond dLR,
the LOS channel rank approaches unity, propagation-induced
constraints dominate, and all curves converge toward a DOF-
limited floor.
It is worth noting that in all the considered configurations,
the SIM phases are optimized at dlink = dLR and remained
fixed across the entire distance range. The graceful degradation
observed in Fig. 8 thus confirms that the proposed correlation-
driven design is robust to link-distance mismatch: once opti-
mized for a target operating point, the SIM configuration does
not require distance-specific re-optimization. This robustness
stems from the inter-modal crosstalk suppression capability of
deeper stacks, which remains effective even as the channel
rank decreases with distance.

Fig. 9 illustrates the convergence profile of the transmit-

larger L consistently achieves a lower final loss and faster con-
vergence, indicating a more favorable optimization landscape
attributable to the increased DOF and enhanced EM wavefront
shaping flexibility of the deeper stacks. These results confirm
that the proposed multi-start Adam strategy yields a reliable
convergence across all considered configurations.

Numerical results reveal a clear decoupling between the
SIM aperture, which limits the maximum supportable mode
order via spatial sampling, and the layer depth, which mainly
governs inter-modal crosstalk suppression with rapidly di-
minishing returns beyond moderate stack sizes. Specifically,
deploying denser apertures significantly sharpens the per-
mode correlation, whereas expanding the layer count primarily
enhances the system robustness against phase discretization
constraints.
Transmit/receive asymmetries significantly affect the perfor-
mance, with radiated-field imperfections causing substantially
stronger throughput loss than non-ideal reception, highlighting
the critical role of transmit-side mode synthesis. Overall, SIM-
based HMIMO transceivers enable efficient near-field spatial
multiplexing and exhibit graceful distance-dependent degrada-
tion without requiring distance-specific SIM re-optimization.
