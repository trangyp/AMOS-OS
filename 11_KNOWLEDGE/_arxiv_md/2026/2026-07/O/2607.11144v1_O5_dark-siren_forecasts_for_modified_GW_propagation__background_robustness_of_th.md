Abstract. Binary black hole mergers without electromagnetic counterparts are expected to
dominate O5 gravitational-wave catalogs. Recent CHIMERA 2.0 forecasts typically fix Ωm
to a CMB-informed value and use spectroscopic hosts when available, but the corresponding
sensitivity of the Ξ posterior has not been assessed for pure dark sirens on the public O5
mock catalog. We analyze 300 O5-sensitivity mock events without galaxy catalogs, varying
Ωm over [0.20, 0.35] (including Planck 0.315), and compare fixed-background inference with
joint (H0, Ωm, Ξ) inference.
The marginalized Ξ posterior is 0.9783 ± 0.3548 and shows
no change across this interval.
Only GW luminosity distances enter the analysis, so the
likelihood constrains Ξ DEM
L (H0, Ωm); when Ωm is changed, H0 shifts to compensate and
the Ξ marginal remains unchanged.
Joint inference gives Ξjoint = 0.9550 ± 0.3710, with
|ρ| ≲0.05 for the Ωm–Ξ and H0–Ξ pairs, whereas ρH0Ωm ≃−0.4 and the H0 median moves
by ≃4.2 km s−1 Mpc−1 over the adopted Ωm range. Galaxy-catalog analyses on the same
events at fixed Ωm = 0.3 reach ∼7.5% precision on Ξ, compared with ±36.3% here; the larger
uncertainty is driven mainly by missing host redshifts. Sub-percent Ξ tests will therefore still
require measured redshifts even if dark sirens dominate the detection rate.

Introduction
Late-time and early-time measurements of the Hubble constant differ by several σ [1, 2].
Gravitational-wave (GW) standard sirens offer an independent distance ladder [3, 4]. The
only unambiguous bright-siren event to date, GW170817, gave H0 = 70.0+12.0
−8.0 km s−1 Mpc−1
[5], and the O5 observing run is expected to yield hundreds of compact-binary detections [6].
GW distances also permit model-independent tests of General Relativity (GR) through
the phenomenological relation DGW
L

L
≡Ξ [7]. With CHIMERA 2.0 [8, 9], 300 O5-like
events and spectroscopic galaxy catalogs can constrain Ξ to ∼7.5% when Ωm is held fixed.
Most O5 events are expected to be dark sirens—mergers without identified electro-
magnetic counterparts—particularly among binary black holes (BBHs) [10, 11]. Cosmology
and modified GW propagation in this limit have been studied in several forecasts (Table 1).
Finke et al. [11] and Leyde et al. [12] used mock data from LIGO/Virgo and future networks
to forecast constraints on phenomenological modified-gravity parameters. Mancarella et al.
[13] extended the analysis to GWTC-3. Tagliazucchi et al. [9] forecast O5 performance with
CHIMERA 2.0 and galaxy catalogs at a single fixed Ωm = 0.3. That analysis does not test
whether other CMB-allowed Ωm values would shift the Ξ posterior, nor does it address the
dominant O5 population of BBH dark sirens without host redshifts.

In this paper we use the same public CHIMERA 2.0 O5 mock catalog as [9], but with-
out galaxy catalogs, varying Ωm over [0.20, 0.35] to ask a question their fixed-background
forecast leaves open: does adopting a single CMB-informed Ωm bias Ξ when host redshifts
are unavailable? We map the response of the Ξ posterior, compare inference at each adopted
Ωm with joint (H0, Ωm, Ξ) inference, and quantify on the same events how much Ξ precision
is lost relative to the galaxy-catalog analysis of [9]. The invariance of Ξ under background
variation is itself a forecast result: it validates fixed-Ωm pipelines for modified-gravity moni-
toring at O5 sensitivity and separates that systematic from the far larger degradation caused
by missing host redshifts.

2.4
Grid-based inference
Following [9], who used MCMC with Ωm fixed at 0.3, we use a parameter grid that can be
evaluated at multiple background inputs. A single inference with fixed Ωm, as in [9], does
not reveal how the Ξ posterior responds when the assumed Ωm is changed. We therefore
vary Ωm over the CMB-allowed range and, at each adopted value, evaluate (H0, Ξ) on a
two-dimensional grid.
For each fixed Ωm ∈{0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.315, 0.35}, we evaluate the likelihood on a
24×24 uniform grid with H0 ∈[55, 85] km s−1 Mpc−1 and Ξ ∈[0.5, 1.5]. Posterior summaries
are obtained with flat priors on the inferred parameters and include the CHIMERA selection-
function normalization. One-dimensional H0 constraints are derived by marginalizing over Ξ
on the same grid. For the event-number scaling test, we use subsets of 30, 50, 100, 200, and
300 events with Ωm = 0.25.

3.5
How Ξ responds to variations in Ωm
Table 4 reports five independent (H0, Ξ) analyses, one at each adopted Ωm, and constitutes
the central robustness test of this work. The repeated Ξ entries are the forecast outcome,
not an absence of new physics: they show quantitatively that the fixed-Ωm = 0.3 strategy
of [9] can be extended across CMB-allowed backgrounds without shifting the Ξ constraint,
while H0 absorbs the change.
Table 4 lists the main numerical result. For pure dark sirens, the data constrain the
combination Ξ DEM
L (H0, Ωm): increasing Ωm lowers DEM
L
at fixed H0, and the posterior
compensates by shifting the marginalized H0 median downward by ≃4.2 km s−1 Mpc−1 from
Ωm = 0.20 to 0.35 (Eq. (3.2)), preserving the distance scale and hence the marginalized Ξ
distribution. Because |ρH0Ξ| < 0.15 and the Ξ posterior is broad (±36.3%), this compensation
is absorbed almost entirely by H0. Full-precision recomputation finds no variation in the Ξ
median, credible-interval width, or ∆Ξ = −0.0217 offset from GR across [0.20, 0.35]; the
repeated table entries are therefore a direct consequence of the H0–Ωm distance degeneracy,
in agreement with Eq. (2.4).

4
Discussion
Our H0 precision at 300 events agrees with earlier O5 dark-siren forecasts [11, 12, 16], as
does the broad Ξ posterior found without host redshifts [11–13]. The additional step taken
here is to vary Ωm over [0.20, 0.35] in the CHIMERA 2.0 O5 mocks while keeping the
pure dark-siren setup. Across that interval we find Ξ = 0.9783 ± 0.3548, with H0 shifting
by up to ∼4.2 km s−1 Mpc−1. The same behavior appears in the joint (H0, Ωm, Ξ) analysis
(Table 2) and in the grid/MCMC comparison at Ωm = 0.25 (Table 3), and follows the familiar
dark-siren distance degeneracy discussed in Secs. 3.3 and 3.5.
For O5 pipeline design, a CMB-informed fixed Ωm is unlikely to add a further Ξ system-
atic at the precision reached here. The comparison in Table 5 shows that the main penalty

relative to galaxy-catalog forecasts on the same catalog is the absence of measured host red-
shifts. Dark sirens alone are unlikely to reach percent-level Ξ sensitivity, and host follow-up
remains necessary for tighter modified-gravity tests [7, 9, 17].
The analysis has several limitations. Population hyperparameters are fixed to the in-
jected values, and population-model uncertainties can bias cosmological inference at levels
comparable to the statistical errors of small samples [18, 19]. Varying γ, κ, or the mass
model would be a natural extension. The mock catalog contains only BBHs; adding neutron-
star binaries would change both the counterpart fraction and the redshift distribution. We
kept the dark-energy equation of state and Ξ(z) models fixed [20, 21]. The extrapolation to
N ≃1.4 × 104 events reaches Planck-like H0 precision only statistically; the Ωm systematic
remains important for dark-siren H0. Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer may detect
∼105 events per year [22]. At that event rate, the H0–Ωm degeneracy found here will domi-
nate the H0 error budget, whereas percent-level Ξ measurements will require both dark sirens
and deep galaxy catalogs.
5
Conclusions
We analyzed 300 pure dark sirens in the public CHIMERA 2.0 O5 mock catalog with Ωm
varied between 0.20 and 0.35. Without host redshifts, the marginalized Ξ posterior is 0.9783±
0.3548 (±36.3%) and is independent of the adopted background over that interval (Table 4).
The data constrain Ξ DEM
L (H0, Ωm), so a change in Ωm is absorbed by H0 (Eq. (3.2)) and
the Ξ marginal is unchanged. Joint (H0, Ωm, Ξ) inference gives Ξjoint = 0.9550 ± 0.3710
and |ρΩmΞ| ≲0.05. H0 remains strongly background dependent (67.9 ± 3.1 km s−1 Mpc−1 at
Ωm = 0.25, 66.7±3.4 km s−1 Mpc−1 in the joint fit, and a median shift of ≃4.2 km s−1 Mpc−1
from Ωm = 0.20 to 0.35) and should always be reported with the assumed or inferred Ωm. On
the same events, the main obstacle to percent-level Ξ constraints is the lack of host redshifts
(Table 5: ±36.3% versus ∼7.5% with galaxy catalogs), not the choice of Ωm within the
CMB-allowed range.
