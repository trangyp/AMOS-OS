Abstract
A number of the form as+adϵ, where as, ad ∈Cn×n and ϵ is an infinitesimal unit
satisfying ϵ2 = 0, is called a dual number. A matrix with dual number entries is
known as dual matrix. The objective of this article is to derive a polar decomposition
of dual matrices and to study the existence and uniqueness conditions. To illustrate
these results, some examples are constructed.
Keywords: Dual complex numbers; Dual matrix ; Unitary decomposition; Polar de-
composition.

Introduction
Dual numbers were first introduced by [1] as a part of his extensive research on clifford
algebras. They take the form a = as + adϵ where as and ad are real or complex numbers
and ϵ is the infinitesimal unit satisfying ϵ2 = 0. Accordingly, we call A = As + Adϵ with
As and As ∈Cm×n a dual complex matrix. The set of this kind of matrices is denoted as
DCm×n. Dual numbers and dual matrices have found applications in various fields such as
robotics [2,3] , classical mechanics [1,4] and computer vision [5]. Moreover they are also
linked with screw theory [6]. For decompositions of dual matrices, some authors discussed
singular value decomposition [7,8], QR decomposition [9,10], UTV decomposition [11] and
rank decomposition [12]. In this paper, we focus on Polar decomposition and Symmetric
unitary polar decomposition of dual matrices.
Motivated by [13] we introduce the factorization of a square(rectangular) dual complex
matrix A of the form
A = PU . . . . . . (1) and
A = SU . . . . . . (2)
where P is positive semi definite, S is complex symmetric and U is unitary. We call
these factorizations as Dual Polar Decomposition and Dual Symmetric Unitary Polar
Decomposition (DSUPD) respectively.
It is shown that the DSUPD exists for every
matrix A and it always non unique. Even the dual symmetric factor S on be chosen
in infinitely many ways. where as dual polar decomposition exists for every matrix A
and it is unique if A is nonsingular. i.e the matrix P is uniquely determined as the dual
Hermitian positive semidefinite square root of AA∗and U is uniquely determined by (1)
for a nonsingular A.
If A is singular, then A admits infinitely many representations of form (1). The dual
polar decomposition and DSUPD can, in principle, be extended to rectangular matrices.
In this article, we present the formulation of polar decomposition for both square and
rectangular matrices. However, for the development and analysis of DSUPD, we restrict
our attention to the case of square matrices. More precisely, factorization (1) should be
termed as left dual polar decomposition because A can be factored as

In this section, we first briefly explain some of the terminologies used in this article.
The set of real numbers, complex numbers, dual numbers, and dual complex numbers
are denoted by R, C, D, DC. A dual number is called appreciable if its standard part is
nonzero; otherwise, it is called infinitesimal. Suppose we have two dual complex numbers
a = as + adϵ and b = bs + bdϵ. Then their sum is a + b = (as + bs) + (ad + bd) ϵ, and
their product is ab = asbs + (asbd + adbs) ϵ. The multiplication of dual complex numbers
is commutative. We recall the total order ’ ≤′ ’over D defined by Qi et al. [14]. Suppose
that a = as + adϵ, b = bs + bdϵ ∈D. Then,
(i) a = b if as = bs and ad = bd;
(ii) a < b if as < bs, or as = bs and ad < bd;
(iii) a ≤b if as < bs, or as = bs and ad ≤bd.

It is classical that an n × n real symmetric or complex Hermitian matrix has n real
eigenvalues, and this matrix is positive semidefinite (or definite, respectively) if and only
if all of these n eigenvalues are nonnegative (or positive, respectively). In 1997, Zhang [15]
extended this to quaternion Hermitian matrices. In 2023, Qi and Luo [16] further extended
this to dual quaternion Hermitian matrices. This is actually also true for dual symmetric
or dual complex Hermitian matrices. We now state these in our general frame.
Let A ∈DCn×n be a Hermitian matrix and x ∈DCn, we have (x∗Ax)∗= x∗Ax. This
implies that x∗Ax is a dual number, by [14], we may distinguish that x∗Ax is nonnegative,
or positive, or not. If for all x ∈DCn, x∗Ax is nonnegative, then we say that A is positive
semidefinite. If for all x ∈DCn and appreciable, x∗Ax is positive, then we say that A is
positive definite.
