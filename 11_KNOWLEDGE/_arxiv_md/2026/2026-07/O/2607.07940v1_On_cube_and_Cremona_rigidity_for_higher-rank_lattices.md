ON CUBE AND CREMONA RIGIDITY FOR
HIGHER-RANK LATTICES
SHENGYUAN ZHAO
In memory of Misha Kapovich
Abstract. For irreducible lattices in semisimple Lie groups of real rank
at least 2, we prove a cohomological vanishing result implying that any
action on a CAT(0) cube complex fixes a vertex whenever every hyper-
plane stabilizer is solvable. As an application, we prove regularizability
for all actions of higher-rank lattices by birational transformations on
projective surfaces.
We first use superrigidity for actions on infinite-
dimensional real hyperbolic spaces to reduce to the de Jonquières group,
and then apply our fixed-point theorem to the Jonquières complex. Our
proof bypasses the direct use of property FW.
1. Introduction
1.1. Zimmer’s Program. Motivated by rigidity results for irreducible lat-
tices in semisimple Lie groups of higher rank, especially Margulis superrigid-
ity and Zimmer cocycle superrigidity, Zimmer [44] formulated conjectures
predicting that actions of higher-rank lattices on manifolds are highly con-
strained. Rigidity problems for non-linear actions of lattices are now known
as Zimmer’s program. We refer to the recent works [12], [11], [20], [26], and
to the references therein, for historical accounts and various formulations.
This paper concerns Zimmer’s program in algebraic geometry, where one
studies actions on projective varieties by automorphisms, or more generally
by birational transformations. Over the complex numbers, the automor-
phism case for smooth projective varieties is a special case of the original
Zimmer program for diffeomorphisms of compact manifolds; see [16, 21].
The birational case is more intriguing. On the one hand indeterminacy loci
make the action on the variety non-set-theoretic and may cause non-compact
phenomena. On the other hand, the algebraicity can impose stronger rigid-
ity than in the differentiable setting.
Progress on the birational Zimmer program has been made in [28, 17, 20,
19, 25, 33]. In dimension two, Cantat [17, § 4] classified all birational actions
of lattices with Kazhdan’s property (T). In arbitrary dimension, Cantat–
Xie [20] proved that higher-rank lattices with Kazhdan’s property (T) and
the congruence subgroup property admit no infinite birational action on
varieties whose dimension is too small compared with the real rank of the

property (T) to all higher-rank lattices. We confirm this in dimension two,
including cases where property FW is not presently known.
1.2. Regularizability. We refer to [3] for background on birational geom-
etry of surfaces, and to [6, 18, 17] for the background results on groups of
birational transformations of surfaces. For simplicity, we work over the field
of complex numbers.
Let G < Bir(Y ) be a group of birational transformations on a projective
variety Y . We say that G is regularizable if there exists a quasi-projective
variety Z and a birational map f : Y 99K Z such that fGf−1 is a group
of automorphisms of Z. If Z can moreover be chosen projective, then G is
projectively regularizable. If, in addition, Z can be chosen smooth projective
and there exists a finite-index subgroup G′ < G such that fG′f−1 is con-
tained in Aut0(Z), the connected component of identity of Aut(Z), then G
is virtually isotopically projectively regularizable.
If Z is a smooth complex projective variety, then Aut0(Z) is a connected
Lie group. Since homomorphisms from higher-rank lattices to Lie groups
are strongly constrained by Margulis superrigidity, the classification of bi-
rational actions of higher-rank lattices naturally splits into two parts. The
first is to prove that every infinite birational action is virtually isotopically
projectively regularizable. The second is to determine which connected al-
gebraic groups can occur as Aut0(Z). The second problem is a classification
problem of algebraic varieties and is completely understood in dimension
two; see [6]. We shall therefore focus on the regularization problem.
Cantat–Cornulier [19] showed that regularization problems in birational
geometry are closely related to property FW. Subsequently, Cornulier [25]
and Lonjou–Urech [33] proved that every birational action of a group with
property FW is regularizable, and is projectively regularizable in dimen-
sion two. Cornulier [23, 24] also proved that many lattices without Kazh-
dan’s property (T) have property FW; this includes irreducible lattices in

1.3. Main results. Let G1, · · · , Gn be non-compact connected center-free
simple linear algebraic groups. Let G = G1 × · · · × Gn and assume that the
real rank of G is ≥2. Let Γ < G be an irreducible lattice.
Theorem A. Let S be a complex projective surface and let ρ : Γ →Bir(S)
be a homomorphism. Then ρ(Γ) is virtually isotopically projectively regular-
izable.
As explained above, Theorem A, together with Margulis superrigidity and
the classification [6] of actions of connected algebraic groups on projective
surfaces, gives in principle a list of all possible birational actions of higher-
rank lattices on projective surfaces, up to birational conjugacy. We shall
not give this list explicitly. The only case requiring (arguably) a separate
discussion concerns actions of lattices in PSL(2, R)n ×PGL(2, C)m on Hirze-
bruch surfaces Fk, k > 1; this case is described briefly in §6. The following
is a short statement in the spirit of Zimmer’s original conjectures:
Theorem B. Let S be a complex projective surface.
Assume that none
of G1, · · · , Gn is PSL(2, R), PGL(2, C), PU(1, 2), PSL(3, R) or PGL(3, C).

The proof of Theorem A uses the following two results of independent
interest, proved using Borel–Serre duality and Steinberg modules.
Theorem C. There exists a finite-index torsion-free subgroup Γ′ < Γ such
that, for any solvable subgroup K ⊂Γ′, for any field F,
H1(Γ′, F[Γ′/K]) = 0.
Theorem D. If Γ acts on a CAT(0) cube complex C and if the stabilizer
of every hyperplane of C is solvable, then C has a Γ-fixed vertex.
Cornulier [24, Prop. 7.I.3] showed that property FW for Γ is equivalent
to the vanishing H1(Γ, Z[Γ/K]) = 0 for every subgroup K. In particular,
for lattices whose property FW is known, Theorem C and D are merely
special consequences of Cornulier’s theorems [23]. However our proof is very
different. The weaker vanishing statement in Theorem C turns out to be
sufficient for the proof of Theorem A, thanks to the following result which
is a consequence of Monod’s superrigidity [36] and a result of Caprace–
Lytchak [22]:
Theorem E. Assume that ρ : Γ →Isom(HE) is a non-elementary isometric
action on an infinite-dimensional real hyperbolic space HE. There exists a
unique minimal ρ-invariant closed hyperbolic subspace Hρ ⊂HE. There exist
i ∈{1, · · · , n} and a continuous homomorphism ρi : Gi →Isom(Hρ) such
that the restriction of ρ to Hρ factors through the composition of ρi with the
projection G →Gi.
