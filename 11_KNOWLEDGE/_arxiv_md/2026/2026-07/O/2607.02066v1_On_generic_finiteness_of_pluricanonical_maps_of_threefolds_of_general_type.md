Abstract. We prove that |6KX| defines a generically finite map for all minimal
3-folds X of general type with P2(X) ≥2, which is optimal. We also prove that
|nKX| defines a generically finite map for all minimal 3-folds X of general type
when n ≥38. The essential technical ingredients of this paper are a new generic
finiteness criterion for surfaces and an effective comparison inequality under a
special resolution.

1. Introduction
Classifying varieties is one of the most fundamental problems in algebraic geom-
etry. By criteria for embedding a polarized variety into a weighted projective space
in [7], generic finiteness of pluricanonical maps plays an important role in construct-
ing the moduli spaces of designated classes of 3-folds. Thus the study of generic
finiteness of pluricanonical maps is of great value for classifying varieties.
There are many classical works in this aspect. For a minimal surface S of general
type, Enrico Bombieri, Yoichi Miyaoka and Fabrizio Catanese proved that |nKS|
defines a generically finite map when n ≥3 ([1, Main Theorem], [2, Theorem 3],
[3], [4]); Gang Xiao proved that |2KS| defines a generically finite map if and only if
S is not a (1, 0)-surface ([5, Theorem 1]). For a minimal 3-fold X of general type,
Kan Wu proved that |3KX| defines a generically finite map when pg(X) ≥4 ([20,
Theorem 1.1]); Meng Chen, Chen Jiang and Jianshi Yan proved that |2KX| is not
composed with a pencil when pg(X) ≥202 ([8, Theorem 1.2]).

There are many classical works about birationality in parallel. For a minimal
surface S of general type, Enrico Bombieri proved that |nKS| defines a birational
map when n ≥5 ([1, Main Theorem]). For a minimal 3-fold X of general type, Meng

Jungkai A. Chen proved that |nKX| defines a birational map when n ≥57 ([17,
Theorem 1.1], [16, Theorem 6.2]).
Our main results are the following three theorems.
Theorem 1.1. Let X be a minimal 3-fold of general type with P2(X) ≥2. Then
|6KX| defines a generically finite map.
Theorem 1.2. Let X be a minimal 3-fold of general type with Pm(X) ≥2. Then
|nKX| defines a generically finite map when n ≥3m + 2.
Theorem 1.3. Let X be a minimal 3-fold of general type. Then |nKX| defines a
generically finite map when n ≥38.
The following two examples show that both the integer 6 and the integer 2 are
optimal in Theorem 1.1.
Example 1.4 ([19, II.5.4]). X6,18 in P(2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 9) is a minimal 3-fold of general
type such that P2 = 2 and |nK| does not define a generically finite map for all n ≤5.
Example 1.5 ([19, II.5.1]). X30 in P(2, 3, 4, 5, 15) is a minimal 3-fold of general type
such that P2 = 1 and |nK| does not define a generically finite map for all n ≤7.
There are many challenges in the study of the P2 ≥2 case since we no longer
have pg > 0. Moreover, the failure of generic finiteness of Φ|2K| of (1, 0)-surfaces
causes many difficulties. To characterize generic finiteness precisely, we establish a
new generic finiteness criterion for surfaces, and we prove an effective comparison
inequality using a special resolution which is a central new technique in [7].
When studying generic finiteness of 3-folds with large pluricanonical section index,
we establish a better non-vanishing theorem than [15, Theorem 1.1 (1)], and we prove
several criteria to trace the relationship between K3 and Pn carefully. Moreover,
we introduce a modification of baskets that helps us focus on a finite number of
possibilities.
