ON SUPER DELTA-POINTS AND THE CONVEX-DLD2P IN
ABSOLUTE SUMS
JUAN GUERRERO-VIU AND JOANNA MARKOWICZ
Abstract. We partially answer two open questions concerning diameter two properties
in absolute sums. First, we identify the conditions that a super ∆-point in an absolute
sum of Banach spaces imposes on the coordinates. Secondly, we show that the convex
diametral local diameter two property (convex-DLD2P) passes from an absolute sum
X ⊕N Y to its factors whenever N is not the ℓ∞-norm.
Let X be a Banach space over the scalar field K, which can be either R or C. The closed
unit ball and the unit sphere of X is denoted by BX and SX, respectively. We write X∗
for the topological dual space of X. Moreover, given x∗∈BX∗and α > 0, the slice of BX
produced by x∗is the (open) set S(BX, x∗, α) = {x ∈BX : Re x∗(x) > 1 −α}.
Let x ∈SX. According to [3] and [8], it is said that
(1) x is a ∆-point if for every slice S of BX with x ∈S and for every ε > 0, there is
some y ∈S such that ∥x −y∥⩾2 −ε;
(2) x is a super ∆-point if for every relatively weakly open set W of BX with x ∈W
and for every ε > 0, there is some y ∈W such that ∥x −y∥⩾2 −ε.
These notions have received significant attention in recent years [1, 3, 7, 8, 10], since they
can be regarded as natural local versions of some of the classical diameter two properties.
Standing in sharp opposition to the Radon–Nikod´ym property and the convex point of
continuity property, diameter two properties ensure that certain subsets of the unit ball
are as large as possible in terms of diameter. Recall that X has the local diameter two
property (LD2P) if every slice of BX has diameter two; and the diameter two property
(D2P) whenever every non-empty relatively weakly open subset of BX has diameter two.
More recently, several stronger variants of these notions were considered in [5]. Namely,
we say that X has the diametral local diameter two property (DLD2P) if given any slice S
of BX, x ∈S ∩SX and ε > 0, there exists y ∈S such that ∥x −y∥⩾2−ε (equivalently, if
every x ∈SX is a ∆-point). On the other hand, it is said that X has the diametral diameter
two property (DD2P) if given any relatively weakly open subset W of BX, x ∈W ∩SX
and ε > 0, there exists y ∈W such that ∥x −y∥⩾2 −ε (equivalently, if every x ∈SX is
a super ∆-point). There are also corresponding versions of the above properties involving
convex combinations of slices of BX, but we will not deal with them here.
Furthermore, lying between the LD2P and the DLD2P, and motivated by the problem
of determining the minimum amount of ∆-points required to ensure that every slice has
diameter two, the following property was introduced in [3].

The second question we address concerns the convex-DLD2P. In [3, Problem 1] (see also
[9, Problem 9]), it was asked whether the convex-DLD2P in X ⊕N Y forces the factors to
have the same property. We answer this question for every absolute sum different from
the ℓ∞-sum.
The main difficulty in dealing with the ℓ∞-sum stems from the existence of ∆-points
whose coordinates need not be ∆-points in the corresponding factors (see [9, Exam-
ple 3.4.6]).
Proposition 2. Let X and Y be Banach spaces and let N be an absolute normalised norm
on R2 different from the ℓ∞-norm. If X ⊕N Y has the convex-DLD2P, then both X and
Y have the convex-DLD2P.
Proof. Suppose that X fails the convex-DLD2P (a similar argument is valid for Y ), so
there is some non-empty slice S = S(BX, f, α) that does not contain any ∆-point. Since
N is different from the ℓ∞-norm, consider t := max{s ∈[0, 1]: N(s, 1) ⩽1} which clearly
verifies t < 1. Hence, up to consider a smaller slice inside S if necessary, we may assume
that 1 −α > t. Next, define the slice
eS := {(x, y) ∈BX⊕NY : x ∈S} = S(BX⊕NY , (f, 0), α),
which is non-empty because S ̸= ∅. Therefore, there is some ∆-point (ax, by) ∈eS, where
x ∈SX, y ∈SY , a, b ⩾0 and N(a, b) = 1. Furthermore, as ax ∈S, then
a = ∥ax∥⩾Re f(ax) > 1 −α > t.
Thus, by the maximality of t, we have b ̸= 1 which yields to x being a ∆-point (see [9,
