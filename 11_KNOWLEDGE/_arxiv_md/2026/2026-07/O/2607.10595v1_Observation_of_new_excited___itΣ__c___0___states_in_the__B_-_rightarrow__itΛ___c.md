Abstract
An amplitude analysis of the B−→Λ+
c pπ−decay is performed using proton-proton
collision data, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9 fb−1, collected with
the LHCb detector at center-of-mass energies of 7, 8, and 13 TeV. In the Λ+
c π−
invariant-mass spectrum, the known Σc(2455)0, Σc(2520)0, Σc(2800)0 baryons are
observed, along with two new states: Σc(2900)0 and Σc(3200)0. The masses and
widths of the three high-mass states, modeled with Breit–Wigner line shapes, are
determined. This result expands the landscape of charm baryon spectroscopy and
provides crucial data for understanding charm baryon structure and dynamics.

Hadrons are composite systems of quarks bound by the strong interaction. According
to the quark model [1–3], hadrons are classified as mesons, with a minimum content of a
quark and an antiquark, and baryons, built minimally from three quarks. Besides ground
states, hadrons exhibit a spectrum of excited states resulting from distinct radial, spin or
orbital angular momentum configurations. The nonperturbative nature of low-energy QCD
makes it difficult to calculate hadron properties, requiring the development of effective
theoretical approaches for their study [4,5].
Baryons containing a single heavy quark are an ideal laboratory to understand the
nature of low-energy strong interactions and verify theoretical models [6]. The spectroscopy
of singly heavy baryons is determined by the flavor and angular-momentum structure of
the light quarks. The flavor structure of the two light quarks can be either symmetric
or antisymmetric, which is the case for Λ+
c and Σc baryons, respectively. Excited singly
heavy baryons arise from radial and orbital excitations, either between the light quarks (ρ
mode) or between the light quark system and the heavy quark (λ mode), combined with
spin interactions, leading to a rich resonance spectrum. While several Λ+
c baryons have
been discovered, only few Σc hadrons are established [7]: the lower mass states Σc(2455)
and Σc(2520) observed by the CLEO collaboration [8,9], and the excited state Σc(2800)
found by the Belle collaboration [10]. The Σc(2800) state is usually interpreted to be
one of the seven 1P-wave λ-mode excitations, with masses predicted in the range 2.7 to
2.9 GeV/c2 [11–13]. In 2008, the BaBar collaboration reported the observation of these
three Σ0
c states in the B−→Λ+
c pπ−decay through a one-dimensional analysis of the
Λ+
c π−mass spectrum [14].1 However, the measured Σc(2800)0 mass is inconsistent with
the Belle analysis, hinting at a new state or a mixing of several states. In the higher-mass
region, 2S-, 1D- and 2P-wave excitations from both the ρ-mode and λ-mode would emerge
according to calculations [15–23], demanding experimental verification. Measurements of
masses and widths enable testing various theoretical calculations, and help to understand
the dynamics of baryons containing both heavy and light quarks.
This Letter presents an amplitude analysis of the B−→Λ+
c pπ−decay, where the
Σ0
c resonances are searched for in the Λ+
c π−final state.
The analysis is performed
using proton-proton (pp) collision data collected by the LHCb experiment, corresponding
to an integrated luminosity of 3 fb−1 at center-of-mass energies of 7 and 8 TeV, and
6 fb−1 at 13 TeV. The LHCb detector is a single-arm forward spectrometer covering the
pseudorapidity range 2 < η < 5 described in detail in Refs. [24,25]. Simulation, required
to model the effects of the detector acceptance and the imposed selection requirements, is
produced and corrected using data with software packages described in Refs. [26–35].
The events used in this analysis are selected by topological triggers [36,37]. Offline,
the B−candidates are formed by combining Λ+
c , p and π−candidates, where the Λ+
c
baryons are reconstructed through the Λ+
c →pK−π+ decay, with the invariant mass
mpK−π+ required to be consistent with the known Λ+
c mass [7]. The final-state tracks are
required to be identified as pions, kaons or protons by LHCb’s ring imaging Cherenkov
detectors, to have a large momentum transverse to the beam (pT), and to be well separated
from any primary collision vertex (PV). The B−and Λ+
c candidates are required to have
reconstructed decay vertices of good fit quality. In addition, the decay vertex of the B−
meson is required to be significantly displaced from its associated PV, defined as the PV
that aligns best with the B−flight direction. To improve the experimental resolution on

The signal model includes Σ∗∗0
c
and ∆

c π−and pπ−channels,
respectively, and a nonresonant (NR) component in the Λ+

known states, Σc(2520)0, Σc(2800)0, ∆(1232)−−, ∆(1600)−−, ∆(1620)−−and ∆(1700)−−,
are initially included in the fit. Since there is a clear structure around 3.2 GeV/c2 in mΛ+
c π−
and the bump around 2.8 GeV/c2 is asymmetric in the Λ+
c π−spectrum, two additional
Σ∗∗0
c
states, Σc(2900)0 and Σc(3200)0, are found to be needed to describe data. For
the Σ∗∗0
c
states, the spin-parity (JP) quantum numbers are not known, and therefore
six possible hypotheses 1/2±, 3/2± and 5/2± are tested in the fit. For the Σc(2520)0
baryon, the spin-parity is measured to be 3/2+ with a significance exceeding 6 standard
deviations (σ) relative to other hypotheses. This result is consistent with the quark-model
expectations [7]. The ∆(1620)−−component has a significance below 1σ and is therefore
not included in the baseline model. The mass-dependent lineshape of each resonance
is modeled using a relativistic Breit–Wigner function, where the masses and widths
are floated for the three Σ∗∗0
c
states with mass larger than 2.6 GeV/c2, while they are
fixed to their known values [7] for the remaining states. Blatt–Weisskopf form factors
are used to model the angular momentum barrier, where the resonance size is set to
4 (GeV/c)−1 ≈0.8 fm [48]. An S-wave NR contribution is also needed to model the mΛ+
c π−
distribution. This mass-dependent lineshape is described by an exponential function,
where the slope is allowed to vary. The contribution of a P-wave NR component is also
tested but found to be negligible and therefore not considered in the subsequent studies.
The fit is performed by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (NLL). However, so-
lutions of multiple JP configurations yield similar minimum NLL values, preventing an
unambiguous determination of the spin-parity quantum numbers. Therefore, the solutions
with twice the likelihood difference 2∆ln L < 25 relative to the fit with smallest NLL
are grouped into two categories (A and B) according to the fit results, which are similar
within each group but different between the two groups. The two sets of solutions are
reported separately. The configuration yielding the smallest NLL includes three Σ∗∗0
c
states: Σc(2800)0, Σc(2900)0, and Σc(3200)0, with spin-parity assignments JP = 3/2+,
1/2−, and 3/2−, respectively. This solution, together with other JP configurations that
yield similar masses and widths for the Σ∗∗0
c
states, is classified as group A. For solutions
in group B, the measurements of the mass and width of the Σc(2800)0 state and the
width of the Σc(2900)0 state differ significantly from those in group A, while those for the
Σc(3200)0 state are compatible. Among the spin-parity assignments for solutions in group
B, that with JP = 1/2−, 1/2+, and 3/2−for the three Σ∗∗0
c
states yields the smallest
NLL. The likelihood difference 2∆ln L between the best-fit solutions in groups A and B
is less than 1, indicating that the two solution families are statistically indistinguishable.

Various systematic uncertainties are considered for the masses, widths and relative
fit fractions of the Σ∗∗0
c
states, as summarized in Tables 3 and 4 in the End Matter.
The uncertainty associated with the invariant-mass fit is evaluated by repeating the
amplitude fit while varying the background fraction within its statistical uncertainty and
by modifying the mass-fit parametrization. Efficiency uncertainties across the Dalitz
plot arise from several sources: the binning scheme of the efficiency map, assessed using
alternative binning schemes; limited simulation sample sizes, estimated by resampling of
the efficiency map; imperfections in track-efficiency and modeling of the hardware-level
trigger responses, corrected using the data [33–35]; and the discrepancy between the data
and simulated samples on variables used in the BDT training, corrected by weighting
simulated samples to data. The impact of the Λ+
c decay is accounted for by weighting
simulation samples to match data. Since the weighting depends on the chosen phase
space, different phase-space regions are used to assess this effect, and the largest resulting
differences are taken as a systematic uncertainty. The influence of the Λ+
c polarization
is also considered by comparing data and simulation for distributions related to the Λ+
c
polarization and is found to be negligible. Uncertainties arising from fixed parameters are
also taken into account, including the resonance radii and the ∆
∗∗−−resonance parameters.
The uncertainty from the resonance radii is studied by varying the baseline values of
4 (GeV/c)−1 to 3 or 5 (GeV/c)−1. The uncertainty associated with fixing the ∆
∗∗−−masses
and widths is evaluated by allowing these parameters to float in the amplitude fit, with
Gaussian constraints centered on their known values. For uncertainties associated with
alternative models, instead of a coherent sum of Breit–Wigner amplitudes, K-matrix
models [50] are used to describe the ∆
∗∗−−contributions with JP = 3/2−and Λ+
c π−
contributions with JP = 1/2−, respectively. Different nonresonant models, such as second-
order polynomial functions, are tested. Since the JP quantum numbers of the three Σ∗∗0
c
states cannot be determined unambiguously, the spread among solutions within each
group is treated as a source of systematic uncertainty, labeled as the JP configurations.
The systematic uncertainty associated with the possible contribution of a Λ+
c p resonance is
evaluated by tightening the selection on mΛ+
c p, which has a negligible influence. The total
systematic uncertainties are determined by combining all contributions in quadrature.
According to the results summarized in Table 1, in group A two states, one rel-
atively narrow and one broader, are present in the 2.8 GeV/c2 mass region, while in
group B, the two Σ∗∗0
c
states have similar width. Fit fractions of the three high-mass
excited Σ0
c states to the B−→Λ+
c pπ−decay are found to be comparable to that of
the Σc(2455)0 baryon.
Compared with previous measurements, the Σc(2800)0 state
reported by the BaBar collaboration in the B−→Λ+
c pπ−decay is resolved into two
states in this analysis. The mass and width of the Σc(2800)0 baryon in group A are
consistent with those reported by the Belle collaboration in inclusive production [10].
The measured properties of the Σc(3200)0 state are consistent between groups A and B,
allowing the results to be combined. The group A results are taken as baseline. The
systematic uncertainty is taken as the larger one in groups A and B. The difference

Further experimental measurements, including other decay channels, will help to shed
further light on the nature of these states.
In conclusion, an amplitude analysis of the B−→Λ+
c pπ−decay is performed using pp
collisions at center-of-mass energies of 7, 8 and 13 TeV collected by the LHCb detector,
corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9 fb−1. In addition to the Σc(2455)0 and
Σc(2520)0 states, three other excited Σ∗∗0
c
states, Σc(2800)0, Σc(2900)0 and Σc(3200)0, are
needed to describe the Λ+
c π−mass spectrum. The spin of the Σc(2455)0 state is measured
to be 1/2 and the spin-parity of the Σc(2520)0 state is determined to be 3/2+. Two
distinct solutions are obtained from the amplitude fit. The masses, widths, and fit fractions
of the Σc(2800)0 and Σc(2900)0 states exhibit significant differences between the two
solutions, whereas the corresponding parameters for the Σc(3200)0 state are compatible.
The Σc(2800)0 state reported by the BaBar collaboration in the decay B−→Λ+
c pπ−is
found to be consistent with a superposition of two overlapping resonances. The mass and
width of the Σc(2800)0 from solution group A are consistent with the Belle measurement,
which does not find evidence for the Σc(2900)0 contribution, while the results from solution
group B show discrepancies with the Belle results. The new Σc(2900)0 and Σc(3200)0
states represent the first observation of excited Σ0
c states in over two decades. This
analysis significantly extends the spectroscopy of Σ0
c states and helps to understand the
internal structure and low-energy dynamics of baryons containing a heavy quark.
