ON THE SIMPLICITY OF KATSURA ALGEBRAS
JOSIAH AAKRE AND NÓRA SZAKÁCS
Abstract. We give a complete characterization of the (purely infinite) sim-
plicity of Katsura algebras and the associated Steinberg algebras.
This is
achieved by characterizing when the singular ideals vanish via the self-similar
groupoid model derived from Exel and Pardo.
Analogous results are given
for the algebras arising from the faithful quotient of the self-similar action.
Finally, we describe polynomial-space algorithms to determine if each singular
ideal vanishes and provide the first non-Hausdorff examples of non-contracting
self-similar groupoids for which simplicity is algorithmically decidable.
1. Introduction
A key milestone in the classification theory of C∗-algebras is the Kirchberg-
Phillips theorem, stating that all nuclear, separable, purely infinite simple C∗-
algebras in the UCT class – UCT Kirchberg algebras for short – are classified by their
K-theory. In an influential paper [11], Katsura defined a family of C∗-algebras OA,B
built from a pair of integer matrices A and B which contains all UCT Kirchberg
algebras, moreover, the K-theory of OA,B is easily computed from the matrices A
and B. The algebra OA,B is unital exactly if A, B are finite dimensional. While all
Katsura algebras are nuclear, separable and in the UCT class, they are not always
purely infinite simple, and the main goal of this paper is to characterize when this
happens in terms of the matrices A and B, focusing on the unital case.
Exel and Pardo realized [6] that unital Katsura algebras can be viewed as C∗-
algebras associated to self-similar actions of Z on the path spaces of finite graphs.
This led them to introduce the notion of self-similar group actions on graphs, and
associated C∗-algebras, which generalize C∗-algebras of self-similar groups intro-
duced by Nekrashevych [17] in the same way that graph C∗-algebras generalize
Cuntz algebras. This is further extended by [13] where the acting group is more
generally replaced by a (discrete) groupoid. In this setting, Katsura algebras arise
from a self-similar action of a bundle of infinite cyclic groups, termed Katsura-
Exel-Pardo- or KEP-groupoids for short, and this has now become the standard
framework in which they are studied [10, 19, 18, 14].
Crucially, the approach of Exel and Pardo also gave Katsura algebras an ample
groupoid model (not to be confused with the self-similar groupoid mentioned above).
The simplicity of nuclear Hausdorff groupoid C∗-algebras is well-understood, and
was used in [6, Section 18] to study when OA,B is purely infinite simple, obtaining
a full classification in the Hausdorff case.
Katsura algebras do not always come from Hausdorff groupoids however, and
simplicity in the non-Hausdorff setting is much more challenging to understand, the
difficulty being that the C∗-algebra contains non-continuous functions. A general
theory of non-Hausdorff simplicity developed in [4] identifies the so-called singular

methods extend seamlessly to Steinberg algebras over any field, so we frame our
work in this broader setting.
Our main result about unital Katsura algebras is Theorem 5.7, which gives a
complete characterization of when the singular ideal of the Steinberg algebra van-

in the Katsura algebra. We apply the result to obtain a complete characterization
of simplicity of Katsura algebras in Corollary 5.8. In Section 7.1, we show that the
conditions of Theorem 5.7 are effectively decidable, namely in polynomial space
on the input A, B. By known results it follows that the simplicity of the Katsura
algebra (and equivalently, any Steinberg algebra) is also decidable in polynomial

The self-similar action of a KEP-groupoid may fail to be faithful, in which case
we refer to the corresponding quotient as the faithful KEP-groupoid. The associated
C∗-algebra is a natural quotient of the Katsura algebra, and was first studied in
[10]. It turns out that the singular ideal of the Katsura algebra always arises from
the non-faithfulness of the action, in particular, whenever the action is faithful
and the associated ample groupoid is minimal and topologically free, the Katsura
algebra is simple.
This naturally motivates the study of when faithful KEP-groupoid (C∗)-algebras
have a vanishing singular ideal. This turns out more difficult to characterize, but
we obtain analogous results to the non-faithful case.
Theorem 6.14 provides a
characterization of when the singular ideal of the Steinberg algebra vanishes, which
again is independent of the field and equivalent to the vanishing of the singular
ideal in the C∗-algebra. This is applied to obtain our simplicity result for faithful
KEP-groupoid (C∗)-algebras in Corollary 6.15. The singular ideal characterization
is again decidable in polynomial space on the input A, B (Section 7.2), and thus
by known results, so is the simplicity.
We remark that in the setting of finite
matrices, simplicity implies pure infiniteness for both Katsura algebras and their

In both cases, the singular ideal of the Steinberg algebra is studied by viewing it
