Abstract
In this paper, we show that there is a countable Noetherian complete lattice L and an order-compatible
d-topology τ on L such that (L, τ) is not well-filtered, and there exist a dcpo P and an order-compatible well-
filtered topology τ on P but the Scott topology σ(P) is not well-filtered. For such poset P and topology
τ, let Y = (P, τ) and X = 1 (the topological space with single point), then the function space C(X, Y )
equipped with the Scott topology is not well-filtered. These results answer three open problems concerning
the well-filteredness of T0-spaces.
Keywords:
Well-filtered space; sober space; d-space; function space; Scott topology
2000 MSC: 54D10; 54C35; 54F05; 06F30
In domain theory, the d-spaces and sober spaces are two important classes of T0-spaces (see [2, 5, 7, 23,
38]). In [8], Heckmann introduced a new types T0-spaces — well-filtered spaces, which lie between d-spaces
and sober spaces. The well-filteredness has emerged as a very useful and important property for T0-spaces
(see [5, 7, 11, 12, 14-20, 22, 24-38]).
In this paper, we give answers to three open problems concerning the well-filteredness of T0-spaces. The
paper is organized as follows.
Section 1 provides some fundamental definitions and notations about topology and lattice-ordered struc-
tures which will be used in the whole paper. Also the filtered version of topological Rudin lemma and some
characterizations of d-spaces and well-filtered spaces are listed.
Section 2 gives a countable Noetherian complete lattice L and an order-compatible d-topology τ on L
such that (L, τ) is not well-filtered, which gives an answer to [28, Question 4.7].
In Section 3, we show that if J⊤is the dcpo obtaining by adding a top element ⊤to Johnstone’s dcpo J
and τ = {∅} ∪{U ∪{⊤} : U ∈σ(J) \ {∅}}, then (J⊤, τ) is sober (and hence well-filtered) but the Scott space
ΣJ⊤is not well-filtered, which answers [29, Question 64 and Question 67]. Furthermore, we show that the
function space C(1, (J⊤, τ)) (1 is the topological space with single point) equipped with the Scott topology
is not well-filtered, which gives an answer to [29, Question 70].
1. Preliminaries

this element the least upper bound and write it as ∨E or sup E (for supremum). Dually, the greatest lower
bound of E in P is written as ∧E or inf E (for infimum). The poset P is called an inf semilattice (shortly

A nonempty subset D of a poset Q is directed if every two elements in D have an upper bound in D.
The set of all directed sets of Q is denoted by D(Q). The poset Q is called a directed complete poset, or dcpo
for short, if for any D ∈D(Q), its supremum ∨D exists in Q. The notion x = W↑G is a convenient device

A subset U of a poset P is Scott open if (i) U = ↑U, and (ii) for any directed subset D for which ∨D
exists, ∨D ∈U implies D ∩U ̸= ∅. All Scott open subsets of P form a topology, and we call this topology
the Scott topology on P and denote it by σ(P). The space ΣP = (P, σ(P)) is called the Scott space of P.
The upper topology on a poset P, generated by {P \ ↓x : x ∈P} (as a subsase), is denoted by υ(P). The
upper sets form the (upper) Alexandroff topology α(P).
For a T0-space X, we use O(X) (resp., Γ(X)) to denote the set of all open subsets (resp., closed subsets)
of X. The closure of a subset A in X is denoted by clXA (or simply by clA if there is no ambiguity) or A,
and the interior of A will be denoted by intXA or simply by intA. We use ≤X to represent the specialization
order of X, that is, x ≤X y iff x ∈{y}. We will use ΩX or even X to denote the poset (X, ≤X). In what
follows, when a T0-space is considered as a poset, the order always refers to the specialization order if no
other explanation is given. Let Sc(X) = {{x} : x ∈X} and Dc(X) = {D : D ∈D(X)}. For a poset P, a
T0-topology τ on P is said to be order-compatible if ≤τ agrees with the original order on P. It is easy to
verify that τ is order-compatible iff υ(P) ⊆τ ⊆α(P).
Let Set denote the category of all sets and mappings. The category of all T0-spaces with continuous
mappings is denoted by Top0. A T0-space X is called a d-space (or monotone convergence space) if X (with
the specialization order) is a dcpo and O(X) ⊆σ(X) (cf. [5, 23]). For a set Y and a topology τ on Y , we
call τ a d-topology on Y if (Y, τ) is a d-space. A topology δ on a poset P is said to be an order-compatible
d-topology if δ is order-compatible and (P, δ) is a d-space. Clearly, δ is an order-compatible d-topology on
P iff P is a dcpo and υ(P) ⊆δ ⊆σ(P).
Proposition 1.1. ([31, Proposition 3.3]) For a T0-space X, the following two conditions are equivalent:
(1) X is a d-space.
(2) Dc(X) = Sc(X).
A nonempty subset A of a T0-space X is said to be irreducible if for any {F1, F2} ⊆Γ(X), A ⊆F1 ∪F2
implies A ⊆F1 or A ⊆F2. Denote by Irr(X) (resp., Irrc(X)) the set of all irreducible (resp., irreducible
closed) subsets of X. Clearly, every directed subset of X (with the specialization order) is irreducible. The
space X is called sober, if for any A ∈Irrc(X), there is a unique point x ∈X such that A = {x}.
A subset A of a T0-space X is called saturated if it equals the intersection of all open sets containing it
(equivalently, A is an upper set with respect to the specialization order). We use Q(X) to denote the set of
all nonempty compact saturated subsets of X.
Definition 1.2. A T0-space X is said to be well-filtered if for any open set U and filtered family K ⊆Q(X),
T K⊆U implies K⊆U for some K∈K.

UK-admitting space in [8].

Proposition 1.7. Every sober space is well-filtered and every well-filtered space is a d-space.
A poset P is said to be Noetherian if it satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC for short): every
ascending chain has a greatest member.
Proposition 1.8. ([39, Proposition 5.4 and Theorem 5.7] and [30, Proposition 3.8]) For a poset P, the
following conditions are equivalent:
(1) P is Noetherian.
(2) Every directed subset of P has a largest member.
(3) P is a dcpo and ↑x ∈σ(P) for all x ∈P.
(4) P is a dcpo and σ(P) = α(P).
(5) (P, α(P)) is a d-space.
(6) (P, α(P)) is well-filtered.
(7) (P, α(P)) is sober.
In order to provide a uniform approach to d-spaces, sober spaces and well-filtered spaces and develop a
general framework for dealing with all these spaces, Xu [26] introduced the following concepts.
Definition 1.9. ([26, Definition 3.1 and Definition 3.2]) (1) A covariant functor H : Top0 →Set is called
a subset system on Top0 provided that the following two conditions are satisfied:
(i) S(X) ⊆H(X) ⊆2X (the set of all subsets of X) for each X ∈ob(Top0).
(ii) For any continuous mapping f : X →Y in Top0, H(f)(A) = f(A) ∈H(Y ) for all A ∈H(X).
(2) A subset system H : Top0 →Set is called an irreducible subset system, or an R-subset system for short,
if H(X) ⊆Irr(X) for all X ∈ob(Top0).
In what follows, the capital letter H always stands for an R-subset system H : Top0 →Set. For a
T0-space X, let Hc(X) = {A : A ∈H(X)}.

(1) S (for X ∈ob(Top0), S(X) is the set of all single point subsets of X).
(2) C (for X ∈ob(Top0), C(X) is the set of all chains of X).

2. Order-compatible d-topologies on complete lattices
For the sobriety of upper topology on a poset, we have the following result.
Proposition 2.1. ([31, Proposition 2.9]) For a poset P, the space (P, υ(P)) is sober iff ∨A exists in P for
all A ∈Irr((P, υ(P)). Therefore, for any complete lattice L, (L, υ(L)) is sober.
In [24], the following useful result was given.
Proposition 2.2. ([24, Corollary 3.2]) If L is a complete lattice, then ΣL is well-filtered.
By Proposition 1.7, Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2, the upper topology υ(L) and Scott topology
σ(L) on a complete lattice L are well-filtered. One might naturally wonder whether any order-compatible
d-topology on L is well-filtered. So in [28, Question 4.7] Xu posed the following question:
Questian A. Is every order-compatible d-topology on a complete lattice a well-filtered topology?
Or
equivalently, is there a complete lattice L and a topology υ(L) & τ & σ(L) such that (L, τ) is not well-
filtered?
In the following example we give a countable Noetherian complete lattice L and an order-compatible
d-topology τ on L such that (L, τ) is not well-filtered, which gives an answer to Question A.

3. Function spaces equipped with Scott topology
Function spaces (equipped with certain topologies) are important structures in topology and domain
theory (see [4, 5]). Cartesian closed categories of domains (more generally, certain topological spaces) are
appropriate for models of various typed and untyped lambda-calculi and functional programming languages
(see [5]). Since whether certain properties of topological spaces are preserved when passing to function
spaces is connected with the cartesian closed category of topological spaces, this question has attracted
considerable attention in domain theory and non-Hausdorff topology, especially for domains (endowed with
the Scott topology), sober spaces, d-spaces and well-filtered spaces (see [3, 5, 19, 26]).

Example 3.8. Let X = 1 be the topological space with single point and L the complete lattice constructed

topology on C(X, Y ) does not have property S with respect to R.
It is still not known whether a similar result to Proposition 3.6 holds for WD (or RD) and well-filtered
spaces. So in [29] Xu et al. posed the following question (see [29, Question 64 and Question 67]).
Question B. For T0-spaces X and Y , whether the Scott topology on C(X, Y ) has property S with respect to
WD or RD? Especially, for a T0-space X and a well-filtered space Y , whether the Scott topology on C(X, Y )
has property S with respect to WD or RD? Or equivalently, is the function space [X →Y ]Σ well-filtered?
The following related question was also posed in [29, Question 70].
Question C. Is there a well-filtered space Y such that ΣY (i.e., ΣΩY ) is not well-filtered? Or equivalently,
is there a dcpo P and a topology υ(P) ⊆τ ⊆σ(P) such that (P, τ) is well-filtered but (P, σ(P)) is not
well-filtered?
Remark 3.9. If the answer of Question C is “Yes", then the answer of Question B is “No"! Indeed, suppose
that Y is any well-filtered space for which the Scott space ΣY is not well-filtered and X = 1 (the topological
space with single point). Then the function space C(X, Y ) equipped with the Scott topology is not well-
filtered since [X →Y ]Σ and ΣY are homeomorphic (see Example 3.8). Hence by Theorem 3.5 (for H = WD
or H = RD), the Scott topology on C(X, Y ) does not have property S with respect to WD and RD.
