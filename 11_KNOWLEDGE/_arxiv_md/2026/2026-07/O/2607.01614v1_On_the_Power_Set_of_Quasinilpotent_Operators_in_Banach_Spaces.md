1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, we denote by X a complex Banach space, and by H a complex
Hilbert space. The symbol B(X) stands for the set of bounded linear operators on X. For
T ∈B(X), let σ(T) be the spectrum of T. A closed subspace M ⊂X is called an invariant
subspace under the operator T if TM ⊂M. The famous invariant subspace problem asks:
Does every bounded linear operator on a separable, infinite dimensional, complex Hilbert
space have a non-trivial closed invariant subspace?
The invariant subspace problem is still unsolved. Nevertheless, numerous results indicate
that spectral theory and the invariant subspace problem are closely related. For instance,
it is well known that the Riesz decomposition theorem implies that if the spectrum of a
bounded linear operator T on X is not connected, then T has a non-trivial invariant subspace.
Brown, Chevreau and Pearcy [3] proved that if T ∈B(X) with ∥T∥= 1 and sup{|f(z)| : z ∈
σ(T)∩D} = ∥f∥∞for every f in H∞(D), then T has a non-trivial invariant subspace. Brown
[2] showed that a hyponormal operator T has a non-trivial invariant subspace whenever
R(σ(T)) ̸= C(σ(T)), where C(σ(T)) denotes the Banach algebra of all continuous functions
defined on the spectrum of T and R(σ(T)) is the closure in C(σ(T)) of the algebra of rational
functions with poles off σ(T). For more introductions on the invariant subspace problem,
one can see [5,21,23].
A special case for which the invariant subspace problem is still open is that of quasinilpo-
tent operators on H. Recall that an operator T is said to be quasinilpotent if σ(T) = {0}.
Since the spectrum of a quasinilpotent operator is a singleton, no further information can
be obtained from it. Therefore, the invariant subspace problem for quasinilpotent operators
is particularly difficult. We refer the readers to [9–11,22,24] and the references therein for
more information along this line.

There has been a series of works exploring the quasinilpotent operator by the power set.
The interested readers can refer [13, 14, 16, 19] for more details. Comparing with the facts
that σ(A) is compact for every A in B(H) and each nonempty compact subset of C is the
spectrum of some operators in B(H). Ji and Zhang [17] proved that the power set Λ(T)
of a quasinilpotent operator T on H must be right closed (that is, sup E ∈Λ(T) for every
nonempty subset E of Λ(T)).
Furthermore, they also showed that for each right closed
subset σ of [0, 1] containing 1, there exists a quasinilpotent operator T ∈B(H) such that
Λ(T) = σ. However, this result requires the mild assumption that 1 ∈σ. So, one may
naturally consider the following question.
Question 1. Whether or not 1 is always in the power set of every quasinilpotent operator?
Recall that an infinite-dimensional Banach space X is said to be hereditarily indecompos-
able (briefly HI) if every closed infinite-dimensional subspace of X cannot be written as the
direct sum of two infinite-dimensional closed subspaces. Such spaces were first exhibited by
Gowers and Maurey in [12]. One of the main properties of an HI space X is that it has
few operators, namely, every T ∈B(X) is a multiple of the identity plus a strictly singular
