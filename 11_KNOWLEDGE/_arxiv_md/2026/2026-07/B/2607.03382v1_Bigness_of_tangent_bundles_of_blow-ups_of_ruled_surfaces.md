BIGNESS OF TANGENT BUNDLES OF BLOW-UPS OF RULED SURFACES
HOSUNG KIM AND JEONG-SEOP KIM
Abstract. We determine when the bigness of the tangent bundle is preserved under point blow-ups
of a ruled surface S = PC(E), giving a sharp bound on the number of blow-up points in terms of the
first Segre invariant s1(E).
1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, all varieties are defined over the field of complex numbers.
Positivity
properties of the tangent bundle impose strong restrictions on the geometry of a smooth projective va-
riety. Mori’s solution to Hartshorne’s conjecture characterizes projective space by the ampleness of TX
[Mor79], and the Campana–Peternell conjecture predicts an analogous rigidity for Fano manifolds with
nef TX [CP91]. Weaker positivity notions, especially bigness and pseudo-effectivity of TX, have re-
cently been studied from several viewpoints, including VMRT methods, Fano classifications, dynamical
rigidity, and surface geometry; see [Hsi15, Mal21, HLS22, HL23, SZ25, JLZ25, KKL25, KKL24]. In
this paper, we say that a vector bundle V is big on X if the tautological line bundle OPX(V )(1) is big
on PX(V ).
For a ruled surface S = PC(E) over a smooth projective curve C, it was proved that TS is big if
and only if E is unstable or C = P1 [Kim23]. It is then natural to ask how this bigness behaves under
blow-ups at points. This is motivated by the known result for blow-ups of P2 in general position,
where bigness holds precisely for at most 4 points [HLS22]. The purpose of this paper is to answer
this question for ruled surfaces.
Main Theorem. Let C be a smooth projective curve, and let S = PC(E) be a ruled surface over C
with the first Segre invariant s1(E) = −n. Let X be the blow-up of S at m points in general position.
If C ≃P1, then TX is big if and only if either m ≤3 for n = 0, 1 or m ≤n + 1 for n ≥2. Otherwise,
if C ̸≃P1, then TX is big if and only if m ≤n −1 and n ≥1.
The notion of general position appearing in the statement above will be made precise in Definition 1.
The proof of the theorem is given separately in Theorem 3.2 for the case C ̸≃P1 and in Theorem 3.4 for
the case C ≃P1. For the bigness assertion, the proof in the case C ≃P1 relies on explicit calculations
of effective divisors on P(TX) arising from families of rational curves, while in the case C ̸≃P1, it
follows more directly from the bigness of the relative tangent bundle. For the non-bigness assertion,
we reduce the problem, via elementary transformations, to the cases treated in [KKL24] when C ≃P1
and in [Kim23] when C ̸≃P1.

Definition 1. Let S = PC(E) be a ruled surface associated with an unstable vector bundle E over C.
If s1(E) < 0, then we say that points x1, . . . , xm ∈S are in general position if none of them lies on
C0 and no two of them lie on the same fiber of π : S →C. Here, C0 denotes the unique section with
negative self-intersection C2

If s1(E) = 0 and C ≃P1, then we say that points x1, . . . , xm ∈S are in general position if no two
of them lie on the same fiber of either of the two projections S →P1. We remark that in this case
S ≃P1 × P1.
In the theorems in the subsequent sections, the above definition specifies which configurations of
points are allowed as centers of blow-ups of a ruled surface.
Lemma 2.2. Let C be a smooth projective curve, and let S = PC(E) be a ruled surface over C.
Assume that s1(E) = −n < 0, and let C0 be the minimal section of S. If D is an irreducible section
of S, then either D2 = −n or D2 ≥n. Moreover, D2 = −n if and only if D = C0.
Proof. Since D is a section of S, there exists a divisor b on C such that D ∼C0 + bf. Put b = deg b.
Then D2 = −n+2b and D.C0 = −n+b. If D = C0, then D2 = −n. Assume now that D ̸= C0. Since D
and C0 are distinct irreducible curves, we have D.C0 ≥0. Thus b ≥n, and hence D2 = −n+2b ≥n.
□

In the opposite direction, elementary transformations away from the negative section may increase
the first Segre invariant. We record the following consequence, which will be used later.
Lemma 2.3. Let C be a smooth projective curve, and let S = PC(E) be a ruled surface over C such
that s1(E) = −n < 0. If S′ = PC(E′) is the ruled surface obtained from S by taking elementary
transformations at n points x1, . . . , xn in general position, then E′ is semistable.
Proof. Suppose that E′ is unstable. Equivalently, s1(E′) < 0. Then there exists an irreducible section
D′ of S′ with D′2 < 0. Let D be the strict transform of D′ on S under the elementary transformations.
Let k be the number of centers among x1, . . . , xn lying on D. By the self-intersection formula for
blow-ups and contractions, we have
D′2 = D2 + n −2k.
Since D′2 < 0 and k ≤n, it follows that D2 < 2k −n ≤n, and hence D = C0 by Lemma 2.2.
By the general-position assumption, none of the centers x1, . . . , xn lies on C0. Thus k = 0, and the
above formula gives D′2 = C2
0 + n = 0, which contradicts D′2 < 0. Therefore, E′ is semistable.

2.3. Criteria for the bigness of vector bundles. Let V be a vector bundle on a smooth projective
variety X. We say that V is big if the tautological line bundle OP(V )(1) is big on P(V ). We write
ζ = c1(OP(V )(1)) and denote the natural projection by Π : PX(V ) →X. Throughout the remainder
of this paper, we will mainly consider the case V = TX.
Lemma 2.4 ([HLS22, Lemma 6]). Let V be a vector bundle on a smooth projective variety X. Then
V is big if and only if kζ −Π∗H is pseudoeffective on PX(V ) for some k > 0 and some big divisor H
on X.

Lemma 2.5. Let W be a subbundle of a vector bundle V . If W is big, then V is big.
Proof. The inclusion W ⊂V induces an inclusion Symi W ⊗OX(−jH) ⊂Symi V ⊗OX(−jH). Thus,
if H0(X, Symi W ⊗OX(−jH)) ̸= 0, then H0(X, Symi V ⊗OX(−jH)) ̸= 0. Therefore, the statement
follows from Lemma 2.4 and [HLS22, Lemma 2.2].

3. Proof of the Main Theorem
Proposition 3.1. Let S = PC(E) be the ruled surface associated with an unstable vector bundle E
over a smooth projective curve C, and assume that s1(E) = −n. If X is the blow-up of S at m < n
points lying on distinct fibers of π : S →C, then TX is big.
Proof. After twisting E by a line bundle, we may assume that E = π∗OS(C0). Let e = det E∨, and let
Ei be the exceptional divisor over xi ∈S, where xi lies on the fiber over pi ∈C. Then the relative
tangent bundle of the conic bundle X →C is given by
TX/C ∼2C0 + π∗e −E1 −E2 −· · · −Em.
Note that
TX/C ≥2C0 + π∗(e −p1 −p2 −· · · −pm),
and the divisor e −p1 −· · · −pm is big on C, since deg(e −p1 −· · · −pm) = n −m > 0. Thus TX/C is
big by Lemma 2.4, and hence TX is big by Lemma 2.5.
□

Together with the converse, this yields the following theorem in the case C ̸≃P1.
Theorem 3.2. Let C be a smooth projective curve of genus g > 0, and let S = PC(E) be a ruled
surface over C such that s1(E) = −n < 0. If X is the blow-up of S at m points in general position,
then TX is big if and only if m ≤n −1.
Proof. The bigness assertion has already been proved in Proposition 3.1.
It remains to prove the
non-bigness assertion. By Lemma 2.6, it suffices to prove the assertion in the case m = n.
By the definition of general position, we may assume that the centers x1, . . . , xn of the blow-up
X →S are in general position. By contracting the strict transforms of the fibers passing through the
points xi on X, we obtain another ruled surface S′ associated to a vector bundle E′ over C; that is,
S′ = P(E′).
By Lemma 2.3, the vector bundle E′ is semistable. Hence, by [Kim23], the tangent bundle TS′ of
S′ = P(E′) is not big. Since X is also obtained as the blow-up X →S′, Lemma 2.6 implies that TX is
not big.
□
