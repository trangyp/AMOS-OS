BANACH SPACES WITH THE
WEAK DIAMETRAL DIAMETER TWO PROPERTY
JUAN GUERRERO-VIU, MIGUEL MART´IN, AND ABRAHAM RUEDA ZOCA
Abstract. We introduce and systematically study the weak diametral diameter two property
(weak-DD2P), a new geometric property that lies strictly between the diametral diameter two
property and both the diameter two property and the convex diametral local diameter two property.
A necessary condition for the weak-DD2P is obtained through the structure of the extreme points of
the dual unit ball, which leads to characterisations for several classical classes of spaces, including
C(K) spaces, L1-preduals, unital uniform algebras, and (vector-valued) function algebras.
We
establish stability results under standard constructions such as absolute sums, K¨othe–Bochner
spaces, and projective (symmetric) tensor products. Moreover, we provide complete descriptions
of the weak-DD2P for vector-valued spaces of the form L1(µ, X), L∞(µ, X), and C(K, X). These
results yield a wide range of new examples and show that the weak-DD2P exhibits a behaviour
genuinely different from that of other diameter two properties.

1. Introduction
The study of the geometry of slices, relatively weakly open sets, and convex combinations of
slices has played a central role in several developments in Functional Analysis. On one end of the
spectrum, the existence of slices (respectively, non-empty relatively weakly open subsets, convex
combinations of slices) of arbitrarily small diameter or arbitrarily small radius in every closed,
convex and bounded subset of a Banach space yields characterisations of fundamental geometric
properties, namely the Radon–Nikod´ym property (RNP), the convex point of continuity property
(CPCP), and strong regularity (SR), respectively.

At the opposite extreme, the study of large slices, relatively weakly open subsets, and convex
combinations of slices has been carried out through the so-called diameter two properties. Recall
that a Banach space X is said to have the strong diameter two property (SD2P), respectively the
diameter two property (D2P) or the local diameter two property (LD2P), if every convex combina-
tion of slices, respectively every non-empty relatively weakly open subset, or every slice of the unit
ball of X has diameter two. We refer the reader to [2, 6, 7, 12, 14] and the references therein for
background on these properties. Stronger notions, incorporating explicit diametrality conditions,
were later introduced in [13] as follows.
Definition 1.1. Let X be a Banach space. We say that X has the:
(1) the diametral local diameter two property (DLD2P) if given any slice S of BX, x ∈S ∩SX
and ε ∈R+, there exists y ∈S such that ∥x −y∥> 2 −ε.
(2) the diametral diameter two property (DD2P) if given any non-empty relatively weakly open
subset W of BX, x ∈W ∩SX and ε ∈R+, there exists y ∈W such that ∥x −y∥> 2 −ε.
Observe that a diametral version for the strong diameter two property was also defined in [13], but
V. Kadets proved that this property is equivalent to the classical Daugavet property [31, Theorem
2.3].
It is clear that each of the above properties implies its corresponding non-diametral version.
Moreover, while it is known that the LD2P does not imply the D2P [12], the question of whether
the DLD2P implies the DD2P (or even the D2P) remains open [13, Question 4.1].
More recently, localised versions of the above properties have been studied in depth, together
with the question of how these local notions influence the topology and geometry of a given Banach
space. To this end, we introduce some standard notation.
Definition 1.2. Let X be a Banach space and let x ∈SX. We say that x is
[3] a ∆-point if, for every slice S of BX with x ∈S and every ε > 0, there exists y ∈S such
that ∥x −y∥> 2 −ε;
[42] a super ∆-point if, for every non-empty relatively weakly open subset W of BX with x ∈W
and every ε > 0, there exists y ∈W such that ∥x −y∥> 2 −ε;
[42] a ccs ∆-point if, for every convex combination of slices C of BX with x ∈C and every
ε > 0, there exists y ∈C such that ∥x −y∥> 2 −ε.
The notions introduced above can be viewed as localisations of the diametral diameter two
properties. Indeed, a Banach space X has the DLD2P (respectively, the DD2P) if and only if every
point of SX is a ∆-point (respectively, a super ∆-point).
A substantial body of research has emerged on ∆-points and super ∆-points since their introduc-
tion, covering a wide range of directions: their study in specific classes of Banach spaces [35, 36, 48],
the analysis of the geometric consequences of their presence in a Banach space [1, 5, 42], and the
investigation of the minimal number of ∆-points or super ∆-points required to ensure diameter two
properties [3, 6, 21].
Besides, the question of how many ∆-points a Banach space needs in order to have the LD2P
has attracted considerable attention. Observe that the mere presence of ∆-points is not sufficient
in order to guarantee that a Banach space X has the LD2P as shows the fact that there exists
an equivalent renorming of ℓ1 with a ∆-point [1, Theorem 4.1], while the space satisfies the RNP.
However, if we require that the set of ∆-points generates the unit ball by taking closed convex hull,

The present work is concerned with the following question: given a Banach space X, how large
does the set of super ∆-points need to be for X to have the D2P?
Observe that requiring the set of super ∆-points to generate the unit ball by closed convex hull is
not enough. Indeed, in [21] it is proved that there exists a Banach space X isomorphic to L∞([0, 1])
where the set of super ∆-points of SX generates the unit ball by closed convex hull and, at the
same time, there are non-empty relatively weakly open subsets of arbitrarily small diameter [21,
Theorem 1.3]. This points out that the next step could be the possibility that the set of super
∆-points be weakly dense in the unit ball. This motivates us to consider the following definition.

It is immediate that a Banach space X has the weak-DD2P if and only if the set of super ∆-points

the DD2P implies the weak-DD2P, and the latter implies the D2P. We show in Example 2.3 that
none of these implications can be reversed.
The present work contributes to the ongoing programme aimed at understanding the fine geomet-
ric structure of Banach spaces through the behaviour of slices and relatively weakly open subsets of
the unit ball. While diameter two phenomena have been extensively studied in recent years, both
in their global and local forms, several natural questions remain open concerning the precise hierar-
chy and mutual independence of the corresponding properties. In this context, the weak diametral
diameter two property captures, as we will see, a genuinely intermediate geometric behaviour. Our
results reveal that this property cannot be reduced to any of the previously known diameter two
conditions and, at the same time, exhibits a remarkable stability under a wide range of classical
constructions. This interplay between geometric rigidity and flexibility leads, as shown throughout
the paper, to unexpected examples and sharp characterisations in fundamental classes of Banach
spaces, highlighting new structural features that were not detected by earlier approaches.
The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 is devoted to the study of the weak-DD2P in several
families of Banach spaces. In Proposition 2.1 we characterise the spaces C(K) with the weak-DD2P

the DD2P, the weak-DD2P and the convex-LDD2P, and it leads to a necessary condition for the
weak-DD2P formulated in terms of the extreme points of the dual unit ball (Proposition 2.5).
Next, we establish sufficient conditions for the weak-DD2P for L1-preduals and for unital uniform
algebras (Corollaries 2.9 and 2.10), as particular cases of a general result for spaces with a norming
ℓ1-structure (Theorem 2.7). Finally, the last subsection is devoted to the slightly more general

µ is atomless or the space X has the weak-DD2P. For L∞(µ, X), we prove that the weak-DD2P is
equivalent to any of the following conditions: µ is not purely atomic, µ has infinitely many atoms,
or X has the weak-DD2P.
For spaces of the form C(K, X) we obtain the following characterisation:
- if K′ = ∅, then C(K, X) has the weak-DD2P if and only if X has the weak-DD2P;
- if K′ is a non-empty finite set, then C(K, X) has the weak-DD2P if and only if X is
infinite-dimensional;
- if K′ is infinite, then C(K, X) has the weak-DD2P regardless of the space X.
As a noteworthy consequence of this description, we show that a space of the form C(K, X) may
have the weak-DD2P even when both C(K) and X fail to satisfy the property, as illustrated by
the example c(ℓ2) (see Remark 4.5).

Concerning the Banach space property that every convex combination of slices contains a ccs

(1) This property implies that every convex combination of slices has diameter two, since it

Theorem 4.1. Let (S, A, µ) be a complete σ-finite measure space and let X be a Banach space.
Then the following statements are equivalent:
(i) The space L1(µ, X) has the weak-DD2P.
(ii) The measure µ is atomless or the Banach space X has the weak-DD2P.
Proof. (ii)⇒(i). If µ is atomless, then L1(µ, X) has the Daugavet property (see, for instance, [33,

(Alternatively, one may apply [42, Theorem 4.8].) If, on the other hand, X has the weak-DD2P,
then the conclusion follows from Corollary 3.8.
(i)⇒(ii). It is a folklore result, as a consequence of the decomposition of a positive measure into

L1(µ, X) ∼= L1(ν, X) ⊕1 ℓ1(I, X),
where ν is atomless (possibly null) and I is a (possibly empty) index set. By Proposition 3.4, if
