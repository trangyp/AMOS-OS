ABSTRACT
Despite the growing number of compact broad-line (BL) active galactic nuclei (AGN) at z > 4 discovered by James Webb Space Telescope (JWST),
their formation and evolution remain poorly understood. In this paper, we investigate the large-scale environment of GN-77652, a compact BL
AGN at z = 5.229 observed as part of the JWST NIRSpec IFU Large Program BlackTHUNDER and complemented by deep multi-band NIRCam
imaging. GN-77652 lies in close proximity to a 12 kpc-long filament composed of multiple sources at z ≃5.23, spanning a remarkable range in
stellar masses (M⋆= 0.7 – 13 × 108 M⊙), gas phase metallicities (12 + log (O/H) = 7.6 – 8.5) and star formation rates (SFR = 0.4 – 6 M⊙yr−1). The
[O iii]λ5007 kinematics reveals a smooth large-scale velocity gradient centred on the massive (M⋆≃1.1 × 109 M⊙) and metal rich (Z ∼0.6 Z⊙)
system residing at the centre of the complex. In this source, only 2.4 kpc (projected) from GN-77652, [O iii]λ4363 auroral-line diagnostics provide
possible evidence for a second AGN. GN-77652 exhibits a shallow (−30 to +20 km s−1) velocity gradient that is consistent with disk rotation
according to dynamical modelling. The Lyman-Werner radiation field produced by the filament is too weak for the black hole (BH) in GN-77652
to have formed recently via direct collapse. However, the required conditions may have existed at earlier epochs, or alternative scenarios, like a
recoiling BH ejected from the filament, could also be plausible. Owing to their small separations, the multiple sources are expected to coalesce
in 150 −440 Myr, also motivating an exploration of the system’s future evolution through toy-model extrapolations and numerical simulations.
Although robust constraints cannot be placed, our analysis suggests that the compact BL AGN appearance of GN-77652 represents a transient
evolutionary phase, consistent with the apparent decline with redshift in number density of compact, red AGN identified with JWST.

1. Introduction
The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has discovered a
novel population of broad-line (BL) active galactic nuclei (AGN)
at z > 4 (Harikane et al. 2023; Greene et al. 2024; Maiolino
et al. 2024a; Matthee et al. 2024; Hviding et al. 2025; Tay-
lor et al. 2025; Juodžbalis et al. 2026a) of moderate luminosity
(Lbol ∼1044 −1046 erg s−1), powered by a central accreting black
hole (BH). These JWST-discovered AGN feature peculiar spec-
tral properties, such as X-ray (Ananna et al. 2024; Yue et al.
2024; Maiolino et al. 2025; Mazzolari et al. 2025) and radio
weakness (Gloudemans et al. 2025; Mazzolari et al. 2026). For
typical host galaxies with stellar mass of M⋆∼108 – 1010 M⊙,
single-epoch scaling relations imply BH masses of MBH ∼106 –
108 M⊙for these AGN, thus BHs 10 – 100 times more mas-
sive (Kocevski et al. 2023; Kokorev et al. 2023; Pacucci et al.
2023; Übler et al. 2023; Furtak et al. 2024; Juodžbalis et al.
2024; Maiolino et al. 2024a; Napolitano et al. 2025; Tripodi
et al. 2025) than expectations from local MBH −M⋆scaling re-
lations (e.g. Reines & Volonteri 2015; Kormendy & Ho 2013).
Assuming that the BH masses are accurately measured, this sig-
nificant offset could be partly a consequence of: selection biases
(i.e. finite detection limit and required detection of broad lines),
which would favour the detection of more massive, highly ac-

creting BHs (Volonteri et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025; Ziparo et al.
2026); and/or underestimated host stellar mass, due to difficul-
ties in constraining via spectral energy distribution (SED) mod-
elling the relative AGN and stellar contribution to the total ob-
served emission (Pérez-González et al. 2024; Ma et al. 2026b).
Yet, even attributing all continuum emission to the stars in the
SED modelling, Maiolino et al. (2024a) found that the JWST BL
AGN still remain well above the local expectations.
Early overmassive BHs are predicted to form by several the-
oretical models via a combination of seeding and growth mech-
anisms (e.g. see Volonteri et al. 2021; Schneider et al. 2023):
light mass seeds (∼102 M⊙) as remnants of metal-free stars (e.g.
Bromm et al. 2002; Madau et al. 2014; Pezzulli et al. 2016),
or intermediate mass seeds (∼103−4 M⊙) from runaway stellar
mergers or via gas accretion in dense star clusters (e.g. Devec-
chi & Volonteri 2009; Davies et al. 2011), accreting at super- or
nearly-Eddington rate; or heavy seeds (∼104−6 M⊙) formed as
direct collapse black holes (DCBHs; e.g. Loeb & Rasio 1994;
Begelman et al. 2006; Habouzit et al. 2016), also allowing for
sub-Eddington accretion.
Another striking hallmark of many JWST BL AGN is their
‘little dot’ appearance in rest-frame optical imaging (i.e. sizes
≲0.15′′, corresponding to < 1 kpc at z > 4), with most of them
being unresolved or marginally resolved by JWST. Recently,
Brazzini et al. (2026) proposed a unified picture that divides
compact JWST BL AGN into two categories based on their SED

JWST BL AGN are increasingly found to feature off-centre,

preted them as young, star-forming companions of modest stel-

Werner (LW) radiation intense enough to suppress H2 gas cool-
ing and trigger DCBHs. These results point towards a possible
heavy-seed channel for at least some JWST BL AGN.
In this paper, we present a detailed study of the large-
scale environment of JADES-GN+189.29323+62.199 (hereafter
GN-77652), a BL AGN at z = 5.229 that resides close to a
12 kpc-extended filament of massive sources at the same red-
shift (Fig. 1). Located in the GOODS-N field (Giavalisco et al.
2004), GN-77652 was first identified as a compact source in
deep multi-band JWST/NIRCam imaging, obtained as part of the
JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey (JADES; Eisenstein
et al. 2023b), and then spectroscopically followed up in JADES
(Bunker et al. 2024; Curtis-Lake et al. 2026; D’Eugenio et al.
2025a) with the JWST/NIRSpec Micro Shutter Array (MSA;
Ferruit et al. 2022). The NIRSpec MSA medium-resolution
spectra revealed a broad Hα component consistent with the
presence of a moderate-luminosity (Lbol ∼1044 erg s−1) BL
AGN, powered by an accreting BH of mass MBH ≃4.2 × 106 M⊙
(Juodžbalis et al. 2026a; see also Maiolino et al. 2024a). This
BH appears overmassive by more than two orders of magnitude
relative to the local MBH −M⋆relation of Reines & Volonteri
(2015), with a host stellar mass of (M⋆≃2 × 108 M⊙; Juodžbalis
et al. 2026a). Moreover, as is the case for the vast majority of
high-z JWST AGN, GN-77652 is undetected at X-ray (Maiolino
et al. 2025) and radio frequencies (Mazzolari et al. 2026), and
features a monotonically decreasing SED shape at increasing
wavelengths (βUV = −2.10, βopt = −1.56; Mazzolari et al., in
prep.), which classifies it as a LBD.
GN-77652 and its immediate (< 15 kpc) environment were
next observed in JWST/NIRSpec IFU mode (Jakobsen et al.
2022; Böker et al. 2022) by the BlackTHUNDER program (PID:
5015; PIs: H. Übler, R. Maiolino), which acquired the spatially
resolved high- (R ∼2700) and low-resolution (R ∼100) spec-
troscopy presented in this work. Exploiting all available JWST
data, in this paper we investigate gas kinematics, interstellar
medium (ISM) and stellar properties across the entire z ≃5.23
complex, finding differences by up to an order of magnitude in
M⋆, star formation rate (SFR) and gas phase metallicity for in-
dividual sources. The close proximity of the compact BL AGN
to the filament also allows us to explore early BH formation sce-
narios for GN-77652, and the future coalescence of the whole
complex into a single massive source.

2. Data
Figure 1 shows all JWST data of GN-77652: multi-band NIR-
Cam imaging and NIRSpec MSA spectroscopy of GN-77652
from JADES (JADES ID: 1077652); and the NIRSpec IFU
observations from BlackTHUNDER, covering the crowded
∼3.7′′ × 3.7′′ field-of-view (FoV) around GN-77652. In the fol-
lowing, we describe the various data and reduction procedure.
2.1. JADES JWST/NIRCam imaging
GN-77652 benefits from a rich dataset including deep NIRCam
multi-band imaging obtained as part of the JADES program 1181
in GOODS-N (PI: Eisenstein; Eisenstein et al. 2023b), which
consists of seven broad-band filters (F090W, F115W, F150W,
F200W, F277W, F356W, F444W) and two medium-band filters
(F335M, F410M). Additional NIRCam data from general ob-
server (GO) programs were included in the JADES Fifth Data
Release (DR5; Johnson et al. 2026), which released total multi-
band mosaics of the GOODS-N and GOODS-S fields. At the
location of GN-77652, supplementary coverage is provided by
the JWST GO programs 2674 (PI: Arrabal Haro) and 6434 (PI:
Eiichi Egami), leading to total exposure times of 3.1−6.6 hr and
2.0 −4.5 hr for the short- and long-wavelength filters, respec-
tively. F182M imaging is also available for GN-77652 from the
program 2674, but too shallow (∼0.9 hr) to robustly detect emis-
sion across the whole structure (see Fig. A.1). We retrieved the

2.2. JADES JWST/NIRSpec MSA spectroscopy
JADES also obtained NIRSpec MSA spectroscopy of GN-
77652, as part of program ID 1181 in the Medium JWST
GOODS-N tier of JADES (Eisenstein et al. 2023b). The expo-
sure times of the MSA observations of GN-77652 are about 3060
sec with PRISM-CLEAR, G140M-F070LP, G235M-F170LP,
G395M-F290LP and G395H-F290LP. The total exposures were
split into two repeated 3-position nodding sequences, using the
NRSIRS2 readout pattern. In this paper, we use the 1D spectra
of GN-77652 released as part of the JADES DR4 (Curtis-Lake
et al. 2026; Scholtz et al. 2026a), extracted from the central 3
pixels of each 2D spectrum (i.e. the central 0.3′′ region), which
maximises the signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio for compact sources.
Low- and medium-resolution spectra of GN-77652 from previ-
ous JADES reductions were analysed by Maiolino et al. (2024a)
and more recent ones by Juodžbalis et al. (2026a), establishing
the BL AGN nature of GN-77652 and providing a first char-
acterisation of its properties. Here, we perform an independent
spectral fitting of the available MSA data, using more recent re-
ductions and exploiting high-resolution (G395H-F290LP) data
for accurate line modelling, in particular of broad Hα emission.
Unlike in the NIRSpec high-resolution IFU observations from
BlackTHUNDER, Hα does not fall into the detector gap in the
NIRSpec MSA data, since in this mode the wavelength cutoffs
depend on the shutter position, in addition to the physical edges
of the two detectors and the separation between them.
2.3. BlackTHUNDER NIRSpec IFU observations
GN-77652 was next observed in NIRSpec IFU mode by the
BlackTHUNDER survey (PID: 5015; PIs: Übler, Maiolino),
on March 10-11, 2025 with a medium cycling 8-point dither-
ing pattern and a total integration time of 5.1 hr and 1 hr
with the G395H-F290LP (∆λ = 2.87 –5.14 µm; R ∼2000 – 3500)
and PRISM-CLEAR (∆λ = 0.6 – 5.3 µm; R ∼30 – 300) configu-
rations, respectively. We retrieved the raw data files from the
Barbara A. Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) por-
tal2, and processed them with the JWST Science Calibration
pipeline3 version 1.15.0 under the Calibration Reference Data
System (CRDS) context jwst_1293.pmap. To improve the data
reduction, we applied some modifications to the standard re-
duction steps (see Perna et al. 2023 for details). We corrected
the count-rate frames for 1/f noise via a polynomial fit, and
masked in the calibration stage 2 regions in each frame affected
by failed open MSA shutters, cosmic rays and bad pixels. Fol-
lowing D’Eugenio et al. (2024), we thus flagged the outliers
identified in individual exposures, and rejected the 98th (99.5th)
percentile of the resulting distribution in the grating (prism) data.
The final cubes were combined using the ‘drizzle’ method with
a pixel scale of 0.05′′, and cover a total 3.7′′ × 3.7′′ FoV.

3. GN-77652 and its complex environment
3.1. A overdense filament of multiple sources at z ≃5.23
As shown by Fig. 1, GN-77652 is surrounded by several sources
and extended structures within the NIRSpec IFU FoV, for which
spectroscopic redshifts can now be measured robustly. The main
filamentary structure, extending from NW to SE, is composed
of multiple sources (see also Fig. A.1) at the same redshift of
GN-77652 (z ≃5.23), as shown by the [O iii] channel map in the
left panel of Fig. 2. Five main sources can be visually identi-
fied in the [O iii] channel map: GN-77652 (labelled as A), and
four sub-sources along the filament (B, C, D, E). Interestingly,
the compact BL AGN is offset with respect to the overdense fil-
ament. In addition, seven foreground objects are detected in the
NIRSpec IFU low-resolution data (Appendix B).
To characterise the five main sources at z ≃5.23, we fit five
2D (spatial) Gaussian components (one for each source) to the
[O iii] and F444W continuum images, tracing gas and stars, re-
spectively. The main best-fit parameters are listed in Table 1,
and the resulting [O iii] (F444W) 2D Gaussian models are plot-
ted in the left panel of Fig. 2 as solid (dashed) elliptical apertures
with the major and minor axes equal to the best-fit FWHMs. The
average projected separations between closest pairs of sources
is 5 kpc. The integrated NIRSpec IFU low-resolution spectra of
the various sources (right panel), extracted using the [O iii]-based
apertures, exhibit differences in both the continuum shape and
the relative emission-line intensity, which point to intrinsically
different properties between the multiple sources.
Most of the sources feature a relative offset between the
[O iii] gas distribution and the F444W stellar continuum emis-
sion, and/or a more extended [O iii] morphology (see Table 1).
Such differences could be partially due to the gravitational in-
teraction between the sources, during which gas and stars are
affected differently because of their dissipative and collisionless
nature, respectively. The largest relative offset is observed in D
(δc = 0.23′′), where continuum emission is more elongated and
becomes brighter towards the South than [O iii]. Such difference
can also be appreciated in the left panel of Fig. 3, which dis-
plays the [O iii] flux map obtained from the spectral fitting of the
NIRSpec IFU high-resolution datacube, with the F444W contin-
uum emission contours drawn on the top. Moreover, the PSF-
corrected size of GN-77652 in [O iii] line emission (Re ∼0.1′′)
is larger by more than a factor of 2 than continuum emission
(Re ∼0.04′′), suggesting a more extended gas distribution.

3.3. Resolved rotation in GN-77652
The small inset in the middle panel of Fig. 3 displays a zoomed-
in view of the velocity field of GN-77652, centred on its sys-
temic velocity. The map reveals a shallow velocity gradient
(−30 km s−1 to 20 km s−1), roughly along the WE direction and
encompassing at least three resolution elements of the NIRSpec
PSF derived by Jones et al. (2026) (FWHM = 0.18′′). This kine-
matic pattern indicates possible resolved disk rotation, so far de-
tected in only a handful of JWST BL AGN at z > 4 (Übler et al.
2023, 2025; Parlanti et al. 2024; Juodžbalis et al. 2026b), as a
consequence of their compact morphology (barely resolved by
JWST) and the elusive emission of their host galaxy (e.g. Xiao
et al. 2025; Akins et al. 2026; Mazzolari et al. 2026).
To test whether the velocity gradient observed in GN-77652
is associated with disk rotation, we carry out a dynamical mod-
elling of GN-77652 using DysmalPy (Davies et al. 2004; Cresci
et al. 2009; Genzel et al. 2023; Übler et al. 2018; Price et al.
2021; Lee et al. 2025b; Jolly et al. 2026), a 3D forward-
modelling code that accounts for beam smearing, line broaden-
ing and finite spatial resolution. As detailed in the following,
we only fit the (high-resolution) [O iii] kinematics (i.e. velocity
and velocity dispersion corrected for instrumental broadening),
while fixing the morphological parameters derived in Sect. 3.1
and the centre of the model to the central flux-peak pixel.
Our model only includes a baryonic disk, since the dark mat-
ter (DM) component is expected to be negligible at such small
(< 1 kpc) radii. Moreover, as will be discussed in Sect. 5.3, the
whole source complex is likely to be embedded in a common
DM halo, which makes uncertain any constraint on the DM halo
properties derived from the stellar component (e.g. the stellar-to-
halo mass relation; Girelli et al. 2020). For the disk, we fix the
following structural parameters: the Sérsic index n = 1 (i.e. we
assume an exponential disk); the effective radius Re,d = 0.6 kpc
(inferred from [O iii] in Sect. 3.1); and the minor-to-major axis
ratio b/a = 0.95, as measured via 2D Gaussian fit to the sharp
F115W imaging. The scale height of the disk is tied to Re,d
(see Eq. 2 in Lee et al. 2025b). Assuming a thick disk with a
scale height-to-length ratio of q0 = 0.2 (e.g. Wuyts et al. 2016),
b/a = 0.95 corresponds to an inclination i = 18◦. We adopt a
flat prior on the intrinsic velocity dispersion σ0, with bounds
of 5 km s−1 and 100 km s−1 and assumed to be constant and
isotropic throughout the disk, and allow the position angle (PA)
to vary in the range PA = [250◦, 290◦] (measured with respect to
the blueshifted part, from North to East). Finally, we model the
PSF as a Gaussian profile with FWHM = 0.18′′, which approxi-
mately corresponds to the NIRSpec PSF derived by Jones et al.
(2026) at the [O iii] wavelength.

5.2. Possible BH formation scenarios for GN-77652
From our broad Hα best fit in the NIRSpec MSA high-
resolution data of GN-77652, we infer MBH = 1.1+0.4
−0.3 × 107 M⊙
(Appendix E). Thus, for its host stellar mass of M⋆= 1.7 ±
0.8 × 108 M⊙(inferred in Sect. 3.5), GN-77652 falls a factor
∼300 above the local MBH −M⋆relation by Reines & Volon-
teri (2015). According to theoretical models (e.g. Volonteri et al.
2021; Schneider et al. 2023), early overmassive BHs can form
from light (∼102 M⊙; e.g. Bromm et al. 2002; Madau et al. 2014;
Pezzulli et al. 2016) or intermediate seeds (∼103−4 M⊙; e.g. De-
vecchi & Volonteri 2009; Davies et al. 2011; Rantala et al. 2025;
Vergara et al. 2025), followed by super- or nearly-Eddington
growth; or, alternatively, heavy seeds (∼104−6 M⊙; e.g. DCBHs,
Loeb & Rasio 1994; Begelman et al. 2006; Habouzit et al. 2016)
accreting at sub-Eddington rate.
The frequent detection of UV-bright extended sources at a
few kiloparsec from JWST BL AGN (i.e in ∼43% of LRDs;
Baggen et al. 2026) has motivated several studies to explore
DCBHs as a possible BH formation mechanism for these ob-
jects (e.g. Cenci & Habouzit 2025; Baggen et al. 2026; Fei
et al. 2026; Pacucci et al. 2026). In this picture, the nearby ex-
tended UV-bright emission has been interpreted as young star-
forming companions, which could produce a LW radiation field
intense enough to suppress H2 gas cooling (Draine & Bertoldi
1996) and trigger rapid direct collapse, with no intermediate
gas fragmentation (Loeb & Rasio 1994; Bromm & Loeb 2003;
Volonteri & Rees 2005). In order to occur, this process requires
nearly metal-free gas (Z < 10−5 Z⊙) and an incident LW field
in the dimensionless form J21 (i.e. specific intensity divided by
1021 erg s−1 cm−2 Hz−1 sr−1) that exceeds a critical threshold
Jcrit ≈103 (Sugimura et al. 2014; Wolcott-Green et al. 2017).
The close proximity of the massive filament of sources gives
us the opportunity to explore whether this could have enabled
BH formation in GN-77652 via DCBH, despite the inferred
Z ∼0.4 Z⊙metallicity (Sect. 5.1), far from metal-free condi-
tions. As observed in many other JWST BL AGN (e.g. Übler
et al. 2023, 2024; Labbe et al. 2024; D’Eugenio et al. 2025b;
Jones et al. 2026), the enriched chemical abundance of GN-
77652 could indeed be a consequence of either enriched gas
from close companions, or SF taking place during or after the
initial collapse (e.g. Natarajan et al. 2017). Following Baggen

5.3. Rapid evolution to final coalescence
The compact configuration of the (12 kpc) extended complex,
consisting of multiple massive, kiloparsec-size sources with gas
velocity dispersion of about 50 km s−1, suggests that all sources
are gravitationally bound systems embedded in a common DM
halo. In fact, the stellar-to-halo mass relation at z = 5 by Shuntov
et al. (2025) predicts a halo mass of about 2 × 1011 M⊙for the
most massive sources B and D, yielding an expected virial ra-
dius Rvir ∼30 kpc and virial velocity Vvir ∼170 km s−1, which
correspond to a dynamical time τdyn = Rvir/Vvir ∼170 Myr. This
strongly supports that the whole source complex is within a com-
mon host halo, and will ultimately merge into a more massive
galaxy, as predicted by the ΛCDM hierarchical merging scenario
(Lacey & Cole 1993; Cole et al. 2000).

(Chandrasekhar 1943). At kiloparsec separations, strong gravi-

coalescence depending on the local dynamical timescale (e.g.
Lacey & Cole 1993; Boylan-Kolchin et al. 2008; Solanes et al.

expected τmerger for our z ≃5.23 source complex, we use Eq. 9
of Jiang et al. (2008), assuming a minimum and a maximum
primary-to-satellite mass ratio of 0.1 and 1, based on the in-

between closest pairs of sources, as measured, and a circular
velocity Vvir ∼170 km s−1. We thus find a range of τmerger =
150−440 Myr, consistent with N-body/hydrodynamical simula-
tions (e.g. Lotz et al. 2008), implying rapid coalescence by z ∼4.

better assess how common groups of multiple massive compo-

2025a). According to our toy model at constant tdepl = 0.7 Gyr
(violet dotted line) or constant SFR = 17 M⊙yr−1 (violet dashed
line), the gas reservoir of the overall system, within z = 4, will
get depleted by about a factor of 6 and 10, respectively, that is
well below the expectations for a M⋆= 1010 M⊙main-sequence
galaxy, yet comparable with other AGN at z ∼3. In compari-
son, the TNG100 group features a similar decreasing trend until
z = 4.2, although with absolute µgas values larger by a 2−3 factor.
From z = 4.2 to z = 3.5, the trend in µgas flattens around a value
of 2, as the system starts interacting with a more massive group,
with which it will merge after z = 3. Such interaction funnels gas
into the group while increasing the SFR and thus maintaining
µgas constant. The intense BH growth starting at z = 4 (Fig. 8,
left panel) is likely triggered by such gas accretion, which also
leads to reduced gas reservoir and SF after z = 3.5 (µgas = 0.34 at
z = 3), possibly due to AGN feedback at play.
Although we cannot robustly constrain the future evolution
of the system, the merger with the nearby (diverse and extended)
sources is likely to drive the transition of GN-77652 from its cur-
rent compact, red appearance to a more typical AGN. A similar
environment-driven evolutionary scenario has recently been pro-
posed by Mérida et al. (2026) for a possible emerging or fading
LRD, as part of a galaxy group at z = 5.12. Such a transient evo-
lutionary phase would be consistent with the apparent decline in
the number density of compact, red AGN identified with JWST
from the first few billion years to the present day (Kocevski et al.
2025; Inayoshi 2025; Ma et al. 2026a).
6. Conclusions
In this paper, we studied the large-scale environment of GN-
77652 (Fig. 1, dubbed A), a compact BL AGN at z =5.229 with
a nearby (a few kpc apart) extended (∼12 kpc) filament of four
main kiloparsec-size sources (B, C, D and E) at the same red-
shift of GN-77652. By combining JWST/NIRSpec IFU spec-
troscopy and multi-band JWST/NIRCam imaging, we investi-
gated the morphology, gas kinematics and the physical proper-
ties of the individual sources, and their mutual connection. The
main results are summarised in the following:
– The [O iii] and F444W continuum emission of the various
sources exhibit different morphologies and/or spatial offsets
(Fig. 2), partly as a possible consequence of the dissipative
and collisionless behaviour of gas and stars during gravita-
tional interactions, respectively.
– The large-scale [O iii] kinematics reveals a smooth South-to-
North velocity gradient (∆v < 70 km s−1; Fig. 3) across the
entire complex, centred on the source B, which is one of the
two most massive sources (along with D, M⋆∼109 M⊙) and
likely residing at the centre of the complex in the 3D space.
The continuous filamentary structure also suggests possible
gas flows converging into B, both along the main NW-SE
filament and from GN-77652, as an alternative explanation
to the observed kinematic pattern.
– We detect a shallow velocity gradient (−30 to +20 km s−1)
in GN-77652 (Fig. 4), indicative of resolved disk rotation.
Our [O iii] dynamical modelling yields Vrot/σ0 = 2.0 and
Mdyn = 5.2+3.1
−1.9 × 109 M⊙, consistent with M⋆+ Mgas.
– The various sources feature diverse properties (Figs. 5 and
7), despite individually following main galaxy scaling re-
lations. In particular, they span about one order of magni-
tude in stellar mass (M⋆= 0.7 −13 × 108 M⊙), SFR = 0.4 –
6 M⊙yr−1 and gas phase metallicity (12 + log(O/H) = 7.6 –
8.5), suggesting they are in distinct evolutionary stages.
– The source B is one of the most massive systems (M⋆=
(11 ± 2) × 108 M⊙) and the metal-richest one of the com-
plex (12 + log(O/H) = 8.49 ± 0.15), only 2.4 kpc apart (in
projection) from GN-77652. The [O iii]λ4363 auroral dia-
grams also show evidence for AGN ionisation in this source
(Fig. 6), likely due to another AGN residing in B. If con-
firmed, this would be one of the few dual (or multiple) AGN
at a few kiloparsec separations discovered at z > 3 so far.
– The LW field emitted by the filament is too weak for the BH
in GN-77652 to have formed as a DCBH. More favourable
conditions for direct collapse could have taken place in the

– The multiple sources should coalesce in 150 – 440 Myr (i.e.

Appendix C: Spectral fitting
For the spectral modelling of the NIRSpec datacubes of GN-
77652, we use the fitting code presented in Marasco et al. (2020),
designed for IFU data. The same procedure is applied to the in-
tegrated NIRSpec IFU spectra extracted in Sect. 3.1 and to the
JADES NIRSpec MSA spectra of GN-77652. To accurately es-
timate the best-fit values and errors of the various parameters,
we adopt a Monte Carlo (MC) approach and repeat the spectral
fit 200 times, after randomly perturbing the observed spectrum
with its associated noise. From the final resulting parameter dis-
tributions, we then adopt the 50th percentile and the standard
deviation as the best-fit value and associated error, respectively.
The best-fit results are summarised in Tables C.1 and C.2.
Appendix C.1: NIRSpec IFU low-resolution data
As shown in Fig. 2, we detect emission lines and continuum
emission over the full 0.6 – 5.3 µm wavelength range of NIRSpec
PRISM-CLEAR data (i.e. rest-frame 0.960 – 8500 Å), at variable
relative intensity across the source complex. Since the NIRSpec
spatial resolution varies with wavelength (from FWHM = 0.13′′
at 1 µm to FWHM = 0.22′′ at 5 µm; D’Eugenio et al. 2024), we
first create a PSF-matched version of the PRISM-CLEAR dat-
acube by using a 2D Gaussian kernel to match the FWHM of the
NIRSpec PSF at 4.1 µm (FWHM4.1µm ∼0.16′′; D’Eugenio et al.
2024), that is the observed wavelength of Hα, the reddest bright
emission line detected. In addition, we spatially smooth the PSF-
matched data in each spectral channel by means of a Gaussian
kernel of width σk = 0.05′′ (i.e. 1 spatial pixel, corresponding
to a FWHM of 0.12′′). Since the kernel size is smaller than
FWHM4.1µm ∼0.16′′, this step enhances the visibility of faint re-
gions, without deteriorating the spatial resolution of the data.
After these preliminary steps, we perform the spectral fit-
ting by using the Penalized PiXel-Fitting code (pPXF; Cappel-
lari 2023) to simultaneously model continuum and line emis-
sion in each pixel, aiming to accurately reproduce (and then
remove) the former. In particular, continuum emission is mod-
elled through a linear combination of stellar templates from the
MILES SSP library (Vazdekis et al. 2010), plus a first-degree
polynomial to account for AGN emission in GN-77652, and
possibly in the other unknown sources; whereas emission lines
are fitted with Gaussian components constrained to have the
same kinematics (i.e. v and σ). The [O iii]λ5007/[O iii]λ4959,
[O i]λ6300/[O i]λ6364 and [Nii]λ6584/[Nii]λ6548 line ratios are
fixed to their theoretical values of 2.98, 3.08 and 2.94 (Oster-
brock & Ferland 2006), respectively, based on atomic physics.
After running the fit with pPXF, we subtract pixel by pixel the
best-fit continuum model from the PRISM-CLEAR datacube,
and obtain a continuum-subtracted cube that only contains emis-
sion lines. With this cube free of continuum emission, we per-
form a refined modelling of the emission lines with a custom
python procedure, benefiting from more flexibility compared to
pPXF. To deal with the NIRSpec PRISM-CLEAR spectral reso-
lution varying with wavelength (i.e. R ∼30 – 300), we separately
fit with independent kinematics the emission lines bluer and red-
der than 3.4 µm (i.e. 5500 Å rest-frame), still adopting fixed line
ratios for the [O iii], [O i] and [Nii] line doublets. Best-fit model
plots are shown in Fig. C.1.
For the source C, our low-resolution data best fit likely un-
derestimates the Hβ flux (Fig. C.1, lower left panel), as a conse-
quence of the poor quality of the data. Therefore, for this source,
we derive the low-resolution Hβ flux from [O iii] by assuming
the same Hβ/[O iii] ratio as measured in the high-resolution spec-
trum (see Table C.2) and a 30% error.

Appendix F.1: Toy model until z = 4
We extrapolate the evolution of the complex from z = 5.23 to
z = 4 (i.e. over 400 Myr) using a toy model that relies on simpli-
fied assumptions, including no further gas or star accretion onto
the system. All the multiple components (i.e. GN-77652 plus the
sources along the filament) are considered as part of a whole sin-
gle system, with M⋆, Mgas and SFR equal to the sum of the indi-
vidual source values (M⋆= 2.7 × 109 M⊙, Mgas = 1.2 × 1010 M⊙,
SFR10 = 17 M⊙yr−1). The global system is also assumed to host
a central BH equal to that of GN-77652 (MBH = 1.1 × 107 M⊙,
λEdd = 0.18, derived in Appendix E).
We consider two possible growth modes for the host galaxy,
at constant SFR = 17 M⊙yr−1 and at constant tdepl = 0.7 Myr.
For the central BH, we instead assume accretion at constant
λEdd = 0.18, with an AGN duty cycle of 10%. Under these as-
sumptions over a time frame of about 400 Myr, we obtain
a BH growth of 17% (MBH = 1.3 × 107 M⊙at z = 4), along
with a host stellar mass growth of only a factor 2 – 3 as-
suming constant gas depletion (M⋆= 8.2 × 109 M⊙) or SFR
(M⋆= 1.0 × 1010 M⊙). The two evolutionary paths lead to
Mgas = 6.3 × 109 M⊙and Mgas = 4.5 × 109 M⊙, corresponding to
µgas = 0.77 and µgas = 0.45, respectively.
Appendix F.2: Predictions from numerical simulations
For independent insights into the possible evolution of the source
complex, we look at the predictions for similar systems from
the numerical hydrodynamic simulation IllustrisTNG (Springel
et al. 2018; Nelson et al. 2018; Pillepich et al. 2018; Naiman
et al. 2018; Marinacci et al. 2018). In particular, we consider the
intermediate cosmological volume with side length of 100 Mpc
(TNG100). In the snapshot at z = 5 (snapshot 17), we search
for groups containing at least three subhalos with similar stellar
masses (0.5 × 108 M⊙< M⋆< 20 × 108 M⊙) and with galaxy-
to-galaxy distances smaller than 15 kpc, similar to the observed
source complex. We thus find five groups satisfying these cri-
teria, among which: one is composed of galaxies that are close
satellites of a much more massive system (M⋆> 1010.7 M⊙);
three reside at the outskirts of massive proto-clusters and are
surrounded by several lower mass galaxies; finally, one isolated
compact group consisting of four galaxies with M⋆= 0.51−8.1×
108 M⊙forms a structure with less than 10 kpc radius, plus other
two lower mass satellites (M⋆∼0.4 × 108 M⊙) at larger (10
and 31 kpc) distances. Since no other system at z ≃5.23 is found
within Rvir = 30 kpc, as expected for the halo hosting the whole
complex (Sect. 5.3), according to the JADES DR5 photometric
redshifts (Robertson et al. 2026), we focus on the evolution of the
only isolated group of galaxies (group ID 1010 in the snapshot
17), which more closely resembles the observed source complex.
To derive the gas masses, we only consider gas particles that
are forming stars (i.e. SFR > 0 M⊙yr−1) to best match what is
measured in observations. We follow the evolution of the group
forward in time by identifying its descendants in the the SubLink
merger tree (see Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2015). Already by the
next simulation snapshot (z = 4.6), three galaxies of the group
coalesce with the central galaxy, while other two by the further
next one (z = 4.4), thus reaching final coalescence in less than
200 Myr. At z = 3, the galaxy then undergoes a major merger
with a much larger system, which dramatically affects its subse-
quent evolution and properties. For this reason, in Sect. 5.4, we
only track the evolution of this group from z = 5 to z = 3.
