Barium-based Rydberg atom quantum technologies with long Rydberg coherence
Xiao-Feng Shi
Center for Theoretical Physics and School of Physics and Optoelectronic Engineering, Hainan University, Haikou 570228, China
(Dated: July 14, 2026)
The short Doppler-limited coherence time of the laser-excited Rydberg state, usually orders of
magnitude shorter than the lifetime of the Rydberg state, hinders the Rydberg-mediated quantum
technologies.
Here, we show that a 649 nm −658 nm two-photon excitation of the 6sng 1G4
Rydberg state from a long-lived d-orbital clock state of barium can be achieved with a two-photon
wavevector that is tiny, which effectively removes the Doppler-limited decoherence. Moreover, the
6sng 1G4 Rydberg state has strong dipole-dipole interaction due to small F¨orster defect with nearby
Rydberg states and possesses long radiative lifetime. These can benefit quantum computing based
on individually trapped neutral atoms, and can enable long-lived Rydberg polaritons in atomic
media, which brings fresh opportunities in all-optical quantum information.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Rydberg interactions between neutral atoms [1] can
be used for studying quantum information [2–7] and
quantum many-body physics [8–12], usually with atomic
qubits trapped in optical microtraps [13–28] or with pho-
tonic qubits hosted in atomic media [29–35]. A stumbling
block in these studies is the short ground-Rydberg coher-
ence time T2,gR. The Rydberg Doppler dephasing time
T2,D is the major contribution to the ground-Rydberg
coherence time T2,gR compared to Rydberg radiative de-
cay, atom position variations, and laser noise [22]. T2,D
is inversely proportional to k√Teff [13, 36–38] with k the
wavevector of the Rydberg excitation and Teff the tem-
perature characterizing the motional state of the atom.
When a two-photon Rydberg excitation is used with two
lasers of wavelengths k1 and k2, the smallest possible
k is |k1 −k2| when the two lasers counter propagate.
In Rydberg excitation of alkali-metal atoms like rubid-
ium and cesium, the smallest two-photon k is about
5.0 µm−1 with two counter-propagating lasers of 852 nm
and 509 nm for cesium [39], still leading to T2,D which is
orders of magnitude smaller than the radiative lifetime
of Rydberg states.

The
effect of atomic motion is dominated by the shot-to-shot
Doppler-induced detuning [41], which is an alternative
term for Doppler dephasing [13].
In quantum nonlinear optics based on Rydberg atoms,
experimentally measured T2,gR was on the order of mi-
crosecond and shown to be dominated by T2,D [29, 30,
34, 42, 43]. This makes cooling atoms to ultra low tem-
peratures necessary since storage of single photons in
atomic media is usually required in Rydberg-mediated
quantum information processing [34, 44]. Alternatively,
state mapping between nearby Rydberg states can miti-
gate motional dephasing in Rydberg polaritons [45, 46].
An effective approach to enhancing Rydberg coherence
can be with barium atoms. Barium has a metastable low-
lying d-orbital 1D2 clock state from which the 6sng 1G4
Rydberg states can be excited with a tiny k ≈0.14 µm−1
via two counter-propagating lasers of 649 nm and 658 nm.
Compared to the smallest possible k with rubidium or ce-
sium and also due to the relatively large mass of barium,
the intrinsic T2,D by using barium can be enhanced by
about 40 times with similar motional temperature Teff,
which effectively removes the motional dephasing of Ry-
dberg states.
Further, compared to usually employed
alkali-metal atoms as shown in Table I, the 6sng 1G4
barium Rydberg state possesses long radiative lifetimes.
This brings hope to improve Rydberg-mediated gate fi-
delity based on atomic qubits [2–7], makes it possible to
realize all optical quantum information based on Rydberg
polaritons[54, 55], and can improve the controllability
in Rydberg-mediated quantum many-body physics [4, 8–
12].
Beside of long Rydberg T2,D and T1, there are two more
strengths in the 6sng 1G4 barium Rydberg state and its
excitation from a low-lying 1D2 clock state. (i) Atoms
in the 6sng 1G4 state will experience a strong dipole-
dipole interaction due to very small F¨orster defect with
the nearby 6snf 1F o
3 Rydberg state [53]. (ii) For the low-
lying 1D2 clock state from which we excite the 6sng 1G4
Rydberg state, it has a strong electric quadrupole allowed
transition to the ground state, enabling fast single-qubit
operations if we encode qubit with the ground and the
low-lying 1D2 clock states.
One thing we need to consider is the relatively short
radiative lifetime of the low-lying 1D2 clock state, in the
range of [242, 296] ms [56]. Thankfully, there is a nearby
low-lying 3D3 clock state which has a lifetime of several
tens of second, and can be excited from the low-lying 1D2
clock state with a strong two-photon Raman transition.

D.
Comparison with existing Doppler-robust
schemes
It is useful to compare the current theory with typical
Doppler-robust CZ gate schemes in, e.g., Refs. [109, 110].
Both Ref. [109] and Ref. [110] proposed to reverse the
Doppler shift either by inverting the direction of the Ry-
dberg laser or by inverting the direction of the atomic
velocity along the Rydberg laser. The latter approach is
based on that the motion of the atom in the harmonic
optical tweezer potential is periodic, so the velocity of
each atom is reversed after each half of the trap period.
To mitigate several disadvantages associated with this
approach of inverting the velocity of the atomic motion,
Ref. [110] further proposed to modulate the trapping po-
tential sinusoidally in time. For the approach of invert-
ing the direction of the Rydberg laser, the relative path
lengths of the two different Rydberg lasers require ex-
perimental calibration.
Both Ref. [109] and Ref. [110]
designed novel Doppler robust gates by executing two
Rydberg pulses, where the Doppler shift in the first pulse
is opposite to that in the second pulse. A compromise is
that the gate duration would be a little longer compared
to their gate schemes without considering suppressing
Doppler shift. In comparison, the theory in this paper
does not depend on reversing the Doppler shift, so that
using the fast gate schemes in, e.g., Ref. [28], would be
enough to suppress the Doppler shift for our case.
The suppression of the Doppler dephasing by the the-
ory in this work depends on the cooling of atoms, while
the Doppler robust gates in Refs. [109, 110] appear not so
dependent on cooling of atoms. For example, Fig. 5(b) of
Ref. [110] shows that their Doppler robust gate nearly has
a constant infidelity 10−4 for motional temperature Teff
from 0 µK to 45 µK. For the theory in this paper, Eq. (4)

Of course, if the decay of Rydberg state can
take the atom to metastable states other than the qubit

principle means that the decay of the barium Rydberg

the extremely long lifetime of barium 6s53g 1G4.
Finally, it is unclear whether the theory in Refs. [109,
110] can suppress motional dephasing of Rydberg po-
laritons.
The theory in this article is based on small
wavevector of the Rydberg excitation, and thus is useful
for suppressing the fast motional dephasing of Rydberg
polaritons which is of practical importance in Rydberg-
mediated quantum nonlinear optics [29–35].
IV.
STRONG RYDBERG INTERACTION
A special character of the Rydberg interaction of the

V.
PROSPECTS ON QUANTUM NONLINEAR
OPTICS WITH BARIUM
It was shown in Ref. [54] that quantum information
processing based on Rydberg polaritons can be realized
based on Rydberg interactions. However, typical experi-
ments involving Rydberg polaritons with rubidium or ce-
sium showed that their T2,D is on the order of microsec-
ond [29, 30, 34, 42, 43]. These make it challenging to
carry out high-fidelity photon-photon entangling gates
based on Rydberg polaritons with cesium or rubidium.
Below, we show that it is possible to explore quantum
nonlinear optics based on barium-based Rydberg polari-
tons with long T2,D.
A.
Rydberg polaritons with long T2,D
The transition in Eq. (3) form a ladder-type three-level
system which is useful for realizing quantum nonlinear
optics with Rydberg polaritons [29–35, 46, 114, 115]. In
these applications, a single photon can be loaded into
an atomic medium as the gate photon, then sending an-
other photon as the target photon through results in an
interaction effect. If there is no Rydberg interaction, the
target photon can propagate through without absorption
when the detuning ∆1F3 at 5d6p 1F o
3 is zero. But be-
cause of Rydberg interaction, the target photon can be
blocked, experiencing loss, or even be completely scat-
tered when ∆1F3 = 0 and the Rydberg interaction is
strong [32, 33, 114, 116]. When ∆1F3 is larger than the
linewidth of 5d6p 1F o
3 , an interaction-induced phase can
appear [34, 115, 117]. In order to have a desired π phase
imprinted so as to realize a CNOT gate [34], the opti-
cal depth of the atomic medium should be large, i.e., the
density of the medium should be high. This causes slow
group velocity for the target photon, so that the time
for the target photon to fly through the atomic medium
can be several microseconds [34].
For rubidium or ce-
sium, the Doppler-limited coherence time of the stored
gate photon in Eq. (4) is on the order of 20 µs with an
atomic temperature 1 µK as shown in Table I. Because
of this, even though cooling atoms to a temperature of
order of 1 µK is very challenging, such cooling is nec-
essary for carrying out deterministic photonic quantum
information processing with alkali metal atoms. For in-
stance, the atomic media were cooled to around 0.5 µK
in Refs. [34, 115].
The applications of Rydberg-mediated quantum non-
linear optics [32–34, 114–117] with barium can benefit
from the long coherence time if one chooses the transi-
tion in Eq. (3). In Table I, one can see that for the same
motional temperature in the atom gas, the motional co-
herence time of barium is about 47 (37) times that of
rubidium (cesium). For an operation time around 5 µs
with the CNOT gate of Ref. [34], the motional coherence
time of barium T2,D ∼90 µs with a relatively high atom
temperature Teff = 100 µK is still long enough to preserve
the capability to achieve high fidelity. But if we choose
rubidium to achieve T2,D ∼90 µs, we should cool atoms
to Teff = 0.05 µK. Thus using the ladder-type transi-
tion in Eq. (3) for Rydberg-mediated quantum nonlinear
optics has a potential advantage.

VI.
DISCUSSIONS
The barium atom has two valence electrons, which is
similar to ytterbium, for which simultaneous trapping
of the ground and high-n Rydberg states were demon-
strated [121]. The reason for this possibility is that the
polarizability for the Rydberg state consists of two parts,
that of the inner valence electron, and that of the outer
Rydberg electron.
In particular, for Rydberg state of
large n and trap waist of order of 1 µm, there is lit-
tle overlap between the Rydberg electron and the trap
light [121]. Then, the polarizability of the Rydberg atom
is mainly from that of the inner valence electron. There-
fore, it is possible to simultaneously trap both the ground
state and the Rydberg state with an optical tweezer of
equal depth for both states.
For the purpose of trapping the 6sng 1G4 barium state,
the concern of such feasibility is whether the polarizabil-
ity of the ion core can be large enough. To analyze this,
we note that for trapping ytterbium Rydberg states in
Ref. [121], the polarizability of the Yb+ core is mainly
from the transitions 6s →6p1/2 and 6s →6p3/2, and
the lifetime of Yb+6p1/2 is 8.12 ns [122]. For 6sng 1G4,
the polarizability of the Ba+ core arises also mainly
from the transitions 6s →6p1/2 and 6s →6p3/2, where
the lifetimes of Ba+6p1/2 and Ba+6p3/2 are 7.9 ns and
6.32 ns[123], respectively. These data show that the tran-
sitions from the ground to the 6p1(3)/2 states can be a
little stronger for barium compared to ytterbium, and,
hence, it is possible to have a large polarizability for the
6s ion core. Therefore, a simultaneous trapping of the
ground and 6sng 1G4 Rydberg states should be possi-
ble. Further, SM [67] shows that there are several magic
wavelengths in the range [665, 1634] nm for trapping the
two qubit states, therefore simultaneous trapping of the
ground and 6sng 1G4 Rydberg states is possible.
Since the trapping of high-n 6sng 1G4 Rydberg states
is mainly due to the polarizability of the inner valence
electron, it is in principle possible to find triple-magic
trapping condition for the two qubit states and the high-
n 6sng 1G4 Rydberg state. In Ref. [124], simultaneous
trapping of the ground and a pair of metastable low-
lying states of 88Sr was experimentally realized via fine
tuning of wavelength and polarization of the trapping
light. In theory, it is even possible to trap four states
simultaneously [125]. Therefore, simultaneous trapping
of the 6sng 1G4, the two qubit states, and a fourth state
such as 6s5d 3D3 may also be feasible. Then, the state
6s5d 3D3 for shelving the qubit state |1⟩, as studied fol-
lowing Eq. (2) can be trapped together with the two
qubit states and the Rydberg state, enabling prospects
of higher-fidelity control for the barium-based Rydberg-
atom technologies.
VII.
CONCLUSION
We have shown that by using barium-138 for defin-
ing qubits with a metastable d-orbital state, it is pos-
sible to realize a two-photon Rydberg excitation with a
small motion-induced Rydberg Doppler dephasing rate,
which is about 47 (37) times smaller than the smallest
Rydberg dephasing rate in a two-photon Rydberg exci-
tation of rubidium (cesium) at a similar motional tem-
perature. Moreover, the excited barium Rydberg states
possess long radiative lifetimes and strong dipole-dipole
interactions. These strengths mean that it is possible to
use barium atoms for realizing quantum entangling gates
with high fidelity. They also bring opportunities to real-
ize all-optical quantum information processing which has
been hindered by the fast Rydberg dephasing.
