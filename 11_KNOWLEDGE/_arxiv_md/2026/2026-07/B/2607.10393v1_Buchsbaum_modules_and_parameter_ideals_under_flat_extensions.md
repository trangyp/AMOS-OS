Abstract 1 Let (R, m) and (S, n) be commutative Noetherian local rings, let φ : R →S
be a flat local homomorphism. In this paper, we first characterize the ascent and descent
of Buchsbaum modules and generalized Cohen-Macaulay modules via the flat extension φ.
Then, we provide relationships between the parameter ideals of a finitely generated R-module
M and those of M ⊗R S under the assumption that n = mS.
1
Introduction
Throughout this paper, let (R, m) and (S, n) denote commutative Noetherian local rings.
Let φ : R →S be a flat local homomorphism and M a finitely generated R-module.
Let dimR(M) = d. For a system of parameters (s.o.p for short) x1, . . . , xd of R, we set
I(x1, . . . , xd; M) = ℓR(M/(x1, . . . , xd)M) −e(x1, . . . , xd; M).
If I(x1, . . . , xd; M) is a constant not depending on the choice of s.o.p x1, . . . , xd, then M is
said to be a Buchsbaum module. The formal definition and systematic theory of Buchsbaum
modules were introduced and developed by J. St¨uckrad and W. Vogel in the 1970s, see [19].
Set I(M) = sup I(x1, . . . , xd; M), where the supremum runs over all s.o.p x1, . . . , xd of M.
Following N. T. Cuong, P. Schenzel and N. V. Trung [6], if I(M) < ∞then M is said to be a
generalized Cohen-Macaulay module. Note that M is generalized Cohen-Macaulay if and only
if Hi

are regular then so is S; if S is regular then R is regular, but S/mS need not be regular.
Also, the ascent and descent under a flat extension for certain classes of modules, such as
Noetherian, Artinian, and injective modules, have been investigated in [3, 7, 8].
It is well-known that M ⊗R S is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if M and S/mS are Cohen-
Macaulay. The first purpose of this paper is to characterize the ascent and descent via the flat
extension φ : R →S for the classes of Buchsbaum modules and generalized Cohen-Macaulay
modules. The following theorem is the first main result of this paper.
Theorem 1.1. Suppose that M is not Cohen-Macaulay. Then
(a) M ⊗R S is generalized Cohen-Macaulay if and only if M is generalized Cohen-Macaulay
and dim(S/mS) = 0. In this case we have I(M ⊗R S) = ℓS(S/mS) I(M).
(b) M ⊗R S is Buchsbaum if and only if M is Buchsbaum and n = mS. In this case we have
I(M ⊗R S) = I(M).
The second purpose of this paper is to study the relationship between parameter ideals
of M and parameter ideals of M ⊗R S. We restrict our work to the assumption n = mS in
the hope of obtaining an elegant relationship between them.
Denote by XM and XM⊗RS respectively the sets of all parameter ideals of M and of
M ⊗R S. Since n = mS, it follows that the extension qS is a parameter ideal of M ⊗R S
for every parameter ideal q of M. Therefore, we have a map φe : XM →XM⊗RS sending
q to qS (we call φe to be the extension map induced by φ). By the faithful flatness of φ,
the extension map φe is always injective, but it need not be a bijection, see Example 2.4.
Note that φe is bijective if and only if each parameter ideal of M ⊗R S is an extension of a
parameter ideal of M.
It is natural to ask whether the contraction Q ∩R is a parameter ideal of M, provided
that Q is a parameter ideal of M ⊗R S. In general, the answer is negative. We will construct
in Example 2.4 a flat local homomorphism (R, m) →(S, n), where R and S are regular,
n = mS and there exists a parameter ideal Q of S such that Q ∩R is not a parameter ideal
of R. This suggests studying the following set

not bijective. If we choose K to be a field extension of infinite degree over Q (for example,
K = R), then ℓR(S/mS) = ∞. For an integer n > 1, if we choose K = Q[α] to be a
field extension of degree n over Q (for example α =
n√
2, the real n-th root of 2), then
ℓR(S/mS) = n.
The result of Theorem 2.7(b) is not sharp in some sense. Even if R is not Gorenstein and
ℓR(S/mS) is arbitrarily large, the contraction map φc can still be a bijection.
Example 2.9. Let t be an indeterminate, R = Q[[t3, t4, t5]] and S = K[[t3, t4, t5]], where
Q ⊂K ⊆R are field extensions. Let m = (t3, t4, t5)R and n = (t3, t4, t5)S denote the
maximal ideals of R and S, respectively. Then the embedding map φ : R →S is a flat

Proof. Let q = t3R. Then q is a parameter ideal of R. We can check that (0 :R/q m) is a

by Remark 2.6(b).
Let M be a finitely generated R-module of dimension d. As dim(R) = 1, we have d ≤1.
Note that the contraction map φc : X∗
M⊗RS →XM is always surjective. If d = 0 then there
is nothing to do. So we can assume that d = 1. Let Q, Q′ be parameter ideals of M ⊗R S
and let q be a parameter ideal of M such that Q ∩R = Q′ ∩R = q. We will show that
Q = Q′ = qS. Suppose to contrary that Q ̸= qS. Since d = 1 and dimS(M ⊗R S) = 1, we

the contraction map φc is injective.
