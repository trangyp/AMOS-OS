ABSTRACT FRIEDRICHS OPERATORS
S. K. SONI
Abstract. The theory of boundary quadruples and boundary triples is well-studied for sym-
metric and skew-symmetric operators and in general for dual-pairs. This paper adapts a suitable
version for abstract Friedrichs operators and addresses the following questions: which param-
eters yield bijective realisations, and which parameters yield m−accretive realisations.
We
study a boundary-quadruple framework in which closed realisations are parametrised by closed
relations in a boundary space. This yields the intrinsic criterion
TΘ is bijective ⇐⇒K = Θ ∔Γ(ker T1) .
For bounded operator parameters φ : K1 →K0 in the boundary space, we introduce the
reference operator
Q0 = Γ1(Γ0|ker T1)−1 ,
prove that ∥Q0∥< 1, and obtain the exact criterion
Tφ is bijective ⇐⇒IK0 −φQ0 is bijective .
Consequently, every non-expansive parameter gives a bijective realisation with signed boundary
map, which is also m−accretive.
An existence criterion for boundary quadruples and boundary triples is established in terms
of (V)-boundary conditions.
The multiplicity of M−operators associated with a fixed (V)-
boundary condition is addressed in an explicit way and a parametrisation of such operators
is given. The theory is illustrated by a first-order ordinary differential operator and by the
stationary diffusion equation, where Q0 is identified as a Cayley transform of the Dirichlet-to-
Neumann operator.
1. Introduction
The theory of Friedrichs systems provides a unified framework for first-order formulations
of elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic problems; see the classical work of Friedrichs [19], the
abstract Hilbert-space formulation in [18, 5], and a nice historical exposition in [22]. In the
abstract setting one starts with a joint pair of minimal operators (T0, eT0) and studies closed
restrictions of the maximal operators T1 and eT1. The choice of a realisation is encoded by
a subspace of the maximal space (the graph space). Intrinsic descriptions of such subspaces
through (V)-, (X)- and (M)-boundary conditions were developed in [18, 1, 2]; the corresponding
classical formulations go back to [19, 20, 24].

The present results differ from the canonical von Neumann parametrisation in [17]: here the
boundary coordinates are fixed in advance and need not be the kernel coordinates from the von
Neumann decomposition. In such arbitrary coordinates, boundedness of the parameter alone
does not guarantee bijectivity; the operator Q0 measures exactly the additional compatibility.
Likewise, the intrinsic existence and construction results for M-operators in [2, Theorem 8] and
[13, Theorem 5.4 and Remark 5.5] are translated here into the prescribed boundary coordinates,
where the admissible negative spaces and a canonical associated family of M-operators are
described explicitly.
Non-expansive parameters correspond exactly to (V)-boundary conditions, while unitary pa-
rameters correspond to self-orthogonal subspaces with respect to the boundary form. We de-
termine precisely when a pair of subspaces satisfying (V)-boundary conditions can be realised
as the kernel-pair of a boundary quadruple, and when a self-orthogonal subspace satisfying
(V)-boundary condition can be realised as the kernel of a boundary triple map. For a fixed
(V)-boundary condition we parametrise the compatible maximal non-positive boundary spaces
by non-expansive operators ψ satisfying the explicit condition that IK1 −ψφ be bijective, which
leads to the parametrisation of the corresponding (M)-operators.

8. Concluding Remarks
We have developed a boundary quadruple description of bijective realisations of abstract
Friedrichs operators. The relation-level criterion
K = Θ ∔Γ(ker T1)
identifies the intrinsic geometric obstruction to bijectivity. For bounded graph parameters this
obstruction is represented by the strict contraction
Q0 = Γ1
 Γ0|ker T1
−1,
and the exact criterion becomes the invertibility of IK0 −φQ0. The canonical von Neumann
parametrisation is recovered when Q0 = 0, while arbitrary prescribed boundary quadruples
generally require the additional factor.
The (V)-boundary conditions are parametrised by the non-expansive parameters and the self-
orthogonal case by unitary parameters. For a fixed (V)-condition, compatible negative boundary
spaces are characterised by the bijectivity of IK1 −ψφ. Choosing the orthogonal complement of
the minimal space produces a canonical family of associated (M)-operators. The ODE example
computes the transfer operator explicitly, and the diffusion example identifies it with a Cayley
transform of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator.
A natural continuation is to replace the distinguished point 0 by a spectral parameter and
to study a family Q(λ). Such a construction should lead to Kre˘ın-type resolvent formulas and
to spectral criteria expressed through I −φQ(λ), in analogy with Weyl-function methods for
symmetric operators and adjoint pairs.
