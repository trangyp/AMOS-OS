BLOCK-EXOTICITY OF REDUCTION SIMPLE FUSION
SYSTEMS ON S(n, p)
JUN LIAO, YIKUN LIU
Abstract. In this paper, we prove that for p ≥13 and 5 ≤n ≤p −3,
every reduction simple fusion system on the finite p-group S(n, p) is block-
exotic. Furthermore, we establish the block-exoticity of three infinite families
of reduction simple exotic fusion systems.
1. Introduction
Let p be a prime and S be a finite p-group. A fusion system on S is a cat-
egory whose objects are the subgroups of S and whose morphisms are injective
group homomorphisms satisfying certain axioms. A fusion system is saturated if
it satisfies two additional axioms, namely the Sylow axiom and the extension ax-
iom. Throughout this paper, all fusion systems are assumed to be saturated unless
otherwise stated.
A fusion system F on S is called realizable if there exists a finite group G with
S ∈Sylp(G) such that F = FS(G), where FS(G) is the fusion system on S whose
morphisms are given by conjugation by elements of G; otherwise, F is called exotic.
Fix an algebraically closed field k of characteristic p. In block theory, a fusion
system can also be constructed from a block b of the group algebra kG. More pre-
cisely, given a finite group G, a block b of kG, and a maximal b-Brauer pair (S, eS),
one obtains a fusion system F(S,eS)(G, b) on the defect group S, whose morphisms
are given by conjugation by elements of G on the b-Brauer pairs contained in (S, eS).
A fusion system F on S is called block-realizable if F = F(S,eS)(G, b), in this case,
b is called an F-block; otherwise, F is called block-exotic.
Recall that Brauer’s third main theorem (see [1, Part IV, Theorem 5.9]) states
that: if G is a finite group and b is the principal block of kG, then for any maxi-
mal b-Brauer pair (S, eS), the subgroup S is a Sylow p-subgroup of G. Moreover,
the associated fusion system F(S,eS)(G, b) coincides with the group fusion system
FS(G). In particular, if a fusion system F is realizable, then it is block-realizable.
That is to say, if F is block-exotic, then F is exotic. However, the converse remains
an open problem:
Conjecture 1.1. [1, Part IV, 7.1] Let F be a fusion system on a finite p-group S.
If F is exotic, then F is block-exotic.
Several families of fusion systems supporting this conjecture have already been
identified. The first family consists of the Solomon systems, defined on a Sylow 2-
subgroup of Spin(7, q). These are conjectured to be the only exotic fusion systems
on 2-groups. Block-exoticity for q = 3 was proved by Kessar in [13], and generalized
to all odd prime powers q in [7, Theorem 9.34]. The second example consists of
the Ruiz–Viruel systems, defined on the extra-special 7-group of order 73; block-
exoticity for these systems was established by Kessar and Stancu in [14]. Serwene
