BOUNDARY BEHAVIOUR OF EIGENFUNCTIONS AND
SUPERHARMONIC FUNCTIONS ON HARMONIC MANIFOLDS OF
PURELY EXPONENTIAL VOLUME GROWTH
UTSAV DEWAN
Abstract. On X, a non-positively curved harmonic manifold of purely exponential
volume growth, of dimension n ≥3, we study certain quantitative aspects of the
boundary behaviour of eigenfunctions and superharmonic functions. More precisely,
we first consider u, complex-valued eigenfunctions lying outside the L2-spectrum of ∆
and prove that/obtain:
(1) specific weighted non-tangential limits of u exist a.e. on the boundary and agree
with the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the absolutely continuous component of
its boundary measure, with respect to the visibility measure;
(2) Hausdorff dimension and Hausdorff measure estimates of the boundary excep-
tional sets where radial limits of u blow-up;
(3) sharpness of the size estimates obtained in (2).
Then we focus on positive superharmonic functions f and prove that/obtain:
(4) f may not have non-tangential limits, by constructing a counter-example whose
non-tangential limits blow up at all boundary points;
(5) if, however, the Riesz measure of f is absolutely continuous with respect to the
volume measure, with density belonging to specific weighted Lp spaces for p >
n/2, then non-tangential limits of f exist a.e. on the boundary and agree with
the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the absolutely continuous component of the
boundary measure of its greatest harmonic minorant, with respect to the visibility
measure;
(6) the integrability range of p > n/2 in (5) is sharp, by means of counter-examples;
(7) if in addition to (5), the density has extra decay near infinity and the asymptotic
spherical averages of f also decay, then f has tangential limits on the boundary
with the exceptional set having prescribed Hausdorff dimension.
The results (2)-(7) are new even for the homogeneous setting of rank one Riemannian
symmetric spaces of non-compact type and Damek-Ricci spaces. Our arguments are
based on potential theory adapted to the intrinsic Gromov hyperbolic geometry of X.

1. Introduction
The connection between the geometry of a complete Riemannian manifold and the ex-
istence of non-constant positive harmonic functions thereon, has long been a focal point
in geometric analysis. On one hand, in the regime of non-negative Ricci curvature, Yau
[Yau75] proved that every positive harmonic function must be constant. On the other
hand, on Hadamard manifolds of pinched negative sectional curvature, Anderson-Schoen
[AS85] exhibited abundance of non-constant positive harmonic functions by establishing
a natural homeomorphism between the Martin boundary and the boundary at infinity.
In [AS85], the authors also proved the existence of non-tangential limits of positive
harmonic functions, almost everywhere on the boundary with respect to the harmonic
measures, obtaining a remarkable generalization of the classical Fatou’s theorem [Fat06].
This study was furthered by Ancona for more general elliptic operators in [Anc87].
Due to the negative curvature, the Laplace-Beltrami operator ∆has a spectral gap
and hence harmonic functions lie outside the L2-spectrum of ∆. Thus in light of the
above results in [AS85, Anc87], one is naturally led to ask about the boundary behaviour
of other eigenfunctions of ∆, lying outside the L2-spectrum:
(Q1) What is the admissible rate of convergence of non-tangential boundary limits
for other eigenfunctions, on a full harmonic measure subset of the boundary at
infinity?
(Q2) How does a positive eigenfunction behave along radial geodesic rays, on the com-
plement of this full measure subset of the boundary? More precisely, how quickly
can a positive eigenfunction grow or how large can the exceptional set (in the
boundary) be where the positive eigenfunction blows up faster than a prescribed
rate?
The above quantitative questions largely remain unanswered in the generality of
Hadamard manifolds of pinched negative curvature. In the simplest case of the real
hyperbolic ball, however, the problem (Q1) is well understood [Min75]. In fact, studies
pertaining to the problem (Q1) extend to the class of homogeneous Hadamard mani-
folds of pinched negative curvature, known as rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces
of non-compact type [Mic73, Sj¨o84, Schl85].

Besides the delicate homogeneous structure of having a large isometry group acting
transitively on the unit tangent bundle, the rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of
non-compact type X enjoy two interesting fine and coarse geometric properties. Firstly,
for any point x ∈X, there exists a non-constant harmonic function on a punctured
neighborhood of x which is radial around x, i.e., only depends on the geodesic distance
from x. Such manifolds are known as harmonic manifolds. On the other hand, they also
satisfy the coarse geometric property of purely exponential volume growth: there exists a
constant h > 0 called the logarithmic volume entropy, such that the volume of geodesic
balls B(x, R) with center x ∈X and radius R > 1 satisfy:
(1.1)
vol (B(x, R)) ≍ehR .
It is noteworthy to mention that the class of harmonic manifolds of purely exponential
volume growth is much larger than the class of rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces
of non-compact type, as it also includes a general class of non-symmetric, non-compact
harmonic manifolds. Introduced by Damek-Ricci in [DR92.1], these are non-unimodular,
solvable extensions of Heisenberg type groups N, obtained by letting A = R+ act on
N by non-isotropic dilations, and have come to be known as harmonic NA groups or
Damek-Ricci spaces. The class of rank one Riemannian Symmetric spaces of noncompact
type (barring the degenerate case of the real hyperbolic spaces) is contained in (and in
fact accounts for a very small subclass of) the more general class of Damek-Ricci spaces
[ADY96, p. 643]. In addition to being non-symmetric, Damek-Ricci spaces are also non-
positively curved and are the only known examples of non-compact, non-flat harmonic
manifolds. In the setting of Damek-Ricci spaces, boundary behaviour of eigenfunctions
lying outside the L2-spectrum of ∆, has recently been studied in [RS23, Dew25].
The harmonic analysis of rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type
and Damek-Ricci spaces depends intrinsically on their Lie algebraic structures. More-
over, their Martin boundary is naturally identified with the one-point compactification
of the homogeneous group N. Thus we are prompted to ask whether similar quantita-
tive behaviours persist in general non-positively curved harmonic manifolds X of purely
exponential volume growth, defined solely by coarse and fine geometric structures, with
their boundary at infinity ∂X formed through an asymptotic dynamical process?

The fractal structure of the boundary at infinity is a classic point of interest in geom-
etry. For Hadamard manifolds of pinched negative curvature, the Hausdorff dimension
of harmonic measures on ∂X has been studied by Kifer-Ledrappier [KiLe90], Benoist-
Hulin [BeHu19]. Recently, Cavallucci [Cav22] proved that for Hadamard manifolds of
pinched non-positive sectional curvature, the (modified) Minkowski dimension of ∂X,
the Lipschitz-topological entropy of the geodesic flow, the logarithmic volume entropy
and the covering entropy of X, all coincide.

strating the influence of eigenfunctions on the geometry at infinity:
Corollary 1.8. Let X be a non-positively curved harmonic manifold of purely exponential
volume growth. Then the Hausdorff dimension of ∂X coincides with the mean curvature
of horospheres and the logarithmic volume entropy.

• The proof of the Fatou theorem for eigenfunctions (Theorem 1.4) is standard.
It relies on bounding the non-tangential maximal function corresponding to
the weighted eigenfunctions, by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of the
boundary measure on ∂X (Lemma 3.6), followed by the weak L1 boundedness of
the latter (Lemma 3.1).

• To study the exceptional sets for radial limits of eigenfunctions (Theorems 1.5
and 1.6), we introduce a truncated maximal function on visual annuli in ∂X (see

• At the core of studying the boundary behaviour of positive superharmonic func-
tions, is the Riesz decomposition (Lemma 2.1). Furthermore, by Theorem 1.4,
examining the non-tangential boundary behaviour of these functions can be sim-

by decomposing the Green potential into polar and non-polar parts:
– the non-polar part: its non-tangential boundary behaviour (Lemma 5.3)
is governed by the Poisson integral of the underlying Radon measure pro-

– the polar part: this is controlled by the local integrability of the Green
function near its pole and the L1(∂X)-integrability of the mass operator on
non-tangential cones (Lemma 5.8).
Ultimately, this local integrability yields the p > n/2 weighted integrability
threshold for the density.
• While studying the tangential boundary behaviour of positive superharmonic
functions in Theorem 1.14, the asymptotic decay of spherical averages given by
(1.15) ensures that the function is purely a Green potential. Then to estimate the
Hausdorff measures of the exceptional sets, we apply Frostman’s lemma (Lemma
2.3) by again transitioning to visual metrics and leveraging the power scaling and
bi-Lipschitz invariance of Hausdorff measures. The approach of decomposing into
polar and non-polar parts yields two separate descriptions of the exceptional sets:
– the non-polar part: the exceptional sets for tangential limits are identified

C1, C2, . . . will be used to specify fixed constants.

will imply that there exists C ≥1 such that f1 ≥Cf2 (respectively, f1 ≤Cf2). The

9. Concluding remarks
In this section, we make some remarks and pose some new problems:
(1) In Theorems 1.4, 1.5 and 1.6, we study the boundary behaviour of generalized
Poisson integrals, corresponding to eigenfunctions of ∆with eigenvalues β2 −ρ2
for β > 0. A natural question now is whether these are all such eigenfunctions?
The key to answering this question lies in obtaining a Martin representation
formula for these eigenfunctions. In the setting of Damek-Ricci spaces, it is well-
known [DR92.2, Theorem 7.11]. Thus a natural attempt would be to generalize
this to harmonic manifolds of purely exponential volume growth.
(2) In Theorems 1.4 and 1.10, we have assumed (∂X, ν, λo) to be h-doubling. This
is indeed true for the known cases of rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of
non-compact type and Damek-Ricci spaces. However, whether this holds true
for general harmonic manifolds of purely exponential volume growth, remains to
be seen.
(3) Whether, the condition on the decay of spherical averages given by (1.15) in
Theorem 1.14, can be replaced by a suitable adaptation of [NRS82, CDS92]
seems to be an interesting problem.
