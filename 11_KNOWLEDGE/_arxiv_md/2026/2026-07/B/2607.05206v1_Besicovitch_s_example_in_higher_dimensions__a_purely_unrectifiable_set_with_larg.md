1. Introduction
One of the main concepts of geometric measure theory is that of d-rectifiable subsets of
Rn, given positive integers 0 < d < n. They are sets which, up to a set of zero Hd-measure,
are contained in a countable union of images of Lipschitz maps with domain in Rd (where
Hd denotes the d-dimensional Hausdorff measure). For example, for d = 1, the 1-rectifiable
sets are those which are contained in a countable union of rectifiable curves, again up to a
set of zero H1-measure.
On the other side of the coin, we have the purely d-unrectifiable
sets, which are those that contain no d-rectifiable subset of positive Hd-measure. One of the
goals of geometric measure theory is to characterize rectifiability in terms of other geometric
or analytical properties.
To that end, one of the basic tools is that of the densities for the Hausdorff measure. Consider
a Borel set E ⊂Rn such that 0 < Hd(E) < ∞for some positive integers 0 < d < n, which we
call a d-set. One defines the upper and lower d-densities of E at a point x ∈Rn, denoted as
Θ∗d(E, x) and Θd
∗(E, x) respectively, as the lim sup and lim inf as r →0 of
Hd(E ∩Br(x))

When both quantities coincide, the limit is called the d-density of E at x. A fundamental
theorem states that a d-set E ⊂Rn is d-rectifiable if and only if the d-density of E exists and is
equal to 1 at Hd-almost all points of E. This is known as the characterization of rectifiability
in terms of densities. This line of study was initiated in the pioneering work of Besicovitch
[Bes38] in 1938, where he established the result for 1-sets in the plane, i.e., the case d = 1 and
n = 2. It was extended to arbitrary dimension over different stages, with the work of Moore
[Moo50] (1950), Marstrand [Mar61] (1961) and Mattila [Mat75] (1975). It was later proven
by Preiss [Pre87] in 1987 that in fact, rectifiability is equivalent to the mere existence of the
density at almost all points.
