ON PLANAR DOMAINS
A. WALTON GREEN, CODY B. STOCKDALE, BRANDON SWEETING,
AND NATHAN A. WAGNER
Abstract. We investigate the relationship between the weak-type regularity
of the Bergman projection, ΠΩ, of a simply connected domain Ω⊂C and the
boundary geometry of Ωin terms of a conformal map ψ : D →Ω. We show
that ΠΩis of weak-type (1, 1) whenever |ψ′| is in the Bekoll´e-Bonami class
B1, give a more general necessary condition for the weak-type (p, p) bounds
of ΠΩwhen 1 ≤p < ∞, and establish sharpened sufficient conditions for the
weak-type bounds when p > 1.
Our results follow from a reformulation in
terms of mixed-weighted weak-type inequalities for ΠD. We provide several
applications.

Our question can hence be addressed by studying the following general problem.
Problem 1.7. Given 1 ≤p < ∞, characterize the weights u such that Πu is
bounded from Lp(D, u2) to Lp,∞(D, u2).
The bound of Πu from Lp(D, u2) to Lp,∞(D, u2) is an instance of a type of bound
first investigated by Sawyer in [32] for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, and
later expanded upon for Calder´on-Zygmund singular integral operators in [11,22].
In particular, Sawyer’s conjecture was proved in [22], that if T is a Calder´on-
Zygmund operator, u ∈A1, and v ∈A∞, then T v := v−1T(v ·) satisfies
∥T vf∥L1,∞(Rn,uv) ≲∥f∥L1(Rn,uv)
for all f ∈L1(Rn, uv). See [7,23,27,28] for more on such mixed-weighted weak-type
bounds for singular integrals with respect to Muckenhoupt weights.
Similar mixed-weighted weak-type (1, 1) bounds were recently established in the
Bergman setting in [8]. Those results, specialized to the case u = v, require v ∈
B1 ∩Bp(v) for some 1 ≤p < ∞. Note that this condition is strictly stronger than
v ∈B1 since it forces v2 to be integrable – in fact, v2, the underlying measure in
Problem 1.7, would be doubling. Indeed, the conformal weight v(z) = |1 −z|−1
arising from ψ(z) = log(1 −z) belongs to B1, but v2 is not integrable up to the
boundary of D.
To establish Theorem 1.1, we prove a different special case of [8, Conjecture 1.6],
namely, the case u = v ∈B1 ∩BHO, where BHO is the class of bounded hyperbolic
oscillation given in Definition 3.4 below.
Theorem 1.8. If u ∈B1∩BHO, then Πu is bounded from L1(D, u2) to L1,∞(D, u2).
