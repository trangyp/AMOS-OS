1. Introduction
The Hardy spaces on Rn were introduced in [9] by C. Fefferman and E. Stein,
and have been extensively studied ever since. A key feature of these spaces is that
they provide an effective alternative to Lebesgue spaces in the range 0 < p ≤1. In
particular, while Riesz transforms fail to be bounded on Lp(Rn) for p ≤1, they do
exhibit boundedness when considered on the corresponding Hardy spaces Hp(Rn). To
investigate the boundedness of operators such as singular integrals or fractional-type
operators on Hardy spaces Hp(Rn), one typically relies on their atomic or molecular
characterizations. This means that any distribution in Hp can be expressed as a sum
of atoms or molecules. The atomic decomposition was first established by Coifman in
one dimension (see [3]) and later, in [16], extended to higher dimensions by Latter.
These descriptions suggest that the boundedness of linear operators on Hp could,
in principle, be reduced to analyzing their action on atoms or molecules. However,
this approach has certain limitations. Indeed, Bownik in [2], building on an example
due to Meyer, constructed a linear functional defined on a dense subspace of H1(Rn)
that maps all (1, ∞, 0) −atoms into bounded scalars, but does not admit a bounded
extension to the whole space H1(Rn). This shows that, in general, it is not sufficient
to verify that an operator sends atoms into bounded elements of a quasi-Banach space
X to guarantee that it extends to a bounded operator on Hp, for 0 < p ≤1.
Nevertheless, such examples can be regarded as somewhat pathological. For clas-
sical operators T, uniform boundedness on atoms implies boundedness from Hp into
