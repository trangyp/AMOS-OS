Graph-Compatible Power Concavity in Weakly Coupled Elliptic
Systems
Jiahuan Li, Hanpeng Zhou
Abstract
We introduce graph-compatible powers determined by the coupling graph for multicomponent
weakly coupled elliptic systems and establish concavity of the associated transformed components
through a concave-envelope method and a weighted viscosity comparison principle. We further
establish a componentwise constant-rank theorem for the transformed Hessians, which yields strict
power concavity under additional assumptions.
Keywords: Power concavity; weakly coupled elliptic systems; graph-compatible powers; concave
envelope; viscosity comparison; constant rank theorem; strict concavity.

Introduction
Concavity properties of solutions to nonlinear elliptic and parabolic equations have played an important
role in the qualitative theory of partial differential equations. They provide information on the geome-
try of level sets, critical points, Hessian structures, and geometric inequalities of Brunn–Minkowski and
Pr´ekopa–Leindler type. In particular, concavity properties of solutions to nonlinear elliptic equations
have become an important tool for understanding the interaction between the analytic structure of
equations and the geometry of their solutions. Early results concerning convexity of the level sets of
Green functions were obtained by Carath´eodory and Gabriel [Ahl73,Gab57]. Makar-Limanov proved
the power-concavity property for the torsion function in planar convex domains [ML71]. Brascamp
and Lieb established the log-concavity of the first Dirichlet eigenfunction of the Laplacian and the
preservation of log-concavity of initial data under the heat flow [BL76].
Two complementary approaches have emerged in the study of concavity of solutions.
For the
macroscopic approach, Korevaar developed the concavity maximum principle for nonlinear elliptic
and parabolic boundary value problems [Kor83].
Subsequently, Kennington developed the power-
concavity theory for nonlinear elliptic boundary value problems, while Kawohl refined Korevaar’s
concavity method and related uniqueness issues [Kaw86,Ken85]. The viscosity framework for concavity
maximum principles was further developed by Alvarez, Lasry, and Lions [ALL97], and Salani extended
concave-envelope techniques to nonlinear elliptic and Hessian-type equations and related convexity
inequalities [Sal12].
The second approach is the microscopic approach. Caffarelli and Friedman established the first
constant-rank theorem for the Hessians of solutions to semilinear elliptic equations, which became a
key tool for obtaining strict convexity [CF85]. Subsequently, Korevaar–Lewis established constant-
rank properties for convex solutions of elliptic equations in higher dimensions [KL87]. Later, Guan
and Ma extended constant-rank techniques to geometric fully nonlinear elliptic equations [GM03].
Caffarelli, Guan, and Ma further developed this theory for fully nonlinear elliptic equations and estab-
lished structural conditions ensuring constant-rank properties [CGM07]. Subsequently, Bian and Guan
established constant-rank theorems for more general fully nonlinear elliptic equations [BG09,BG10].
For weakly coupled systems, Ishige, Nakagawa, and Salani developed a concave-envelope method
and obtained power-concavity results for two-component Lane–Emden type elliptic and parabolic sys-
tems [INS16]. However, extending their approach to general multicomponent systems with nontrivial
coupling structures remains open.
