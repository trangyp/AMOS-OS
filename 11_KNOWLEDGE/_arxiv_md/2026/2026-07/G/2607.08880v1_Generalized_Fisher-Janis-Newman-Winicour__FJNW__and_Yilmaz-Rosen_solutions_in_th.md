Yilmaz-Rosen solutions in the higher-dimensional scalar-tensor
theory with nonminimal coupling
K. K. Ernazarov
Institute of Gravitation and Cosmology,
Peoples’ Friendship University of Russia (RUDN University),
6 Miklukho-Maklaya Street, Moscow, 117198, Russian Federation.
Abstract
This paper investigates higher-dimensional scalar-tensor theories of gravity with
nonminimal coupling, focusing on the reconstruction of exact spherically symmetric
solutions. We systematically apply the formalism developed in previous works, which
demonstrates that any static spherically symmetric metric can be represented as an ex-
act solution of a scalar-tensor theory with specific coupling functions f(ϕ) and potential
U(ϕ). Our analysis centers on two important classes of solutions in arbitrary spacetime
dimensions: the Fisher-Janis-Newman-Winicour (FJNW) metric and its limiting case,
the generalized Yilmaz-Rosen metric.
We derive the key relations for the coupling function f(ϕ) and the scalar field
potential U(ϕ) for both solution families using the master equation formalism in the
Jordan frame. For the FJNW metric, we find that in the special case s = 2, D =
6, the coupling function f(ϕ) is positive in the domain u3 > M, corresponding to
gravitational attraction and a canonical scalar field with vanishing potential U(ϕ) = 0.
In contrast, for the generalized Yilmaz-Rosen metric in arbitrary dimensions D > 4,
the reconstructed coupling function is always negative, f(ϕ) < 0, indicating a phantom
scalar field with negative kinetic energy and repulsive gravity.
We analyze the conformal transformation to the Einstein frame and show that the
sign of f(ϕ) determines both the nature of gravitational interaction and the possibility
of a unitary Einstein frame. The positive f(ϕ) case allows a conformal transforma-
tion with a canonical scalar field, while the negative case yields a phantom field with
no well-defined Einstein frame. A stability analysis of spherically symmetric linear
perturbations reveals that the attractive FJNW solution exhibits a positive effective
potential, ensuring stability, whereas the repulsive Yilmaz-Rosen metric possesses a
negative effective potential, signaling a tachyonic instability. Our results demonstrate
that the sign of the coupling function and the associated constant C0 serve as fun-
damental indicators of the gravitational interaction type and stability properties in
scalar-tensor theories.
Keywords: scalar-tensor gravity, nonminimal coupling, higher dimensions, FJNW met-
ric, Yilmaz-Rosen metric, canonical and phantom scalar fields, stability

Introduction
Scalar-tensor theories of gravity with nonminimal coupling represent a natural extension of
general relativity (GR) that preserve its geometric foundation while introducing additional
degrees of freedom. Historically, such models trace back to the works of Jordan, Brans and
Dicke [1] - [3] and their subsequent extensions, and they are actively studied in the context
of quantum gravity, Kaluza–Klein theories and string scenarios. The presence of a scalar
field nonminimally coupled to curvature can explain the phenomena of dark energy, inflation,
and modified gravitational interaction on large scales. In recent years, scalar–tensor theories
have regained interest both in cosmology and black hole physics [4], [5]. One of the key
issues remains the construction of exact spherically symmetric solutions in higher dimensions,
where the role of extra coordinates is particularly important for unification of interactions
[6].
In the present paper we consider a higher-dimensional scalar–tensor theory with nonmin-
imal coupling, described by the action in the Jordan frame. Unlike the standard minimally
coupled approach, here a function f(ϕ) multiplies the scalar curvature, leading to modifica-
tions of both the gravitational constant and the structure of the field equations. Of special
interest are exact solutions describing static spherically symmetric configurations gener-
ated by a massless scalar field. Among such solutions the Fisher–Janis–Newman–Winicour
(FJNW) metric occupies a central place. It was first obtained by I. Z. Fisher in 1948 [7] and
later rediscovered by Janis, Newman and Winicour in 1968 [8], [9]. This metric describes a
static spherically symmetric spacetime with a naked singularity and represents an important
class of solutions for studying gravitational collapse and the cosmic censorship problem [10]
- [12]. Its generalisation to an arbitrary number of dimensions was obtained later and serves
as a natural testing ground for investigating the influence of the scalar charge on geometry
and causal structure.
Furthermore, in alternative theories of gravity, including scalar–tensor models with spe-
cific parameters, an exponential metric appears, proposed by Yilmaz [13] and Rosen [14],
[15]. It does not satisfy the vacuum Einstein equations, but describes a horizonless field
with a conformally flat spatial part.
This metric is of interest as a post-Newtonian ap-
proximation of higher order compared to the Schwarzschild one, and also as a model for
studying wormholes and regular black holes [16], [17]. In the limit of an infinite parameter
s, the higher-dimensional FJNW metric reduces to the generalised Yilmaz–Rosen metric,
indicating a deep connection between these two families of solutions [18].
It should be noted that the paper [19] generalizes the classical Schwarzschild solution to
the case of multidimensional gravity with additional internal Ricci-flat spaces. The author
considers vacuum Einstein equations Rµν = 0 for a metric containing time, radial, spherical,
and n internal parts. An exact solution is obtained, in which the scale factors of the internal
spaces depend on the radial coordinate and satisfy a quadratic constraint. It is shown that
an event horizon in the four-dimensional section exists only when the scale factors of all
internal spaces are constant. Otherwise, the singularity is not shielded by a horizon. A
scalar-vacuum generalization of the solution is also mentioned. The paper [20] generalizes
the Tangherlini solution to the case of n internal Ricci-flat spaces. It is proved that an event
horizon in the external (2 + d)-dimensional section exists only when the scale factors of the
internal spaces are constant; in all nontrivial cases the horizon is absent. A p-adic analogue

In the paper [21] the D-dimensional gravitational model with nonlinear scalar fields
described by a sigma-model action is considered. General cosmological solutions are found
for a metric on a product of Einstein factor spaces in two cases: when all spaces are Ricci-flat,
and when one space has non-zero scalar curvature while the others are flat. The solutions
reduce to geodesic equations in the sigma-model target space. For the spherically symmetric
subclass, a restricted version of the no-hair theorem is proved: a regular horizon exists only
when the scale factors are constant and the scalar field energy vanishes. A class of latent
solitons is singled out for d1 = 2, whose four-dimensional part has the same PPN parameters
as the Schwarzschild solution. Examples of sigma models with S2 and dS2 target spaces are
given.
The aim of this paper is to systematically apply the formalism of scalar–tensor theory
with nonminimal coupling to these two classes of exact solutions in arbitrary dimension.
Within the approach developed in [22] - [24], where it is shown that any static spherically
symmetric metric can be represented as an exact solution of a scalar-tensor theory with cer-
tain functions f(ϕ) and U(ϕ), we derive the key relations for these functions and reconstruct
their explicit form for the FJNW and Yilmaz–Rosen metrics. Special attention is paid to
the sign of the function f(ϕ), which determines the nature of the gravitational interaction
(attraction or repulsion) and the possibility of a conformal transformation to the Einstein
frame. It is shown that for the FJNW metric in some special cases (e.g., s = 2, D = 6)
the function f(ϕ) is positive, corresponding to attraction and a canonical scalar field. In
contrast, for the generalised Yilmaz–Rosen metric f(ϕ) turns out to be negative, which is
interpreted as a manifestation of a phantom scalar field with negative kinetic energy and
repulsive gravity.
The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 formulates the general theory: the
action in the Jordan frame is written, the field equations are presented, and their reduction
for a static spherically symmetric metric of general form is performed. The main differen-
tial relations are obtained, including a master equation for the function f(ϕ). Section 3
presents the higher-dimensional generalisation of the FJNW metric both in isotropic and
Schwarzschild coordinates; the limiting cases s →1 (Schwarzschild-Tangherlini metric) and
s →∞(Yilmaz–Rosen metric) are discussed. Section 4 is devoted to the direct application
of the obtained formalism: first to the general FJNW solution in arbitrary dimension, then
to the case s = 2, D = 6, where all quantities are expressed in elementary functions. Next,
the limit s →∞, i.e., the generalised Yilmaz–Rosen metric is considered, and for it the ex-
plicit forms of f(ϕ), U(ϕ) and ϕ(u) are also found. Section 5 discusses the relation between
the Jordan frame and the Einstein frame: it is shown that the sign of f(ϕ) determines the
possibility of a conformal transformation and the type of kinetic energy of the scalar field.
The physical interpretation of the attractive and repulsive cases is analysed, as well as the
role of the constant C0 as an indicator of the canonical or phantom regime. The Conclu-
sion summarises the results and outlines prospects for further research, including stability
analysis of the obtained solutions and their cosmological applications.

Examples
In theory of gravity static spherically symmetric solutions describing the fields of massive
objects in the presence of scalar fields take an important place. One such family is the Fisher-
Janis-Newman-Winicour (FJNW) metric, which in D-dimensions arises within the frame-
work of Einstein gravity minimally coupled to a massless scalar field. Another well-known

In classical General Relativity, gravitational interaction is described by a single metric,
and matter fields obey the equivalence principle in its standard formulation.
However,
scalar–tensor theories of gravity, which generalise GR, introduce an additional degree of
freedom - a scalar field ϕ(u) nonminimally coupled to curvature. Such theories are usually
formulated in the so-called Jordan frame, where the action contains an arbitrary function
f(ϕ) multiplying the scalar curvature. This is precisely the frame in which action (2.1) is
written in this paper.

a careful analysis of the sign of the coupling function f(ϕ) and the associated constant C0

type of gravitational interaction within the scalar-tensor theory.

Conclusion
In this work, we have systematically investigated higher-dimensional scalar-tensor theories
of gravity with nonminimal coupling, focusing on the reconstruction of exact spherically
symmetric solutions from given metrics. Our analysis has yielded several significant results
that deepen our understanding of the relationship between spacetime geometry, scalar field
dynamics, and the nature of gravitational interaction in scalar-tensor theories. We have
successfully applied the formalism developed in [22-24] to reconstruct the coupling func-
tion f(ϕ) and scalar field potential U(ϕ) for two important classes of exact solutions in
arbitrary dimensions D: the Fisher-Janis-Newman-Winicour (FJNW) metric and the gen-
eralized Yilmaz-Rosen metric. For both families, we obtained explicit expressions for f(ϕ),
U(ϕ) and the scalar field ϕ(u) in terms of the radial coordinate.
A key finding is the opposing signs of the coupling function for the two solution families:
- For the FJNW metric (particularly the case s = 2, D = 6 we found f(ϕ) > 0 in the
physically relevant domain, indicating gravitational attraction and a canonical scalar field
with positive kinetic energy.
- For the generalized Yilmaz-Rosen metric in arbitrary dimensions D > 4 we obtained
f(ϕ) < 0, which we interpret as a manifestation of a phantom scalar field with negative
kinetic energy and repulsive gravity.
We carefully analyzed the conformal transformation from the Jordan frame to the Ein-
stein frame. We showed that the transition is only possible when f(ϕ) > 0, as the conformal
factor Ω2 = f 2/(D−2) must be real and positive. For the Yilmaz-Rosen case with f(ϕ) < 0 no
unitary Einstein frame exists, and the theory remains intrinsically in the Jordan frame with
repulsive gravitational interactions. This result has profound implications for the physical
interpretation and phenomenological applications of such solutions.
We performed a linear stability analysis of both solution classes under spherically sym-
metric perturbations. The effective potential Veff was derived, and its sign was shown to
be directly determined by the sign of f(ϕ). For the FJNW case with f(ϕ) > 0 we found
a positive effective potential that decays as 1/u3 at infinity, ensuring stability under small
perturbations. In contrast, the Yilmaz-Rosen metric with f(ϕ) < 0 yielded a negative ef-
fective potential everywhere, indicating a tachyonic instability with exponentially growing
modes. This instability is physically attributed to the phantom nature of the scalar field,

which allows energy to be drawn from the gravitational sector, leading to unbounded growth
of perturbations.
We demonstrated that our solutions are naturally embedded into the broader frame-
work of Horndeski (generalized scalar-tensor) theories in D-dimensions. The action (2.1)
corresponds to a specific choice of the Horndeski functions, and the reconstructed solutions
represent exact solutions of these more general theories. This embedding opens avenues for
further analysis using the powerful tools of Horndeski theory, including ghost-free conditions,
gravitational wave speed constraints, and cosmological applications.
Throughout our analysis, the arbitrary constant C0 (arising from solving the master
equation) emerged as a crucial parameter that determines the sign of f(ϕ) and, consequently,
the nature of the gravitational interaction. For both solution families, the choice C0 < 0
ensures a real scalar field, but leads to opposite signs of f(ϕ): positive for FJNW and negative
for Yilmaz-Rosen. Thus, C0 serves as an indicator of whether the theory describes attractive
gravity (with a canonical scalar field and stable configurations) or repulsive phantom gravity
(with instabilities).
The contrasting behavior of the two solution families reflects fundamentally different
gravitational physics. The FJNW metric with s = 2, D = 6 represents a physically viable
configuration with positive gravitational interaction, a canonical scalar field, and a stable
horizon structure (although it is extremal). The solution is asymptotically flat and possesses
a positive effective potential, making it a stable model for studying scalarized black holes in
higher dimensions.
In contrast, the Yilmaz-Rosen metric describes a horizonless, everywhere regular space-
time with repulsive gravity. While mathematically exact, it is dynamically unstable due to
the negative effective potential arising from the phantom scalar field. This instability sug-
gests that such configurations would not be realized in physical evolution without additional
stabilizing mechanisms (e.g., higher-derivative terms or matter couplings).
Nevertheless,
the solution remains of interest as a model for studying phantom dark energy, wormhole
geometries, and the limits of classical scalar-tensor theories.
From a cosmological perspective, the negative f(ϕ) case with f(ϕ) < 0 corresponds to
an effective equation of state ω = −1 for the scalar field, characteristic of phantom dark
energy. Such fields can drive the accelerated expansion of the Universe and potentially lead
to a ”Big Rip” scenario in the future. Our stability analysis reinforces the known result that
phantom fields generically lead to instabilities, unless the theory is suitably modified.
Our results extend and complement previous studies in several important ways. The
reconstruction formalism developed by Bronnikov et al. [22] - [24] is systematically applied
to higher-dimensional solutions providing explicit expressions for the coupling functions.
The connection between the FJNW and Yilmaz-Rosen metrics through the limit s →∞is
rigorously established in the context of scalar-tensor theory. The stability analysis provides
new insights into the dynamical behavior of these solutions, confirming that the sign of f(ϕ)
is not merely a gauge choice but has direct physical consequences.
In conclusion, this paper demonstrates that the formalism for reconstructing scalar-
tensor theories from given metrics is a powerful tool for understanding the relationship
between geometry, scalar fields, and gravitational interactions. The two solution families
studied - FJNW and Yilmaz-Rosen - exhibit opposite signs of the coupling function f(ϕ)
leading to fundamentally different physical behavior: attraction with canonical scalar fields

physical significance, with implications for black hole physics, cosmology, and alternative
