Abstract
Adaptive volumetric finite element meshing is a critical step in computer-aided
engineering and analysis that dictates the computational budget of a given problem.
It traditionally requires iterative PDE solvers or heavily supervised, data-driven sur-
rogates trained on large-scale simulation data. While Multimodal Large Language
Models (MLLMs) excel in 2D visual tasks, their zero-shot capability to semanti-
cally ground regions based on geometric understanding and physics remains an
open question. Overall, this study explores a significant question: can the high-level
semantic understanding of off-the-shelf MLLMs serve as a viable, zero-shot geo-
metric proxy for finite element mesh refinement? To investigate this, we introduce
GReFEM (Geometric Reasoning Enhanced Multimodal LLMs for Finite Element
Meshing), a framework that utilizes MLLMs to visually localize stress-critical
regions based on physics-guided textual prompts. To bridge the gap between 2D
MLLM pre-training and 3D geometries, we introduce orthoViews, a view-selection
module that maximizes the observability of key geometric features. We conduct an
in-depth empirical evaluation across diverse CAD geometries, loading cases, and
SOTA MLLMs, comparing them against a tuned geometric heuristic under a strict,
matched refinement budget. Our findings reveal that MLLMs demonstrate robust
zero-shot capacity to accurately follow complex spatial-physical instructions, isolat-
ing stress-relevant features with higher precision than blind heuristics. By mapping
both the successes and current limitations of MLLMs in physical grounding, this
study defines the frontier of foundation models as semantic assistants in automated
simulation workflows.

Introduction
Recent large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in reasoning and
visual perception, enabling their application across scientific domains that require structured spatial
understanding. In particular, recent work has explored the use of LLMs for 3D CAD modeling, object
generation, and geometric reasoning, leading to notable advances in design automation and shape
synthesis Kienle et al. [2025], Zhou et al. [2025], Yang et al. [2023].
Despite these advances, the role of LLMs in fine-grained numerical simulation workflows remains
largely unexplored. High-fidelity finite element simulation fundamentally relies on discretization
of the problem domain, where mesh resolution must be adaptively concentrated in regions of high
physical variation. While existing approaches adapt LLMs to reason about surface meshes Wang et al.
[2024], Fang et al. [2025] and CAD representations Wu et al. [2023], Alrashedy et al. [2024] of 3D
objects, comparatively little work investigates their effectiveness in guiding volumetric discretization
required to resolve localized physical phenomena while controlling computational cost.
Traditional adaptive volumetric meshing methods rely on PDE solvers to compute error indicators that
identify regions requiring refinement (Zienkiewicz and Zhu [1992], Babuška and Rheinboldt [1978]).
This tightly couples meshing to solver availability and computational expense. In practice, however,
experienced engineers frequently anticipate regions requiring refinement directly from geometric
structure, loading directions, and boundary conditions, without explicitly solving for field quantities.
Several works exploit such geometry-driven heuristics Bronson et al. [2012], Gkrispanis [2024], but
typically assume structured CAD representations that explicitly encode high-level geometric features.
In this work, we investigate a fundamental question: can off-the-shelf MLLMs act as zero-shot
semantic assistants to emulate expert, geometry-driven meshing intuition? To systematically explore
this, we introduce GReFEM, as a framework to evaluate MLLM spatial grounding in 3D engineering
environments. Because MLLMs often struggle with precise pixel-level localization (Fig. 2), we
propose grid-based region localization as a solution and integrate orthoViews, a view-scoring module
to maximize geometric observability. This enables us to reliably project coarse 2D MLLM inferences
back to the 3D domain, steering volumetric mesh refinement without PDE solvers or explicit CAD
annotations.
Through rigorous experiments across CAD geometries, multiple loading conditions, and state-of-the-
art MLLMs, we present the following contributions:
(1) A Semantic Evaluation Framework: We introduce GReFEM (and orthoViews) as a CAD-
agnostic probing pipeline that translates geometric instructions grounded in problem physics into
localized 3D volumetric mesh refinement anchors. By observing mesh refinement metrics and region
localization performance, we evaluate an MLLM’s ability to spatially identify key geometric features
relevant to physics based problems like localizing stress concentration areas under a variety of loading
conditions.
(2) Analysis of Intruction-Driven Grounding: We empirically demonstrate that MLLMs possess
a strong capacity to spatially execute expert physical rules. Providing explicit physical constraints

Related Works
Adaptive Meshing Adaptive meshing non-uniformly allocates resolution to minimize finite element
simulation costs. Traditional pipelines rely on computationally expensive, solver-in-the-loop error
estimators tailored to the governing PDEs ( Babuška and Rheinboldt [1978], Babuvška and Rheinboldt
[1978], Zienkiewicz and Zhu [1992]), though some geometry-driven heuristics avoid explicit PDE
solves using mathematical criteria ( Rivara [1984], Shewchuk [2002], Bronson et al. [2012]). Recently,
deep learning has emerged as a surrogate, using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to learn h-adaptive
sizing fields ( Khan et al. [2024], Freymuth et al. [2025]) or r-adaptive topological optimizations
( Pfaff et al. [2020], Rowbottom et al. [2024]). While these supervised architectures achieve high
accuracy, their strict reliance on massive datasets of converged FEM solutions prevents zero-shot
generalization. Rather than proposing a data-hungry supervised model, GReFEM explores a new
solver-free paradigm: evaluating whether the semantic reasoning of off-the-shelf MLLMs can act
as a natural language-guided proxy for mesh refinement, significantly circumventing the need for
offline FEM training data. Furthermore, supervised GNNs fundamentally solve a different problem:
they act as end-to-end surrogates for a specific, pre-defined PDE and boundary condition distribution.
Adapting a GNN to a new physical phenomenon (e.g., thermal stress instead of linear elasticity)
or a fundamentally different load type requires complete model retraining and a newly generated
FEM dataset. In contrast, GReFEM acts as a domain-agnostic semantic interface. By shifting
the refinement logic from learned network weights to expert-provided natural language prompts,
GReFEM can dynamically adapt to entirely new loading scenarios without a single parameter update.
3D Spatial Reasoning with MLLMs Extending vision-language models to 3D reasoning remains
challenging due to limited 3D training data. Several approaches address this by directly training
LLMs on structured 3D representations, such as 3D feature fields Hong et al. [2023], point clouds
( Xu et al. [2024], Batra et al. [2025]), or radiance fields Kerr et al. [2023]. Alternatively, other works
infer 3D understanding from 2D projections without explicit 3D structures. This includes predicting
3D bounding boxes Cho et al. [2024], optimizing view selection for 3D QA Wang et al. [2025],
regressing geometric attributes via 3D query tokens Yang et al. [2026], estimating absolute distances
from synthetic datasets Chen et al. [2024], progressive prompting for trajectory imagination Ma et al.
[2024], and leveraging video sequences for 3D grounding Zheng et al. [2025]. Our work follows
this latter 2D-to-3D projection paradigm; however, rather than targeting general semantic scene
understanding, we uniquely leverage intelligently ranked 2D orthographic views to infer mechanically
relevant 3D features for downstream engineering discretization.

Method
We propose GReFEM, a semantic spatial grounding testbed framework applied to adaptive finite
element meshing. Rather than relying on solver-based error estimators, GReFEM leverages pretrained
MLLMs and problem specific instructions to identify mechanically salient regions using only visual
observations of geometry. GReFEM operates solely on triangulated surface meshes and requires no
access to B-Rep entities, STEP files, or simulation feedback.
4.1
Problem Formulation
We consider a solid domain Ω⊂R3 with boundary ∂Ωrepresented by a triangulated surface mesh
M = (V, F). The objective is to generate a volumetric finite element mesh that locally refines regions
expected to exhibit large stress gradients under mechanical load, while maintaining coarse resolution
elsewhere to control computational cost. Given M, the method predicts a sparse set of surface-
localized refinement points P = {pi ∈R3}N
i=1, corresponding to geometrically or mechanically
important features, such as sharp edges, filets, holes, and load-bearing faces. The resulting size field
h : Ω→R+, as constructed according to equation 6, is provided to a standard tetrahedral meshing
algorithm, which deterministically generates a conforming volumetric mesh.

orthoViews

Experiments
Training
We train the orthoViews view-selection module on a dataset consisting of 5k diverse
CAD parts from ABC CAD dataset Koch et al. [2019] and FreeCAD Riegel et al. [2016] spanning
mechanical components with varying geometric complexity (e.g., extrusions, holes, fillets, and load-
bearing features). Each object is represented during training by both its triangulated surface mesh and
the corresponding STEP file. Specifically, we leverage high-level geometric primitives available in
STEP (e.g., planar faces, cylindrical axes) for training by scoring each view by the number of features
aligned in its view direction. During inference, our framework can work solely with spherical views
of surface meshes of 3D objects. For more information, the reader is encouraged to read the appendix
section on orthoViews. Training the network on the entire dataset for 4 epochs took a couple of hours
on one NVIDIA H100 GPU.

Qualitative Results
We find that the GReFEM predicted refinement locations consistently align
with stress-critical regions. Notably, the predicted points are not uniformly distributed across all
geometric features such as holes or internal corners, but are selectively localized to those features that
should be mechanically activated under the prescribed loading conditions (for instance Figure 3c).
In particular, while multiple concave features may be present in the geometry, GReFEM, when
provided with physics guided prompts about the problem and physics, attempts to strongly highlight
those regions that correspond to dominant stress concentrations in the ZZ stress field, rather than
marking all such features indiscriminately, as seen in Figure 5.
Matched-Budget Paradigm
Adaptive meshing is fundamentally a resource-constrained optimiza-
tion problem: introducing too many refinement points severely degrades downstream solver efficiency.
Therefore, comparing MLLMs against baseline methods purely on recall is misleading, as a trivial
algorithm could achieve perfect recall by refining the entire mesh. We benchmark the MLLMs against
a robust Mesh-Geometric-Heuristic that explicitly identifies concave edges, fillets, and holes based
on dihedral angles and discrete curvature, independent of loading or problem physics. We subsample
the heuristic’s predictions to match the average refinement point budget allocated by the MLLMs on
average for each geometry (2.0k – 3.6k points).
Metrics
To assess refinement localization, we compute the ZZ stress indicator field ηZZ from the
fine solution (with uniform element size hfine) and extract points with ηZZ in the 99th percentile. We
define Recall@r as the fraction of ground-truth stress-critical points that fall within a volumetric
buffer radius r of any predicted refinement point. We set r = Dmax = 4hmin as acceptable
proximity using Eq,6, since elements lie in the transition zone of the refinement field. Conversely,
Precision@r measures the fraction of the model’s predicted points that successfully land within
radius r of true stress-critical regions, serving as a direct proxy for refinement efficiency. The F1@r
score provides the harmonic mean. We evaluate both the mean performance across all views and the
peak performance using the top 5 orthoViews predictions (P@top5, R@top5, F1@top5).

all evaluated models (Gemini 3, Claude 4.5, GPT-5.4, and Qwen 3), the Load+Features prompts yield
the highest mean Precision. Interestingly, providing Load-Only prompts tends to increase Recall but
sharply degrades Precision. This confirms that MLLMs are highly responsive to explicit mechanical
rules: when provided with expert structural constraints (Load+Features), they successfully act as
surgical semantic assistants. Conversely, when forced to rely purely on their latent knowledge of
’stress’ (Load-Only), their precision sharply degrades, highlighting that their true value currently lies
in spatial instruction-following rather than autonomous physical reasoning.
The Precision-Recall Frontier
Table 1 reveals the critical trade-off at the frontier of zero-shot
3D grounding. The high mean recall of 0.7646 by the heuristic is expected, as it blindly flags all
geometric discontinuities regardless of the loading scenario. However, the MLLMs demonstrate
a distinct advantage in Precision. Gemini-3-flash-preview and Qwen3VL 235B (Load+Features)
achieves mean F1@r scores considerably close to the Mesh-Geometric-Heuristic, with their top-5
views variants (Load+Features and Load+Features+Heuristics respectively) surpassing the Mesh-
Geometric-Heuristic’s 0.5362. This empirical finding highlights the value of the MLLM as an
"Assistant", because it semantically understands the loading axis from the prompt, it ignores geomet-
rically complex but mechanically irrelevant features (e.g., holes far from the load path). Consequently,
the top MLLM variants allocate a much higher density of their refinement budget directly into the
actual stress-critical zones. This highly concentrated, high-precision placement is more valuable for
downstream finite element analysis than the mesh-geometric-heuristic’s sparse, uniform distribution,
which achieves high recall at the cost of sparse allocation of points in key regions. We also see
from Table 1 that GPT-5.4-mini and Qwen3VL-235B on average brute force cell predictions at a
considerably higher rate than Gemini-3-flash-preview and Claude-Haiku-4.5 due to their increased
cells/view predicted counts (more detailed examples visible in the appendix section).
The Role of View Selection (orthoViews)
Figure 6 reports the average normalized energy and
L2 displacement errors over all test geometries, loading cases, and MLLMs as a function of the
number of inference views. Across both view selection strategies, orthoViews (GReFEM) consistently
outperforms random selection, yielding lower errors and demonstrating more effective refinement
placement. The solution errors are the greatest for the first few views, where stress-critical regions
are frequently missed or imprecisely localized. Increasing the number of views improves feature
coverage and refinement accuracy, leading to a monotonic error reduction.

Conclusion and Limitations
This study explored a fundamental question at the intersection of foundation models and compu-
tational engineering: can MLLMs act as zero-shot semantic assistants to bypass expensive, solver-
in-the-loop mesh refinement? By introducing the GReFEM framework, we demonstrated that
off-the-shelf MLLMs possess a robust zero-shot capacity to semantically ground physics-based text

## Page 10


instructions into precise 3D volumetric coordinates. Furthermore, when coupled with the proposed
orthoViews selection strategy, GReFEM reliably localizes stress-critical regions to generate refined
meshes that actively reduce downstream displacement and energy errors. By successfully interpreting
expert rules regarding load paths and boundary conditions, MLLM assistants act as surgical tools,
concentrating strict computational refinement budgets exclusively on mechanically relevant features.
We realise that achieving end-to-end autonomous physical reasoning in 3D remains an open challenge,
as the MLLMs remain grossly dependent on CV pipelines based guard-rails, prompt driven geometric
instructions and heuristics, and presence of certain geometric features to perform mesh refinement
according to the problem physics. Despite these limitations we believe this work establishes founda-
tion models as a highly viable, solver-free interface between human physical intuition and low-level
numerical discretization.

We introduced orthoViews as a view panning module that sorts the rendered views in the order of
