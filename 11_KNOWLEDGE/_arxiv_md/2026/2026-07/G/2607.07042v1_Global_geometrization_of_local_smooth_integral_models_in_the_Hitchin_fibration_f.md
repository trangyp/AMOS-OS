GLOBAL GEOMETRIZATION OF LOCAL SMOOTH INTEGRAL MODELS IN
THE HITCHIN FIBRATION FOR GLn: THE BASS CASE
SUNGMUN CHO AND JUNGTAEK HONG
Abstract. In previous joint work, we proposed a new method to study local orbital integrals for
GLn (where n = 3 or in the Bass case), by employing a smoothening method of a certain scheme
defined over a henselian ring. In this paper, we geometrize this local smoothening method within
the framework of the global Hitchin fibration for GLn in the Bass case. Consequently, we provide
a closed formula for the ℓ-adic cohomology of the compactified Jacobian of a spectral curve over
a finite field with double singularities whose local rings are integral domains, provided that the
normalization of a spectral curve is isomorphic to P1.

1. Introduction
The (local) orbital integral is the geometric side of the celebrated Arthur-Selberg trace formula,
which is a fundamental object for understanding endoscopic transfer. This has been studied by
many mathematicians, such as Ngô Bảo Châu, using the Hitchin fibration. The latter is a global
object on a curve over a finite field. On the other hand, the authors’ former works ([CKL] and
[CHL]) proposed another geometric method, called smoothening. This involves a local object used
to desingularize a certain scheme defined over a henselian ring of arbitrary characteristic. The
main goal of this paper is to compare these two methods (for the Bass case in GLn) and to derive
an explicit formula for the ℓ-adic cohomology of the compactified Jacobian of a spectral curve Yχ
(when the normalization of Yχ is isomorphic to P1
k) having double singularities whose local rings
OYχ,x are integral domains.
1.1. Global object: Hitchin fibration for GLn. For a smooth projective curve C with a large
effective divisor D defined over a finite field k, the Hitchin moduli stack M parametrizes the vector
bundles E on C of rank n along with a morphism θ : E →E(D). The Hitchin fibration Φ is defined to
be the "characteristic polynomial" morphism from M to the affine space A, which parametrizes the
coefficients of the characteristic polynomial χ of an endomorphism. By Ngô’s product formula(cf.
Proposition 4.12), Φ−1(χ)(k) is understood as the product of the affine Springer fiber at each closed
points of C, modulo certain equivalence. Consequently the point count of Φ−1(χ)(k) is (a finite
product of) the orbital integral for GLn corresponding to χ.
Ngô proved the support theorem for the Hitchin fibration. This is a cohomological statement
that allows us to deduce the fundamental lemma solely by studying the regular locus of A, without
calculating the cohomology of Φ−1(χ) and thus of the orbital integral. Over the regular and elliptic
locus, the Hitchin fibers are abelian varieties but in general they are not. On the other hand,
Φ−1(χ) has a geometric moduli description described below.
Theorem 1.1. ([Ngô10, Section 4.4.1]) Φ−1(χ) is isomorphic to the compactified Jacobian
Pic0(Yχ), which parametrizes the rank-1 torsion-free sheaves on a curve Yχ depending on χ, called
the spectral curve.
Thus, an explicit formula for the cohomology of Φ−1(χ) carries rich geometric information, not
only for endoscopic transfer.
1.2. Local object: Smoothening. To explicitly compute the (local) orbital integral for χ, [CKL]
and [CHL] introduced a method called smoothening, which provides another geometric interpre-
tation of the orbital integral in a purely local setting. The key idea is to use geometric measures
to reinterpret the orbital integral. Choose γ ∈gln(o) where o is the ring of integers o of a non-
Archimedean local field, so that its characteristic polynomial χ has coefficients in o. Consider a
morphism φ : gln,o →An
o over o sending a matrix to the coefficients of the characteristic polyno-
mial. Then the orbital integral is reinterpreted as the volume of φ−1(χ)(o). If φ−1(χ) is smooth,
then Weil’s volume formula easily yields the orbital integral:
Theorem 1.2. [Wei12, Theorem 2.2.5] If φ−1(χ) is a smooth scheme over o, then the orbital
integral is #(φ−1(χ)(k))

However, φ−1(χ) is not smooth for most χ of interest. To resolve this issue, [CKL] (for n = 3)
and [CHL] (in the Bass case) stratified φ−1(χ)(o) into a disjoint union of sets of o-points of smooth
schemes over o, so that the volume of each stratum is obtained using Weil’s formula.
We briefly explain the stratification in the case that o[X]/(χ(X)) is a Bass order and an integral
domain, which is treated in [CHL]. In this case, stratification of φ−1(χ)(o) is indexed by a pair
T = (F, l) called a type, which consists of a finite-dimensional k-algebra F and an integer l. Viewing
a matrix m in φ−1(χ)(o) as an endomorphism of a lattice L ∼= on, F represents the cokernel of m
up to isomorphism, and l governs the size of the Jordan blocks of m, the reduction of m modulo a
uniformizer of o. Then, the collection of matrices sharing the same type T is functorially extended
to form a representable subfunctor (φT )−1(χ) of φ−1(χ) as an fppf-sheaf on o, which turns out to
be a smooth scheme over o. A more detailed process of smoothening for Bass orders is explained
in Section 2.2.
Here a Bass order is an order of a number field or a local field whose fractional ideals are generated
by two elements. This often appears in algebraic number theory, algebraic geometry, and topology.
In algebraic geometry, the local ring OYχ,x of a spectral curve Yχ at a singular point x is a Bass
order if and only if x is a double point(cf. Proposition 4.8). We refer to [CHL, Introduction] for
further details on the background of Bass orders. For the rest of Introduction, we will suppose that
the local ring OYχ,x of a spectral curve Yχ at any singular point x is a Bass order (i.e. singularities
of Yχ are double points) and an integral domain.
