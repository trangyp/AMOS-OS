Abstract
We study the problem of recovering the correspondence between a collection of n points in
Rd and a noisy, permuted version of those points. In the high-dimensional regime d = ω(log n),
under a Gaussian model with noise variance σ2 = d/(b log n), prior work identifies b = 2 as the
threshold for almost exact recovery. We prove that this threshold is all-or-nothing: for every
fixed b < 2, no estimator recovers a positive fraction of the matching, and even estimating
the matched point cloud in Euclidean distance is asymptotically no better than ignoring the
correspondence. On the other hand, we consider a multi-view generalization of the problem
where K noisy, independently permuted copies of the same latent point cloud are observed.
Here we show that a simple polynomial-time procedure recovers all relative matchings up to
o(n) errors whenever b > K/(K −1). Thus multiple views can break the impossibility barrier
b = 2 for the original matching problem: in particular, for 3/2 < b < 2, the two-view model has
no nontrivial recovery, but a third view makes all latent correspondences efficiently recoverable.

Introduction
Let X1, . . . , Xn be a point cloud consisting of independent standard Gaussian vectors in Rd. In
the geometric planted matching model, one observes the original point cloud as well as a noisy
permuted copy
Yi = XΠ∗(i) + σZi,

recover Π∗.
This model captures the fundamental task of identifying unknown correspondences between un-
ordered objects from noisy features. Such matching problems arise across science and engineering,
including in single-cell data integration and multi-omics alignment [HLMM18, DSS+22, CJM+22],
particle tracking [CKK+10], image matching [MJF+21], record linkage [SBSBS16], and network
alignment [BGG+09]. Across these settings, the same statistical and algorithmic question recurs:
what level of noise can the observations tolerate so that the latent correspondence remains recov-
erable, and when can this be done efficiently?
Our results and techniques pertain to the high-dimensional regime where d = ω(log n) and
where the noise scales as
σ2 =
d
b log n,
where b > 0 is a fixed parameter. This is the critical scaling in which sharp recovery transitions
occur at constant values of b.
Much of the existing theory for geometric planted matching has focused on exact or almost exact
recovery, where one must identify all but o(n) of the matched pairs. Prior results [KNW22, DCK23,
WWXY22] place the almost exact recovery threshold at b = 2. However, this still leaves open the
nature of the recovery landscape below the threshold for this canonical model. In particular, for
b < 2, it is plausible that an estimator might still recover a positive fraction of the matching, or
might localize the matched point cloud well in Euclidean distance, producing a “something” phase
that straddles between almost exact recovery and total failure.
In this paper we resolve this uncertainty. Our results rule out the presence of a “something”
phase for b < 2. Instead we show that the model is in a strong “nothing” phase: the posterior
assigns exponentially small mass to the set of permutations that agree with the truth on any
fixed positive fraction of indices.
In particular, no estimator can achieve nonvanishing overlap
with the true permutation Π∗. Furthermore, this failure is not only combinatorial: even if one
only asks to reconstruct the matched point cloud (XΠ∗(1), . . . , XΠ∗(n)) in Euclidean distance, the
observations are asymptotically no more informative than in the case where the correspondence is
completely absent. Thus, for b < 2, the data support neither weak recovery of the permutation nor
geometrically accurate matching of the points.

The effect of multiple views is not obvious a priori. Additional observations of the same latent
point cloud are obtained, but more latent matchings have to be estimated. At the same time,
multiple views create consistency checks that are absent from the two-view problem: a proposed
tuple of matches must agree across many pairwise comparisons. Our results show that the balance
comes out favorably: for fixed K, and with an analogous high-dimensional scaling indexed by b, a
polynomial-time algorithm achieves almost exact recovery for b > K/(K −1). Already for K = 3,
this gives a clean illustration of the gain from multiple views: for 3/2 < b < 2, two views give
us nothing, but an additional third view breaks this barrier and permits efficient recovery of all
relative matchings.
As by-products of our arguments, we also obtain analogous negative results for a “d = ∞”
Gaussian weighted matching model of standalone interest, where geometric correlations are replaced
by independent Gaussian noise. In addition, by data processing, our negative results transfers over
to geometric graph matching models [WWXY22] that are stochastically degraded variants of (1).
1.1
Related work
Planted matchings.
The overarching goal in these problems is to uncover a hidden correspon-
dence between two collections of objects based on noisy observations. A mathematical formulation
arose in the context of particle tracking [CKK+10], where geometric observations determine the like-
lihood of pairing particles across images. A parallel, non-geometric line studied independent-edge
planted matching models [SSZ20, MMX21, DWXY23, WM25, ABAL+26]. Closer to the geometric
motivation, [KNW22, DCK23] studied Gaussian point-cloud matching, with related work going
beyond Gaussianity [dRSO24], models with low-rank signals [CJM+22], and uncertainty quantifi-
cation in match probabilities [FWY26]. A multiple view setting in the context of non-geometric
Gaussian graph alignment was studied in [EG25].
High-dimensional geometric matching.
The geometric matching model (1) in the high-
dimensional setting of d = ω(log n) and σ2 = d/(b log n) has been studied by [KNW22, DCK23,
WWXY22]. A major focus in [KNW22, DCK23] is the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE),
which corresponds to the solution of a linear assignment problem with weights induced by the two
observed point clouds.
For b > 4, Kunisky and Niles-Weed [KNW22] proves that the MLE achieves exact recovery. For
b < 4, they instead conjectured that the MLE makes Ω(n) errors. Additionally, a heuristic analysis
of a row-wise inner-product procedure is provided, predicting almost exact recovery for b > 2.
Dai et al. [DCK23] studied an asymptotically equivalent setting from the perspective of database
alignment. Their results determine polynomial error rates for the MLE throughout the intermediate
regime 2 < b < 4. In particular, [DCK23] asserts that the MLE attains almost exact recovery for
every b > 2.
This contrasts with the high-dimensional conjectural picture in [KNW22], while
agreeing with the proved exact recovery threshold at b = 4.
Wang et al. [WWXY22] studied geometric graph observations generated from latent point clouds
(1). In order to prove negative results, [WWXY22] gave a necessary condition ruling out almost
exact recovery for b < 2 in the more stochastically informative model (1), agreeing with and
