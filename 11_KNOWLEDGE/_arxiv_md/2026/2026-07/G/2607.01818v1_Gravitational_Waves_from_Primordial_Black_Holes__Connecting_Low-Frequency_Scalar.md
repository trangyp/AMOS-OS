Gravitational Waves from Primordial Black Holes: Connecting Low-Frequency
Scalar-Induced Signatures to High-Frequency Binary Mergers
Ashu Kushwaha1, ∗
1Department of Physics, Institute of Science Tokyo,
2-12-1 Ookayama, Meguro-ku, Tokyo 152-8551, Japan
Formation of primordial black holes (PBHs) requires a significant enhancement of curvature per-
turbations. This mechanism leaves a twofold gravitational-wave (GW) signature: a low-frequency
stochastic background of scalar-induced GWs (SIGWs) and a distinct high-frequency signal from
subsequent PBH binary mergers. We leverage this shared origin to establish a consistent, model-
independent connection between these two observables for a monochromatic PBH mass function.
Using PBH abundance constraints on the primordial curvature power spectrum, we evaluate the
stochastic SIGW background for spherical and ellipsoidal collapse models, demonstrating that the
ellipsoidal scenario yields a significantly stronger signal. Furthermore, we analyze the GW signal
from PBH binary mergers and find a direct correspondence between the SIGW frequency and the
innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) frequency of the binaries. Because GW emission is nearly
maximal near the ISCO, we additionally show that the peak of the full merger GW spectrum re-
lates to the ISCO frequency via fpeak = 1.79 fISCO, a relation that is independent of the binary
masses.
Remarkably, this unified framework connects these distinct GW channels, enabling the
same primordial fluctuations to be probed across widely separated frequency bands.
Introduction.
Primordial black holes (PBHs) can
form in the early Universe from the gravitational collapse
of large-amplitude density fluctuations [1–11].
Specif-
ically, when enhanced primordial curvature perturba-
tions generated by nontrivial physics during inflation (see
Refs. [12–23]), re-enter the Hubble horizon during radi-
ation domination (RD), they generate overdense regions
that collapse into PBHs if their amplitude exceeds a crit-
ical threshold. Therefore, PBH formation provides a sen-
sitive probe of the primordial power spectrum in regimes
inaccessible to cosmic microwave background (CMB) and
large-scale structure observations.
The enhanced primordial curvature perturbations re-
sponsible for PBH formation inevitably generate a
stochastic background of scalar-induced gravitational
waves (SIGWs) at second order when they re-enter the
Hubble horizon during the RD era [24–26]. Since gravita-
tional waves interact only weakly with matter, they pre-
serve information about their production mechanism over
cosmological timescales [27–30].
Consequently, SIGWs
provide a complementary probe of the small-scale pri-
mordial fluctuations responsible for PBH formation. Var-
ious present and future GW observatories aim to explore
these singlas over a very broad frequency range, includ-
ing pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) [31–33] LIGO-Virgo-
KAGRA [34], LISA [35], DECIGO [36], the Einstein
Telescope (ET) [37], and Cosmic Explorer (CE) [24, 26].
If PBHs form binaries, their mergers generate a sec-
ond distinct GW signal characterized by the total binary
mass, M = m1 + m2 [38–47], a scenario widely investi-
gated to explain the binary black hole merger event ob-
served by the LIGO-Virgo collaboration [48–51].

In this Letter, we formalize the connection between

ment in the primordial power spectrum, we apply re-

abundance [53] to systematically compute the resulting
stochastic SIGW background. Crucially, we evaluate the
GW signals for both spherical and ellipsoidal collapse dy-
namics, presenting the first consistent comparison of the
resulting SIGW spectra. Furthermore, we establish a di-
rect correspondence between the SIGW peak frequency
and the characteristic PBH merger frequency. This uni-
fied framework thus provides a novel, comprehensive ap-
proach to assessing GW production channels and refining
the source modeling of PBHs.

Conclusion.
We established a direct link between
SIGWs and the stochastic background from PBH binary
mergers, assuming a shared origin in enhanced primordial
curvature perturbations. Using observational constraints
on the PBH abundance, we computed the correspond-
ing amplitude of the SIGW background and derived a


## Page 5


5
simple mapping between the SIGW frequency and the
characteristic merger frequency of PBH binaries. As an
application, we presented the first comparison of the pre-
dicted amplitude of SIGW background in the spherical
and ellipsoidal PBH formation scenarios, demonstrating
that the larger collapse threshold in the ellipsoidal case
leads to a stronger GW signal.
Our results provide a unified framework for relating
low-frequency primordial GW signals to high-frequency
PBH merger signals through their common primordial
origin.
This correspondence enables complementary
probes of the same small-scale primordial fluctuations
across widely separated frequency bands and offers a new
way to interpret future GW observations. In particular,
it provides a means to connect existing and forthcoming
low-frequency GW measurements with otherwise inacces-
sible high-frequency PBH merger signals, thereby allow-
ing indirect constraints on the latter in a frequency range
where direct observations remain challenging. The for-
malism can be generalized to extended PBH mass func-
tions and broad primordial curvature power spectra.
