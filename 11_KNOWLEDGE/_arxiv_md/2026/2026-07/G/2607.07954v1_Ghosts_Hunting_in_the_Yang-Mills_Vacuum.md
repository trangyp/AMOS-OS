Abstract
In this work I analyze the zero-modes of a one-loop semi-classical Yang-Mills theory in
3+1d. I find the zero-modes are generated by gauge redundancy of the background field. With
proper gauge fixing, via the introduction of a bosonic ghost term, and the use of zeta-function
regularization, a finite one-loop effective theory is calculated in closed form, reproducing the
well known one-loop Yang-Mills beta-function.

Conclusion
In this work I have identified the zero mode of a covariantly constant and self-dual semi-classical
Yang Mills expansion as resulting from a gauge redundancy of the background field. An additional
bosonic gauge fixing term was added in light of this.
Then with the use of zeta functions the
one-loop path integral was regulated, giving both a zero and non-zero solution to the gap equation,
and the correct beta function. The non-zero solution was interpreted as existing below the Landau
pole scale in the g →0 limit where the semi-classical expansion is well defined. This is a physical
interpretation of the running coupling, however, in a strict mathematical sense the complete picture
of the running coupling includes both a positive and negative coupling regime, and these small
