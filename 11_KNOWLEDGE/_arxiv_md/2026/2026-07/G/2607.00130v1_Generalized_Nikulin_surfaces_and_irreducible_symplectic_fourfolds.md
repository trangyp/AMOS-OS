1. Introduction
Let X be a manifold of K3[2]-type admitting a symplectic involution ι. Then, the fixed locus
of ι consists of a K3 surface F and 28 isolated points. We will call F a generalized Nikulin
surface. The reason for this name is that these surfaces naturally appear as deformation of
the so called Nikulin surfaces, which are minimal resolutions of quotients of K3 surfaces by
symplectic involutions.
A K3 surface (resp. a manifold of K3[2]-type) admits a symplectic involution if and only
if its N´eron–Severi lattice contains primitively E8(−2), [vGS, C, Mon]. The quotient of a K3
surface by its symplectic involution is singular in 8 points and its minimal resolution (the
Nikulin surface) is obtained by blowing up once each of these singular points.
The N´eron–Severi lattice N of a general non projective Nikulin surface is called a Nikulin
lattice and it is the extension of the lattice ⟨−2⟩⊕8 with generators Ei, that are the exceptional
curves of the resolution, by the class 1
2(∑i Ei). A projective K3 surface is a Nikulin surface if
and only if its N´eron–Severi lattice contains primitively N, see [GS, N2].
There is another characterization of the Nikulin surfaces, which motivates the name gener-
alized Nikulin surface for the surface in the fixed locus of a symplectic involution on a manifold
of K3[2]-type. Indeed, if S is a K3 surface with a symplectic involution ιS, then the Hilbert
scheme of two points on S, S[2], is naturally endowed with a symplectic involution ι[2]

by ιS. The surface fixed by ι[2]

involution on a manifold of K3[2]-type, i.e. it is a generalized Nikulin surface.

Given a lattice L an overlattice of finite index of M is a lattice such that there exists an
injective map f ∶L ↪M which respects the bilinear forms and M/f(L) is a finite group of
order r. We will call r the index of the inclusion. The finite index overlattices of L are in 1:1

We will say that an inclusion of lattices f ∶L ↪M is primitive if M/f(L) is a free Z-module.
If R is a finite index overlattice of L ⊕M, then R is generated by the generators of L and M

2.2. Symplectic involutions on K3 surfaces and Nikulin surfaces. An automorphism α
of a K3 surface S is said to be symplectic, if its action on the symplectic form of S is trivial.
The quotient of a K3 surface by a symplectic automorphism is a singular surface, with ADE
singularities, whose minimal resolution is another K3 surface.
In particular, if one considers a symplectic involution ιS on a K3 surface S, then the quotient
S/ι has 8 singular points of type A1, which are the image of the 8 points fixed by ιS on S.
The minimal resolution contains 8 exceptional divisors Ei, which are rational curves. Therefore
E2
i = −2 and EiEj = 0 if i ≠j.
Observe that, by blowing-up S in the 8 points fixed by ιS, one obtains a non-minimal smooth
surface ̃S and ιS lifts to an involution ̃ιS of S which fixes the 8 exceptional divisors. The smooth
surface ̃S/ ̃ιS is isomorphic to the minimal resolution of S/ι and the 2 ∶1 cover ̃S →̃S/ ̃ιS is
branched on the eight curves Ei. This implies that (∑i Ei)/2 ∈NS(S).

S by a symplectic involution ιS. The Nikulin lattice N is the minimal primitive sublattice of
NS(W) which contains all the exceptional curves arising from the desingularization of S/ιS.

We recall that a K3 surface admits a symplectic involution if and only if the lattice E8(−2) is
primitively embedded in its N´eron–Severi lattice and in this case the symplectic involution acts
as −1 on this copy of E8(−2) and as +1 on its orthogonal complement in the second cohomology
group. Moreover, a projective K3 surface is a Nikulin surface if and only if its N´eron–Severi
lattice contains primitively the lattice N. In [GS, Corollary 2.2] the explicit relation between
the N´eron–Severi lattices (and the transcendental lattice) of projective K3 surfaces S admitting
a symplectic involution ι and its Nikulin surface W are explicitly given.
We observe that the Nikulin K3 surfaces are dense in the moduli space of K3 surfaces,
indeed all the Kummer surfaces are also Nikulin surfaces (see [N1]), and the Kummer surfaces
are dense in the moduli space of K3 surfaces (see [PSa]). A different way to state the same result
is that the K3 surfaces which are terminalization of the quotient of K3 surfaces by a symplectic
involution are dense in the family of their deformations (deformations as K3 surfaces, and not
just as terminalization of quotients). Even if the previous formulation is less natural, it is the
one that we can generalize in higher dimension, indeed we will prove that the Nikulin orbifolds
(terminalizations of certain quotients) are dense in the family of orbifolds of Nikulin type (i.e. in
the family of the deformations of Nikulin orbifold), see Theorem 3.1 for the precise statement.

3. The main results
The aim of this section is to present more precisely our three main theorems, which will be
proved in the next sections.
We recalled in Section 2.2 that Nikulin surfaces are characterized by a lattice-theoretic
condition, i.e. the presence of the Nikulin lattice in the N´eron–Severi lattice. We state the
analogous result for Nikulin orbifolds inside the family of orbifolds of Nikulin type. Also the
density result is the analogue in higher dimension of the density of Nikulin surfaces in the family
of K3 surfaces, see Section 2.2.
Theorem 3.1. A marked orbifold (Y,η) of Nikulin type is bimeromorphic to a Nikulin orbifold
if and only if its N´eron–Severi lattice NS(Y ) contains primitively a class of square (−4) and
divisibility 2 in ΛN.
Moreover, the locus M−4,2 of marked orbifolds of Nikulin type which are bimeromorphic to
a Nikulin orbifold is dense inside any connected component of MΛN .
Definition 3.2. Let X be a manifold of K3[2]-type admitting a symplectic involution ι. We
call generalized Nikulin surface of (X,ι) the 2-dimensional component of
Fixι(X) = {x ∈X such that ι(x) = x}.
A generalized Nikulin surface is a surface that is a generalized Nikulin surface of (X,ι), for some
manifold of K3[2] type X with symplectic involution ι, i.e. which appears as a fixed surface of
the action of a symplectic involution on a manifold of K3[2]-type.
Given a manifold of K3[2]-type X with a symplectic involution ι, there are two symplectic
varieties naturally associated to (X,ι), which are the generalized Nikulin surface of (X,ι)
