We study axial gravitational perturbations of the neutral regular black hole generated by a non-
local, T-duality-inspired zero-point length and the associated gravitational self-energy. In this ge-
ometry, the usual point source is replaced by a regular core, and the zero-point length controls the
departure from the Schwarzschild limit. We compute the fundamental quasinormal modes and sev-
eral overtones using high-order WKB–Padé methods, and we verify the dominant mode via direct
time-domain evolution. When the zero-point length is turned on, the real parts of the ADM-scaled
frequencies increase for the gravitational modes with ℓ= 2, 3, 4, so the ringdown oscillates faster
than in the Schwarzschild limit. The damping rates change more gradually: they initially increase
slightly and then decrease near the largest deformation values considered here. This behavior is
consistent with the eﬀective potential, whose barrier becomes higher as the deformation parameter
increases. We also compute the corresponding excitation factors and ﬁnd that their magnitudes
vary much less strongly than the quasinormal frequencies.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The ringdown of a black hole is governed by its quasi-
normal spectrum, a set of damped oscillations ﬁxed by
the spacetime geometry and by the type of perturbation
under consideration.
These modes provide one of the
cleanest links between the theoretical structure of a com-
pact object and the signal measured after a dynamical
disturbance. Their interpretation, accuracy, and limita-
tions have been developed in a large body of work [1–5].
In particular, diﬀerent parts of the spectrum probe dif-
ferent geometric regions: the near-horizon geometry can
aﬀect the ﬁrst overtones [6], while the eikonal modes are
controlled by the properties of unstable null orbits [7].
For this reason, quasinormal modes are a sensitive diag-
nostic of regular, quantum-corrected, or otherwise mod-
iﬁed black-hole backgrounds.
Regular black holes are especially useful for this pur-
pose because they keep the exterior causal structure of a
black hole while replacing the central singularity with a
ﬁnite core. Such geometries arise in several eﬀective set-
tings, including nonlinear electrodynamics, asymptotic-
safety inspired models, noncommutative constructions,

III.
METHODS FOR QUASINORMAL MODES
The numerical analysis combines a frequency-domain
calculation with a direct evolution of the master equa-
tion. The tabulated spectra are obtained from a high-
order WKB expansion improved by Padé resummation.
This approach is particularly eﬃcient for the axial gravi-
tational potential considered here, because the potential
has the usual single-barrier shape illustrated in Fig. 1.
The time-domain calculation is used as a separate diag-
nostic: it evolves the same wave equation without ex-
panding around the potential maximum and then ex-
tracts the dominant ringing frequency from the wave-
form.

decreases more clearly in the near-extremal regime. Over
the full range now shown, |B20| changes from about 0.100

about 0.071 to 0.065.
Thus, the self-energy deforma-
tion still shifts the pole positions more visibly than it
changes the intrinsic residue of the fundamental gravita-
tional pole.
The ordering with multipole number is also robust:
|B20| > |B30| > |B40| for every value of l0/MADM
in Table III. In this Green-function normalization, the
quadrupole fundamental mode is therefore the most
strongly excited of the three axial gravitational modes
considered here, while the higher multipoles have smaller
residues. The complex phases vary smoothly with the
deformation parameter. This phase motion should not
be interpreted as a separate stability criterion; it reﬂects
the scattering normalization and the convention for r∗.
A physical waveform amplitude is obtained only after
multiplying Bℓ0 by the source or initial-data integral ap-
propriate to the perturbation.
VI.

the regular self-energy black hole generated by a zero-

terms of the ADM mass, because the bare parameter M
is not the mass measured at inﬁnity: for M = 1 one has
MADM = 1 + 3π/(32l0). Expressing the spectra in the
dimensionless variables MADMω and l0/MADM therefore
separates the physical change of the exterior potential

The WKB–Padé data show a clear and stable pattern.
For the fundamental gravitational modes with ℓ= 2, 3, 4,
the real part of MADMω increases monotonically up to
the near-extremal endpoint l0 = 0.82, whereas the damp-

14th-order Padé-resummed WKB values is excellent for
the fundamental modes and remains good for the ﬁrst
overtones, which supports the numerical stability of the
trends.
For the ℓ= 2 overtone sequence, the ADM-
rescaled real parts increase through most of the interval,
but the higher overtones ﬂatten or turn over close to the
near-extremal endpoint, while the damping rates remain
ordered by overtone number.
The time-domain evolution provides an independent
check of the frequency-domain calculation.
The time-
domain integration data is in very good concordance with
the WKB results. The complex relative diﬀerence is only
∼10−5%, and the damping rates diﬀer by sim×10−4%.
At asymptotically late times, we conﬁrm the Price-law
exponent t−(2ℓ+3) and show that the regular self-energy
core does not modify the leading asymptotic tail, as ex-
pected from the asymptotic ﬂatness of the metric and
the Schwarzschild-like long-range structure of the axial
potential.
We have also computed the excitation factors for
the fundamental gravitational modes in a ﬁxed ADM
tortoise-coordinate convention for zero-point lengths up
to l0 = 0.82. The residues are less sensitive to the zero-
point length than the pole locations themselves, but they
decrease gradually in the near-extremal regime. Across
the interval displayed in Table III, the moduli vary from
about |B20| ≃0.100 to 0.092, from |B30| ≃0.082 to 0.075,
and from |B40| ≃0.071 to 0.065. Thus, the principal im-
print of the self-energy correction is still a shift of the
ringing frequencies and damping rates, while the hierar-
chy |B20| > |B30| > |B40| is preserved for all values of
l0/MADM considered here.
These results also indicate how the model could be
confronted with ringdown observations. A Bayesian com-
parison should use l0/MADM as the deformation param-
eter and interpolate the ADM-scaled quasinormal mode
frequencies tabulated here. The likelihood can be built
from damped-sinusoid ringdown templates in which the
mode frequencies and damping times are ﬁxed functions
of l0/MADM, while the amplitudes, phases, start time,
and noise parameters are treated as nuisance parame-
ters.
The excitation factors may guide physically mo-
tivated amplitude priors, but they should not be identi-
ﬁed directly with observed amplitudes, because the latter
also depend on the source and initial data. In such an
analysis, the main discriminants would be the frequency
and damping shifts; the t−7 tail and the weakly vary-

Finally, the quasinormal mode data may be combined

provide a useful extension of the present study, because
the quasinormal frequencies, excitation factors, late-time
tails, and grey-body factors probe complementary as-
pects of wave propagation in the same zero-point-length
background.
Our research could be extended to perturbations of
massive ﬁelds possessing a number of distinctive features
in both time and frequency domains [122–129].
