Dynamic Trajectory Prediction Utilizing Black-Box
Modeling of Inverter-Based Resources
Sungjoo Chung, Student Graduate Member, IEEE, Ying Zhang, Senior Member, IEEE, Meng Yue, Hantao Cui,
Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Black-box modeling of inverter-based resources
(IBRs) has become essential for real-time grid operation and
control in the presence of proprietary electronic control archi-
tectures. Existing machine learning (ML)-based online dynamic
trajectory prediction approaches using IBR black-box models
either significantly accumulate prediction errors when multiple
surrogates are simultaneously used or ignore measurement er-
rors, limiting their deployment in practical grids. To address these
limitations, this paper proposes a novel network interdependency-
informed ML algorithm for online dynamic trajectory prediction
in IBR-integrated power systems. A modular spatiotemporal
attention network (STAN)-based predictor for the black-box
modeling of each IBR unit is first proposed. Utilizing past
measurements, the proposed STAN can effectively capture and
predict the spatiotemporal dynamics of IBRs by employing an
attention mechanism to attend to the most pertinent features
for trajectory prediction. Furthermore, a novel hybrid physics-
informed loss function that integrates a decoupled linearized AC
power flow formulation is proposed. The proposed loss function
effectively ensures physical consistency of predictions within
network operation while avoiding the computational complexity
of iterative power flow solving, thereby enabling efficient gradient
backpropagation and overall improved prediction accuracy. Case
studies on the IEEE 14- and WECC 179-bus systems demonstrate
that the proposed method achieves significant accuracy enhance-
ment and robustness against measurement errors, outperforming
recent ML-based trajectory prediction methods.
Index Terms—Dynamic trajectory prediction, inverter-based
resources, black-box modeling, physics-informed neural net-
works, machine learning, data-driven.
I. INTRODUCTION
I
NVERTER-based resources (IBRs) are at the forefront of
grid modernization to enhance system stability, enable self-
synchronization, and support reliable operation under low-
inertia conditions [1]. As the penetration of variable renew-
able energy continues to grow, inverter-based generation is
projected to contribute more than 50% of the total capacity
at any given time in future grids [2]. Grid-forming inverters
have garnered growing attention due to their ability to ac-
tively control voltage outputs, in contrast to grid-following
inverters. This capability enables them to provide immediate
ancillary services, such as reactive power support, to enhance
grid resilience during contingencies [3]. Unlike synchronous
generators (SGs) that provide abundant inertia to counter
frequency deviations after disturbances, IBRs contribute less
inertia, making grids increasingly susceptible to disturbances.

To this end, accurately predicting transient dynamics becomes
more challenging. Another issue is the proprietary nature
of IBRs, as original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) often
restrict access to detailed control and design information
of IBRs due to confidentiality requirements. This limits the
feasibility of conventional numerical and model-based ap-
proaches for dynamic trajectory prediction, and thus, black-
box modeling approaches for IBRs have attracted significant
interest from academia and industry [4]. This paper thus
focuses on developing and utilizing black-box IBR modeling
to construct an efficient dynamic trajectory prediction method
in IBR-rich power systems, leveraging both physics-based
and data-driven (black-box) modeling approaches. Such online
predictive models provide essential references for various grid
applications, such as transient stability assessment [5], [6] and
online stability control [7].
Conventional model-based approaches to dynamic trajec-
tory simulation or assessment rely primarily on time-domain
simulation [8], [9] by solving nonlinear differential-algebraic
equations (DAEs). While accurate, their reliance on detailed
system information and high computational resources makes
them unsuitable for online deployment. Meanwhile, recent
proliferation in phasor measurement units (PMUs) has in-
creased the availability of high-resolution data. This en-
ables machine learning (ML) models to capture the complex,
nonlinear dynamics of power systems, which has led to a
surge in ML-based approaches for online dynamic trajectory
prediction. Prior studies have shown that an efficient ML
surrogate model can significantly enhance computational ef-
ficiency when (partially) replacing the time-consuming DAE
solving procedure [10]. These ML-based methods for dynamic
trajectory prediction can be mainly divided into 1) data-driven
ML [11]–[16], 2) physics-informed ML, which incorporates
physical constraints and domain knowledge into the learning
algorithm [10], [17]–[20]. In [11], a long short-term memory
(LSTM)-based recurrent neural network is constructed for
online prediction of the dynamic state of an SG, where
voltage measurements from its neighboring buses are used
as additional inputs. PMU noise and time delays are further
considered in the LSTM input in [12]. Yet, both methods
focus on predicting single-component-level dynamics and do
not consider the network coupling of their dynamics, which
can compromise the overall prediction accuracy across the
system. To address this limitation, [13] introduces a similarity
matrix-based graph learning approach to capture correlations
between SGs, enabling topology-informed prediction of power
system transient dynamics. Nonetheless, this approach remains

To improve the prediction accuracy and ensure consis-

have been explored by embedding power flow formulation
[17], governing/swing equations [10], [18], or network topol-

(ACPF)-computed voltages of the neighboring buses of an

the scalability of this approach to a large-scale system with
multiple SGs remains a challenge. Two physics-informed
neural networks (PINNs), which embed the swing equation
into their loss functions, are proposed in [18] to accurately
model the system dynamics under transient stability and
instability conditions. However, the measurement errors of
PMUs are neglected. A plug-and-play PINN, which acts as
a surrogate model at the component level, is integrated into
time-domain system simulation in [10]. Compared with most
existing research on predicting the transient responses of SGs
[10], [17]–[19], the literature on IBR trajectory prediction
remains limited. A Kron-reduction-based optimization is in-
tegrated with a deep neural network in [20]. However, the
IBR modeling method is a white-box approach and requires
complete knowledge of the internal control details.
Existing ML-based dynamic trajectory prediction methods
have several limitations, and only a handful of papers [16],
[20] are designed for IBR-integrated power systems. First,
most of these methods focus on a single dynamic component,
either IBR or SG, and often neglect the network-induced
interdependency between them, which can lead to significantly
accumulated prediction errors when multiple surrogates are
used simultaneously in the grid. Second, these methods often
overlook PMU errors in practical environments. Lastly, the
existing methods developed for SGs might be unsuitable
for IBRs, which demand higher accuracy and computational
efficiency. This is because the absence of inertia in IBRs, along
with their faster control responses compared to SGs, renders
the grid increasingly dynamic and susceptible to disturbances
[7]. These gaps motivate the pursuit of an efficient physics-
informed ML method for dynamic trajectory prediction, ensur-
ing overall accuracy and practical applicability in the presence
of measurement errors.

II. PRELIMINARIES AND PROBLEM FORMULATION
The dynamics of power grids are governed by a set of
nonlinear DAEs. Without loss of generality, the DAEs can
be expressed as:

through the network connections in (2).
The dynamics of a power system are governed by several
factors, such as IBR control structures, generator dynamics,
dynamic load characteristics, and network topology. While the
internal states of SGs are well-defined, the proprietary nature
of inverter controls renders the conventional numeric iterative
approach to solve (1) and (2) impractical [21].

contingencies such as generator loss or load shedding in the
grid, an enhanced black-box modeling of IBR nodes can be
an efficient tool to quickly solve for the voltage status on all
IBR nodes across the grid. As mentioned above, numerical
approaches for such applications prove inadequate due to
inherent uncertainty in contingency information, as well as
the undisclosed physical model of the IBRs. Instead, the
proposed black-box model leverages historical sequences of

III. PROPOSED NETWORK INTERDEPENDENCY-INFORMED
DYNAMIC TRAJECTORY PREDICTION METHOD
A network interdependency-informed STAN-based frame-
work for online dynamic trajectory prediction is proposed.
Illustrated in Fig. 3, with historical PMU measurements and
estimated neighboring bus voltages as the input, each STAN-
based predictor predicts the dynamics of that IBR unit by
capturing the spatiotemporal dependencies. To further enhance
overall prediction accuracy, a novel physics-informed hybrid
loss function is implemented to ensure physical consistency of
the predicted dynamic trajectories of all IBR buses. Moreover,
this modularity allows the proposed method to be easily scaled
to predict the dynamics of IBR and non-metered buses in
large-scale power systems. These predictions are then used
in ACPF calculations to compute system-wide dynamic tra-
jectories for all IBR and non-metered buses efficiently and
robustly.

B. Incorporating Network Interdependency
Solely relying on the data-driven loss function in (14)
may make the black-box model highly susceptible to mea-
surement errors. This issue becomes especially pronounced
when multiple ML-based predictors, locally trained on error-
prone measurements, are collectively utilized for power system
operations. Moreover, minimizing (14) independently for each
IBR bus neglects the coupling among buses imposed by the
underlying power flow equations. Consequently, the predic-
tions may fit local trajectories well but violate global network
interdependencies, leading to physically implausible voltage
predictions. To overcome the limitations of the purely data-
driven loss function, a hybrid physics-informed loss function
is proposed, ensuring system-wide physical consistency of the
predictions by incorporating power flow calculations.

C. Network Interdependency-Informed STAN Gradient Update
The decoupled linearized ACPF model integrated into the
proposed framework ensures that derivatives can be propagated
efficiently through the solver, providing numerically stable
gradients that are essential for the end-to-end training of the
network interdependency-informed STAN architecture.
In (23), the STAN predictions directly influence the power

D. Multi-Timestep and System-Wide Trajectory Prediction
As illustrated in Fig. 3, the proposed STAN predictors serve
as black-box models on each IBR bus, generating predictions
of the dynamic states of these buses at each timestep. Based on
these collective predictions ˆxt, the decoupled linearized ACPF
solution ˆzt is obtained. Together, they form the system-wide
trajectory prediction of all IBR and non-IBR buses. Due to the
modularity of the proposed black-box approach for individual
IBR buses, the proposed surrogate can be used in large-scale
power systems by replacing each IBR bus with the proposed
STAN predictor.
Fig. 4 illustrates the STAN predictor architecture, which
achieves multi-step prediction by modifying the final fully
connected layer to output TR future states according to (6). To
predict the full trajectory up to Tend, the multi-step predictor is
applied recursively. The pseudocode for the training procedure
of the proposed framework is shown in Algorithm 1.
IV. CASE STUDIES
The proposed algorithm is tested on the IEEE 14-bus and
WECC 179-bus systems [26], which are modified by adding
droop-controlled IBRs. Comprehensive control schemes, such
as turbine governors and excitation systems, are adopted to
model SGs and IBRs. To simulate various contingencies,
generator fault and load shedding are set to occur at 0.1s in
ANDES [27]. The base loads are randomly varied between
80% and 120% to simulate different system loading condi-
tions. The PMU measurement errors are considered, and the
maximum errors are set as 1% [28]. The initial input to the
proposed algorithm consists of the initial post-fault voltage
trajectories of all IBR buses over 2.0 s, corresponding to 120
timesteps. The total simulation time is 20 s with a sampling
rate of 60 Hz. A total of 4, 000 trajectories are generated for
each fault scenario, which is partitioned randomly into training
(70%), validation (20%), and testing (10%) subsets. Fig. 5
exemplifies partial voltage magnitude trajectories generated in
the WECC 179-bus system for the offline training purpose.
The performance of the proposed algorithm is compared to
two methods: a purely data-driven LSTM network and a recent
physics-informed ML approach [17] (namely, PF-integrated
ML). The PF-integrated ML method is adapted as an LSTM
black-box for IBR buses that incorporates power flow solutions
as inputs to predict their dynamic trajectories. The trajectory

V. CONCLUSION
This paper proposes a network interdependency-informed
framework for online dynamic trajectory prediction in power
systems with high IBR penetration. The proposed STAN-
based predictor captures temporal dependencies in individual
IBR bus dynamics and spatial interdependencies among neigh-
boring buses, enabling accurate black-box modeling without
access to proprietary control details. By incorporating the
decoupled linearized ACPF equations into a hybrid physics-
informed loss function, the model preserves system-wide phys-
ical consistency and enhances robustness against measurement
