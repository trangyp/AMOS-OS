ABSTRACT
We used the 6-meter BTA telescope to determine radial velocities for 46 galaxies, previously considered to be nearby objects.
Twelve of them have kinematic distances within 13 Mpc and are new probable companions to the bright Local Volume galaxies:
NGC 1068, NGC 2903, NGC 4517, NGC 4565, NGC 4826. We also found a new tiny satellite of the nearby (10.38 Mpc) SMC-
like dwarf galaxy DDO 46. Two isolated dIrr galaxies: dw 1408-0802 and Dw 1552-1027 are probably located inside the Local
Void. The spectrum of the latter shows a rich set of emission lines. Using the radial velocities and projected separations of 13
NGC 1068 satellites, we estimated the total mass of the group to be 𝑀= (2.6 ± 1.0) × 1012 𝑀⊙.
Key words: galaxies – dwarf – distances and redshifts – groups
1 INTRODUCTION
Radial velocity anchors a galaxy’s position in phase space and under-
pins dynamical studies from satellite systems to the local peculiar-
velocity field. Major optical and radio surveys — SDSS (Abazajian
et al. 2009), HIPASS (Koribalski et al. 2004), ALFALFA (Haynes
et al. 2011), FAST/FASHI (Zhang et al. 2024) — have progres-
sively expanded the redshift catalogue, and the latest multiplexed
spectroscopy has pushed the total yield to tens of millions (DESI
Collaboration et al. 2024). Yet nearby dwarf galaxies, especially
low-surface-brightness systems, remain kinematically incomplete:
selection biases in optical spectroscopy and practical limitations of
blind 21-cm surveys at very low heliocentric velocities leave a per-
sistent gap.
The Local Volume (LV; 𝐷≤11–12 Mpc) is the regime where this
incompleteness matters most. The UNGC/LVGDB1 (Karachentsev
et al. 2013) currently lists more than ∼1500 LV galaxies, yet radial
velocities are still missing for a significant fraction. The DESI Legacy
Imaging Surveys (Dey et al. 2019) have accelerated the discovery of
new dwarf candidates (e.g. Karachentsev et al. 2023, 2025); targeted
follow-up is beginning to close the kinematic gap: Karachentsev
et al. (2025) obtained optical long-slit velocities for 40 candidates
and identified multiple new LV companions, while Nazarova et al.
(2025) measured HI velocities for 105 nearby dwarfs with the GBT
(77 detections). Where velocities are available, they provide the basis
for preliminary kinematic distances via the Numerical Action Method
(NAM) (Shaya et al. 2017; Kourkchi et al. 2020).
Here we present new BTA long-slit radial velocities for 46 LV
dwarf candidates selected from the DESI Legacy Imaging Survey,
with the aim of improving velocity completeness, refining group
membership, and providing NAM distance estimates. The observa-
tions and reduction are described in Section 2; results are given in

2 OBSERVATIONS WITH THE 6-METER BTA
TELESCOPE
We performed long-slit observations at the 6-m telescope of the
Special Astrophysical Observatory of the Russian Academy of Sci-
ences with the multi-mode focal reducers SCORPIO-1 (Afanasiev &
Moiseev 2005) and SCORPIO-2 (Afanasiev & Moiseev 2011). The
VPHG1200R grating was used with SCORPIO-1, providing a spec-
tral resolution of 6 Å over 5700–7500 Å, while the VPHG1200@540
grating was used with SCORPIO-2, providing a spectral resolu-
tion of 5.2 Å over 3650–7300 Å. The slit width and length were
(1.0–1.2)′′ × 5′, respectively. The observations were carried out in
late 2024, throughout 2025 and in early 2026 under typical seeing of
1–2′′, with exposure times of 300–2700 s.
We performed data reduction using standard procedures imple-
mented in an IDL-based pipeline, as described in detail by Egorov
et al. (2018). Line-of-sight velocities along the slit were measured by
fitting Gaussian profiles to the H𝛼and other emission lines, when the
S/N ratio was sufficiently high. The barycentric velocity correction2
was applied using the Astropy package (Astropy Collaboration et al.
2013). We computed the Local Group correction assuming a Solar
apex with 𝑙= 93◦, 𝑏= 4◦, and 𝑣⊙= 316 km s−1 (Karachentsev &
Makarov 1996). The typical accuracy of the radial velocity measure-
ments was ∼10 km s−1 for galaxies with strong emission lines. Since
our observations were carried out episodically over 2024–2026, a
significant overlap with objects from the DESI spectroscopic sur-
vey (DESI Collaboration et al. 2024) and Nazarova et al. (2025) was
found a posteriori.

3 RESULTS
We observed a total of 62 galaxies. Strong emission lines (at least
H𝛼) are present in 34 objects, 12 additional galaxies show only weak
H𝛼emission with very low signal-to-noise ratios, and 16 galaxies
do not exhibit detectable emission lines. Representative spectra for
both cameras are shown in Fig. 1. The results of the measured radial
velocities are presented in Tables A1 and A2.
The kinematic distances in column (7) were derived using the
NAM Distance–Velocity Calculator (Kourkchi et al. 2020), which
computes expectation distances from radial velocities based on
a smoothed local velocity field within 38 Mpc. The underlying
model (Shaya et al. 2017) reconstructs physically consistent, fully
nonlinear gravitational orbits for 1382 galaxies and groups, con-
strained by Cosmicflows survey data, thereby correcting for peculiar
motions taking into account the local flow towards the Virgo cluster
and the Local Void expansion that would otherwise bias a simple
Hubble-law distance conversion. The distances measured in the cur-
rent work include a systematic correction of Δ𝐷= +0.3 Mpc, derived
from a comparison of NAM and TRGB distances for 430 LV galax-
ies (Karachentsev & Popova 2024). The typical random uncertainty
of individual NAM distances within the LV is 1.6 Mpc, applicable
to galaxies lying outside the ∼25◦exclusion zone around the Virgo
cluster.
4 DISCUSSION
Among the observed dwarf galaxies with measured radial velocities,
12 objects have NAM distances less than 13 Mpc. Two and five
galaxies in our sample are identified as members of the NGC 1068
and NGC 4151 groups, respectively.

4.2 NGC 1068 group
The group of galaxies around the luminous spiral galaxy NGC 1068
was presented in the catalogue of nearby groups by Makarov &
Karachentsev (2011). Based on data for ten of its members with
known radial velocities, the authors estimated the radial velocity
dispersion in the group to be 𝜎𝑣= 80 km s−1, the projected har-
monic radius to be 387 kpc, the integrated 𝐾-band luminosity of
the group to be log(𝐿𝐾/𝐿⊙) = 11.54, and the virial mass to be
log(𝑀𝑣/𝑀⊙) = 12.79 at a group distance of 15.9 Mpc. Similar val-
ues for the parameters of this group were also obtained by Kourkchi
& Tully (2017). Over the past decade, several more members of this
group have been discovered due to the ALFALFA sky survey in the
21 cm hydrogen line (Haynes et al. 2018).
We undertook a visual search for new satellites of NGC 1068
in the sky region bounded by the coordinates 𝛼= [37.0, 44.0]◦
and 𝛿= [−3.5, +4.0]◦, using DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys (Dey
et al. 2019). As a result, we found six new candidate satellites:
Dw 0229−0318 = PGC 1073199, Dw 0232−0256, Dw 0242+0133,
Dw 0242−0009, Dw 0243−0108 and Dw 0244−0015. The last one
was identified as the dwarf spheroidal galaxy SMDG 0244−0015 in
the catalogue by Zaritsky et al. (2022). Our measurements of ra-
dial velocities showed that PGC 1073199 and Dw 0232−0256 are
background galaxies beyond the group, while Dw 0242+0133 and
Dw 0243−0108 are true satellites of NGC 1068. The dwarf irregu-
lar galaxy Dw 0242−0009 has a very low surface brightness, which
makes it difficult to measure its optical velocity. A list of 17 supposed
members of the NGC 1068 group is presented in Table A3. Three
galaxies in the group have individual distance estimates via surface
brightness fluctuations (Carlsten et al. 2026). Based on the 21 cm
linewidth data, 𝑊, from LEDA, we estimated the Tully–Fisher dis-
tances to eight galaxies using the relation 𝑀𝐵= −7.27 log𝑊−19.99
(Tully et al. 2008), which is an empirical correlation between a
galaxy’s rotation velocity – measured from the width of its 21 cm
line – and its intrinsic luminosity.
This method yields distance uncertainties of approximately 15 −
20% per spiral disk-dominated galaxy, but for dwarf irregular galax-
ies these are higher, up to 30 −50% depending on their luminos-
ity (Karachentsev et al. 2017). For five galaxies, their distances were
taken to be the same as the host galaxy’s distance derived from
Cepheids.
Distribution of galaxies in the considered sky region is presented
in Fig. 5. According to Markham et al. (2026), the distance of the
group via Cepheids is 10.72 ± 0.52 Mpc, which is significantly less
than that accepted by Makarov & Karachentsev (2011). The average
distance of galaxies in the group derived by TF- and sbf-methods is
11.48 ± 0.55 Mpc, which agrees with the Cepheid distance within
statistical errors. However, the average kinematic distance of the
group, ⟨𝐷NAM⟩= 13.54 ± 0.46 Mpc, is higher.

5 CONCLUSIONS
Deep wide-field imaging surveys continue to outpace spectroscopic
follow-up, leaving a growing fraction of photometric dwarf candi-
dates without kinematic confirmation. Our observations demonstrate
that long-slit spectroscopy at a large telescope remains an efficient
means of closing this gap, even for low-surface-brightness systems.
We observed 62 dwarf galaxy candidates with the BTA 6-m tele-
scope using the SCORPIO-1 and SCORPIO-2 focal reducers. Strong
emission lines were detected in 34 objects; 12 show only weak H𝛼;
16 have no detectable emission. Radial velocities were measured for
46 galaxies in total. The main results are as follows.
• Of the 26 objects drawn from the Local Volume database (Ta-
ble A1), 12 have NAM kinematic distances within 13 Mpc and are
new probable companions to the bright Local Volume galaxies. The
remaining objects from this table turned out to be background galax-
ies; the most distant interlopers are two blue compact galaxies from
Hunter et al. (2025) erroneously assumed to be satellites of NGC 672
and NGC 784.
• Two dIrr galaxies, SMDG 0740+4032 (𝑉ℎ= 363 km s−1) and
dw 0741+4005 (𝑉ℎ= 287 km s−1), are new satellites of the nearby
SMC-like dwarf DDO 46 (TRGB distance 10.38 Mpc).
• dw 0936+2135 is identified as a new dSph satellite of NGC 2903
