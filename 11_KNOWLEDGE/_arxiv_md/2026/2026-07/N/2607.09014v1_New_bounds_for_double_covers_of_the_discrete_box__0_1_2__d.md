NEW BOUNDS FOR DOUBLE COVERS OF THE DISCRETE BOX {0, 1, 2}d
PATRICK WHITE
Abstract. A proper sub-box of A = {0, 1, 2}d is a product S1 × · · · × Sd with each ∅̸= Si ⊊
{0, 1, 2}. A double cover is a finite multiset of proper sub-boxes covering every point of A exactly
twice; write f(d) for the minimum number of boxes in a double cover. Leader, Mili´cevi´c and
Tan asked whether f(d) ≥2d for all d (recorded by Buci´c–Lidick´y–Long–Wagner and restated as
Question 4.1 of the PatternBoost paper of Charton–Ellenberg–Wagner–Williamson), in analogy
with the classical theorem of Alon–Bohman–Holzman–Kleitman that a partition into proper
sub-boxes needs at least 2d boxes. Prior to this work no lower bound better than the trivial
volume bound was known, and the question was open for every d ≥2.
We prove the first nontrivial lower bounds. A modular refinement of the parity argument
gives f(d) ≥2d+1/(d + 1); a slicing argument crossed with the volume identity gives f(4) ≥19
and f(5) ≥33, both strictly larger than 2d, thereby resolving the question affirmatively for d = 4
and d = 5 — the first cases beyond the trivially known d ≤3. A finer “line rigidity” argument
yields f(6) ≥60, breaking the barrier that all profile-statistic methods (which we show provably
cannot exceed 57 at d = 6) run into. The entire lower-bound development is formally verified in
Lean 4: the theorem f(6) ≥60 is machine-checked with no hypotheses and depends only on the
three standard axioms of the Lean/Mathlib kernel.
On the upper-bound side we give a dimension-lifting construction f(r+3) ≤6 · 2r + 3f(r),
yielding f(6) ≤81 (improving the previously best known 82) and the asymptotic bound
f(d) ≤( 6

1. Introduction
1.1. The problem. Fix the three-element alphabet [3] := {0, 1, 2} and the discrete box A := [3]d.
A proper sub-box of A is a set
B = S1 × S2 × · · · × Sd,
∅̸= Si ⊊[3] (1 ≤i ≤d),
so each side Si has one or two elements. Write k(B) := #{i : |Si| = 2} for the number of
two-element sides, so that |B| = 2k(B).
A classical theorem of Alon, Bohman, Holzman and Kleitman [1] (answering a question of
Kearnes and Kiss) states that any partition of A into proper sub-boxes — a family in which
every point lies in exactly one box — has at least 2d members, and this is tight. The proof is a
parity argument over “odd” sub-boxes that we recall and refine in Section 3.
Leader, Mili´cevi´c and Tan asked the natural relaxation: what if every point must be covered
exactly twice?
Definition 1.1. A double cover of A = [3]d is a finite multiset B = {B1, . . . , Bm} of proper
sub-boxes such that every point of A lies in exactly two of the Bj (counted with multiplicity).
Let f(d) denote the minimum size m of a double cover of [3]d.

Question 1.2 (Leader–Mili´cevi´c–Tan; [2, §5], [4, Question 4.1]). Is f(d) ≥2d for every d?
We attribute Question 1.2 to Leader, Mili´cevi´c and Tan, who pose it—in a more general
form—as Question 4 of their paper Decomposing the complete r-graph [3]; it is restated for double
covers in the concluding section of Buci´c–Lidick´y–Long–Wagner [2, §5] and as Question 4.1 of
Charton–Ellenberg–Wagner–Williamson [4, §4.5].1
The question is genuinely harder than its partition analogue, and the reason is structural. The
Alon–Bohman–Holzman–Kleitman argument is a count modulo 2: it survives whenever every
point is covered an odd number of times. Cover every point exactly twice and the parity signal
vanishes — 2 ≡0 (mod 2) — so the tool that resolves the partition problem is blind to the
double cover. Recovering information from the surviving “2” is the central difficulty.

to beat 2d”. The PatternBoost authors [4, §4.5], using a machine-learning-guided local search,

The construction frontier. In Section 8 we study when a “skeleton-plus-holes” construction

8. The construction frontier and its relation to Question 1.2
Both lifts above share a shape: a set of S all-pair “skeleton” boxes that double-cover [3]j
off a hole-set H, together with c proper “mask” boxes partitioning H. We record a numerical
regularity and explain why understanding it in full is equivalent to Question 1.2.
Call such a configuration a (j, S, c) skeleton construction: a word-set T ⊆[3]j with |T| = S
whose all-pair boxes At = Q

9. Is the conjecture true?
A reader is entitled to ask what we think the answer to Question 1.2 actually is. We lean
firmly toward yes: we conjecture that f(d) ≥2d for every d. Our reason lies almost entirely on
the construction side rather than the lower-bound side.
The lower-bound methods of this paper provably stop short of 2d for d ≥6. The profile ceiling
(Proposition 6.1) caps every bound that reads only the k-profile at 57 < 64, and the line-rigidity
invariant caps at exactly 60 < 64 (Section 6). So our inability to prove f(6) ≥64 measures the
reach of the present tools, not the location of the answer. What persuades us is the construction
wall of Theorem 8.1: every skeleton construction we have examined — the (3, 6, 3) leapfrog,
the (4, 8, 9) four-step, a (5, 10, 23) example, and exhaustively all 279 all-pair skeletons at j = 3
— satisfies S + c = 2j + 1, giving asymptotic constant S/(S −1) > 1, never ≤1. The ABHK

every claim, by independent computation and by formal proof, before accepting it. The reasoning
