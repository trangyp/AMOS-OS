N. MORA CUELLAR, A. IOSEVICH, N. KULKARNI, I. ROJAS ARAVENA, AND A. YAVICOLI
Abstract. A set E ⊂R is measure universal if every set of positive Lebesgue measure contains
an affine copy of E. By a theorem of Bourgain, a sum of three infinite sets is never measure
universal, while the two-set regime is one of the central open cases of the Erd˝os similarity
conjecture.
We develop a finite-grid method for the two-set regime, based on Kolountzakis’
finite-gap criterion: non-universality follows whenever one can construct arbitrarily large finite
blocks at bounded scale with minimal gap at least e−o(n).
Our first main result is phrased through counting functions.
If S1, S2 contain lacunary
subsequences whose counting functions I(W), J(W), defined as the numbers of terms above
e−W , satisfy lim supW →∞I(W)J(W)/W = ∞, then S1 + S2 and S1 −S2 are not measure
universal.
No scale-separation or relative-decay hypothesis relates the two sequences; their
decay rates may trade off against each other.
The key ingredient is a near-additive-energy
estimate: the cross-sums of two lacunary sequences have uniformly controlled clustering, so a
positive proportion of them are well separated at every scale.
Our second main result is the endpoint of this trade-off, where one summand is as dense as
a lacunary sequence can be. If S contains a lacunary subsequence (bi) with −log bi = O(i), for
instance any geometric sequence, then S + A and S −A are not measure universal for every
infinite set A ⊂R; in particular {2−n} + A is never measure universal. To our knowledge this is
the first two-set non-universality theorem in which one summand is completely arbitrary. In fact,
lacunarity is needed on only one factor: a packing-function variant, proved by a related near-
energy estimate, asks only that the other factor carry enough metric mass, with no lacunarity
or sequence structure required of it.
These theorems recover the standard geometric and stretched-exponential double-sum exam-
ples and extend them to the asymmetric range 1/α1 + 1/α2 > 1. The same method applies to
difference sets, and no comparison between the scales of the two summands is required.
1. Introduction
Let E ⊂R. We say that E is measure universal if for every measurable L ⊂R with m(L) > 0
there exist x ∈R and λ ̸= 0 with
x + λE ⊂L.
Determining which infinite sets are measure universal is the Erd˝os similarity problem. By the
Lebesgue density theorem every finite set is measure universal, and Erd˝os conjectured that these
are the only ones: no infinite set is measure universal. The conjecture remains open. Since no
unbounded set, and no set without an accumulation point, can be measure universal, it is enough
to consider infinite sets with an accumulation point. After translating and passing to an infinite
subsequence, such a set contains a decreasing sequence an →0. By monotonicity of measure
universality under inclusion, the conjecture therefore reduces to showing that every decreasing
sequence an →0 is not measure universal.
In this reduced setting the decisive parameter is the rate of decay. Falconer [3] and, inde-
pendently, Eigen [2] proved that sublacunary sequences — those with an+1/an →1 — are not

1.1. Results. The purpose of this paper is to develop a finite-grid implementation of Kolountza-

separation by a near-additive-energy estimate, and to extend the resulting argument from two
lacunary sequences to a lacunary sequence plus an arbitrary infinite set.
Our organizing tool is a finite-block mechanism for proving non-universality.

4. Further remarks
Remark 25. All results of this paper consume Theorem 9 as a black box: the proofs construct
the required blocks and use nothing else about the criterion. Consequently, any strengthening of
Theorem 9 transfers verbatim to Theorems 1–3 and their corollaries. Two caveats delimit what
such strengthenings can say. First, for the sumsets of sequences considered here — which are
countable — an avoiding set of full measure is impossible: as observed in [9, §1.3], if E ⊆[0, 1] has
measure 1 and A is bounded and countable, then almost every translate of a suitably contracted
copy of A lies in E. (This is in contrast with uncountable sets, for which full-measure avoidance
can hold [9, Theorem 1.3], [4, 11].) Natural quantitative substitutes are avoiding sets of measure
arbitrarily close to 1, which the construction in [8] already provides, and statements in which
the exceptional set of dilations is null, as in [8, Theorem 2]. Second, the “large sets” of [10] —
sets of density ≥1 −ϵ in every unit interval avoiding copies of a given unbounded sequence —
concern the Erd˝os problem in the large, a different regime from the null sequences studied here;
see also [5].
