Introduction.—Annual-modulation searches rest on a
time-domain signature: a rate component Sm cos ω(t−t0)
with a one-year period and a predicted phase.
In the
standard halo model (SHM) the expectation t0 ≃152.5 d
(June 2) follows from the solar motion toward Cygnus
combined with the Earth’s orbital velocity [1, 2]. The
DAMA/NaI and DAMA/LIBRA experiments report a
modulation of single-hit events in the low-energy region
of NaI(Tl) detectors satisfying this signature, most re-
cently at 15.3σ over a cumulative exposure of 3.40 t yr
[3, 5], while the same-target experiments ANAIS-112 and
COSINE-100 exclude a modulation of the DAMA ampli-
tude at 4.7σ (1–6 keV) [6]; for a review of the direct-
detection landscape see Ref. [7].
The June phase has long served as the principal ar-
gument for a galactic origin of the effect: it matches no
natural timescale of the Solar System—perihelion falls
on January 3, aphelion on July 4, and seasonal back-
grounds peak in local summer—so a signature peaking
in early June appeared explicable only by the Earth’s
velocity relative to an external, galactic frame [3]. This
argument by elimination, however, overlooks exactly one
Solar-System structure: the solar equatorial plane. New-
tonian gravity is blind to it—the monopole field of a
sphere retains no memory of the rotation axis—and it be-
comes locally observable only in the parity-odd sector of
the Sun’s Kerr geometry, through the Chern–Pontryagin
density ∗R R ∝cos θ. The Earth crosses this plane on
June 6–7 (ascending) and December 7 (descending); we
show below that the fitted phase of the resulting annual-
modulation template lies, for one sign of the coupling,
six days from the dark-matter expectation.
The two hypotheses differ in the rigidity of their phase.
The halo value t0 = 152.5 d presupposes a fully virialized,
nonrotating local halo; admitting bulk rotation, streams
such as S1 [10], debris flows, or gravitational focusing by
the Sun, the predicted phase ranges over the entire year
and acquires an energy dependence [8, 9]. The geometric
phase derived in this Letter is, by contrast, a two-element
set fixed by the anomaly equation and the ephemerides,
t∗∈{158.7 d, 341.4 d}; the only freedom is the sign of
the coupling. The construction uses nothing beyond the
rotation of the Sun and the geometry of the Earth’s orbit,
and it predicts, in addition to the phase, its secular drift,
its harmonic content, and a set of null tests. Confronted
with the DAMA data the template is statistically indis-
tinguishable from the SHM cosine, while being falsifiable
at the level of ephemeris precision.

On the elliptic orbit the exact waveform is not a pure
cosine and its maximum falls on day 156.6; a cosine fit,
however—the operation performed by every modulation
analysis—measures the phase of the annual Fourier com-
ponent, and the eccentricity distortions are semiannual,
hence orthogonal to it. The fitted phase is therefore the
date at which the Earth’s mean longitude crosses the
node of the solar equator: day 158.7 (June 7–8) for a
coupling that suppresses the observed rate, or day 341.4
(December 7–8) for one that enhances it—the difference
between the Carrington node longitude and the longi-
tude of perihelion. Numerically, a free-phase cosine fit to
the exact Kepler waveform returns 158.66 d, and 158.6 d
when the waveform is evaluated on the actual DAMA
live-time grid (52 bins with calibration gaps, published
uncertainties as weights). No velocity distribution, halo
model, or astrophysical assumption enters this date.
The six-day separation from the dark-matter expecta-
tion has a simple origin: it is the angle between two un-
related directions on the sky. The halo phase (June 2) is
set by the direction of the solar motion toward Cygnus—
galactic kinematics; the node date (June 7–8) by the ori-
entation of the solar rotation axis—a relic of the proto-
solar angular momentum. The two dates lie 6.2 days—
6.1◦of mean longitude—apart. This coincidence is why
the node date remained hidden inside the “June window”
for three decades: the standard argument by elimina-
tion checked perihelion, aphelion, and the seasons, and
an early-June phase appeared uniquely galactic. Had the
solar axis pointed elsewhere, the two hypotheses would
have been separable from the first annual cycle.

Two consistency conditions constrain the interpreta-
tion exactly as they constrain dark matter. First, DAMA
observes no modulation of the bulk rate in the phase2-
empowered data (R90 amplitude −0.011 ± 0.073 cpd/kg;
6–10 keV amplitude 0.0011±0.0011 cpd/kg/keV [5]), and
decay-constant metrology bounds generic annual varia-
tions of radioactivity at the 10−4–10−5 level [23]; the
coupling must therefore be selective to the process popu-
lating the 0.5–6 keV single-hit window. Second, the solar
Kerr field is identical at Canfranc, Yangyang, and Gran
Sasso, so the null results of ANAIS-112 and COSINE-100
[6] bear on this channel exactly as they bear on the SHM
interpretation.
The free-period fit of the full exposure, T = 0.99748 ±
0.00053 yr [5], nominally 4.8σ below one year, corre-
sponds to a phase drifting earlier by 0.92 d yr−1. The
node-locked drift is +0.0142 d yr−1: opposite in sign and
a factor of 65 smaller. A per-era phase analysis can deter-
mine whether the period preference reflects instrumental
inhomogeneity between detector generations; if the drift
is physical, it falsifies the node-locked template and the
SHM alike.
Discussion.—The SHM also predicts a date, but that
date is inherited from an assumed velocity distribution
and is known to shift with halo substructure and with
gravitational focusing, acquiring in general an energy de-
pendence [8, 9]. The node-locked phase admits no such
adjustment: it is computable to 0.1 d over the next cen-

locked June 7–8 clock, while testing the predicted energy
independence, common phase, and fixed semiannual har-
