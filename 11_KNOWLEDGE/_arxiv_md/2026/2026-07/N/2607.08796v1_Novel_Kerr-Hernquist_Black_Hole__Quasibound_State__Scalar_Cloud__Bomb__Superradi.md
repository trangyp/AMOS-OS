We present a novel rotating black hole solution surrounded by a Hernquist dark
matter halo, obtained by applying the Newman–Janis algorithm to the exact
Schwarzschild–Hernquist spacetime. The resulting Kerr–Hernquist geometry provides
an axisymmetric background for investigating scalar-field dynamics in realistic dark
matter environments. Using the analytical asymptotic matching method, we derive
the quasibound-state spectrum, identify the conditions for scalar cloud formation and
the black hole bomb instability, and obtain an analytic expression for the superradi-
ant scattering amplification factor. We show that the halo preserves the hydrogen-like
structure of the quasibound-state spectrum while introducing corrections governed
by the combination ρ0r3
0. Increasing the halo density and scale radius enhances
the scalar-field binding energy, lowers the critical field mass for scalar cloud forma-
tion, suppresses the growth rate of the superradiant instability for co-rotating modes
(mℓ> 0), and accelerates the decay of counter-rotating modes (mℓ< 0). Furthermore,
the dark matter halo reduces both the magnitude and frequency range of superradiant
amplification, thereby weakening energy extraction from the black hole. These results
demonstrate that the Kerr–Hernquist geometry provides a unified framework for
studying quasibound states, scalar clouds, black hole bombs, and superradiant scat-
tering, while revealing how a Hernquist dark matter halo leaves observable imprints
on the spectrum and stability of rotating black holes.

1 Introduction
Black holes in realistic astrophysical environments are not isolated objects, but are
instead surrounded by baryonic matter, plasma, and dark matter. Observational evi-
dence strongly supports the existence of supermassive black holes at galactic centers
[1, 2], while galaxies themselves are embedded within extended dark matter halos [3].
Consequently, the idealized description of black holes as vacuum spacetimes should
be refined by incorporating the gravitational effects of the surrounding matter distri-
bution, particularly in studies of dynamical processes sensitive to the global structure
of spacetime.
Among the most important phenomena associated with rotating black holes are
quasibound states (QBSs) and superradiant scattering, both of which arise from the
interaction between bosonic fields and curved spacetime. Quasibound states corre-
spond to long-lived oscillatory modes localized outside the event horizon due to the
presence of an effective potential well capable of trapping perturbations [4–7]. Their
complex frequencies encode both oscillation and decay rates and therefore provide
sensitive probes of the underlying geometry. In rotating spacetimes, these modes
are closely related to superradiant energy extraction and may signal the onset of
instabilities.
Superradiance is a wave amplification process in which an incident bosonic field
scatters off a rotating black hole and emerges with enhanced amplitude by extract-
ing rotational energy from the horizon when the superradiant condition is satisfied
[8]. This mechanism plays an important role in black hole bomb scenarios, ultralight
boson phenomenology, and stability analyses of rotating compact objects. The cor-
responding amplification factor provides a quantitative measure of the efficiency of
energy extraction and constitutes a key observable in black hole scattering theory.
Since both quasibound spectra and superradiant amplification are highly sensi-
tive to the structure of the effective potential, they provide a natural framework for
probing environmental effects around black holes. In particular, the presence of a sur-
rounding dark matter halo can deform the gravitational potential, modify the trapping
structure of scalar perturbations, shift resonant frequencies, and alter the efficiency of
superradiant scattering. Understanding these effects is therefore important for deter-
mining how realistic astrophysical environments influence the dynamical response of
rotating black holes.
Motivated by these considerations, considerable effort has been devoted to con-
structing black hole solutions embedded in dark matter halos. Different halo profiles
produce distinct modifications of the spacetime geometry and its associated observ-
ables. Black hole shadows, geodesic motion, and wave propagation have been studied
in Hernquist-type geometries [9, 10], while alternative models include universal rota-
tion curve spacetimes, piecewise halo metrics, and rotating black holes immersed
in cold or scalar-field dark matter environments [11–13]. Additional investigations
have explored gravitational lensing, thermodynamics, optical properties, and photon
dynamics in these systems [14–21]. These studies indicate that environmental effects
may leave observable imprints across a wide range of black hole phenomena.

the spacetime geometry and the underlying matter distribution.

perturbations in the resulting spacetime. Our main objective is to determine how the
presence of the Hernquist dark matter halo affects the quasibound-state spectrum and
the superradiant scattering properties of the rotating black hole.

method [27–30]. In the near-horizon region, the radial equation reduces to a hyper-
geometric differential equation with solutions expressed in terms of 2F1 functions
satisfying purely ingoing boundary conditions at the horizon. In the far-field region,
the equation takes the form of a confluent hypergeometric equation whose solutions
are described by 1F1 functions. Matching the two asymptotic solutions in the interme-
diate region yields analytic expressions for both the quasibound-state spectrum and
the superradiant amplification factor.

4.1 Quasibound States
We now investigate the quasibound states of a massive scalar field in the rotating
Schwarzschild–Hernquist background. These states correspond to long-lived resonant
modes localized outside the event horizon by an effective potential well. Because the
black hole horizon acts as an absorptive boundary, the associated frequencies are
generally complex,
ω = ωR + iωI,
(47)
where the real part ωR determines the oscillation frequency, while the imaginary
part ωI characterizes the decay or growth rate of the mode. In particular, ωI <
0 corresponds to damped modes, whereas ωI > 0 signals an instability driven by
superradiant amplification.
The quasibound-state solutions are defined by the following boundary conditions:
• purely ingoing behavior at the event horizon, ensuring that no radiation emerges
from the black hole interior,
• exponentially decaying behavior at spatial infinity, guaranteeing that the scalar
configuration remains localized around the black hole.

5 Summary
We have explored the dynamics of scalar fields in a rotating black hole spacetime
immersed in a Dehnen dark matter halo, focusing on the interplay between quasibound
states and superradiant scattering as complementary manifestations of the same
spectral structure. Starting from a rotating geometry generated through the Newman–
Janis algorithm, we formulated the covariant Klein–Gordon equation and analyzed
the scalar dynamics using the analytical asymptotic matching (AAM) method. This
framework allows one to consistently connect the near-horizon and asymptotic regions,
providing a unified analytical description of both resonant bound states and scattering
phenomena.
In the quasibound-state sector, we derived the complex frequency spectrum and
identified the distinct physical mechanisms governing its real and imaginary com-
ponents. The real part of the spectrum is determined primarily by the asymptotic
structure of the spacetime and exhibits a hydrogenic form, modified by the presence of
