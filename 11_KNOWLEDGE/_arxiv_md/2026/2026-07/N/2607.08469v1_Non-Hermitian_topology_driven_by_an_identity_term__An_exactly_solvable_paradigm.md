Non-Hermitian topology driven by an identity term: An exactly solvable paradigm

An identity term in the Hamiltonian is conventionally regarded as spectrally inert—it shifts energies but
does not alter eigenstate topology.
We show that under non-Hermitian skin pumping, this paradigm fails:
a momentum-dependent identity term actively deforms the generalized Brillouin zone, thereby challenging
established topological criteria that rely on ﬁxed complex contours. Here, by introducing spin-orbit coupling
into a Hatano-Nelson chain, we present an exact analytical solution for the entire non-Hermitian eigensystem
under open boundary conditions. Our solution reveals how inter-cell spin-orbit coupling, synergizing with
this non-trivial identity term, induces topological edge states and robust zero modes in the complete absence
of chiral symmetry. This work establishes an exactly solvable paradigm for non-Hermitian topology beyond
symmetry protection, and provides a rigorous benchmark for testing topological invariants in systems with
momentum-dependent identity terms.
Non-Hermitian physics [1–8] has unveiled a host of intrigu-
ing phenomena beyond the Hermitian framework, with several
cornerstone discoveries such as the non-Hermitian skin ef-
fect (NHSE) [9–28], non-Bloch topological phases [29–38],
and exceptional point physics [39–51]. The NHSE, where an
extensive number of bulk eigenstates accumulate at bound-
aries, deﬁes the conventional bulk-boundary correspondence
[52–59] and necessitates the use of the generalized Brillouin
zone (GBZ) for a correct description of topology in one-
dimensional (1D) non-Hermitian systems [32, 55, 60]. This
non-Bloch band theory has successfully classiﬁed topologi-
cal phases and predicted novel boundary phenomena in di-
verse non-Hermitian platforms such as cold atoms [61–64],
electric circuits [65–70], mechanical metamaterials [71–74],
photonic lattices or arrays [75–80], and quantum systems [81–
85]. However, a signiﬁcant portion of this progress has been
anchored in models possessing chiral or particle-hole symme-
try [2, 29], such as the celebrated Su-Schrieﬀer-Heeger (SSH)
model [56, 78, 86], where the energy spectra are symmetric
about zero, hence greatly simplifying the analysis of topolog-
ical edge modes [2, 32, 33].
Yet most realistic systems lack such protecting symmetries,
falling into the broad class of non-chiral models [2, 87–89].
Existing analytical progress in this direction has primarily ad-
dressed systems where the Hamiltonian can be expressed in
or built upon the standard two-band form H (훽) = d(훽) · σ
[90–94]. In such cases, the non-chirality arises solely from the
non-planarity of d(훽), but non-Bloch topological invariants
have still been established [91, 94].
A more formidable challenge arises, however, when the
Hamiltonian contains a non-vanishing, momentum-dependent
identity term, i.e., H (훽) = 푑0(훽)1 + d(훽) · σ with 푑0(훽) ≠0
[95–97]. This identity term actively deforms the GBZ, render-
ing inapplicable topological criteria that rely on a ﬁxed GBZ
contour, including those based on winding-number diﬀerences
[32, 98] or geometric constructions [10, 99, 100], even for non-
chiral systems [91, 94]. While a distinct topological invariant
deﬁned on the Riemann surface for such generic models has re-
cently been proposed [97], an exact analytical solution yielding
explicit edge-state energies and phase boundaries for any spe-
ciﬁc model has remained elusive. Consequently, probing the
topological physics in this regime still largely relies on case-
by-case numerical GBZ computations [96], an approach that
is demanding and can become intractable for complex GBZ
morphologies [101–105], leaving a gap between the topologi-
cal classiﬁcation and quantitative prediction.
In this Letter, we resolve this challenge by presenting an
exactly solvable paradigm for non-Hermitian topology driven
by a non-trivial identity term. Concretely, we introduce spin-
orbit (SO) coupling [106–109] into the non-Hermitian Hatano-
Nelson (HN) chain—a prototypical non-chiral system that ex-
hibits the NHSE [110–116] and captures the essential topo-
logical physics of the most general symmetry-free two-band
model solved in Supplemental Material [117]. We demon-
strate that the inter-cell SO coupling 훿2, in synergy with this
identity term, gives rise to a rich landscape of topological phe-
nomena, including topological islands carved out of a broad
trivial phase, robust zero modes that require neither chiral sym-
metry nor (퐸, −퐸) spectral pairing, as well as a topological
phase transition driven purely by the GBZ deformation (see
End Matter), in an otherwise topologically trivial HN chain.
Crucially, we achieve a complete analytical solution for the
entire eigensystem under open boundary conditions (OBC),
culminating in closed-form expressions for the edge-state en-
ergy and its existence condition. Our paradigmatic solution
thus transcends symmetry-based classiﬁcations [2, 29, 118],
establishing inter-cell SO coupling as a generic mechanism for
non-chiral topology and providing an analytical benchmark
for testing topological invariants [97] in identity-term-driven
systems with complex GBZ morphologies.

In conclusion, we have established an exactly solvable
paradigm for non-Hermitian topology driven by a momentum-
dependent identity term, realized via an SO-coupled non-
Hermitian HN chain. Our solution reveals that this identity
term, synergizing with inter-cell SO coupling, actively deforms
the GBZ and thereby can induce topological edge states, in-
cluding robust zero modes that require neither chiral symmetry
nor the (퐸, −퐸) spectral pairing. This paradigmatic solution
transcends standard symmetry-classiﬁed topology and oﬀers a
pathway to topological protection under relaxed symmetries.
Moreover, our analytical approach, which directly addresses
the challenge posed by a GBZ-modifying identity term, read-
ily extends to the most general two-band models without any
protecting symmetry (see Sec. VIII in Ref. [117]), enabling
exact studies in broader settings [93–97]. These ﬁndings open
new avenues for designing robust non-Hermitian topologi-
cal states [6–8], with implications for topological frequency
combs [122, 125, 126], protected entanglement sources [127],
and quantum simulation circuits [68, 128, 129].

ROBUSTNESS OF TOPOLOGICAL EDGE STATES AGAINST GENERIC DISORDERS
In the main text (Fig. 4), we have demonstrated that the topological edge states are robust against a local perturbation that
modiﬁes the hopping phase at a single unit cell. To further assess their stability under more generic and spatially uncorrelated
perturbations, we now test the eﬀect of random on-site potentials and random hopping amplitudes.
The random on-site potential is implemented by adding a diagonal matrix 푉diag to the real-space Hamiltonian (S18), whose
entries are independent random variables uniformly distributed in [−휖푟, 휖푟] with zero mean, where 휖푟measures the disorder
strength relative to the bulk gap. We begin with the ﬁnite-energy topological edge states from Fig. 2 (point III) of the main text.
Under weak on-site disorder (휖푟= 0.02), the edge state remains clearly isolated in the OBC spectrum and its wavefunction stays
sharply localized at the boundary [Supplemental Figs. 8(a) and 8(b)]. When the disorder strength is increased to 휖푟= 0.3, the
bulk states shift signiﬁcantly, yet the edge state persists at essentially the same energy and retains its boundary localization; only

and 8(d)]. We next turn to the topological zero modes from Fig. 3(e) (the 훼푅= 훼퐿case with strictly zero energy). Under the
same random on-site disorder perturbations, the zero mode remains exactly pinned at 퐸= 0 and its wavefunction stays localized
at the right boundary [Supplemental Figs. 8(e)–8(h)]. In neither case do the ﬁnite-energy edge states or the zero modes merge
into the bulk continuum, conﬁrming their exceptional robustness against on-site disorders.
