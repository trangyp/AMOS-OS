Non-Local Magic from the Entanglement Spectrum
Gianpaolo Torre,1 Fabio Franchini,2 and Salvatore Marco Giampaolo2
1Jozef Stefan Institute, Jamova cesta 39, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia
2Institut Ruder Boˇskovi´c, Bijeniˇcka cesta 54, Zagreb 10000, Croatia
Non-local magic has recently emerged as a fundamental resource for characterizing genuinely non-local non-
stabilizer correlations. However, its direct calculation is an intractable numerical problem, except for small
systems, and its understanding remains limited. We derive a representation of non-local magic in terms of
the Walsh–Hadamard autocorrelations of the entanglement spectrum. Our representation makes the underlying
harmonic structure explicit and enables a systematic analysis of its behaviors for various scenarios. We prove
that non-local magic can be upper-bounded by an entanglement entropy and we derive exact analytical results
for broad classes of quantum states, characterizing the scaling of non-local magic for volume-law states, as well
as ground states of one-dimensional gapped and critical systems. Our results identify the spectral organization
of the entanglement spectrum as the key ingredient governing non-local magic and provide a framework for
further systematic analytical investigation.
Understanding the complexity of quantum many-body
states is one of the central challenges of modern quantum
physics. Over the last two decades, quantum entanglement
has profoundly reshaped our understanding of this prob-
lem, providing a unified language for phenomena as diverse
as quantum criticality, topological phases of matter, tensor-
network methods, and quantum dynamics [1–6]. The entan-
glement entropy and, more generally, the entanglement spec-
trum have revealed universal structures that are largely in-
dependent of microscopic details, establishing entanglement
as one of the fundamental organizing principles of modern
many-body physics [7–10].
Nevertheless, entanglement captures only one aspect of
quantum complexity. While it quantifies the amount of quan-
tum correlations shared between two subsystems, it does not
distinguish between correlations that admit an efficient stabi-
lizer description and those requiring genuinely non-stabilizer
(also known as ’magic’) quantum resources [11]. This dis-
tinction has become increasingly important with the develop-
ment of quantum information theory, where quantum magic
has emerged as the fundamental resource underlying univer-
sal fault-tolerant quantum computation, quantum advantage,
and the classical hardness of quantum simulation [12–15].
More recently, magic has also attracted considerable atten-
tion in quantum many-body physics, where it has been shown
to reveal physical properties that remain invisible to conven-
tional entanglement measures, establishing new connections
between quantum information, condensed matter physics, and
quantum complexity [16–19].
Among the different notions of magic, the non-local magic
introduced in [20] occupies a particularly important position.
By minimizing the stabilizer R´enyi entropy over all local uni-
tary transformations, it isolates the genuinely non-local com-
ponent of magic, providing a basis-independent characteriza-
tion of the non-stabilizer resources shared between two sub-
systems. In close analogy with the relation between coherence
and entanglement, the non-local magic removes all contribu-
tions that can be generated locally and identifies only the irre-
ducible many-body component of the resource [24–26]. This
makes it a natural candidate for characterizing the intrinsic
complexity of bipartite quantum states.
Despite its conceptual importance, our analytical under-
standing of non-local magic remains remarkably limited. Its
original definition involves a highly non-trivial (and gener-
ally intractable) optimization over local unitary transforma-
tions and, although an exact Schmidt-gauge formulation has
recently been established [20], relatively little is known about
the analytical properties of the resulting spectral representa-
tion. Consequently, some of the most fundamental questions
remain essentially unanswered. What determines the scaling
of the non-local magic? What is its precise relation to en-
tanglement? Which properties of the Schmidt spectrum are
responsible for large non-local magic? Does the non-local
magic exhibit universal behavior in quantum critical systems?
In this work we answer these questions by deriving an
equivalent representation of the Schmidt-gauge non-local
magic in terms of the Walsh–Hadamard autocorrelations [21,
22] of the entanglement spectrum. This formulation makes
the underlying harmonic structure explicit and provides a rep-
resentation in terms of the Schmidt coefficients [23] that nat-
urally lends itself to analytical investigations. Building on
this representation, we derive exact analytical results for sev-
eral physically relevant classes of quantum states, establish
the universal upper-bound for the non-local magic in terms of
an entanglement entropy, and uncover a unified physical pic-
ture describing the behavior of the non-local magic in maxi-
mally entangled states, Haar-random states, one-dimensional
gapped phases, and quantum critical systems.
More generally, our results establish a direct connection
between non-local magic and the spectral properties of the
entanglement spectrum.
This connection places the study
of non-local magic within the broader framework of entan-
glement spectroscopy [8–10], allowing analytical techniques
originally developed for entanglement spectra to be directly
applied to the characterization of non-local magic. We expect
this perspective to provide a useful framework for investigat-
ing non-local magic in interacting many-body systems, topo-
logical phases, quantum dynamics, and other strongly corre-
lated quantum systems.

A nearly flat entanglement spectrum, as realized by
Haar-random states, produces only finite non-local magic de-
spite exhibiting volume-law entanglement. States with inde-
pendent entanglement modes instead generate additive contri-
butions, leading to extensive non-local magic. Finally, critical
systems interpolate between these two limits: the number of
relevant entanglement modes grows only logarithmically with
the subsystem size, resulting in a universal logarithmic scaling
of the Schmidt-gauged non-local magic.
Conclusions.— Building on the Schmidt-gauged formula-
tion introduced in Ref. [20], we have shown that the corre-
sponding non-local magic admits an equivalent representation
as the fourth Walsh moment of the autocorrelations of the en-
tanglement spectrum. This representation makes the under-
lying harmonic structure explicit and provides a natural ana-
lytical framework for the study of non-local magic. Exploit-
ing this formulation, we established three main results. First,
we derived exact analytical expressions for broad families of
quantum states, revealing how the spectral organization of the
Schmidt eigenvalues determines qualitatively different behav-
iors of the non-local magic. Second, we proved the universal
bound M Sch
2
≤2S2, identifying extensive entanglement as
a necessary, but not sufficient, condition for extensive non-
local magic and immediately obtaining an area law for one-
dimensional gapped systems.
Finally, we showed that the
Schmidt-gauged formulation naturally explains the behavior
of critical systems in terms of the proliferation of low-energy
entanglement modes, predicting a universal logarithmic scal-
ing for one-dimensional quantum criticality.
Beyond these specific results, our work suggests a broader
perspective on the study of non-local magic. The Schmidt-
gauged formulation transforms a quantity originally defined
through a highly non-trivial optimization into an object that
can be investigated using the standard tools of entanglement
spectroscopy.
This opens the possibility of studying non-
local magic through the entanglement Hamiltonian, the struc-
ture of the entanglement spectrum, and their universal prop-
erties, placing the resource theory of magic in direct contact
with quantum many-body physics. We therefore expect this
framework to provide new analytical tools for investigating
non-local magic in interacting systems, higher-dimensional
phases, topological matter, nonequilibrium dynamics, and
mixed states. More generally, we expect the Schmidt-gauged
formulation to establish entanglement spectroscopy as a nat-
ural analytical language for the study of non-local magic in
quantum many-body systems.
