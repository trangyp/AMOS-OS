1.
Introduction
One topic of interest in quantum field theory over many years has been to understand the
structure of perturbative renormalization group functions at very high loop order. In particular
cases it has been shown that multiple zeta values will appear in scalar field theories, [1]. More
recently Schnetz has demonstrated that new periods will occur at seven loops in scalar 𝜙4 theory
[2]. Over the last four decades the general overview is that we now understand the connection
of multiple zeta values with loop order in a variety of core theories with different symmetries.
Aside from flavour and colour group properties, characterized by the generic parameter 𝑁, the main
symmetries are supersymmetry, gauge and chiral symmetry. The latter three are strictly only present
in integer spacetime dimensions. When perturbative renormalization is carried out in dimensionally
regularized extensions one has to address potential inconsistencies that can arise in the regularized
version before the limit to the critical dimension can be taken. To tackle such high perturbative
orders requires a significant amount of resources. However a particular technique that can probe
very high order perturbation theory is the 1/𝑁expansion when 𝑁is large and it effectively equates
to carrying out perturbation theory in a related but different ordering of the underlying Feynman
graphs. This method was used to study multiple zetas at high order in powers of 1/𝑁but all orders
in the coupling constant for scalar field theories in [3] based on the results of [4–6].
In one application of the large 𝑁method the 𝑂(𝑁) Wess-Zumino model, [7], was examined
in [8] to third order. An interesting feature emerged which was that the multiple zetas that arose
in the 𝑂(𝑁) scalar theories of [3] were absent to the same order in 1/𝑁in the Wess-Zumino
model. That model is a supersymmetric theory of scalar chiral fields which is renormalizable in
four dimensions. As it possesses two of the main continuous symmetries of that dimension it was
not immediately clear which of the two symmetries was driving the absence of multiple zetas to
that order. Indeed it is already well-established that supersymmetry is related to the principle of
maximal transcendentality, [9]. In order to test which of the two symmetries is at the heart of the
multiple zeta absence one would need to study the theories that relate to the separate boson and
fermion sectors of the Wess-Zumino model. While the fermionic sector was examined at 𝑂(1/𝑁3)
in [10] the bosonic sector has yet to be accessed to that order in 1/𝑁. We therefore report on
progress with regard to this using the 1/𝑁expansion. Here chirality means vertices are composed
of directed edges with the flow either completely towards or away from the interaction point.

To complete the investigation into whether multiple zetas are absent in 𝜂3 in the Wess-Zumino
model as a result of supersymmetry or due to chirality we recall the situation with the fermion sector.
This was examined in [10] where the underlying universal large 𝑁Lagrangian was used but based
on the chiral Gross-Neveu model of [20]. That Lagrangian is defined in terms of Majorana fermions
meaning 𝛾5 was explicitly present in the interaction. The application of the large 𝑁conformal
bootstrap computation then followed the parallel path as that of [6]. However since the leading
large 𝑁term of 𝜒vanished, in order to avoid potential singularities as well as an intermediate 0/0
issue, a non-abelian generalization of the universal Lagrangian was introduced, [10]. This meant 𝜂3
was computed as a function of Lie group Casimirs. It contained 𝐼(𝜇) but not ˆΩ(𝜇). Moreover the
0/0 issue could be traced to terms involving the ratio of purely non-abelian Casimirs which were
non-problematic due to cancellations prior to taking the abelian limit. Thus in the final expression
for 𝜂3 in the chiral Gross-Neveu universality class the coefficient of 𝐼(𝜇) was zero. The non-abelian
generalization could not be readily applied for (13) since the consistency equation would have
involved all the topologies of equation (10) of [6].

Discussion
To summarize we have discussed large 𝑁structural properties in different theories in respect
of 𝜂3. In particular the large 𝑁analysis of the model corresponding to the bosonic sector of the

Wess-Zumino model has been completed at 𝑂(1/𝑁3). This demonstrated that neither the scalar or
fermion sector contain multiple zeta values at this order in the respective matter field wave function
renormalization. To answer an earlier question the structural reason for this is the so-called chirality
property which excludes graphs which have odd-edge subgraphs rather than supersymmetry. In
related fermionic theories, such as the chiral Gross-Neveu model, this translates into the choice of
Weyl fermions whence their algebraic properties circumvent the treatment of 𝛾5 in 𝑑-dimensions
up to the loop and large 𝑁orders studied here. This qualification follows in light of recent four
loop computations in [22] which suggest that care has to be taken when treating 𝛾5 at six loops. If
so the large 𝑁critical point approach would not be exempt from having to find a parallel resolution
which would appear first at 𝑂(1/𝑁4). Another future direction to pursue is to see whether using the
universal theory at the Wilson-Fisher fixed point with Weyl fermions for perturbative computations
could provide an alternative or easier route to treating 𝛾5 or supersymmetry within dimensional
regularization.
