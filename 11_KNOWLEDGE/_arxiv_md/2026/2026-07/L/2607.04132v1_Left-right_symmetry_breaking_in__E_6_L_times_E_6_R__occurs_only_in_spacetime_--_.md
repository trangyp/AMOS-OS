Abstract
The octonionic EL
6 ×ER
6 program aims to unify the Standard Model with gravitation by
trinifying each exceptional factor, E6 →SU(3) × SU(3) × SU(3). The two-sided structure
then carries two colour groups: the visible SU(3)c from the left factor, and a second SU(3)c′
from the right. Gauged like the first, this second colour would give the charged lepton a
colour index, and so must be suppressed by hand — for instance as a global symmetry,
broken at the electroweak scale. We argue that no such device is needed: SU(3)c′ is not a
second colour at all, but the same group as the visible SU(3)c.
The reason is that the left–right symmetry relating the two factors lives in spacetime, not
in the internal sector. In the gravi-weak reading the right-sector SU(2)R is the gravitational
frame group, not a second weak force, so the left–right exchange acts on the Dirac field
as ordinary spacetime parity. A spacetime operation cannot reach into the internal sector
and double the colour that lives there; it merely relates the one internal colour as seen in
the two octonionic frames. Colour is therefore single and vector-like, SU(3)c′ = SU(3)c,
and the charged lepton is a colour singlet — never a coloured triplet, so there is nothing
to suppress.
What the right sector does contribute is a single colour-blind datum: the
square-root of mass √m, whose values are an exceptional-Jordan input (the colour-counting
operator NR fixes only the colour representation, never the value of √m). The two-step right-
sector breaking SU(3)R →SU(2)R × U(1)Ydem →U(1)dem, the exact mirror of SU(3)L →
SU(2)L × U(1)Y →U(1)em, does leave an unbroken dark-electromagnetic gauge factor;
but an anomaly no-go proven here (Appendix A) shows that on the visible fermions every
anomaly-free abelian charge lies in the span of electric charge and B −L, which the √m
pattern does not — no sign convention, chirality assignment, or redefinition rescues it. The
gauged U(1)dem therefore carries the parity-mirror of electric charge, its charged matter is
the dark sector, and the visible fermions are dem-neutral, coupling at most through kinetic
mixing; no visible fifth force is predicted.
The √m values enter visible physics only as
the spectral labels of the exceptional-Jordan mass operator — the Dynkin-swap image of
the dark charge lattice — which settles, in the mirror-canonical direction, the question of
which pattern the gauged charge carries. The same recognition also fixes the Standard-
Model hypercharge as Y = Q −T 3
L, with electric charge Q = N/3 from the Cl(6) number
operator and no right-sector generator — a consistency relation recovered in a sector where
the answer is independently known.

Introduction
The strong-CP problem — why the QCD vacuum angle ¯θ = θQCD+arg det M, rendered physical
by the chiral anomaly and instantons [2], is experimentally consistent with zero, ¯θ ≲10−10 (from
the neutron electric-dipole-moment bound [3] via the QCD estimate of dn(¯θ) [4, 5]), despite there
being no Standard-Model reason for it to be small — has three broad classes of resolution.
A Peccei–Quinn symmetry [6] promotes ¯θ to a dynamical field, at the cost of an axion [7, 8].
Spontaneous CP violation of Nelson–Barr type [9, 10] makes CP a symmetry of the Lagrangian,
broken so as to leave arg det M zero at tree level. Spontaneous parity breaking, finally, uses
an exact parity to forbid the θ-term while a Hermitian mass matrix keeps arg det M = 0 [11].
The parity route needs no new light particle, but it requires two structural ingredients to be
supplied: an exact parity in the unbroken theory, and a mechanism rendering the fermion mass
matrix Hermitian.
This paper concerns a setting in which both ingredients are not imposed but inherited. In the
octonionic unification program built on the complexified exceptional Jordan algebra J3(OC), one
fermion generation is realized as a Clifford module over the complex octonions, electric charge
is the central U(1) of that module, and fermion masses arise as the Jordan eigenvalues of an
octonionic-Hermitian operator [12, 13]. The construction places the visible and mass sectors
in two factors of an EL
6 × ER
6 product, related by a left–right symmetry. We will see that the
parity needed for strong CP is that left–right symmetry, correctly interpreted, and that the
reality needed for arg det M = 0 — real, positive quark masses together with flavour rotors of
real determinant — is supplied by the same octonionic and flavour structure already in place
for the masses and their mixings.
That interpretation is the subject of the paper, and it resolves two structural tensions in the
two-sided construction. The right-sector trinification produces a factor SU(3)c′ — a nominal
“second colour” — which, taken as an ordinary gauged interaction, would give the charged
lepton a colour index unless suppressed by hand: a group defined by what it must not do.
Separately, a Standard-Model hypercharge built from right-sector Cartan generators requires
an extra interface assumption. We argue that both tensions trace to a single over-extension —
treating the left–right structure as acting, and breaking, in the internal sector — and that one
recognition removes both.
The recognition is this: left–right is a spacetime symmetry. It is realized on the Dirac field
as parity, so its breaking is confined to the spacetime (gravi-weak) frame; on the internal sector
it acts trivially — identifying the two would-be colour groups as one — and so is never broken
there.
The right-sector SU(2)R is the gravitational frame group, not a second electroweak
interaction. Left–right is thus broken in the spacetime sector and preserved in the internal sector,
and from this single asymmetry several consequences follow. Hypercharge is built without any
right-sector generator, Y = Q −T 3
L, and reproduces the Standard-Model assignments exactly
(Sec. 3). The “second colour” dissolves: its non-abelian part is identified with the one physical
colour, while √m survives as a distinct, colour-blind grading — not the colour-counting NR,
and, by an anomaly no-go proven in Appendix A, not a gauged charge on the visible fermions
either: the gauged U(1)dem of the right electroweak chain carries the mirror of electric charge,
on the dark sector, and the visible √m is the spectral label of the Jordan mass operator (Sec. 5,
Appendix A); the electron is then a colour singlet bearing that grade, never a coloured triplet
(Sec. 6). The flavour-sector Dynkin swap that relates the down and lepton mass ladders is
a distinct operation and is untouched, so the mass-ratio program is unaffected (Sec. 7). And
from a single vector-like colour together with the spontaneous parity follows the conservation
of strong CP (Sec. 8). We present the hypercharge case first, as a check in a sector where the
answer is independently known, and then the colour case, where the same move yields strong
CP as its payoff. Figure 1 gathers this anatomy in a single picture — the split-bioctonionic
dimensions dividing into a spacetime block (which breaks, yielding the two Higgs, the (3, 3)

Loop regeneration of ¯θ
Sections 4 and 8 establish ¯θ = 0 at tree level. The physical strong-CP parameter is, however,
radiatively sensitive: after the spontaneous parity breaking σ ̸= 0, loops can in principle re-
generate a nonzero ¯θ, and a solution is only as good as its stability against this. This section
is where the “tree level” qualifier carried through Sec. 8 is discharged — and we discharge it
honestly, because the model-specific computation cannot yet be done. We separate three things:
the known two-loop obstruction that defeats minimal parity solutions, the structural reason the
present framework may escape it, and the residual model-specific calculation, which is open.
The two-loop obstruction in minimal parity solutions.
It would be wrong to quote the
favourable one-loop heuristic as a generic expectation. In the minimal Babu–Mohapatra parity
model [11] — the closest standard analogue — ¯θ vanishes only up to one-loop order; the leading
two-loop contribution was computed by de Vries, Draper and Patel [1], who found, contrary to
earlier estimates, that it is not suppressed by the weak scale and is of order 10−3–10−2 times
unknown mixing angles and phases.
Such a model therefore does not generically solve the
strong-CP problem: it satisfies ¯θ ≲10−10 only in restricted corners of parameter space, and
even there ¯θ is expected to sit just below current bounds, sourcing observable hadronic electric
dipole moments. Any parity-based proposal must now confront this result; we do so directly,
rather than lean on the weak-scale-suppressed estimates that Ref. [1] overturned.
Why the present framework is not in that class — a candidate escape.
The de
Vries–Draper–Patel contribution is generated by diagrams running the gauged right-handed
WR of a genuine SU(2)R gauge theory. In the present framework SU(2)R is the gravitational
frame group (Sec. 2.2); there is no gauged WR coupling to fermions (a point already noted in
the scope statement of Sec. 8), so precisely the diagrams driving the 10−3–10−2 estimate are
absent. To this extent the framework is not the minimal gauged-SU(2)R parity model and is not
directly bound by that result. Below we sharpen this from assertion to derivation: the absence
of a right-handed mixing matrix VR follows from AR acting on the spin index rather than on
isospin, so the residual is not the frame sector but the scalar (bridge) sector, as we discuss.

Epilogue: what kind of structure is this?
This closing section is interpretive. It introduces no results, carries no epistemic tags, and can
be skipped by the reader interested only in the physics; but the construction assembled above
invites a question that deserves to be posed in print, and answered with the same candour the
ledger enforces elsewhere.
If the structure is right, E8 × ωE8 buys gravity and the Standard Model and nothing
else, with the exceptional Jordan algebra left to fix the coupling constants. What
kind of strange structure is this?
A rigidity program.
Taxonomically, it is a rigidity program: the wager that the actual
world is the unique model of a terminal mathematical object. What makes the wager struc-
turally unusual is that the terminality is triple. Hurwitz ends the normed division algebras at
O [19]; Jordan, von Neumann and Wigner end the Jordan algebras at the one exceptional case,
J3(O) [31]; the Cartan–Killing classification ends the exceptional simple Lie algebras at E8. The
program sits at the simultaneous endpoint of three independent classification theorems, and the
ω-doubling then exhausts even the residual binary freedom — which chirality, which leaf. A
structure like that has no dials. That is the precise sense in which “gravity and the Standard
Model and nothing else” could ever be more than a slogan: not that the theory declines to add
particles, but that there is nowhere to put them — no N to raise, no representation to append,
no continuous deformation. Georgi–Glashow unification always carried the embarrassment of
the infinite SU(N) ladder behind it; string theory carries a landscape. A terminal object carries
neither. If any program can make anti-anthropic reasoning honest — no scan, hence no mul-
tiverse needed and none available — it is one of this shape. The slogan, however, hides three
equivocations, and the strangeness of the structure is located exactly in them.
First equivocation: in what sense does E8 × ωE8 buy anything?
One must be precise
about what the terminal object actually does in this program. It is not the gauge group: only a
subgroup is gauged. It is not the fermion carrier: the chiral matter lives in Cl(6) minimal ideals
of the split bioctonions, not in E8 representations — which is precisely how the Distler–Garibaldi
no-go is evaded, by exiting the category [33, 34]. The companion analysis of the residual 288 says
the quiet part in its title: those directions are scaffolding labels, an ontology of bookkeeping [34].
So the terminal object currently functions as the theory’s classifying chart — constitutive of
the ontology (what exists, how it is labelled, which misalignments are possible) but not of the
nomology; the dynamics is imported from elsewhere, trace dynamics and the spectral action [14].
This split — kinematic uniqueness without dynamical uniqueness — has an exact historical
isomorph the program must keep in view: Kepler’s Mysterium Cosmographicum [58]. The five
Platonic solids are also a true, terminal classification theorem; nesting them fixed the planetary
architecture beautifully; and it was wrong, because the classification, while genuinely available,
was not dynamically operative. The single theorem that would separate this program from
Kepler’s solids is a derivation of the dynamics from the algebra — why this action and no other
— which the spectral-action route gropes toward and has not delivered. Until it exists, the
honest description is: a kinematically terminal, dynamically imported structure. Eddington [59]
is the other cautionary isomorph, and the coupling-constant ambition is where his ghost lives. A
concrete standard of seriousness, to which the program’s own exploratory claim of αs/αem = 16
must be held: such a relation has its entire physical content in the scheme-and-scale statement,
since αs(MZ)/αem(MZ) ≈15.1 while αs(MZ)/αem(0) ≈16.2; a derivation that lands on 16 only
by evaluating the two couplings at different momenta is Eddington, not physics.
