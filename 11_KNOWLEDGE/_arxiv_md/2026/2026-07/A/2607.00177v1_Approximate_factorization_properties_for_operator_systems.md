Abstract. We show that many of the standard nuclearity properties considered in the literature
for the hierarchy of operator system tensor products can be expressed as approximate factor-
ization properties, generalizing the well-known Completely Positive Approximation Property for
nuclear C*-algebras due to Choi and Effros and its generalization to nuclear operator systems
due to Han and Paulsen.
1. Introduction
A C*-algebra A is nuclear if for every other C*-algebra B the algebraic tensor product A ⊗B
possesses a unique C*-norm. A foundational result in the theory C*-algebras due to Choi and
Effros [5] (and independently to Kirchberg [18]) says that a C*-algebra A is nuclear if and only if it
has the competely positive approximation property. In the unital case, this means that there exists
a net of integers {nλ} and unital completely positive (ucp) maps φλ : A →Mnλ and ψλ : Mnλ →A
such that ψλ ◦φλ(x) →x in norm for every x ∈A.

Through the work of many authors the tensor theory of operator systems has been developed and
rigorously studied. Operator systems are unital self-adjoint subspaces of C*-algebras abstractly
characterized as matrix-ordered ∗-vector spaces possessing an Archimedean matrix order unit.
Given two operator system S and T , an operator system tensor product is a matrix ordering on the
algebraic tensor product S ⊗T making it into an operator system with Archimedean matrix order
unit IS ⊗IT . A variety of tensor product structures for operator systems have been developed,
elucidating deep connections between those tensor products and important properties of operator
systems and C*-algebras such as the exactness, the local lifting property, and the weak expectation
property, to name a few. These connections led to operator system reformulations of many im-
portant problems in C*-algebra theory, for instance Kirchberg’s conjecture and the Smith-Ward
problem [19].
An operator system S is called nuclear if for every operator system T , S ⊗T possesses a unique
matrix ordering making S ⊗T into an operator system with its natural order unit. The completely
positive approximation property was generalized to the context of operator systems by Han and
Paulsen. Specifically, they showed that an operator system S is nuclear if and only if it possesses
the operator system completely positive approximation property. This means that there exists a
net of integers {nλ} and ucp maps φλ : S →Mnλ and ψλ : Mnλ →S such that ψλ ◦φλ(x) →x in
norm for every x ∈S. Han and Paulsen provided an original proof of this property which applies
both to operator systems and to unital C*-algebras.
In this paper, we show that a broad class of the operator system nuclearity properties studied
in the literature is equivalent to natural “triangular” approximate factorization properties. Specif-
ically, we formulate approximate factorization properties equivalent to several central nuclearity
properties, including (min,c), (min, el), (min,er), (el,c), (el,max), and (c,max)-nuclearity.

2.2. Tensor products. Let S and T be operator systems with units e and f, respectively. A
tensor product structure S ⊗T is a matrix ordering {Cn} on S ⊗T such that
(1) (S ⊗T , {Cn}, e ⊗f) is an operator system,
(2) P ⊗Q ∈Cpq for every P ∈Mp(S)+ and Q ∈Mq(T )+, and
(3) φ⊗ψ : S ⊗T →Mpq is completely positive for every ucp φ : S →Mp(C) and ψ : T →Mq.
An operator system tensor product ⊗τ is a mapping that assigns to any pair of operator systems
S and T a corresponding operator system tensor product structure on S ⊗T , denoted S ⊗τ T .
If this mapping is a functor on the category of operator systems (with ucp maps as morphisms),
then the tensor product is called functorial. Specifically, we say that ⊗τ is functorial if for any
ucp maps φ : S →S′ and ψ : T →T ′, it follows that φ ⊗ψ : S ⊗τ T →S′ ⊗τ T ′ is ucp. A tensor
product is left injective (resp. right injective) if φ⊗id : S ⊗τ T →S′ ⊗T (resp. id⊗φ : S ⊗τ T →
S ⊗T ′) is a unital complete order embedding whenever φ (resp. ψ) is a unital complete order
embedding. If ⊗τ is both left and right injective, it is called injective. Similarly, we may define left
projective, right projective, and projective tensor products if the tensor product preserves complete
quotient maps on the left factor, right factor, or both factors. A tensor product is associative if
S ⊗τ(T ⊗τ R) ∼= (S ⊗τ T ) ⊗τ R for all operator systems S, T , and R. It is symmetric if the map
x ⊗y 7→y ⊗x from S ⊗τ T to T ⊗τ S is a complete order isomorphism.
The following tensor products were all defined in [22]. In the following descriptions, fix operator
system S and T with units e and f, respectively.
• The minimal tensor product: We say that x ∈Mn(S ⊗min T )+ provided that (φ ⊗
ψ)(n)(x) ≥0 for every φ : S →Mp and ψ : T →Mq for all p, q ∈N. The minimal
tensor product is known to be functorial, injective, associative, and symmetric. It is also
completely isometrically isomorphic with the operator space minimal tensor product of S
and T as operator spaces (see e.g. Corollary 4.9 of [22]). Finally, injectivity of the minimal
tensor product is often described concretely as follows: whenever π : S ⊆B(H) and
ρ : T ⊆B(K) are unital complete order embeddings, then π ⊗ρ : S ⊗min T ⊆B(H ⊗K)
is a unital complete order embedding.

2.3. k-minimality and k-maximality. A useful fact about operator systems is the following: an
element x ∈Mk(S) is positive if and only if φ(k)(x) ≥0 for all ucp φ : S →Mk. An operator system
S is k-minimal if whenever φ(n)(x) ≥0 for every ucp φ : S →Mk, it follows that x ∈Mn(S)+. The
following theorem collects standard characterizations of k-minimal operator systems, due largely
to Xhabli [31], together with the formulation in item (4) from [1].
Theorem 2.1. Let S be an operator system. The following statements are equivalent.
(1) S is k-minimal.
(2) There exists a compact Hausdorf space X and a unital complete order embedding j : S →
C(X, Mk).
(3) For every operator system T and k-positive map φ : T →S, φ is completely positive.
(4) Whenever x ∈Mn(S) and α∗xα ∈Mk(S)+ for all α ∈Mn,k, it follows that x ≥0 (see
[1]).
From (2), it is evident that Mn is k-minimal as an operator system whenever n ≤k. The same
holds for any l∞direct sum of matrix algebras ⊕λMnλ with nλ ≤k for all k, or any subsystem of
such a matrix algebra. The following was shown in [1]:
Theorem 2.2. Let S be a k-minimal operator system. If φ : C∗
e (S) →B(H) is irreducible, then
dim(H) ≤k.
Since any C*-algebra may be represented faithfully into the l∞-direct sum of images of its
irreducible representations, the C*-envelope embeds isometrically into an l∞direct sum of matrix
algebras of size no more than k×k. As such a direct sum is injective, we conclude that the injective
envelope I(S) is k-minimal. In fact, I(S) is itself isometric to an l∞direct sum of matrix algebras
no larger than k × k. See also [19] for more discussion on k-minimal operator systems.
In duality with k-minimal operator systems, we have the family of k-maximal operator systems.
A k-minimal operator system is an operator system S for which the matrix cone {Mn(S)+} is the
smallest matrix cone generated by Mk(S)+. More explicitly, Mn(Sk−max)+ is the archimedean
closure of the cone of elements of the form α∗diag(P1, P2, . . . , Pm)α where P1, . . . , Pm ∈Mk(S)+
and α ∈Mmk,n. The k-maximal operator systems are also characterized the by following property:
an operator system S is k-maximal if and only if every unital k-positive map φ : S →T from S
to an operator system T is ucp [31].
