A Smoothness Principle for Branch Selection Across Black-Hole Horizons
Yoon-Seok Choun1, ∗and Ki-Seok Kim1, 2, †
1Department of Physics, POSTECH, Pohang, Gyeongbuk 37673, Korea
2Asia Pacific Center for Theoretical Physics (APCTP), Pohang, Gyeongbuk 37673, Korea
(Dated: July 7, 2026)
Many black-hole wave equations reduce to radial ordinary differential equations with regular sin-
gular points at horizons, where local solutions split into distinct branches. We propose a simple
branch-selection principle to isolate the physically distinguished branch: removing the leading hori-
zon behavior and imposing C∞smoothness on the residual field. Using an exactly solvable scalar field
in JT/AdS2, we demonstrate that this single principle successfully organizes three seemingly dis-
tinct structures: smooth continuation across the black-hole horizon, the ambiguity of the boundary
retarded Green function (pole-skipping), and the lowest-weight structure of an SL(2, R) representa-
tion. Rather than treating these phenomena as isolated boundary or horizon properties, our results
unify them into a cohesive bulk-to-boundary dictionary, proving that the boundary pole-skipping
ambiguity is the precise holographic manifestation of horizon-residual smoothness. Furthermore, the
group-theoretic identification reveals that this physical branch selection is not a mere mathemat-
ical artifact, but is fundamentally governed by the underlying spacetime symmetries. Finally, we
apply this framework as a diagnostic tool to a static holographic superconductor model, revealing
that a genuine pole-skipping point requires both simultaneous residual smoothness and strict linear
independence of the boundary branches to prevent deceptive branch-collapse artifacts.
INTRODUCTION
Many wave equations in black-hole backgrounds re-
duce to radial ordinary differential equations with reg-
ular singular points. A black-hole horizon is the most
familiar example. Near the horizon, a local solution usu-
ally splits into different branches, corresponding for in-
stance to waves that enter the black hole and waves that
do not admit the same smooth exterior-interior contin-
uation. This raises a basic question that is independent
of any particular observable: how should one select the
physically distinguished branch of a singular radial equa-
tion?
In this Letter we formulate a simple answer. We
remove the leading horizon behavior that carries the
branch singularity and impose C∞smoothness on the
remaining function. This residual smoothness condition
is not merely a technical regularity requirement. We show
that it acts as a branch-selection principle. In the solv-
able JT/AdS2 scalar problem studied below, this single
principle organizes three apparently different structures:
smooth continuation across a black-hole horizon, ambi-
guity of a boundary retarded Green function, and the
lowest-weight structure of an SL(2, R) representation.
The JT/AdS2 scalar problem is used here not because
the mechanism is special to this model, but because it is
the simplest setting in which the boundary data and the
horizon data can both be followed analytically. In more
complicated holographic systems, coupled equations and
numerical matching can obscure which part of the prob-
lem is a boundary statement and which part is a horizon
branch statement. The solvable JT/AdS2 model removes
these distractions and provides a minimal benchmark for
the residual-smoothness criterion.
The boundary aspect of the story appears in the stan-
dard holographic expansion [1, 2]

The question is what this boundary ambiguity means in
the bulk.
Our first result is that the two boundary coefficients A
and B have separate horizon meanings. After the leading
ingoing behavior at the horizon is removed, the vanish-

merely algebraic statements about the ratio B/A. They
are two separate smoothness conditions for two boundary
branches viewed from the horizon.
Our second result concerns the remaining ambiguity

horizon contains one additional free coefficient, denoted
below by an. This coefficient is the bulk counterpart of
the path-dependence of the boundary ratio B/A. How-
ever, the ordinary ingoing solution is unique away from
the 0/0 point. If we require the solution at the 0/0 point

DIAGNOSTIC APPLICATION: STATIC HSC
SECTORS
For the diagnostic application we consider the stan-
dard Abelian–Higgs holographic superconductor in an
AdS–Schwarzschild background [14–18]. The explicit ac-
tion, static equations, Frobenius recurrences, and branch-
collapse analysis are given in the Supplemental Mate-
rial. Here we use this system only to illustrate the crite-
rion: horizon smooth matching can occur without genuine
pole-skipping.
There are two distinct smoothness tests in the static
HSC problem. The first is a global interior–exterior
matching problem. One sets the boundary source coef-
ficient to zero, keeps the response branch, and continues
this exterior solution to the horizon with residual C∞
regularity. Independently, one constructs a center-regular
interior solution and continues it outward to the hori-
zon. Intersections between these two loci in parameter
space are global smooth matching points. They are not
boundary Green-function pole-skipping points, because
the boundary source and response branches are not both
being tested as independent exterior branches.
The second test is the genuine pole-skipping test.
One separately continues the boundary source branch
and the boundary response branch to the horizon and
imposes residual C∞regularity on each. In the static
HSC sector, the apparent intersections of these two exte-
rior smoothness loci occur at boundary-resonant branch-
collapse points: the two boundary branches that ap-
pear distinct at the boundary become linearly depen-
dent rather than providing two independent horizon-
regular data. Equivalently, W[R1, R2] = 0. Moreover,
these branch-collapse points do not coincide with the
center-regular interior smoothness locus. Thus they do
not represent global modes that penetrate smoothly from
the boundary through the horizon into the interior.

HSC sector shows how apparent pole-skipping intersec-
tions can fail through boundary-branch collapse.
CONCLUSION
We have formulated residual C∞smoothness as a
branch-selection principle for black-hole radial equations
with regular singular points, and demonstrated it in an
exactly solvable JT/AdS2 scalar problem. In this setting,
the boundary 0/0 ambiguity of pole-skipping is separated
into two bulk ingredients: residual smoothness of the two
boundary-normalized branches and an unfixed horizon
coefficient an. The non-resonant pure-ingoing continu-
ation selects an = 0, giving a canonical pure-ingoing
representative with definite boundary data. This does
not remove the pole-skipping ambiguity of the Green
function; it identifies the bulk representative compatible
with the original radial branch. The HSC example then
shows that apparent pole-skipping intersections must be
checked against boundary-branch collapse.
The same viewpoint suggests applications beyond the
solvable model studied here. In more complicated holo-
graphic superconductors, nonzero-frequency modes in-
volve simultaneous boundary, outer-horizon, and inner-
horizon branch selections. In asymptotically flat black-
hole perturbation problems, one encounters analogous
questions of canonical interior continuation after remov-
ing horizon prefactors. These extensions require addi-
tional system-specific analysis, but the organizing prin-
ciple is the same: the physically distinguished branch is
selected by smoothness of the residual field after the sin-
gular prefactor has been removed.
Looking forward, our residual-smoothness criterion
opens up several promising avenues for future explo-
ration. First, it would be highly interesting to extend
this framework to spinning or charged black hole back-
grounds, where coupled gravitational and electromag-

splitting behaviors. Second, the group-theoretic identi-

framework provides a robust computational guideline for
scanning large-scale numerical data in holographic se-
tups, ensuring that newly discovered pole-skipping points
are structurally genuine rather than numerical or coor-
dinates artifacts. Ultimately, this residual smoothness
framework provides a universal geometric bridge con-
necting horizon regularity, boundary ambiguities, and the
macroscopic signatures of quantum chaos.

This diagnostic clarifies the criterion used in the JT/AdS2 analysis. Genuine pole-skipping requires two linearly
independent boundary-normalized branches whose residual fields are simultaneously smooth at the horizon. If the
