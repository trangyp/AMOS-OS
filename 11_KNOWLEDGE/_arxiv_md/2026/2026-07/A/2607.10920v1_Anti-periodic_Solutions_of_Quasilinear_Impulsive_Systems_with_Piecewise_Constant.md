Abstract
This study is devoted to anti-periodic solutions of quasilinear impulsive systems with piecewise constant
argument of generalized type.
We rigorously prove the existence and uniqueness of an anti-periodic
solution making use of the Banach fixed point theorem. The presence of a term with piecewise constant
argument is the main novelty. Appropriate examples which support the theoretical findings are provided.
Keywords: impulsive systems, piecewise constant argument of generalized type, anti-periodic solution

Introduction
Studies on differential equations with piecewise constant argument of generalized type was initiated by
Akhmet [1, 2]. Such arguments generalize the ones based on the greatest integer function utilized in
the papers [3, 4]. Impulsive differential equations, on the other hand, are useful to model processes in
which sudden changes occur [5, 6]. The reader is referred to the book [7] for applications of systems
with impulses. In the present study, we focus on impulsive systems with piecewise constant argument
of generalized type and demonstrate that bounded as well as anti-periodic solutions take place in the
dynamics under sufficient conditions.
Suppose that θ = {θj}j∈Z is a strictly increasing sequence of real numbers such that |θj| →∞as
|j| →∞. We will denote by PC(θ) the set of all functions φ : R →Rn such that for each j ∈Z, the right
and left limits of φ both exist at θj, φ is continuous on the interval (θj, θj+1), and it is left continuous
at θj. The main purpose of the present study is the investigation of anti-periodic solutions of impulsive
systems with piecewise constant argument of generalized type in the form

ii. The functions αk, k = 1, 2, . . . , n, in (1.2) are assumed to be of class C1 on R in [9, 10]. But this is

iii. The Banach fixed point theorem is utilized and the uniqueness of the anti-periodic solution is
obtained in the present study. In the studies [9, 10], Schaefer fixed point theorem is the main tool
and uniqueness is not discussed.
iv. To obtain the existence and uniqueness of an anti-periodic solution we do not use inequalities such
as x(x + Jj(x)) ≥0, Jj(x)(2x + Jj(x)) ≤0, and xJj(x) ≥0. Example 1 provided in Section 5
reveals this distinctiveness.

hand, is devoted to bounded solutions. We provide our main result on the existence and uniqueness of
anti-periodic solutions in Section 4. Section 5 is concerned with appropriate examples supporting the
novel theoretical results. Finally, some concluding remarks are given in Section 6.

Anti-periodic Solutions
In this section, we discuss the existence and uniqueness of an anti-periodic solution of system (1.1) based
on the following definition.
Definition 4.1 ([9]) A function σ ∈PC(θ) is called anti-periodic if there exists a positive real number
ω such that σ(t + ω) = −σ(t) for every t ∈R. The number ω is called an anti-period of σ.
The subsequent assumption on the functions f and h in (1.1) is needed for the verification of anti-
periodicity.
(A6) f(−x, −y) = −f(x, y) and h(−x) = −h(x) for every x, y ∈Rn.

we show that the dynamical behavior of the input is preserved by the impulsive system (1.1).
The examples in Section 5 are constructed in such a way that the number ω in the criteria θj+p =
