ABELIANIZATIONS OF FINITE-INDEX SUBGROUPS OF THE
HANDLEBODY GROUP
ANNIE HOLDEN
Abstract. For genus ≥4, it is an open question whether the mapping class group of a
handlebody contains a finite-index subgroup with nontrivial rational abelianization. In
this paper, we provide evidence that no such subgroup exists. First, we prove that, for all
such finite-index subgroups Γ, meridian multitwists vanish in H1(Γ; Q). Next, we show
that H1(Γ; Q) = 0 for finite-index subgroups Γ containing the handlebody Torelli group,
or large enough subgroups of the twist group or the handlebody Johnson kernel.
1. Introduction
Let Σg be an oriented surface of genus g. The mapping class group Mod(Σg) is the group
of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of Σg. A famous question of
Ivanov asks whether Mod(Σg) admits a finite-index subgroup with infinite abelianization.
See [11] for an overview and see Problem 2.11 (A) in Kirby’s list [14]. This question is
open for g ≥3. In contrast, the analogue of Ivanov’s question for the outer automorphism
group of a free group, Out(Fg), has been completely determined. A deep theorem of
Kaluba–Kielak–Nowak (g ≥6) [13], Kaluba–Nowak–Ozawa (g = 5) [12], and Nitsche (g = 4)
[19] says that Out(Fg) has Kazhdan’s Property (T) when g ≥4. This implies that all
finite-index subgroups have finite abelianization.
Fix a handlebody Vg with ∂Vg = Σg. The handlebody group Hg is the subgroup of
Mod(Σg) consisting of mapping classes that extend to Vg. The handlebody group acts on
π1(Vg), inducing a surjection from Hg onto Out(Fg). In this way, Hg is a natural bridge
between Mod(Σg) and Out(Fg). With this motivation, Hensel [8, Question 8.7] posed the
following “likely very hard” question: for g ≥4, does Hg contain a finite-index subgroup
with infinite abelianization? Equivalently, for g ≥4, does Hg contain a finite-index subgroup
Γ such that H1(Γ;Q) ≠0?
Remark 1.1. The groups Out(F2) and Out(F3) have finite-index subgroups with infinite
abelianizations, and so the same is true for H2 and H3. We therefore restrict our attention
to the cases of g ≥4.
In this paper, we present evidence that Hg does not admit such a finite-index subgroup.
Since our approach is motivated by analogous results for Mod(Σg), we review what is
known about Mod(Σg) to place our work in context.

1.3. Subgroups containing the handlebody Torelli group. The group Mod(Σg)
acts on H1(Σg;Z) and the kernel of this action is the Torelli group, denoted Ig. Hain
[7] and McCarthy [16] showed that any finite-index subgroup Γ of Mod(Σg) containing
the Torelli group satisfies H1(Γ;Q) = 0. The handlebody Torelli group is the intersection
HIg ∶= Hg ∩Ig. Equivalently, HIg is the subgroup of Hg that acts trivially on H1(Σg;Z).
We have the following theorem.
Theorem C (Subgroups containing the handlebody Torelli group). For g ≥3, let Γ
be a finite-index subgroup of Hg that contains the handlebody Torelli group HIg. Then
H1(Γ;Q) = 0.
Remark 1.2. The handlebody group also acts on H1(Vg;Z) and the kernel of this action is
another Torelli subgroup, denoted HBIg. Since HBIg contains both Tg and HIg, Theorem
B (for g ≥4) and Theorem C (for g ≥3) independently tell us that any finite-index subgroup
containing HBIg has finite abelianization.

3. Group homology
We are interested in the group homology of handlebody groups and their subgroups. A
good source on group homology is [2], and an important result in group homology is the
five-term exact sequence, which we introduce in the following subsection.
3.1. Five-term exact sequence. Consider a short exact sequence of groups
1 →K →G →Q →1.
The group G acts on its normal subgroup K by conjugation, inducing an action on H1(K;R)
