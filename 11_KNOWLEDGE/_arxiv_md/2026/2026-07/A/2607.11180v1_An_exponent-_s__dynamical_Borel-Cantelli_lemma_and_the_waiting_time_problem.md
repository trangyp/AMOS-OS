Abstract. Galatolo and Kim proved that the dynamical Borel-Cantelli property for decreas-
ing sequences of balls is tightly connected with the waiting time problem: in systems where
all such sequences are Borel-Cantelli, the time needed to enter a small ball B for the first
time scales as µ(B)−1, and conversely waiting time estimates yield Borel-Cantelli results for
sequences of balls whose radii decrease in a controlled way. We extend this correspondence to
the exponent-s setting introduced by Tseng: for s ≥1, the s-exponent monotone shrinking
target property (sMSTP) requires the Borel-Cantelli conclusion only for decreasing sequences
of centered balls satisfying the stronger divergence condition P
n µ(Bn)s = ∞. We prove that
sMSTP forces the lower waiting time exponent, measured on the scale of −log µ(B(y, r)), to
lie in the interval [1, s] almost everywhere. That a quantitative (s-strong) form of the property
bounds the upper exponent by s and that, conversely, an exponent-s waiting time estimate
implies the Borel-Cantelli property for decreasing sequences of centered balls whose radii obey
the calibrated decay condition matching the critical divergence exponent s. We also obtain
the corresponding quantitative orbit approximation statement lim infn nβ d(T nx, y) = 0 for
β < 1/(s dµ(y)), show that the universal lower bound with exponent 1 pins the theory to
s ≥1, and discuss sharpness on circle rotations, where by results of Kurzweil, Kim-Seo and
Tseng the picture is governed by the Diophantine type of the rotation number.
1. Introduction
Let (X, µ) be a probability space and let {An}n∈N be a sequence of measurable subsets of
X. The classical Borel-Cantelli lemma asserts that if P
n µ(An) < ∞then µ(lim supn An) = 0,
while if the sets are independent and P
n µ(An) = ∞then µ(lim supn An) = 1. In a measure
preserving dynamical system (X, T, µ) one asks the analogous question for the sets T −nAn:
writing
lim sup
n
T −nAn = {x ∈X : T nx ∈An for infinitely many n},
a sequence {An} is called a Borel-Cantelli (BC) sequence for T if µ(lim supn T −nAn) = 1.
Since independence essentially never holds along an orbit, the divergence half of the classical
lemma fails in general, and results guaranteeing that geometrically nice sequences with diver-
gent measure sums are BC are known as ynamical Borel-Cantelli lemmas. When the sets are
balls centered at a fixed point they are called shrinking targets after Hill and Velani [12]. Such
lemmas have been established in a wide variety of situations: for number-theoretic expanding
maps by Philipp [18], for toral translations by Kurzweil [17], for Gibbs measures of expanding
and hyperbolic maps by Chernov and Kleinbock [3], for partially hyperbolic systems by Dol-
gopyat [4], for flows on homogeneous spaces by Kleinbock and Margulis [16] (with logarithm
laws as an application), for interval maps by Kim [14], and for inner functions by Fern´andez,
Meli´an and Pestana [6], among many others.
