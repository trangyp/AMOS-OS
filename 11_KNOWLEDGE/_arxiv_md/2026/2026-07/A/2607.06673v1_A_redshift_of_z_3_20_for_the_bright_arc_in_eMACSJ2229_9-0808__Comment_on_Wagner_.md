A redshift of z = 3.20 for the bright arc in eMACS J2229.9−0808
Comment on Wagner & Falco (2026) “Hamilton’s Object revisited: A challenging
source redshift of a strong lensing conﬁguration”
Ian Smail1⋆, Johan Richard2, H. Ebeling3, and A.C. Edge1
1 Centre for Extragalactic Astronomy, Department of Physics, Durham University, Durham DH1 3LE, UK
2 Centre de Recherche Astrophysique de Lyon UMR5574, CNRS, Univ Lyon, Univ Lyon 1, Ens de Lyon, F-69230 Saint-Genis-
Laval, France
3 Institute for Astronomy, University of Hawaii, 640 N. Aohoku Place, Hilo, HI 96720, USA
Received: May 25, 2026 / Accepted: July 5, 2026
ABSTRACT
We re-analyse near-infrared and optical spectroscopy from the Subaru, Gemini, and Keck telescopes of the bright gravitational arc seen
in the z = 0.62 X-ray cluster eMACS J2229.9−0808. The 22 strongest spectral features we identify uniquely determine the redshift of
the galaxy as z = 3.20, as previously reported by Ebeling et al. (2025), not z = 0.82 as claimed by Wagner & Falco (2026).
Key words. Gravitational lensing: strong -– Techniques: spectroscopic -– Galaxies: high-redshift

eMACS J2229.9−0808 (z = 0.62, Ebeling et al. 2025, here-
after eMACS J2229.9), which has been variously termed
“Hamilton’s Object” (Griﬃths et al. 2021; Wagner & Falco
2026) or “The Scream” (Ebeling et al. 2025).
Griﬃths et al. (2021) analysed GMOS longslit and KCWI
integral-ﬁeld unit (IFU) optical spectroscopy of the three images
of the lensed arc and reported a redshift of z = 0.82 (which would
be noteworthy as this redshift puts the strongly lensed galaxy
only slightly beyond the lensing cluster, which they placed at
z = 0.526 based on photometric redshifts).
The redshift for the arc was corrected by Ebeling et al.
(2025) who used MOIRCS near-infrared spectroscopy along
with a re-reduction and re-analysis of the KCWI IFU observa-
tions and derived a systemic redshift for the galaxy of z = 3.201
(they also established the cluster redshift as z = 0.62 based on the
spectroscopic redshifts of 65 members).
Recently, Wagner & Falco (2026) have published a re-
reduction and re-analysis of the KCWI and MOIRCS observa-
tions of the arc, claiming that the former supports z = 0.82 and
the latter is inconclusive.
We present here a comprehensive re-analysis of the available
spectroscopy and photometry which demonstrates that they un-
ambiguously determine the redshift of the lensed galaxy to be
z = 3.20, as previously reported by Ebeling et al. (2025).
2. Observations and Data Reduction
Imaging of the ﬁeld of eMACS J2229.9 with Hubble Space Tele-
scope (HST), using WFC3 (F160W, GO 13305, PI: C. Vill-
forth), ACS (F606W and F814W, GO 13671, PI: H. Ebeling)
⋆Corresponding author: ian.smail@durham.ac.uk
and WFC3 (F110W and F140W, GO 14098, PI: H. Ebeling)
was obtained in September/October 2013, 2015, and 2016, re-
spectively. A colour image of eMACS J2229.9 constructed from
these data (Fig. 1a) shows the location of the two images com-
prising the primary fold arc (A/B, following the naming conven-
tion of Griﬃths et al. 2021, at 22h30m09.s7 −08◦09′40′′, J2000),
the counter-image (C), as well as the faint arc (named here D)
identiﬁed by Griﬃths et al. (2021), along with the “Lyman-α
blob” (LAB) they report (see also Wagner & Falco 2026). We
measure total magnitudes for A, B, and C from these images and
plot their V606 −I814 and I814 −H160 colours in Fig. 1b.
The ﬁrst spectroscopic data obtained of the bright arc was a
K-band spectrum of image A taken on 2016 September 20 us-
ing the MOIRCS spectrograph on the Subaru Telescope (PI: H.
Ebeling). The source was nodded along the slit for a total expo-
sure of 11.5 ks; details of the observational setup and reduction
are provided in Ebeling et al. (2025). The summed spectrum of
A from the nodded pair (Fig. 2c) shows strong [Oiii] 4959,5007
emission and potentially Hβ (although this spectral feature lies
on a sky line) yielding a robust redshift of z = 3.201. We can-
not explain why Wagner & Falco (2026) were unable to extract
a similar spectrum from their attempt to re-reduce the MOIRCS
observations.
On 2018 August 14, a longslit spectrum of A/B was ob-
tained with the Gemini-N GMOS spectrograph (program GN-
2017A-Q-48, PI: R. Griﬃths). The data we analyse totalled
5.4-ks integration using the R400 grating with a central wave-
length of 7000–7100Å. Data and associated calibration frames
were retrieved from the Gemini archive and reduced using
the DRAGONS pipeline (Labrie et al. 2023), including standard
bias subtraction, ﬂatﬁelding, image combination, spectral ex-
traction, wavelength calibration and ﬂuxing. The resulting spec-
trum (Fig. 2b) shows several strong absorption lines, including
a broad absorption feature at ∼6500Å, which we identify as the
Civ1548,1550 doublet at z ∼3.20.

3. Analysis and Results
Ebeling et al. (2025) brieﬂy reported the results of our re-
analysis of the KCWI observations and listed the identiﬁcation
in the spectrum of A+B of some of the stronger features, includ-
ing Lyman-α; Lyman-β; the Lyman break; Siii 1260; Oi 1302;
and Cii 1335 interstellar medium (ISM) lines at z ∼3.20, in
agreement with the redshift derived from the [Oiii] lines in
their MOIRCS spectrum. They noted that these spectral fea-
tures had been previously identiﬁed by Griﬃths et al. (2021) as
Mgii 2796,2803; Feii 2365; not noted; Feii 2905; Fei 3004; and
Tiii 3074, respectively.
Recently, Wagner & Falco (2026) have also re-reduced the
KCWI observations using PypeIt and re-analysed them. How-
ever, their analysis of the spectrum of the lensed galaxy involved
subtracting a smoothed version (using a Gaussian ﬁlter with a
FWHM of 23.5 Å) from the data, before attempting to identify
features in the residuals. They tabulate 17 absorption features,
including several reported by Griﬃths et al. (2021), as well as 10
emission features, identifying many of these with low-equivalent
width lines that are typically only visible in extremely deep inte-
grations or stacked spectra, and state that these support a redshift
of z = 0.82 for the source.
We do not give a detailed analysis of the features reported
by Wagner & Falco (2026), nine of which appear to correspond
to real absorption lines in Table 1 (although four of these lack
a proposed identiﬁcation at z = 0.82 in Wagner & Falco 2026).
Instead, we note that the scale of the 23.5-Å FWHM ﬁlter used
by them to create a “continuum-subtracted” spectrum, is far nar-
rower than some of the real features in the spectrum (Fig. 2a).
We also repeat their processing steps on the three spectra plotted
in Fig. 2a and show these in Fig. 3 (which can be compared to
Fig. 4 in Wagner & Falco 2026). It is evident that the subtraction
of such a smoothed version of the spectrum from itself not only
removes real information from broad features, but also signiﬁ-
cantly confuses the subsequent identiﬁcation and interpretation
of real features. We therefore focus our analysis on the features
visible in the spectra shown in Fig. 2.

and GMOS (Figs. 2a & 2b) shows a large number of absorption

the lensed galaxy from the restframe optical emission lines, com-
pared to the median redshift of z = 3.1960± 0.0005 derived from
the restframe UV absorption lines (Table 1), i.e., the UV lines
are oﬀset by −380 ± 40 km s−1. Such blue shifts are frequently
seen in the ISM absorption lines in LBGs and arise from out-
ﬂows (e.g., Shapley et al. 2003).

galaxy are consistent with a redshift of z = 3.20, rather than
z = 0.82 (Griﬃths et al. 2021; Wagner & Falco 2026), using the
V606 −I814 versus I814 −H160 colour-colour diagram in Fig. 1b.

4. Discussion
The 22 strongest absorption and emission lines listed in Ta-
ble 1 and plotted in Fig. 2 show conclusively that the bright
lensed galaxy in eMACS J2229.9 has a redshift of z = 3.20, as
ﬁrst reported by Ebeling et al. (2025), not z = 0.82 as claimed by
Wagner & Falco (2026).
In Fig. 1a we plot the contours corresponding to a magni-
ﬁcation of µ = 50 as dashed lines, showing that the fold image
of the arc (A/B) is highly magniﬁed. From the lenstool lens
model presented in Ebeling et al. (2025), we estimate that A+B
have a total area-weighted magniﬁcation of 57 × (indicating an
intrinsic brightness of H160 ∼24.6). Searching the UDS galaxy
catalogue used in Fig. 1 for z ∼3.2 galaxies with H160 ∼24.6
and colours matching the arc, we use their physical proper-
ties, derived from magphys modelling of their ∼20-band pho-
tometry by Dudzeviˇci¯ut˙e et al. (2020), to estimate the proper-
ties of the galaxy. This suggests that the galaxy is likely to be a
strongly star-forming, but sub-M∗, system with a star-formation
rate of approximately, ∼15 ± 5 M⊙yr−1 and a stellar mass of
M∗∼(4 ± 2) × 109 M⊙.
We overlay on Fig. 1a a contour showing the bright Lyman-
α emission detected in the KCWI observations at 5106.9Å
(corresponding to z = 3.201) which is reported as a LAB by
Griﬃths et al. (2021), see also Wagner & Falco (2026). This
emission is coincident with the highest-magniﬁcation part of the
faint arc D, a connection which had not previously been made,
indicating that it too lies at z = 3.20. From the z = 3.2 magniﬁ-
cation contour we see that this source (which is only visible in
F606W and F814W) is highly magniﬁed, with a magniﬁcation of
∼50–60×, corresponding to an intrinsic brightness of V606 ∼30
and suggesting it is probably a low-mass galaxy. However, the
observed Lyman-α size of this source likely does not reﬂect its
intrinsic extent, but results instead from the signiﬁcant shear of
the image and the low spatial resolution of the wide-ﬁeld KCWI
observations. Whether this source is an intrinsically spatially ex-
tended LAB thus remains unclear.
We note that the identiﬁcation of arc D as the claimed “LAB”
refutes one argument made by Wagner & Falco (2026) against
the bright arc lying at z = 3.20. Their claim was that this would
require that the z = 3.20 “LAB” should show a similar multi-
ple image conﬁguration to that displayed by bright arc images,
A/B/C, but that only one image of the LAB was seen within the
KCWI ﬁeld. However, our identiﬁcation of the bright Lyman-α
emitter as D removes this objection, as D actually comprises two
highly magniﬁed images in a fold conﬁguration. The predicted
counter image is expected to be ∼10 × fainter, making it unde-
tectable in the existing HST imaging (observed V606 ∼28), and
is located near 22h30m08.s72 −08◦09′22.′′3, which falls outside
of the KCWI footprint.
5. Conclusions
We conclude that the redshifts for the lensed arc (A/B/C,
z = 0.82) and the cluster (a photometric redshift of z = 0.526),
originally proposed in Griﬃths et al. (2021), and reiterated in
Wagner & Falco (2026) are both in error. Ebeling et al. (2025)
has provided extensive spectroscopy of cluster members show-
ing that the cluster redshift is z = 0.62. They also reported a red-
shift for the bright fold arc of z = 3.20, correcting the value re-
ported in Griﬃths et al. (2021), a fact that has been disputed by
Wagner & Falco (2026). Our re-reduction and re-analysis of all
of the spectroscopy of this source uncovers 22 robust spectral
features which show deﬁnitively that the lensed galaxy has a
