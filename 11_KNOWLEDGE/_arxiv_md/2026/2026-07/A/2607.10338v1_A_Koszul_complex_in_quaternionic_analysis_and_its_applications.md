1. Introduction
The quaternion algebra H, with its familiar basis {1, i, j, k} and multiplication rules i2 =
j2 = k2 = ijk = −1, was introduced by Hamilton [Hamil66]. Quaternionic analysis, broadly
understood as a generalization of complex analysis to the non-commutative setting, has
since evolved into a rich subject encompassing several distinct but interrelated frameworks.
The most direct quaternionic counterpart of holomorphic function theory is the theory of
quaternionic regular functions, initiated by Fueter [Fuet35]. A differentiable function
f is called (left) regular if it is annihilated by the Cauchy–Fueter operator
D =

∂x1

Fueter showed that these functions enjoy many properties parallel to those of holomorphic
functions — notably Laurent expansions and a Cauchy integral formula [Fuet35, Fuet36]
— and they have found numerous applications in theoretical physics [Adler86, Lanc29].
This theory has been extensively developed over the ensuing decades [Sudb79, CSSS04]
and has been subsumed into the broader programme of Clifford analysis [BDS82, Gilb91,
LY24, CSSS04].
A natural higher-spin generalization arises from the k-Cauchy–Fueter operator D(k)
0
(see Definition 2.5), which originates in physics as the elliptic incarnation of the spin-
k/2 massless field equations on Minkowski spacetime [CMW16, EPW80, PR84, PR86].
Functions annihilated by D(k)
0
are called k-regular functions; they reduce to Fueter’s
regular functions when k = 1, while the case k = 0 corresponds to harmonic functions.
Thus the k-regular hierarchy interpolates between harmonic and regular functions, offering
a flexible scale of regularity.
The extension of the k-Cauchy–Fueter operator to several quaternionic variables was car-
ried out by Wang [Wang10], where the resulting k-regular functions simultaneously gener-
alize Fueter’s one-variable theory and the several-variable regular functions introduced ear-
lier by Pertici [Pert88]. In this multivariable setting, there is a one-to-one correspondence
between 1-regular functions and quaternionic regular functions. Kang and Wang [KW13]
subsequently established a Taylor expansion for k-regular functions via the Penrose integral
formula. The associated Cauchy–Fueter complex — the quaternionic analogue of the
Dolbeault complex — has been studied by several authors [ALPS97, ABLSS99, Wang10],
and a comprehensive treatment of k-Cauchy–Fueter operators and k-regular functions can
be found in the series of works [Wang10, Wang15, Wang19, Wang25, RZ23].
The Koszul complex is a very important tool in algebra [Buch64, BR64, GH78, CSSS04].
As an application in the theory of several complex variables, the Koszul complex provides
a resolution of the ideal sheaf IX, where X is an analytic subset of Cn and IX is defined as
the sheaf of holomorphic functions vanishing on X. This resolution further shows that IX
is a coherent analytic sheaf, and the complex has many applications and generalizations in
several complex variables [GH78, Ji12, JY26].
We illustrate with the example
