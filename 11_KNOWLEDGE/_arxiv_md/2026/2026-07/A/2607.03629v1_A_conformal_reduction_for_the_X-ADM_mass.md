A CONFORMAL REDUCTION FOR THE X-ADM MASS
STEPHEN MCCORMICK
Abstract. The X-ADM mass is a geometric invariant for asymptot-
ically flat manifolds, recently introduced by Mantegazza and Oronzio
[15], generalising the weighted mass of Baldauf and Ozuch [1] which
itself is a generalisation of the well-known ADM mass.
We show that the positivity of the X-ADM mass is in fact equivalent
to the standard positive mass theorem for the ADM mass by way of a
conformal reduction argument, which in particular proves the X-positive
mass theorem in all dimensions, which previously was only established
in dimension 3 under a topological condition. As a corollary, we obtain
the Riemannian mass–charge inequality of general relativity in all di-
mensions. Finally, we prove a version of the X-positive mass theorem
for a manifold with boundary.
1. Introduction
The Riemannian positive mass theorem for asymptotically flat manifolds
is a cornerstone of mathematical general relativity and a central result in
scalar curvature geometry. It has a long history with many contributors,
but the first proofs were by Schoen and Yau [20] using minimal surface
techniques, and by Witten [23] using a spinor argument. Witten’s proof
holds in all dimensions for spin manifolds [2] while Schoen and Yau’s proof
does not impose a spin condition and holds up to dimension 7 [22]. There
has been considerable work by many authors extending the positive mass
theorem to higher dimensions [4, 8, 7, 14, 21], culminating in the very re-
cent work by Brendle and Wang [5] giving a full proof of the positive mass
theorem in all dimensions.
Roughly, the positive mass theorem says that the ADM mass of an asymp-
totically flat manifold with non-negative scalar curvature is non-negative,
and zero only when the manifold is isometric to Rn. Recently, Mantegazza
and Oronzio [15] introduced a generalisation of the ADM mass depending
on a given vector field X called the X-ADM mass. If X is a gradient vec-
tor field, then the X-ADM mass reduces to the weighted mass of Baldauf
and Ozuch [1], and if X vanishes it reduces to the standard ADM mass.
The positivity of the X-ADM mass also implies the mass–charge inequality
of general relativity, essentially unifying several different positive mass-type
theorems. Mantegazza and Oronzio were able to prove a positive mass theo-
rem for this X-ADM mass (see Theorem A below) in dimension 3, assuming
an additional topological condition.
