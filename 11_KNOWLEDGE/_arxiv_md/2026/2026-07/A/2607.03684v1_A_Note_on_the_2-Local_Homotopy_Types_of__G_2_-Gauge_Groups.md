A NOTE ON THE 2-LOCAL HOMOTOPY TYPES
OF G2-GAUGE GROUPS
PHUC VO DANG
Abstract. In a recent preprint [2] , Kameko presented a substantial completion of the
2-local classification of G2-gauge groups over S4, extending earlier work by Kishimoto,
Theriault, and Tsutaya. The central strategy relies on reducing the 2-local classification
to the order of the Samelson product ⟨i3, 1⟩and separating specific gauge group homotopy
types. The purpose of this note is to provide necessary mathematical refinements and
localization clarifications to several key steps in the proof of the classification theorem.
Specifically, we refine the integral isomorphism claim for gauge group homotopy to its
correct 2-local form, resolve an EHP sequence extension regarding the injectivity of the
Hopf invariant, and make explicit the Postnikov layer conventions required for the mod
2 Leray–Serre spectral sequence calculations. We confirm that with these adjustments,
the main 2-local classification theorem in [2] also holds as claimed.
1. Introduction
The classification of gauge groups over spheres is a central problem in unstable homo-
topy theory. In a recent preprint [2], Kameko gave a convincing completion of the 2-local
part of the classification of G2-gauge groups over S4, continuing the foundational work of
Kishimoto, Theriault, and Tsutaya [3]. The overarching strategy (namely, the reduction
of the 2-local classification to the order of the Samelson product ⟨i3, 1⟩and the separa-
tion of G4 from G0) is natural and compatible with Lang’s identification of the boundary
map with the triple adjoint of a Samelson product [4]. Furthermore, the computations
utilizing Toda’s notation for ¯ν6, ϵ6, ζ6, the relative Samelson product calculations based
on James’ formula [1], and the parity arguments involving p11q are structurally robust,
drawing upon classical homotopy-group inputs from Mimura [5] and Toda [6].
The objective of this note is to strengthen the theoretical foundation of the classifica-
tion in [2] by addressing two mathematical inaccuracies in the intermediate arguments,
providing a necessary localization clarification regarding connective covers, and refining
several technical expressions. It should be emphasized that these modifications do not
invalidate the main conclusion of [2]; rather, they place the proof of the main theorem on
completely rigorous ground.

3. Injectivity of the Hopf Invariant and EHP Sequences
A second refinement is required in the first paragraph of the proof of Proposition 2.2 in
[2], where the vanishing of π10(S5) is assumed in order to infer the injectivity of the Hopf
invariant
H : π11(S6)(2) −→π11(S11)(2).
According to Toda’s tables [6], the integral homotopy group is non-zero:
π10(S5) ∼= Z/2.
While this observation affects the immediate rationale, the intended injectivity conclusion
remains valid and can be rigorously established through the following modified argument.
Consider the relevant portion of the EHP exact sequence:
π10(S5)(2)
Σ−→π11(S6)(2)
H−→π11(S11)(2).
Here, π10(S5)(2) ∼= Z/2, whereas π11(S6)(2) ∼= Z(2){∆(ι13)} is torsion-free.
Since any
homomorphism from a torsion group (Z/2) to a torsion-free group (Z(2)) must be the zero
map, the suspension homomorphism Σ vanishes identically. Exactness therefore implies
ker H = 0, proving that H is indeed injective on π11(S6)(2).
Because the localization map π11(S6) ∼= Z →π11(S6)(2) is injective, this 2-local injec-
tivity is entirely sufficient to deduce the subsequent integral equality in the infinite cyclic
group π11(S6). The Hopf invariant computation establishing
(2ι6) ◦∆(ι13) ≃4∆(ι13)
then proceeds without alteration, preserving its application in Proposition 3.9 of [2].

5. Further Refinements and Notational Corrections
For the completeness of the mathematical record, we note several smaller technical and
notational adjustments that refine the arguments in [2]:
Cofiber Sequences in Proposition 2.4. In [2, Proposition 2.4] , the assertion
[P 13(G2), G2] ∼= 0
should be corrected to read
[P 13(2), G2] ∼= 0.
This notation aligns with the preceding cofiber sequence S12 →P 13(2) →S13.
Parity and Multiplication Factors in Proposition 2.5. In [2, Proposition 2.5], the
conclusion drawn from the parity of a should not be written as (ψ ◦s′′) ≃4⟨¯ν6 + ϵ6⟩.
Because the calculation evaluates 84(ψ ◦s′′), the conclusion is more accurately expressed
as
84 · (ψ ◦s′′) ≃4 · ⟨¯ν6 + ϵ6⟩
after suppressing the odd unit in the cyclic 2-primary summand. More explicitly, the
proof yields
84 · (ψ ◦s′′) ≃4a · ⟨¯ν6 + ϵ6⟩
with a odd, which is the exact form required upon precomposition with Σ3(p11q).
Typographical Corrections in Propositions 3.3 and 4.3. In [2, Proposition 3.3], the
displayed expression ⟨¯ν6 + ϵ⟩should explicitly read ⟨¯ν6 + ϵ6⟩. Similarly, in [2, Proposition
4.3], the final displayed computation for the case γ = ϵ6 should begin with ⟨i3, ϵ6⟩r rather
than ⟨i3, ¯ν6⟩r. The stated result ⟨i3, ϵ6⟩r ≃2¯ν6 ◦ν14 is unaffected.

6. Conclusion
By incorporating the 2-local refinement of [2, Proposition 6.2], establishing the injectiv-
ity of the Hopf invariant via the torsion-free property of π11(S6)(2), and adopting explicit
2-local Postnikov conventions in Section 5, the logical architecture of [2] becomes entirely
rigorous. [2, Proposition 6.1] successfully distinguishes G1 and G2 from G0 and G4 through
the computation
π13(Gk) ∼= Z/(8, 2k) ⊕Z/2.
The separation of G4 from G0 relies strictly on 2-local information on π3, for which the 2-
localized Proposition 6.2 is completely adequate. Consequently, the determination of the
2-primary order of ⟨i3, 1⟩stands, and the complete 2-local classification by (k, 8) follows
as claimed in [2, Theorem 1.1].
