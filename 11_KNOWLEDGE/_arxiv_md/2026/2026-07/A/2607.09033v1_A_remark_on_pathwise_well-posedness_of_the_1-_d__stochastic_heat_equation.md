A REMARK ON PATHWISE WELL-POSEDNESS OF
THE 1-d STOCHASTIC HEAT EQUATION
YUFEI SHAO, JIAWEI LI, AND TADAHIRO OH
Abstract. We study pathwise well-posedness of the stochastic heat equation (SHE) with
a multiplicative noise on the circle. By combining the convolution Young and rough in-
tegration theory, introduced by Gubinelli and Tindel (2010), with the random tensor esti-
mate approach to pathwise well-posedness of stochastic dispersive PDEs with multiplicative
noises, introduced by Chapouto and the second and third authors (2026), we establish path-
wise well-posedness of SHE in both the Young and rough cases, improving the results in
Gubinelli and Tindel (2010). In particular, in the rough case (= the white-in-time case), our
result covers the case of almost space-time white noise, thus establishing an optimal result
within the framework of one-parameter rough paths.

Our main goal in this paper is to revisit pathwise well-posedness issues for SHE (1.1),
previously studied in [18, 19, 21, 14]. In [19], Gubinelli and Tindel generalized Lyons’ rough
path theory [23] and developed an algebraic integration theory adapted to an analytic semi-
group. More precisely, they extended the sewing lemma and controlled paths, introduced
in a seminal work [15] by Gubinelli, to the current convolution setting.4 In particular, by
constructing the stochastic convolution Ψ(u) in (1.4) as a convolution Young / rough integral,
they proved well-posedness of SHE (1.1) with ϕ = ⟨∂x⟩−ν. See Subsections 4.1 and 5.1 below
for a review on the algebraic part of their approach.
In this paper, we will employ the algebraic structure introduced in [19] but introduce an
improvement on the analytical side by incorporating the random tensor estimate, originally
introduced in the context of random variables by Deng, Nahmod, and Yue [12], to the current
stochastic setting.
In a series of recent works [6, 7, 4, 27, 5], the last two authors with
A. Chapouto developed pathwise well-posedness theory of stochastic dispersive PDEs with
multiplicative noises, which had been open for more than several decades since the inception
of Ito solution theory (and random field solution theory) in [33] for wave equations and
in [10, 11] for Schr¨odinger equations. A key ingredient in this development was the random
tensor estimate for multiple stochastic integrals (for both the fractional-in-time and white-
in-time cases); see Lemma 3.5. See Section 3 for a further discussion on the random tensor
estimate for multiple stochastic integrals. The random tensor estimate allows us to estimate
operator norms of relevant (random) drivers in a much more effective manner as compared
to [19], where the operator norm was crudely bounded by the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, since
the latter is more amenable to stochastic analysis (in particular, the Wiener-Ito isometry).

The main task is then to construct the convolution Young integral IX1(u). In doing so, we
follow the incremental formulation as in [15, 19], where the matter is reduced to establishing
almost sure regularity properties of the random driver X1. In [19], Gubinelli and Tindel
estimated the operator norm of X1 by its Hilbert-Schmidt norm, which led to a loss. In our
novel approach, we study regularity properties of X1 by applying the random tensor estimate
for (multiple) stochastic integrals (Lemma 3.5), developed in [30, 6, 7]; see Subsection 4.2. A
primary reason for working with the Hilbert-Schmidt norm in [19] (which may be viewed as
the ℓ2-norm for eigenvalues in some setting) is its compatibility with the Wiener-Ito isometry
(related to the L2(Ω)-norm). On the other hand, the operator norm may be regarded as the
ℓ∞-norm, which is not compatible with the L2(Ω)-norm for the Wiener-Ito isometry. The
main idea behind the random tensor estimate, due to Deng, Nahmod, and Yue [12], is as
follows; instead of working with the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a random operator (X1 in our
setting), we work with the Hilbert-Schmidt norm (= the ℓ2-norm) of a very high power of
X1, say the kth power. This gives a control on the ℓ2k-norm of (the kernel of) X1, providing
a good approximation for the ℓ∞-norm (= the operator norm) when k ≫1, which is far more
effective than approximating the ℓ∞-norm by the ℓ2-norm. In the proof of the random tensor
estimate, the kernel of a high power of X1 is estimated in an inductive manner. We point
out that the Wiener chaos estimate (Lemma 3.2) plays a crucial role in reducing the matter
to estimating the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a high power of X1 (more precisely, the ℓ2-norm
of the kernel of a high power of X1); see [30, 6] for the proofs. We note that a precursor of
this idea (of working with a higher power of a random operator) already appears in a seminal
work [1] by Bourgain. See also Remark 3.6. Lastly, we remark that, as pointed out in [3,
Remark 6.6], the random tensor estimate is closely related to operator bounds for structured
random matrices and can be proven, using the non-commutative Khintchine inequality [32,
Theorem 3.2]. See [3, 22] for a further discussion.
Once we establish almost sure regularity properties of the driver X1 (Proposition 4.2), we
can simply apply the convolution sewing lemma (Lemma 4.1) to construct the convolution
Young integral IX1(u) in a pathwise manner.
