1. Introduction
The main problem we address in this paper is whether a graph G contains many vertex-disjoint
cycles of different lengths. Bensmail, Harutyunyan, Le, Li, and Lichiardopol [BHL+17] proved that
the answer is yes for graphs with large minimum degree.
Theorem 1.1 (Bensmail et al. [BHL+17]). For every k ≥1, every graph of minimum degree at
least 5k2+5k−2
2
contains k vertex-disjoint cycles of different lengths.
Rather than a sufficient condition, we instead prove a rough structure theorem for the set of graphs
which do not contain many vertex-disjoint cycles of different lengths. The related problem of when a
graph contains many vertex-disjoint induced cycles of different lengths was very recently considered
by Chudnovsky and Maier [CM26]. Our theorem can be viewed as an Erdős-Pósa type theorem,
which is a reference to the following classic result by Erdős and Pósa.
Theorem 1.2 (Erdős-Pósa Theorem [EP65]). There exists a function f(k) ∈O(k log k) such that
the following holds. For every k ∈N and every graph G, G contains k vertex-disjoint cycles, or
there exists a set X ⊆V (G) with |X| ≤f(k) such that G −X is a forest.
The Erdős-Pósa Theorem has been hugely influential in graph structure theory and has been
studied in numerous other settings, including minors [RS86b, CHJR19, PPTW24], topological
minors [Tho88, Liu22, PPTW24], immersions [Liu21, KK18], directed cycles [RRST96], matroid
circuits [GK09], and vertex minors [CGH+26].
Moreover, there is an extensive line of work on the Erdős-Pósa property for cycles satisfying various
constraints including triangles [Tuz90], odd cycles [Ree99, KKKX25], even cycles [BHJ18, GKKW24,
Gor24], cycles of length 0 (mod m) [Tho88], cycles of length ℓ(mod p) with p prime [TY23], long
cycles [BBR07b, CvBJU20, MNvW17], holes [KK20, HK24], S-cycles [KKM11], and (S1, S2)-cycles
[HJW19]. Most (but not all) of the aforementioned results on cycles now follow from very general
theorems for cycles in graphs with edge-labels from multiple abelian groups [HJW19, GHK+25,
GHK+24]. For more details, we refer the interested reader to [RT17] for a general survey on
Erdős-Pósa theorems.
All of the above results can be stated in terms of packings and covers in hypergraphs, as we now
describe. Given a hypergraph H, a packing is a set of disjoint hyperedges, and a cover is a set of
vertices X such that e ∩X ̸= ∅for all e ∈E(H). We define ν(H) := max{|M| : M is a packing}
and τ(H) := min{|X| : X is a cover}. Clearly, τ(H) ≥ν(H) for every hypergraph H. We say that
a family H of hypergraphs satisfies the Erdős-Pósa property if there exists a function f such that
τ(H) ≤f(ν(H)) for every H ∈H. For example, for every graph G, we can make a hypergraph HG,
where V (HG) = V (G) and E(HG) = {V (C) | C is a cycle of G}. The original theorem of Erdős
and Pósa is simply the assertion that the family of hypergraphs H := {HG | G is a graph} has
the Erdős-Pósa property. Moreover, maximum packings and minimum covers are optimal integral
solutions to two dual linear programs, and the Erdős-Pósa function f measures the integrality gap
between the two linear programs.
Interestingly, maximising the number of vertex-disjoint cycles of different lengths in a graph G
is not a maximum packing problem in a hypergraph with vertex set V (G).

2. Disjoint cycles with distinct lengths
The proofs in this section rely heavily on the graph minor structure theory of Robertson and
Seymour, in particular on treewidth and arguments on tree-decompositions.
Let us first define what we mean by a tree-decomposition. A tree-decomposition of a graph G is a
pair T = (T, β), where T is a tree and β : V (T) →2V (G) such that
• for every edge e ∈E(G), there exists a t ∈V (T) such that e ⊆β(t), and
• for every vertex v ∈V (G), the set β−1(v) induces a connected, non-empty subtree of T.
The width of T is the maximum value of |β(t)| −1 over all t ∈V (T) and the treewidth tw(G) of G
is the minimum width over all tree-decompositions of G.
If T has a designated root r, we call (T, r, β) a rooted tree-decomposition. Given a tree T with a
root r and a vertex t ∈V (T), we denote by Tt the subtree of T that contains all vertices whose path
to r in T contains t. Further, if (T, r, β) is a rooted tree-decomposition of a graph G, we denote by
Gt the subgraph G[S
s∈V (Tt) β(s)].
We first show that graphs with bounded treewidth have our desired packing-covering property for
cycles of distinct lengths. This is derived from the following lemma.
Lemma 2.1. Let k ∈N+. Let T be a tree and T1, . . . , Tm be non-empty subtrees of T. Let c be a
colouring of the subtrees T1, . . . , Tm. Then there exists k vertex-disjoint subtrees each given distinct
colours by c, or there exists a set X ⊆V (T) with |X| ≤k −1 such that there are at most k −1
colours among the subtrees disjoint from X.
Proof. We proceed by induction on |V (T)|. Clearly, the result holds for |V (T)| = 1. Suppose
|V (T)| ≥2 and let v ∈V (T) be a leaf. If there exists an i ∈[m] such that V (Ti) = {v}, then we
add Ti to our collection. We then update our collection by removing all subtrees which have colour
c(Ti) or contain the vertex v, and we apply induction to T −v. If we find k −1 disjoint subtrees of
distinct colours in this updated collection, then together with Ti, this is the desired collection. If we
find a set X′ ⊆V (T −v) of at most k −2 vertices hitting all subtrees with colours outside a set of
at most k −2 colours, then X = X′ ∪{v} is the desired hitting set.
If none of the subtrees T1, . . . , Tm contains only the vertex v, then we delete v from every subtree
and apply induction to T −v. A hitting set X in T −v is also a hitting set in T. On the other
hand, disjoint subtrees in T −v lift to disjoint subtrees in T. This completes the proof.

Our next goal is to prove that in graphs of high treewidth, we can find a subgraph certifying the

3. Lower bounds
In order to quantify the tightness of our bounds, we introduce the following definition. Let f
and g be functions N →R. We say that (f, g) is a binding pair if for all k ∈N, every graph G
contains k vertex-disjoint cycles of distinct lengths, or a set of at most f(k) vertices X such that
|Λ(G −X)| ≤g(k). We can restate our main theorem in terms of binding pairs as follows.
Theorem 1.3. Let g(k) = k −1. Then there exists a function f(k) ∈O(k6polylog(k)) such that
(f, g) is a binding pair.
We now show that g(k) = k −1 is optimal for binding pairs (f, g) in the following strong sense.
Theorem 3.1. If (f, g) is a binding pair, then g(k) ≥k −1 for all k ∈N.
Proof. Let G be the disjoint union of f(k) + 1 copies of Kk+1. Then G does not contain k vertex-
disjoint cycles with distinct lengths, but |Λ(G −X)| ≥k −1 for all X ⊆V (G) with |X| ≤f(k).
On the other hand, we now show that f(k) ∈O(k log k) is best possible for binding pairs (f, g) in
the following strong sense.

Theorem 3.2. If (f, g) is a binding pair, then f ∈Ω(k log k).

contradiction. Thus, G −X is a forest, and so |X| ∈Ω(k log k), as required.
4. Coloured faces of embedded graphs
For our second main theorem, our goal is to pack or cover faces with distinct lengths in embedded
graphs. We, in fact, prove a more general theorem for coloured faces, where the colour of a face is
not necessarily equal to its length. We begin by reviewing some basic definitions from topological
graph theory. We refer the reader to [MT01] for more details. The Euler genus of a surface obtained
from the sphere by h handles and c crosscaps is 2h + c.
4.1. Embedded graphs, surfaces, and colouring
Embeddings
Given a graph G, we say that G has an embedding ψ into a surface Σ, if ψ is a
function with domain V (G) ∪E(G) such that
i) ψ(v) is a point of Σ for each v ∈V (G),
ii) ψ(u) ̸= ψ(v) for all distinct u, v ∈V (G),
iii) ψ(uv) is a simple curve in Σ with the ends ψ(u), ψ(v) for all uv ∈E(G),
iv) ψ(uv) ∩ψ(wx) ⊆{ψ(u), ψ(v)}, for all distinct uv, wx ∈E(G), and
v) ψ(uv) ∩ψ(w) = ∅for all uv ∈E(G) and w ∈V (G) \ {u, v}.
We let ψ(G) be the union of all points and curves in its image and call the connected components of
Σ \ ψ(G) the faces of ψ, and we let F(ψ) be the set of faces of ψ. If every face of ψ is homeomorphic
to an open disk, we say that ψ is a 2-cell embedding. If, in addition, the boundary of each face is a
simple closed curve, then ψ is a closed 2-cell embedding.
A particular advantage of closed 2-cell embeddings is that they allow us to define simple dual graphs
of embedded graphs. Let G be a graph with a closed 2-cell embedding ψ in some surface. Then the
dual graph G∗of G is a graph with vertex set F(ψ) and the edge FF ′ ∈E(G∗) if the facial cycles of
the faces F and F ′ of ψ intersect in an edge. Under our assumptions, G∗is a simple graph [MT01,
Proposition 5.5.11.]. We generally drop the reference to ψ, as in our applications, the embedding is
clear from the context. By a slight abuse of notation, we let V (F) be the vertices of G which are on
the boundary of F.

6. On possible extensions of our results
Improved bounds.
Given Theorem 1.5, it appears quite challenging to improve our bounds without
completely new ideas. Nonetheless, the following conjecture could be true.
Conjecture 6.1. There exists a function f(k) ∈O(k log k) such that every graph G contains
k vertex-disjoint cycles with distinct lengths, or a set X of at most f(k) vertices such that
|Λ(G −X)| ≤k −1.
By Theorems 3.1 and 3.2, Conjecture 6.1 would be simultaneously optimal for both |X| and
|Λ(G −X)|. Moreover, the transformation presented in the proof of Theorem 3.2 shows that
Conjecture 6.1 also implies the classic Erdős-Pósa theorem.
It is also natural to ask if our results can be extended to lengths which are not only distinct but also
satisfy some additional nice properties. This appears quite challenging given the following negative
results.
Consecutive lengths.
Does there exist a function f(k) such that every graph G contains k vertex-
disjoint cycles whose lengths are k consecutive integers or a set X of at most f(k) vertices such that
Λ(G −X) does not contain f(k) consecutive integers? The following simple example shows that the
answer is negative. Let Ca denote the cycle of length a. Given A ⊆N, let GA = S
a∈A Ca, where
there exists a vertex x ∈V (GA) such that V (Ca) ∩V (Cb) = {x} for all distinct a, b ∈A. Let A be
a family of subsets of N such that each A ∈A is a large subset of consecutive integers and A ∪A′ is
not a set of consecutive integers for all distinct A, A′ ∈A. Let GA be the disjoint union of GA, over

other hand if |X| < |A|, then Λ(GA −X) still contains long sequences of consecutive integers (since

Arithmetic Progressions.
What if instead of asking for consecutive lengths, we ask for lengths
which form an arithmetic progression? There are two natural ways to formalise this question,
depending on whether we fix the common difference of the arithmetic progression or not. To be
precise, recall that a d-arithmetic progression is a sequence of integers whose consecutive terms
differ by d. A sequence is an arithmetic progression if it is a d-arithmetic progression for some d.
Fix d ≥1. Does there exist a function f(k) such that every graph G contains k vertex-disjoint
cycles whose lengths form a d-arithmetic progression or a set of at most f(k) vertices X such that
Λ(G −X) does not contain a d-arithmetic progression of size f(k)? Alternatively, we can allow d to
vary. That is, does there exist a function f(k) such that every graph G contains k vertex-disjoint
cycles whose lengths form an arithmetic progression or a set of at most f(k) vertices X such that
Λ(G −X) does not contain any arithmetic progression of size f(k)?
A slight modification of the previous counterexample shows that the answer is negative for both
questions. For the first question, this is straightforward by taking a (d −1)-subdivision of the
previous counterexample. For the second question, we say that a family A of subsets of N is
(p, ℓ)-good if each A ∈A is a p-arithmetic progression of length ℓand every arithmetic progression
of length at least 3 in S A is a subset of A for some A ∈A. We claim that for all p, ℓ∈N, there
exist arbitrarily large (p, ℓ)-good families. To see this, observe that if A is (p, ℓ)-good, then A ∪{A}
is (p, ℓ)-good, where A is a p-arithmetic progression of length ℓwhose smallest element is at least
triple the maximum element of S A. Let A be a large (p, ℓ)-good family, with p arbitrary and ℓ
large. Then the graph GA (as defined above) does not contain three vertex-disjoint cycles whose
lengths form an arithmetic progression, but Λ(GA −X) contains an arithmetic progression of length
ℓfor all X with |X| < |A|.
Prescribed lengths.
Given a subset L ⊆N, do there exist functions fL : N →N and gL : N →N
such that every graph G contains k vertex-disjoint cycles with distinct lengths from L or a set X of
at most fL(k) vertices such that |Λ(G −X) ∩L| ≤gL(k)? In recent work, Gorsky, Hendrey, and
Huynh [GHH26] show that fL and gL do not exist if L is the set of prime numbers. More generally,
they prove that fL and gL do not exist if L is infinite and N \ L contains arbitrarily long sequences
of consecutive integers.

H-minor-free graphs.
In a different direction than cycles, it is natural to ask whether Theorem 4.5
holds more generally for H-minor-free graphs. That is, we leave the following as an open problem.
Question 6.4. Let k, d ∈N+. Let G be a graph forbidding H as a minor and let c be a colouring
of V (G). Do there exist functions f, g depending only on H such that there exist k vertices given
pairwise distinct colours by c and pairwise lie at distance at least d, or there exists a set X ⊆V (G)
of order at most f(k, d) such that after deleting N⌊d/2⌋(X), V (G) has at most g(k) remaining
colours?
It is not too hard to see how to do this “locally” by using Theorem 4.5. Graphs with a forbidden
H-minor have a tree-decomposition into parts such that every part has bounded local treewidth
after deleting a small number of apices (see [Gro03]). Thus, for a single part, we can recolour all
vertices within distance ⌊d/2⌋from the apices by a single special colour and apply Theorem 4.5, and
then we can add these apices to any hitting set. It is unclear how to extend these local solutions
“globally” along the entire tree-decomposition.

A. An algorithmic proof of Theorem 1.3
We now present an algorithmic proof of Theorem 1.3. The key ingredient is the following theorem
of Visser and Bodlaender [VB25], which shows that the cycle spectrum of a graph can be computed
in fixed-parameter time in graphs of treewidth at most k.
Theorem A.1 (Visser and Bodlaender [VB25]). Let ℓ, γ ∈N and let G be a graph with tw(G) ≤ℓ.
Then there is an algorithm that correctly decides if γ ∈Λ(G), which runs in time O(18ℓγ4|V (G)|).
If one does not care about the dependence on ℓ, then Theorem A.1 also follows from a version of
Courcelle’s Theorem [CM93], which can output the set of all possible sizes of a set X satisfying an
MSO-formula φ on bounded treewidth graphs.
We also need to efficiently find cycles of each length in the cycle spectrum of a graph with bounded
treewidth. The algorithm of Visser and Bodlaender [VB25] does not output these cycles, although
it can probably be adapted to do so. However, the following naive approach works, at the cost of a
slightly worse runtime.
Lemma A.2. Let ℓ∈N and let G be a graph with tw(G) ≤ℓ. Suppose γ ∈Λ(G). Then we can
find a cycle of length γ in time O(ℓ18ℓγ5|V (G)|2).
Proof. If |E(G)| = γ, then E(G) is the set of edges of a cycle of length γ, so we are done. Thus, we may
assume that |E(G)| > γ. By applying Theorem A.1 to G −e for γ + 1 arbitrary edges e of G we can
find in time O(18ℓγ4|V (G)|(γ+1)) an edge e ∈E(G) for which γ ∈Λ(G −e). Iterating this |E(G)|−γ
times, we reduce to the case that |E(G)| = γ. Thus, in time O(18ℓγ4|V (G)|(γ + 1)|E(G)|) ⊆
O(ℓ18ℓγ5|V (G)|2) we can find a cycle of length γ in G.
