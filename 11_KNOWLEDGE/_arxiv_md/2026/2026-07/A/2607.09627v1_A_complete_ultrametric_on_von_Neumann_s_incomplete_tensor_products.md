Abstract
We revisit von Neumann’s theory of infinite tensor products of Hilbert
spaces. On the set Γ of equivalence classes of C0-sequences, which labels
the incomplete tensor products inside the complete tensor product, we intro-
duce a natural pseudo-ultrametric d: the distance between two classes is the
convergence exponent of the series P
j |⟨φj, ψj⟩−1| formed from any pair
of representatives. We show that d is well defined on equivalence classes,
satisfies the strong triangle inequality, and is complete. Distinct classes may
lie at distance zero, so d separates points only after passing to the quotient
eΓ of Γ by the relation d = 0; the pair (eΓ, d) is then a complete ultramet-
ric space. As an application, we show that a product unitary N
j U whose
factor U satisfies inf∥x∥=1 |⟨x, Ux⟩−1| > 0 (in particular, a unitary on a fi-
nite dimensional space with 1 /∈σ(U)) displaces every class to the maximal
distance 1. Guided by the intended application – a caricature of Everettian
branching, in which the sectors of the infinite tensor product play the role of
worlds – we also develop a gauge-invariant variant ˜d of the metric, based on
von Neumann’s weak equivalence and matched to the quasi-equivalence of
product states on the quasi-local algebra. The displacement of a class under a
product unitary, measured by ˜d, is class dependent and realizes every value in
[0, 1]. We interpret ˜d as a decoherence exponent: it measures the polynomial
rate at which two branches of the universal state vector become operationally
distinct as ever larger portions of the environment are monitored.

The motivation for these results is a toy mathematical model of the many-
worlds (Everett) interpretation of quantum mechanics [5], described in Section
6: the universe is an infinite tensor product of qubits, its worlds are the sectors
Nc Hj, time evolution proceeds in discrete unitary steps, and conditioned product
unitaries split the universal state vector into branches inhabiting mutually disjoint
worlds. The sector decomposition is a superselection structure relative to the quasi-
local observables (Proposition 6.1), the physically meaningful notion of a world is
a weak equivalence class, and the metric ˜d acquires the interpretation of a deco-
herence exponent: up to subpolynomial corrections, the overlap of two worlds, as
seen by an observer monitoring the first N degrees of freedom of the environment,
decays like exp(−N ˜d). Section 6 also locates the model relative to Hepp’s quasi-
local measurement model [7] and Bell’s critique of it [4], to the failure of strong
continuity for product one-parameter groups [14], which forces the discreteness of
the time steps, and to recent work of Svozil [15] on sectorization as a mechanism
for irreversibility.
One caveat is in order. The definition of d uses the weights j−p and thus presup-
poses a fixed enumeration of the index set; different enumerations lead, in general,
to different values of d (see the remark at the end of Section 3). Accordingly, from
Section 3 on we assume that I = N with its standard enumeration. The material in
Section 2 is valid for index sets of arbitrary cardinality. In the physical reading of
Section 6, the enumeration dependence acquires content: the enumeration encodes
the structure of the environment, and d, ˜d measure rates of decoherence relative to
that structure.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews von
Neumann’s theory and records the weighted transfer lemma used later. Section 3
introduces the pseudo-ultrametric d, proves the completeness theorem, and charac-
terizes the permutations of the index set that leave d invariant. Section 4 develops
the gauge-invariant variant ˜d. Section 5 studies the displacement of classes under
product unitaries, including the qubit and Bell-pair examples. Section 6 presents
the branching model and its interpretation.

6.5
The role of the pseudometric
The metric structure is not merely descriptive of the branching; it organizes it, and
its degeneracies carry physical content.
First, the branching process (33) generates a binary tree of worlds. After n
steps the state is a superposition of 2n branch vectors, labeled by the friend record
ϵ ∈{↑, ↓}n, the branch ϵ inhabiting the sector Uϵc obtained by applying the ap-
propriate product of the step unitaries. The strong triangle inequality of Lemma
4.2(v) forces these 2n leaves to organize into a nested hierarchy of ˜d-balls: two
branches lie in a common ball of radius r precisely when they have not yet sepa-
rated by more than r, and the finer branchings are nested inside the coarser ones.
This hierarchical, dendrogram-like structure is exactly what an ultrametric encodes
– a finite ultrametric space is precisely the leaf space of a weighted rooted tree –
and it is the structural reason that an ultrametric, and not an ordinary metric, is the
natural object here: the space of worlds is the leaf space of the branching tree.
Second, the identity of a world and its metric distance from other worlds are
genuinely different pieces of information, and the difference is where the “pseudo”
in pseudometric resides. The identity of a world is carried by the weak equivalence
relation ∼w; the vanishing of ˜d is strictly coarser. The relation ˜d = 0 is, by the
ultrametric inequality, itself an equivalence relation, and each of its classes may
contain a whole family of pairwise weakly inequivalent – hence disjoint – worlds.
The passage to the quotient metric space of Theorem 4.3, which turns the pseudo-
metric into a genuine metric, therefore identifies physically distinct worlds; it is the
wrong operation for the branching analysis, which must be carried out on the un-
quotiented space. Far from being an artifact to be removed, the degeneracy locus
{ ˜d = 0} is populated by exactly those branch pairs that have genuinely separated
– disjoint states of A, no local recombination – but only at a subpolynomial rate.
In the qubit family of Example 5.5 with sin2(2γj) = j−s, this is the boundary case
s = 1: for s > 1 the branch does not split at all (φ ∼w Uφ), for 0 < s < 1 it
splits at rate ˜d = 1 −s, and exactly at s = 1 it splits into a distinct world with
vanishing exponent. The marginal stratum of the branching transition lies entirely
inside the degeneracy set of ˜d, invisible to the metric yet perfectly visible to the
superselection structure.

Discussion
Three remarks locate this toy model within the literature.
Locality. A local unitary, i.e., one supported on finitely many factors, changes
finitely many components of a C0-sequence and therefore preserves every class:
under strictly local dynamics, the universal state can never leave its sector in finitely
many steps. Rigorous models of measurement built on quasi-local dynamics, such
as Hepp’s [7], achieve disjointness of the branches only in the limit t →∞; Bell’s
critique [4] of the Hepp model turns precisely on the fact that at any finite time
some (global) observable still detects interference between the branches. Our prod-
uct unitaries make the complementary idealization: they achieve exact disjointness
in a single step, at the price of acting on infinitely many factors simultaneously. A
“time step” should accordingly be read as an effective description of an interaction
that has already propagated through the entire environment.

Precedents. Taking the full infinite tensor product seriously as a state space is not
new: it underlies the infinite tensor product extension of loop quantum gravity of
Thiemann and Winkler [16], where the sectors appear as superselected subspaces,
and, recently, Svozil [15] has argued that sectorization of infinite tensor products
by itself provides a mechanism for the emergence of irreversibility and of apparent
state reduction from unitary dynamics, in a framework of nested Wigner’s friends
qualitatively close to the picture sketched here. What the present paper adds to
this circle of ideas is the quantitative layer: the sets of sectors and of worlds are
not mere index sets but complete ultrametric spaces, and the dynamics moves states
through them at definite rates – decoherence exponents – which the metrics d and ˜d
are designed to measure. We do not claim that the model addresses the conceptual
difficulties of the Everett interpretation, such as the preferred basis problem or the
origin of probabilities [18]; it is offered as a mathematically controlled setting in
which the statement “the universe branches into inequivalent worlds” is a theorem
rather than a metaphor.
