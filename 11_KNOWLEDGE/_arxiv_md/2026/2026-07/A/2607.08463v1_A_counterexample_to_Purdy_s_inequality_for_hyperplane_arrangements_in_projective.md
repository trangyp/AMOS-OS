A counterexample to Purdy’s inequality for hyperplane arrangements
in projective three-space
Mateusz Michałek and Piotr Pokora
July 10, 2026
Abstract
We record an explicit counterexample to a refined form of Purdy’s inequality for essential
hyperplane arrangements in projective three-space. Let A be an arrangement of n hyperplanes in
P3
C. Let ℓbe the number of distinct intersection lines of A, and let p be the number of intersection
points, where an intersection point means a point at which at least three hyperplanes meet. The
expected inequality is
p −ℓ+ n + 2 ≥0.
The classical obstruction is the rank 2 + 2 product arrangement, or dually a configuration of
points contained in two skew lines. We explain this obstruction first, and then show that it is
not the only one. The reflection-arrangement search leads naturally to a subarrangement of the
monomial reflection arrangement of type G(3, 3, 4). Looking dually, this configuration is not
contained in two skew lines, and has
f0(S) = 12,
f1(S) = 58,
f2(S) = 43.
Therefore its dual arrangement has
n = 12,
ℓ= 58,
p = 43,
and hence
p −ℓ+ n + 2 = −1.
Thus the refined statement excluding only the two-skew-lines obstruction is false.

Introduction
Let A be an arrangement of n hyperplanes in the complex projective space P3
C. We write ℓ= ℓ(A)
for the number of distinct intersection lines of the arrangement, and p = p(A) for the number
of distinct intersection points, where an intersection point means a point at which at least three
hyperplanes meet. The numerical expression
p(A) −ℓ(A) + n + 2
is the projective-arrangement counterpart of Purdy’s point-line-plane problem in three-dimensional
incidence geometry. In the dual point language it becomes
f2(S) −f1(S) + f0(S) + 2,
where S ⊂(P3
C)∨is the dual point configuration and fi(S) denotes the number of i-dimensional
projective flats spanned by S. For more details please consult Purdy works [7, 8, 9], the survey of
Erdős–Purdy [1], and the later incidence-theoretic work of Lund [5].

## Page 2


A natural motivation for Purdy’s inequality comes from the enumerative geometry of flats
spanned by finite point configurations. This is a classical topic, with well-known results like de
Bruijn and Erdős theorem on the number of lines spanned by points in the plane. It is also a
very active area of research, see e.g. a very recent counterexample to Mason conjecture [4]. In a
setting related to ours, Huh and Wang proved a top-heavy theorem for realizable matroids: if E is
a spanning finite subset of a vector space, then, in the lattice of subspaces spanned by subsets of E,
there are at least as many flats of complementary higher dimension as flats of lower dimension in the
appropriate range [2]. In dimension three this philosophy predicts strong constraints on the possible
numbers of points, lines, and planes spanned by a configuration. Purdy’s proposed inequality may
be viewed as a sharper, low-dimensional numerical refinement of this top-heavy principle: for a
point configuration S ⊂P3, or equivalently for the dual arrangement of hyperplanes, it asks whether
the defect
f2(S) −f1(S) + f0(S) + 2
is always non-negative. A first tempting formulation is that every essential arrangement in P3 should
satisfy
p(A) −ℓ(A) + n + 2 ≥0.
(1)
This is false. The standard counterexample is a rank 2 + 2 product: take a hyperplanes in one
pencil and b hyperplanes in another pencil with skew axis. Dually, this is the configuration of a
points on one line and b points on a skew line. This gives
n = a + b,
ℓ= ab + 2,
p = a + b,
and therefore
p −ℓ+ n + 2 = 2a + 2b −ab,
which is negative whenever (a −2)(b −2) > 4.
This classical obstruction suggests the refined statement obtained by excluding point configu-
rations contained in the union of two skew lines. The purpose of this paper is to record that this
refinement is also false. The counterexample is small, structured, and arises naturally from the
reflection-arrangement search. It is a µ3-balanced K2,2-configuration inside the monomial reflection
arrangement of type G(3, 3, 4). More precisely, let ζ be a primitive third root of unity and consider
S = {[ei −ζaej] | i ∈{0, 1}, j ∈{2, 3}, a ∈Z/3Z} ⊂P3
C.
We prove that
(f0(S), f1(S), f2(S)) = (12, 58, 43).
Consequently, the dual arrangement has
(n, ℓ, p) = (12, 58, 43),
and therefore
p −ℓ+ n + 2 = −1.
Moreover, S is not contained in two skew lines. Thus this example is a counterexample not only to
the naive essential version of Purdy’s inequality, but also to the refined version in which only the
two-skew-lines obstruction is excluded.
The organization is as follows. Section 2 recalls the precise dual dictionary between hyperplane
arrangements and point configurations. Section 3 records the classical two-skew-lines obstruction.
Section 4 explains how the reflection arrangement search leads to the new example: the full
irreducible reflection arrangements satisfy the inequality, but a natural gain-graphic subarrangement
of G(3, 3, 4) does not. Section 5 gives the complete count for the µ3-balanced K2,2-configuration
and proves the counterexample.

Dual formulation
Let S ⊂P3
C be a finite point set. We put
f0(S) = |S|,
f1(S) = #{lines spanned by pairs of points of S},
and
f2(S) = #{planes spanned by triples of non-collinear points of S}.
We also write
∆(S) = f2(S) −f1(S) + f0(S) + 2
and call ∆(S) the Purdy defect of S.
Let
A = {H1, . . . , Hn}
be an arrangement of hyperplanes in P3
C. Let
S = {q1, . . . , qn} ⊂(P3
C)∨
be the dual point configuration, where qi corresponds to the hyperplane Hi.
Proposition 2.1 (Dual dictionary). With the notation above,
n = f0(S),
ℓ(A) = f1(S),
p(A) = f2(S).
Consequently,
p(A) −ℓ(A) + n + 2 = ∆(S).
Moreover, A is essential if and only if S spans the whole dual space (P3
C)∨.
Proof. The equality n = f0(S) is immediate. The intersection line Hi ∩Hj corresponds dually to
the line ⟨qi, qj⟩spanned by the two dual points. Therefore distinct intersection lines of A are in
bijection with spanned lines of S, which gives ℓ(A) = f1(S).
Similarly, a point at which at least three hyperplanes meet corresponds dually to a plane spanned
by at least three non-collinear points of S. This gives p(A) = f2(S). The formula for the defect
follows.
Finally, the arrangement is essential precisely when the defining linear forms span the full dual
vector space. Projectively, this says that the points qi span (P3
C)∨.
Thus Purdy’s inequality for arrangements is equivalent to
∆(S) = f2(S) −f1(S) + |S| + 2 ≥0
for the dual point configuration.

The two-skew-lines obstruction
We recall the standard obstruction. Let
S = A ∪B ⊂P3,
where A ⊂L, B ⊂M, and L, M ⊂P3 are two skew lines. Write
|A| = a,
|B| = b,
and assume a, b ≥2. Then S spans P3.
Proposition 3.1. With the notation above,
f1(S) = ab + 2,
f2(S) = a + b,
and therefore
∆(S) = 2a + 2b −ab.
In particular, ∆(S) < 0 if and only if
(a −2)(b −2) > 4.
Proof. The spanned lines are the two lines L and M, together with the ab joining lines
⟨ai, bj⟩,
ai ∈A, bj ∈B.
Hence f1(S) = ab + 2.
The spanned planes are exactly the planes
⟨L, bj⟩,
bj ∈B,
and
⟨M, ai⟩,
ai ∈A.
Thus f2(S) = a + b. Since |S| = a + b, we get
∆(S) = (a + b) −(ab + 2) + (a + b) + 2 = 2a + 2b −ab.
The final assertion follows by rewriting ab > 2a + 2b as (a −2)(b −2) > 4.
Dually, Proposition 3.1 gives an essential arrangement of planes in P3 violating (1). In coordinates
[x : y : z : w], this arrangement has the form
x −λiy = 0,
i = 1, . . . , a,
together with
z −µjw = 0,
j = 1, . . . , b,
where all λi’s are distinct and all µj’s are distinct. It is an essential rank 2 + 2 product arrangement.
For example (a, b) = (5, 5) gives
n = 10,
ℓ= 27,
p = 10,
p −ℓ+ n + 2 = −5.
This is the classical obstruction in Purdy’s problem.

reflection arrangements satisfy Purdy’s inequality, while the counterexample appears as a natural
subarrangement of an imprimitive reflection arrangement. We refer to Hunt’s PhD thesis [3, Chapter
2] for the numerical data describing reflection arrangements listed below.
