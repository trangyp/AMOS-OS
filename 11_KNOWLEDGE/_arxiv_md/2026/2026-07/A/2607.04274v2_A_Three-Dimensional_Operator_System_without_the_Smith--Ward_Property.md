A Three-Dimensional Operator System without the
Smith–Ward Property
Marcel Scherer
July 14, 2026
Abstract
Harris recently showed that a non-liftable injective representation into the Calkin
algebra gives explicit four-dimensional operator systems in the Calkin algebra without
the lifting property, and hence a counterexamples to the generalized Smith–Ward
problem for four-dimensional operator systems. The main obstruction also appears
in an earlier work by Paulsen on this problem. We isolate the relevant part of this
argument and replace the four-dimensional operator system by a three-dimensional
hyperrigid operator system inside a matrix amplification of
C∗
r (F2).
The resulting operator system in the Calkin algebra is of the form span{1, q(D), q(K)},
where D and K are selfadjoint operators, and the identity map on this operator system
has no unital completely positive lift.
Equivalently, the operator D + iK gives a
counterexample to the Smith–Ward problem.
By a result of Kavruk, the dual of this operator system fails to be exact, and hence is
the first example of a three-dimensional operator system that is not exact.

Introduction
Let H be a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and let
q : B(H) −→Q(H) := B(H)/K(H)
be the quotient map onto the Calkin algebra. The Smith–Ward theorem says that, for
an operator T ∈B(H) and a fixed N ∈N, the first N essential matrix ranges of T can
be realized exactly as the first N matrix ranges of a compact perturbation of T [10].
The classical Smith–Ward problem asks whether this compact perturbation can be chosen
independently of N.
As shown in [8], the Smith–Ward problem is equivalent to the lifting problem for three-
dimensional operator systems of the form
span{q(I), q(T), q(T)∗} ⊆Q(H),
that is, whether the identity map on this operator system has a u.c.p. lift to B(H). More
generally, for an operator system S ⊂B(H), the generalized Smith–Ward problem asks

In [8], it was shown that there exists a 5-dimensional operator system in Q(H) without the
lifting property, and this was improved to a 4-dimensional operator system in [7]. Harris
gives an explicit four-dimensional example: from a non-liftable injective representation
into the Calkin algebra, one obtains a four-dimensional operator subsystem of a matrix
amplification whose identity map has no u.c.p. lift, see [6, Theorem 2 and Corollary 9]. His
construction, as well as the construction in [8, Theorem 3.3], is based on a multiplicative-
domain argument.
The purpose of this note is to replace Harris’ four-dimensional operator system by a three-
dimensional one. The new point is to construct a hyperrigid operator system
S = span{1, D, K} ⊆M4(C∗
r (F2)),
where D is a diagonal selfadjoint matrix with four distinct eigenvalues and K is a selfad-
joint matrix whose non-zero entries are labelled by the two canonical unitaries of C∗
r (F2)
and by the unit.
Although Arveson’s original definition of hyperrigidity in [12] is phrased in terms of ap-
proximation by u.c.p.
maps, we use the equivalent unique-extension formulation: for
every unital representation π of C∗(S), the restriction π|S has a unique u.c.p. extension
to C∗(S), namely π itself. In this situation, one says that π|S has the unique extension
property. The proof that S is hyperrigid starts by taking a u.c.p. extension of π|S to C∗(S)
and dilating the extension via Stinespring to a unital representation ρ of C∗(S). Then we
will show that the dilation ρ|S of π|S is already trivial, meaning that if π : S →B(H),
ρ : S →B(L) and V : H →L is an isometry with
π(s) = V ∗ρ(s)V,
s ∈S
then V π(s) = ρ(s)V for all s ∈S. See [13] for more details on the relation between unique
extensions and trivial dilations.
Combining this three-dimensional hyperrigid operator system with Harris’ obstruction to
the Smith–Ward property gives the following result.
Theorem 4.2. There exists an operator T ∈B(H) such that no compact operator L ∈
K(H) satisfies
W(T + L) = W(q(T)),
