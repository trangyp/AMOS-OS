Abstract
We use linear programming to prove a separable version of Ky Fan’s majorization relation
for a sum of two operators that are each a tensor product of n positive semidefinite operators.
We give an example showing that such a relation does not hold in general for sums of three or
more tensor products of three or more positive semidefinite operators.

Introduction
Majorization relations are immensely useful for proving inequalities.
They are also rare; Robin
Hood [5] is elusive, understandably. So it is exciting when a new majorization relation is found.
We say that a vector x ∈Rd majorizes a vector y ∈Rd if for each k ∈{1, . . . , d} \ {d} the sum
of the k largest coordinates of x is greater than or equal to the sum of the k largest coordinates
of y, and the two vectors satisfy Pd
i=1 xi = Pd
i=1 yi. This relation, which we denote by y ⪯x, is
equivalent to the existence of probabilities p1, . . . , pm and permutation matrices S1, . . . , Sm satisfying
Pm
i=1 piSix = y. It is also equivalent to the statement that the value of every symmetric concave
function, entropy for example, at x is less than or equal to its value at y [6].
Majorization can be extended to self-adjoint operators via their eigenvalues. We denote the eigen-
values of a self-adjoint operator X on Cd by λ1(X) ≥· · · ≥λd(X) and define λ(X) := (λi(X))d
i=1.
For two such operators X and Y , we say X majorizes Y whenever λ(Y ) ⪯λ(X). In analogy with
vectors in Rd, such a relation is equivalent to the existence of probabilities p1, . . . , pm and unitary
operators U1, . . . , Um satisfying Pm
i=1 piUiXU∗
i = Y . Analogously as well, X majorizes Y if and
only if the value of every unitarily invariant concave function, quantum entropy for example, at X
is less than or equal to its value at Y [4].
