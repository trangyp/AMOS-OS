The TRIUMF UltraCold Advanced Neutron (TUCAN) collaboration has completed a new su-
perthermal source for ultracold neutrons (UCNs) at TRIUMF. It uses neutrons from a spallation
target driven by TRIUMF’s main cyclotron. Heavy water and liquid deuterium serve as neutron
moderators, and inelastic scattering inside superfluid 4He at around 1.1 K slows the neutrons down
to become ultracold. During commissioning runs with the completed source, including the deuterium
moderator, up to 1.34(1) × 107 UCNs were detected in the experimental area after irradiating the
target and accumulating UCNs in the source for 60 s. Up to 6.75(3) × 105 UCN/s were detected
during continuous operation, more than at any other source in the world.
Keywords: Fundamental particle physics, ultracold neutrons, spallation
Introduction— Ultracold neutrons (UCNs) are free, un-
bound neutrons with extremely low kinetic energies (typ-
ically below 300 neV) and correspondingly velocities <
8 m s−1. Their defining property is that they reflect off
certain material surfaces at any angle of incidence, al-
lowing them to be physically trapped in “bottles” for
hundreds of seconds. This long observation time allows
exceptionally precise measurements of the neutron’s fun-
damental properties, such as the neutron lifetime [1, 2],
its electric dipole moment (EDM) [3–5], β-decay correla-
tion parameters [6] and investigations of quantum states
of neutrons in the Earth’s gravitational field [7, 8]. To-
gether, they provide fundamental tests of the Standard
Model of particle physics and cosmology.
These experiments are, in many cases, limited by sta-
tistical precision, mainly due to the low achievable phase-
space density.
Statistical uncertainties scale inversely
with the number of detected UCNs and their survival
probability during storage, transport, and measurement.
As a result, increased UCN production rates and reduced
losses are key drivers for improved sensitivity across these
experiments, as well as improved studies of systematic ef-
fects.
For decades, the UCN source at the Institut Laue-
Langevin (ILL) provided the highest UCN densities to
experiments [9]. It Doppler-shifts neutrons from the cold

source of the research reactor [10] by reflecting them from

The neutrons lose a significant portion of their kinetic en-
ergy and become ultracold. Recently, superthermal UCN
production has been proven to surpass Doppler shifters:
The mechanism relies on the inelastic down-scattering of
incident cold neutrons to the UCN regime through the
coherent emission of collective excitations (e.g., phonons
or rotons) within a cryogenic converter. The reverse pro-
cess (upscattering) is suppressed by a low density of ex-
citations at low temperatures.
Several superthermal UCN sources are currently oper-
ational: The Paul Scherrer Institute (PSI) and the Los
Alamos National Lab (LANL) employ spallation-driven
neutron sources utilizing solid ortho-deuterium (sD2) as
converter [11, 12]. At the Mainz UCN source an sD2 con-
verter is coupled to a pulsed TRIGA reactor [13]. The Su-
perSUN project uses a superfluid 4He (He-II) source in a
reactor beamline for cold neutrons at ILL [14]. The TRI-
UMF UltraCold Advanced Neutron (TUCAN) source,
the subject of this paper, employs a He-II converter di-
rectly coupled to a spallation target. Although the UCN
production cross section is larger in sD2 [15–17] than in
He-II [18], UCN lifetimes in sD2 are limited by upscat-
tering and material effects (order ms) [19–21]. In He-II,
losses are dominated by phonon upscattering and pre-
dominantly scale with temperature as T 7 [22–24]; below
1 K, storage lifetimes exceeding 100 s have been demon-
strated [24].
The TUCAN He-II source utilizes a proton beam to
drive its dedicated spallation target [25]. H−ions are in-
jected and accelerated in the TRIUMF cyclotron; a thin
carbon foil strips their two electrons, and 483 MeV pro-
tons are extracted into beamline 1 (BL1). A fast kicker
magnet [26] diverts a fraction of the proton pulses onto
the tungsten neutron-spallation target in beamline 1U
(BL1U) [27]. Heavy water (D2O) and liquid deuterium
(LD2) moderators increase the cold neutron flux inside
the volume where the UCN are produced. We have previ-
ously reported results from the partially completed TU-
CAN source without the LD2 moderator [28]. This letter
contains results of source characterization with a filled
LD2 moderator vessel, and with detection moved to the
experimental area in the Meson Hall, outside the radia-
tion shielding.

spallation in the water-cooled tungsten (W) target [27]

at 25(1) K moderate neutrons as shown in Fig. 1. Both
are held in aluminum vessels. A graphite layer, approxi-
mately 10 cm thick, surrounds the moderator region and
acts as a neutron reflector. The central volume contains
the He-II at 1.1(2) K, which converts cold neutrons to
ultracold neutrons. Its aluminum vessel is coated with

UCN production and storage in the source— We used
a very similar UCN production cycle as in previous ex-
periments [28, 33], which we denote batch production:
the two gate valves shown in Fig. 1 were closed while
the spallation target was irradiated with proton currents
between 1 and 33 µA. The design current of 40 µA could
not be reached for most of the experiments due to limita-
tions of the current control firmware of the kicker magnet.

With higher beam currents, the
UCN production increases, but the production volume
also receives more heat from the spallation target, lead-
ing to higher He-II temperatures. Monte Carlo simula-
tions [29] predict a production rate of 2.7 × 105 s−1 µA−1
and a heat load of 0.167 W µA−1. Since UCN upscatter-
ing scales as T 7, shorter storage lifetimes of UCNs in the
source are expected. Therefore, we anticipate the depen-
dence of the UCN counts as a function of beam current
to deviate from linearity at higher currents. However, as
reported in [28], a nearly linear correlation is observed:
trend lines in Fig. 2 using only the data points up to
5 µA and extrapolated to higher currents indicate basi-
cally no deviation at high currents. The highest number
of neutrons measured outside of the radiation shielding
for batch production was 1.34(1) × 107 for a current of
26.6(3) µA. With a source volume of 88.6 L up to gate
valve 1, this amounts to a density of 151(2) cm−3 in the
source that can be extracted and detected.
A comparison of the linear fit constants shows that
the UCN counts increased by a factor of 19.9(2) between
production runs without deuterium, where UCN are de-
tected inside the radiation shielding (green data points,
from [28]), and production runs with deuterium, where
UCN are detected outside the radiation shielding and the
path length increases by around 2.5 m (red data points).
Adding the Ti foil reduced the UCN counts to 38.38(2)%
(red→blue).

neutron scattering kernel specifically designed for He-II is
employed [35]. UCN production in the converter volume

from [23, 36]. Fig. 4 shows the UCN count data as a func-

Storage outside the shielding— We have also delivered
(unpolarized) UCN to a full-size prototype EDM cell
(volume 25.4 L) in the experimental area and detected
4.207(3) × 106 and 7.21(1) × 105 UCN after storing them
for 0 s and 170(1) s, respectively, after background sub-
traction. This corresponds to densities of 165.6(1) and
28.40(4) UCN/cm3. Filling optimization and storage life-
time experiments in this cell will be described in a future
publication.
Summary and outlook— With all major source sys-
tems now in operation, including the LD2 moderator, the
TUCAN source has demonstrated unprecedented UCN
yields delivered to the experimental area. The measure-
ments reported here establish the performance of the
source during its first full-system operation, while leaving
room for further gains through optimization of the sys-
tems. Up to 1.34(1) × 107 neutrons were detected after
irradiating the spallation target with a proton current of
26.6(3) µA for 60 s while keeping the UCN valve closed
and then immediately opening it to the UCN detector
(batch production). The UCN yield increases by a factor
of 28.73(28) with LD2 moderator. UCN count rates as
high as 6.75(3) × 105 UCN/s were demonstrated in the
experimental area for continuous UCN production with
UCN valves open. For both batch and continuous UCN
production, the yield increases nearly linearly with higher
proton currents. UCN counts of up to 2 × 106 for batch
production and 1 × 106 UCN per second are achievable
once we begin operating the TUCAN source at the nom-
inal current of 40 µA. Filling the heavy water moderator
and the UCN production vessel completely is expected to
further increase the UCN production. This opens up new
possibilities to increase sensitivities for ultracold neutron
experiments.

UCN counts and count rates— UCN counts in the
lithium-glass detector are isolated from electronic noise
and γ-ray backgrounds using pulse shape discrimination
techniques detailed in Ref. [39]. For batch production and
storage lifetime experiments, residual backgrounds are
quantified and subtracted using dedicated closed-valve
cycles as baselines. Time-varying backgrounds (e.g ac-
tivation) are negligible, uncertainties are evaluated via
Poisson statistics and Gaussian error propagation. Con-
tinuous UCN production and detection experiments ex-

riods (inset, Fig. 5). The fast component reflects UCN
drainage through the guide system, while the slow com-

detector:

Beamline 1U current— The proton current injected
into beamline 1 is derived from the electron current mea-
sured at the cyclotron’s extraction foil and foil holder,
where a fraction of the two electrons stripped from the
circulating H−ions are captured.
This extraction foil
system is calibrated annually against a toroidal, non-
intercepting current monitor (TNIM) located in beam-
line 1 [41, 42]. The TNIM is itself calibrated annually
using an integrated, in-situ calibration wire to inject a
reference current.
Through this calibration chain, the
overall relative uncertainty of the primary current mea-
surement is estimated to be approximately 1%. To feed
beamline 1U (BL1U), a fast kicker diverts exactly one
out of an adjustable number of proton pulses from the
main cyclotron beam [26] away from beamline 1. The
resulting BL1U current is calculated by multiplying the
primary beamline 1 current by this known kick fraction.
These values are recorded at a 1 Hz sampling rate; aver-
aging over the irradiation period of the neutron spallation
target yields the final BL1U current, with the standard
deviation of the dataset defining the experimental error
bars.
Fill volume of deuterium moderator vessel— Severe ra-
diation and spatial constraints preclude direct level mea-
surements within the liquid deuterium moderator ves-
sel.
We use a multi-step process to determine the fill
volume and level from pressures and temperatures. (1)
The mass of deuterium recovered into the storage tank,
∆m, is determined from thermo-physical properties using
CoolProp [43] as ∆m = m(∆Ptank, Ttank, Vtank), where
∆Ptank is the pressure change during recovery, and Ttank
and Vtank are the tank temperature and volume, respec-
tively.
(2) The remaining liquid volume is then com-
puted as VLD2 = Vvessel −∆V (Psat, ∆m), where Vvessel
is the calibrated vessel volume, and Psat the saturated
vapor pressure measured above the cryostat determines
the liquid density. (3) Since Ttank is inferred from local
meteorological data, it introduces significant uncertainty.
To quantify this, we correlate tank pressure and temper-
ature fluctuations during a benchmark period of constant
deuterium content post-UCN data acquisition. The re-
sulting uncertainty is bounded by the maximum observed
pressure spread during ambient temperature shifts equiv-
alent to those experienced during the recovery phase.
