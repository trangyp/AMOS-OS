Since the inception of calculus, infinitesimals have been a source of both power
and paradox. Newton and Leibniz freely used them, treating an infinitesimal
h as nonzero when dividing by it, yet zero when discarding it at the end. The
classic computation of the derivative of f(x) = ax2 + bx + c illustrates this:

An explicit field constructed from real sequences offers full transparency:
every object is a concrete sequence, every operation is performed component-
wise or via convolution, and every property can be verified without appealing
to set-theoretic principles beyond ordinary real analysis. This constructive ap-
proach not only yields a computationally tractable model but also reveals a
rich hierarchy of continuity notions, such as (k, n)-continuity, that are invisible
under the classical continuum or under transfer-based hyperreals.

sequences with a finite tail of infinities and a countable tail of infinitesimals. We

axioms, and then using the Chunk and Permeate (C&P) reasoning strategy [2] to
manage the resulting inconsistencies. The C&P approach partitions the theory
into consistent chunks and allows selective information to flow between them,
yielding a concrete, computable model of an infinitesimal-enriched field without
ultrafilters or heavy model theory.

Background: From Transfer Principle to Chunk
and Permeate
We work with a first-order language L containing symbols for every real con-
stant, every real-valued function, and every relation on R. The real line R is the
standard model of L. The hyperreal system ∗R is another model, obtained as an
ultrapower of R modulo a free ultrafilter on N, which extends R with infinites-
imal and infinite elements. Relations and functions are extended pointwise via
the ultrafilter, and the transfer principle guarantees that a first-order sentence
holds in R iff it holds in ∗R.
The transfer principle, however, has serious drawbacks, e.g.
it is non-
constructive (relying on the Axiom of Choice) and computationally opaque.
Moreover, the cost-benefit analysis is poor. One must erect a substantial model
theoretic apparatus to transfer relatively few theorems. These issues have led
to a search for alternative, more elementary treatments of infinitesimals.
A natural idea is to simply take the union of the axioms of R and those of
∗R. This yields an inconsistent theory (e.g. the set of infinitesimals would be
nonempty and bounded above but without a supremum) yet interesting math-
ematics can still be extracted if we manage the inconsistencies carefully. To
handle these inconsistencies we need a logical strategy that tolerates local con-
tradictions without collapsing the whole system. To do so, we adopt the Chunk
and Permeate (C&P) strategy [2], a reasoning method rooted in paraconsistent
logic. A logic is paraconsistent if the explosion rule A, ¬A ⊢B is not generally
valid. Some prominent examples are the Logic of Paradox LP [10] and relevance
logic R [7]. C&P works by splitting an inconsistent theory into several consis-
tent chunks, defining a permeability relation that allows certain formulas to pass
from one chunk to another, and reasoning classically within each chunk. This
prevents global explosion while allowing useful information to cross between
chunks. Consequently, the combined theory becomes a non-trivial inconsistent
theory in which contradictions are localised, i.e. only formulas that permeate
via the relation ρ can be deduced globally.
In our setting, we divide the combined theory of R and ∗R into two chunks:
source chunk ΣS that contains the field axioms, order axioms, and the exis-
tence of a positive infinitesimal, and target chunk ΣT that contains the field
axioms, order axioms, completeness, and the Archimedean property.
The target chunk is modelled by the real numbers R. For the source chunk
we construct an explicit model RZ<, to which we now turn. Since each chunk
is modelled by a set-theoretic structure, both chunks are consistent (relative to
the consistency of ordinary real analysis). Consequently, the combined theory,
although inconsistent when all axioms are taken together, does not trivialise
under the C&P strategy.

Extreme Value, Intermediate Value, and Continuity
We recall the definitions of the extreme value property (EVP) and the inter-
mediate value property (IVP) for functions with values in an ordered field. In
classical real analysis, the ϵ-δ definition of continuity (ED) implies both EVP
and IVP. In RZ< the situation is different, i.e. the valuation topology is totally
disconnected and closed bounded intervals are not compact, so the classical
proofs do not carry over. For instance, the indicator of the infinitesimals, 1∆,
is continuous (ED) but fails both EVP and IVP (it takes only 0 and 1 on [0, 1]
and jumps). Conversely, f(x) = 1/x on (0, 1] satisfies IVP but not EVP. Hence
ED, EVP, and IVP are pairwise independent in RZ<. Figure 3 summarises the

A real number is computable if its binary expansion can be output by a Turing
machine. We extend this to RZ<. An element z is computable if there exists a
computable map f : Z →Rc (the computable reals) such that zn = f(n) for

9
Discussion and Further Work
We have constructed a non-Archimedean ordered field RZ< containing real num-
bers, infinitesimals, and infinities, using only sequences of real numbers and a
convolution product. The logical foundation via Chunk & Permeate resolves the
inconsistencies that arise when merging classical and infinitesimal axioms, with-
out the heavy model-theoretic machinery of traditional nonstandard analysis.
Because the C&P strategy is explicitly formulated in terms of paraconsistent
logic, the entire construction can be seen as a model-building technique for
inconsistent theories, of independent interest to logicians.
The two topologies, valuation and standard, offer complementary views.
The (k, n)-continuity hierarchy, expressed purely in terms of the valuation, is
a fine-grained measure of regularity that generalises Lipschitz conditions and
captures differentiability orders. The Riemann integral and the Fundamental
Theorem of Calculus demonstrate that basic analysis can be carried out within
the model and then permeated to classical results, avoiding the full transfer
principle.
Several directions for future research are apparent: differential equations
via formal series, calibration of proof-theoretic strength in reverse mathematics,
applications in physics, and a precise comparison between C&P permeability
and the transfer principle.
