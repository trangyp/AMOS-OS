Anisotropic vacancy-induced magnetization textures in altermagnets
Ruben Burkard,1 Mathias S. Scheurer,2 and Urban F. P. Seifert1
1Institut für Theoretische Physik, Universität Köln, Zülpicher Straße 77a, 50937 Köln, Germany
2Institute for Theoretical Physics III, University of Stuttgart, 70550 Stuttgart, Germany
(Dated: July 10, 2026)
We study magnetic textures induced by vacancies in altermagnets using microscopic simulations
and low-energy field theory. We show that a vacancy generically produces a real-space anisotropic
distortion of the magnetic order, whose structure encodes the symmetry of the underlying alter-
magnetic state. This impurity response offers a direct route to detecting altermagnetic order with
locally resolved probes. We demonstrate this for both classical altermagnets, where vacancies gen-
erate anisotropic magnetization textures in a transverse magnetic field, and quantum models, where
fluctuations induce longitudinal power-law decaying magnetic distortions even at zero field.
Introduction.—Altermagnetism has emerged as a uni-
fying theme for unconventional magnets featuring time-
reversal-symmetry broken spectra and response func-
tions while the net magnetization vanishes by symme-
try [1, 2]. They not only hold promise for future spin-
tronics applications but the interplay of magnetic, or-
bital and electronic degrees of freedom has also attracted
strong interest into altermagnets as strongly-correlated
systems [3, 4].
Key to the definition of an altermagnetic phase is a
symmetry of joint spin rotations/time-reversal symme-
try and crystal symmetry operations which relate mag-
netic sublattices other than translation and/or inversion.
As a result, spatial multipoles of the uniform magneti-
zation (spin density) become finite and act as secondary
order parameters distinguishing altermagnets from con-
ventional antiferromagnets [5–7].
In itinerant systems,
this leads to momentum-dependent spin splittings, e.g.
with a d-wave form factor, in the electronic spectrum
[1, 2, 8–10].
Significant theoretical and experimental progress has
been made in identifying signatures of altermagnetism,
foremost spectroscopic [11–15] and transport probes
[9, 16–20], which are sensitive to the spin-dependent spa-
tial anisotropy of excitations (electrons or magnons) in
the altermagnetic state. In contrast, in this Letter, we
show that in altermagnets, non-magnetic impurities lead
to distortions in the ordering of local moments which di-
rectly resolve the altermagnetic order itself and could be
probed using nanoscale techniques such as spin-polarized
scanning tunneling spectroscopy [21], NV magnetometry
[22] or x-ray microscopy methods, which have recently
been used to image order parameter defects in alter-
magnets [23, 24]. Indeed, probing the response to im-
purities is a powerful method to investigate a system’s
ground state and its correlations [25].
This includes
vacancy-induced staggered magnetizations near vacan-
cies in quantum antiferromagnets [26, 27], universal scal-
ing forms for impurity-induced textures and dynamics
[28–30] and long-ranged distortions of non-collinear mag-
netic order [31–33].

Symmetry considerations.—An ideal altermagnet (in
the limit of zero spin-orbit coupling) is a state of collinear
local moments where the magnetic sublattices are re-
lated by some point-group operation g, but not trans-
lation or inversion [1, 6]. Thus, Θg is a symmetry of the
altermagnet, with Θ denoting time-reversal.
As a va-
cancy on one magnetic sublattice breaks Θg, we deduce
that (i) a finite magnetization near the vacancy becomes
symmetry-allowed, and (ii) vacancy-induced distortions
of the magnetic order will generally be anisotropic: by
definition, g is not in the site-symmetry group and thus a
single vacancy can not be a symmetric perturbation. We
contrast this with conventional antiferromagnetic order,
which allows scenarios where a vacancy can be placed on
a magnetic site which is invariant under all point group
operations; then, the resulting distortions are fully sym-
metric, as is the case for the square lattice antiferromag-
net [48, 49].
One may characterize altermagnets in terms of a Lan-
dau theory for the staggered magnetization N and the
uniform magnetization M which transform in a non-
trivial (ΓN) and the trivial (Γ1) irreducible representa-
tion (IR) of the point group, respectively [5–7, 50]. Cru-
cially, in the presence of a vacancy, the system is no longer
homogeneous and symmetry-allowed contributions to the
free energy of the form
FAM[N, M] ∼M · D[∂x, ∂y, ∂z]N,
(1)
become important.
Here, D[∂x, ∂y, ∂z] is a derivative
operator that transforms in the same IR of the point
group as N, for example D[∂x, ∂y] = 2∂x∂y for dxy-wave
altermagnets where N transforms, for example, in the
B2 IR of the C4v point group.
Hence, spatial varia-
tions of N can induce non-trivial magnetization textures,
M(r) ∼D[∂x, ∂y, ∂z]N(r), as also observed in altermag-
netic domain walls [43, 51].

Conclusion.—We have shown that the magnetization
texture around a single vacancy in a collinear altermag-
net inherits the symmetry of the altermagnetic order pa-
rameter, both for classical moments and for quantum
spins.
We have demonstrated this explicitly for real-
izations of d-wave altermagnets and provided a general
symmetry-based argument that applies to altermagnets
more broadly. Several directions for future work remain
open: this includes the study of a finite density of va-
cancies and effective interactions between the resulting
impurity-induced textures. Beyond the static response
considered here, it would also be worthwhile to study the
dynamical response associated with quantum impurities.
