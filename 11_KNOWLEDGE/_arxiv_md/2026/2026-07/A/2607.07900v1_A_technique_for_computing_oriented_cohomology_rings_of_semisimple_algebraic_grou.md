A TECHNIQUE FOR COMPUTING ORIENTED COHOMOLOGY RINGS
OF SEMISIMPLE ALGEBRAIC GROUPS
RAJ GANDHI
Abstract. We present a technique for computing a finite set of generators and relations for the ring h∗(G)
in terms of formal Demazure operators, where h∗is an oriented cohomology theory satisfying the localization
axiom and G is a semisimple algebraic group. Using this technique, we give minimal presentations for the
oriented cohomology rings of the adjoint and simply-connected groups of types A1, A2, and B2.

1. Introduction
The Chow ring of a linear algebraic group is a very well-studied invariant.
Noteworthy examples of
computations of these Chow rings include work of Grothendieck [Gro58], who showed that the integral Chow
rings of the complex algebraic groups SLn and Sp2n are isomorphic to Z, and work of Kac [Kac85], who
developed a procedure for calculating the Chow rings of complex reductive algebraic groups with coefficients
in any finite field. The functor that sends a smooth variety to its Chow ring is an example of an (algebraic)
oriented cohomology theory [LM07].
The general study of oriented cohomology rings of linear algebraic
groups is a natural generalization of the study of their Chow rings, though there do not appear to be many
computations of general oriented cohomology rings of linear algebraic groups in the literature. With that
said, partial computations for the algebraic cobordism rings of some simple algebraic groups over C appear
in [Yag05, §5].
In this paper, we describe a complete finite set of generators and relations for the oriented cohomology
rings h∗(G) of all semisimple algebraic groups G over algebraically closed fields of characteristic 0, assuming
that the oriented cohomology theory h∗satisfies the localization axiom (see Definition 6.3.) The presentation
in terms of generators and relations is given in Corollary 7.10. In Algorithm 7.11, we describe a technique
for computing the relations in the ring h∗(G) explicitly. In Section 8, we apply this technique to explicitly
compute all relations in h∗(G), where G is an adjoint or simply-connected group of type A1, A2, or B2; for
these groups, we simplify the relations of Corollary 7.10 by hand to obtain minimal presentations for the
oriented cohomology rings of these adjoint and simply-connected groups, which are displayed in Table 1.
We believe that the techniques used in this paper can be adapted to compute minimal presentations for the
oriented cohomology rings of other semisimple algebraic groups. We will now give a detailed summary of
the techniques used in this paper.

Let h∗be an oriented cohomology theory in the sense of Levine-Morel [LM07]. This is a contravariant
functor from the category of smooth quasi-projective varieties over a field k to the category of commutative,
graded rings satisfying certain axioms. Examples of oriented cohomology theories include the Chow theory
CH∗, Grothendieck’s K0 functor1, and, over a base field of characteristic 0, the universal oriented cohomology
theory called algebraic cobordism Ω∗. Given a vector bundle E →X of rank n on a smooth variety X over
k, there is a set of Chern classes ci(E) ∈hi(X), i ∈{0, . . . , n}. The Chern classes satisfy Quillen’s formula:
given two line bundles L1 and L2 on X, we have
c1(L1 ⊗L2) = F(c1(L1), c1(L2)),
where F is a one-dimensional commutative formal group law over the ring R = h∗(Spec(k)).
Let h∗be an oriented cohomology theory with formal group law F over R = h∗(Spec(k)) satisfying
the localization axiom (Assumption 6.6), and let G be a semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically
closed field k of characteristic 0 with root datum Ψ = (Σ, Λ, Σ∨, Λ∨). Two key tools used in this paper
are the formal group algebra RJΛKF of [CPZ13], which can be viewed as an algebraic substitute for the
torus-equivariant oriented cohomology ring of a point ([CZZ15]), and the subalgebra DF of the algebra
of R-linear endomorphisms of RJΛKF generated by formal Demazure operators and by multiplication by
elements in RJΛKF . We will always assume that RJΛKF satisfies a technical assumption, Assumption 3.8,
which is crucial for the results in Sections 3 and 4 to hold. Fix a Borel subgroup B of G, so that G/B is a
complete flag variety. In [GZ12, Theorem 5.1], Gille-Zainoulline derived a relationship between the oriented
cohomology rings h∗(G) and h∗(G/B) through the first Chern class map c1. The precise relationship is
(1)
h∗(G) ≃h∗(G/B)/ (im (c1|LB)) ,
where c1|LB is the restriction of c1 to the set LB of B-equivariant line bundles on G/B. In [CPZ13], Calm`es-
Petrov-Zainoulline constructed an algebraic model for h∗(G/B) using the augmentation ϵDF of the algebra
DF containing the formal Demazure operators (Section 7).
That is, they showed there is an R-algebra
isomorphism h∗(G/B) ≃(ϵDF )∗, where (ϵDF )∗:= HomR(ϵDF , R) is the R-dual of ϵDF (the R-algebra

Using this
algebraic Chern class map, together with (1), we obtain an algebraic model for h∗(G) in Theorem 7.9. We
then describe this algebraic model in terms of a finite set of generators and relations in Corollary 7.10,
and provide a technique for computing the relations explicitly in Algorithm 7.11. With enough computing
power, a computer can output the list of generators and relations in the ring h∗(G) explicitly, and, from
these relations, one can compute a minimal presentation for h∗(G) in terms of generators and relations by
hand. In Section 8, we perform this calculation for the adjoint and simply-connected algebraic groups of
types A1, A2, and B2.

5. Algebraic groups and homogeneous line bundles
In this section, we recall some facts about algebraic groups and state a key result (Proposition 5.9)
concerning homogeneous line bundles on complete flag varieties.
Fix an algebraically closed field k of characteristic 0. Suppose G is a semisimple algebraic group over
k, and let T be a maximal torus sitting inside a Borel subgroup B of G. The discussion from here up to
Lemma 5.2 closely follows [Hum75]. The set of characters T →Gm, where Gm = Spec(k[x, x−1]), is a lattice
Λ under pointwise multiplication. The set of nonzero weights for the adjoint action of T on the Lie algebra
g of G forms a root system Σ inside the character lattice Λ. Let Λ∨be the lattice dual to Λ, and let Σ∨be
the set of coroots in Λ∨with respect to Σ. The quadruple Ψ = (Σ, Λ, Σ∨, Λ∨) is a semisimple root datum.
Moreover, Ψ is independent of the choice of maximal torus T, and any semisimple root datum gives rise to
a unique semisimple algebraic group G over k with that root datum.
The Weyl group W of G with respect to T is the quotient NG(T)/T, where NG(T) is the normalizer of T
in G, and W is naturally identified with the Weyl group of Ψ. The semisimple algebraic groups associated
with irreducible semisimple root data are called simple. If G is simple and Λ = Λr (resp. Λ = Λw), then the
group G is called adjoint (resp. simply-connected) and we will often denote this group by Gad (resp. Gsc).

Theorem 5.1. (cf. [Hum75, Thm. 32.1]) Let G and G′ be simple algebraic groups over k with isomorphic

6. Oriented cohomology theories
In this section, we will recall the notion of an oriented cohomology theory in the sense of Levine-Morel
[LM07], explain the localization axiom of [Vis19], and review some facts from [CPZ13]. In this section, fix
k, an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.
Let Schk be the category of separated schemes of finite type over k, and let Smk be the full subcategory
of Schk consisting of schemes smooth and quasi-projective over k. An oriented cohomology theory h∗in
the sense of [LM07] is a contravariant functor from Smk to the category of commutative, graded rings that
satisfies certain ‘cohomological-type’ axioms. Given an oriented cohomology theory h∗and a vector bundle
E →X of rank n on a smooth variety X over k, there is a set of Chern classes ci(E) ∈hi(X), i ∈{0, . . . , n}.
In particular, given two line bundles L1 and L2 on X, the first Chern classes satisfy Quillen’s formula:
c1(L1 ⊗L2) = c1(L1) +F c1(L2),
where F is a one-dimensional commutative formal group law over R = h∗(Spec(k)). In particular, every
oriented cohomology theory gives rise to a formal group law. As k has characteristic 0, there is a universal
oriented cohomology theory called algebraic cobordism Ω∗that satisfies the following universal property: for
any oriented cohomology theory h∗, there is a unique morphism of oriented cohomology theories Ω∗→h∗
([LM07, Theorem 1.2.6]). The formal group law corresponding to algebraic cobordism is the universal formal
group law over the Lazard ring R = L.
