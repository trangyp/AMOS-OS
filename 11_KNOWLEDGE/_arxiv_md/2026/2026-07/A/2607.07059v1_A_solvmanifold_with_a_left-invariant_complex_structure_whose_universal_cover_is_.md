A SOLVMANIFOLD WITH A LEFT-INVARIANT COMPLEX STRUCTURE
WHOSE UNIVERSAL COVER IS NOT STEIN
SHUHO KANDA
Abstract. We construct a simply connected solvable Lie group G admitting lattices and a left-
invariant complex structure J such that (G, J) is not Stein. This provides a counterexample to
Hasegawa’s conjecture on the Stein property of simply connected unimodular solvable Lie groups
with left-invariant complex structures.

1. Introduction
1.1. Background. Let G be a simply connected solvable Lie group. If G admits a left-invariant
complex structure J and a lattice Γ, then J naturally descends to the compact manifold Γ\G, and
X = (Γ\G, J) becomes a compact complex manifold. Such a manifold X is called a solvmanifold
with left-invariant complex structure. Solvmanifolds have been actively studied in complex geom-
etry, especially in non-K¨ahler geometry, since they exhibit many unusual phenomena in complex
geometry. When G is nilpotent, X is called a nilmanifold. Although nilmanifolds are easier to
study than general solvmanifolds, many mysteries still remain.
In this paper, we focus on the properties of the universal cover of X. In [Has10], while studying
deformations of complex structures on solvmanifolds, Hasegawa proposed the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.1 ([Has10]). Let G be a 2n-dimensional simply connected unimodular solvable
(nilpotent) Lie group. Then for every left-invariant complex structure J on G, the complex manifold
(G, J) is Stein (biholomorphic to Cn respectively).
Recently, the nilpotent case was settled in [HRSTW26].
That is, if G is nilpotent, then in
the above setting one has (G, J) ≃Cn. The same conclusion does not hold in the solvable case.
For example, Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds, which are higher-dimensional generalizations of Inoue
surfaces, are solvmanifolds with left-invariant complex structures, but their universal covers are of
the form Hs ×Ct, where H denotes the upper half plane. Thus the universal cover need not be Cn.
Conjecture 1.1 asserts, however, that it should still be Stein. Although some evidence supporting
this conjecture had been obtained, for instance in [AT26], essentially no further progress had been
made.
1.2. Result. In this paper, we give a counterexample to Conjecture 1.1. The counterexample
is obtained by putting a different complex structure on the Nakamura manifold [Nak75].
The
Nakamura manifold is the quotient X = Γ\G by a lattice Γ, where G is, up to isomorphism, the
unique non-nilpotent type among 3-dimensional simply connected unimodular complex solvable
Lie groups:
(1.1)
G = C ⋉ρ C2,
ρ(z) = diag(ez, e−z).

With its original complex structure, the universal cover G is of course biholomorphic to C3. How-
ever, we regard G as a 6-dimensional real Lie group and equip it with another left-invariant complex
structure. The precise statement and the explicit description of the complex structure are as fol-
lows.
Theorem 1.2. Let
G = C ⋉ρ C2,
ρ(z) = diag(ez, e−z)
be the simply connected complex solvable Lie group, and let g = C ⋉dρ C2 be its Lie algebra. We
take an R-basis of g as
T = (1, 0, 0),
X = (0, 1, 0),
Y = (0, 0, 1),
T ′ = (i, 0, 0),
X′ = (0, i, 0),
Y ′ = (0, 0, i).
Define a complex structure J on g determined by the complex Lie subalgebra g1,0 ⊂gC given by
g1,0 = SpanC(T −iT ′, X −iX′ + 2i(X′ + T ′), Y −iY ′).
Then the complex manifold G endowed with the left-invariant complex structure induced by J is
not Stein.
The idea of the construction is as follows. In [Sno86], Snow constructed a holomorphic map
Φ : (G, J) →GC/G0,1
for a left-invariant complex structure J on G. Here GC and G0,1 are the Lie groups corresponding
to g ⊗R C and to the (−i)-eigenspace g0,1 of J, respectively. When G is simply connected and
solvable, one can show that GC/G0,1 ≃Cn and that Φ is a covering map onto its image. It follows
from these properties that (G, J) is the universal cover of D = Φ(G) ⊂GC/G0,1 ≃Cn.
When G is equipped with a complex conjugation, we have
GC ≃G × G
and, under this isomorphism, the embedding G ,→GC is given by x 7→(x, x). Thus the problem
becomes whether one can construct g0,1 ⊂gC ≃g ⊕g in such a way that the universal cover of
D = {[(x, x)] ∈GC/G0,1 | x ∈G} ⊂GC/G0,1 ≃Cn
is not Stein. In this paper, by taking G to be the group in (1.1), we construct such an example
for which D ≃(C2\R2) × C. This domain is not Stein, and we also show that its universal cover
is not Stein.

Remark 3.3. In [Has10], it is concluded that, for the Lie group G defined by (1.1), every left-
invariant complex structure J on G makes (G, J) biholomorphic to C3. The example constructed
here appears to provide a counterexample to this conclusion. More precisely, it seems to correspond
to a case which is not covered in the analysis of the possible structures of J in [Has10].
Now, although we have explicitly constructed (G, J), the structure of J was given under the
identification gC = g ⊕g. We describe this explicitly in terms of gC = g ⊗C.
The complex Lie algebra g has a C-basis T, X, Y , while as an R-basis one may take T, X, Y, T ′, X′, Y ′.
Here T ′, X′, Y ′ denote the i-multiples of T, X, Y , respectively. Let Jst denote the natural complex
structure on g viewed as a complex Lie algebra. Then the i-eigenspace of Jst in gC is
g1,0
Jst = SpanC(T −iT ′, X −iX′, Y −iY ′).

Theorem 3.4. Let g = C ⋉dρ C2. Define a complex structure J on g determined by the complex
Lie subalgebra g1,0 ⊂gC given by
g1,0 = SpanC(T −iT ′, X −iX′ + 2i(X′ + T ′), Y −iY ′).
Then the complex manifold G endowed with the left-invariant complex structure induced by J is
not Stein.
