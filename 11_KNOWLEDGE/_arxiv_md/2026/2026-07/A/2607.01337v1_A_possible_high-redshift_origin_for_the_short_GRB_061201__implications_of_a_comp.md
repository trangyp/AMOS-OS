A possible high-redshift origin for the short GRB 061201:

ABSTRACT
Short gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) at redshift z ≳2 remain exceptionally rare, yet they are crucial for
tracing compact binary mergers in the early Universe and understanding their role in the production of
r-process elements. GRB 061201 is an unusual and still debated event: although its optical afterglow
was accurately localized, no secure coincident host galaxy was identified, and the proposed associations
with nearby galaxies all require a large separation between the GRB and its birth site. In this work,
we revisit GRB 061201 and argue that the observations are more naturally explained if the burst
occurred within a faint F322W2 ∼28.4 AB mag galaxy at z ≳2. By combining constraints from the
afterglow and deep near-infrared imaging from JWST, we show that a distant origin (z ≈3 – 4) provides
a coherent explanation of the burst phenomenology. If confirmed, GRB 061201 would represent one of
the most distant short GRBs known, extending the observed compact merger population to an epoch
when the Universe was only about two billion years old.

1. INTRODUCTION
Mergers of two neutron stars (NSs) are loud sources
of gravitational waves (GWs; B. P. Abbott et al. 2017a,
2020), progenitors of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs; D. Eich-
ler et al. 1989; B. Paczynski 1991; C. L. Fryer et al.
1999; L. Rezzolla et al. 2011; B. P. Abbott et al. 2017b)
and the only confirmed cosmic source of heavy r-process
metals (e.g. E. Symbalisty & D. N. Schramm 1982; C.
Freiburghaus et al. 1999; E. Pian et al. 2017; D. Kasen
et al. 2017; N. Domoto et al. 2022; K. Hotokezaka et al.
2023; Y.-H. Yang et al. 2024). However, their poorly
constrained age distribution does not allow us to under-
stand their role in the chemical evolution of our uni-
verse (P. Beniamini et al. 2016; B. Cˆot´e et al. 2019; C.
Kobayashi et al. 2020; J. J. Cowan et al. 2021).
To establish whether compact binary mergers are one
of the main, if not the primary, sources of heavy r-
process elements, it is necessary to estimate how com-
mon they are across cosmic time. A prompt channel of
mergers, characterized by short (≲100 Myr) lifetimes, is
a prediction of stellar evolution models (K. Belczynski
et al. 2006; P. Beniamini et al. 2016; T. M. Tauris et al.
2017; P. Beniamini & T. Piran 2019; J. J. Andrews & A.
Zezas 2019); however, its contribution relative to the tra-
ditional channel of delayed mergers with Gyr lifetimes is
not well constrained. Binary stellar evolution involves
many uncertain physical parameters, such as multiple
phases of mass transfer, mass loss, common envelopes,
and tidal interactions (M. Dominik et al. 2012; K. A.
Postnov & L. R. Yungelson 2014; M. U. Kruckow et al.
2018). All these factors may affect the initial orbital sep-
aration of the two NSs; thus, their lifetime as a compact
binary: the tighter the orbit, the shorter the inspiral
time. Population synthesis models predict a wide range
of possible inspiral times depending on the details of
the simulation, from ≈10 Myr (e.g. J. J. Andrews et al.
2020; P. Beniamini & T. Piran 2024) to ≳10 Gyr (e.g.
A. Vigna-G´omez et al. 2018). These uncertainties trans-
late into a large uncertainty in the theoretical redshift
distribution of NS mergers, especially at redshifts z >2
where few observational constraints currently exist.
The primary channel to pinpoint these mergers at cos-
mological distances are short GRBs and their afterglows.
In the past two decades, thanks to the Neil Gehrels Swift
satellite (hereafter Swift, N. Gehrels et al. 2004) and its
rapid localizations, great strides have been made in de-

galaxy z ≳1.7 (G. Stratta et al. 2007).

was harbored in a faint galaxy (hereafter G3) underly-

localized to date. Our analysis of the Swift Ultravio-
let Optical Telescope (UVOT; P. W. A. Roming et al.
2005) data shows that this interpretation is not excluded
by the UV observations, which provide only weak con-
straints on the distance scale.

of the Swift UVOT dataset, and present new constraints
from near-infrared imaging of the field; in Sect. 3 we re-

2.1. Swift/UVOT
UVOT began settled observations of the field at T0 +
86 s and imaged the field in all six optical and ultraviolet
filters. A detection in the broad white (wh) filter was
reported, along with low-significance signals in the UV
and optical u filters. The marginal detection in the bluer
uvw2 filter was used to set an upper bound of z ≲1.7
on the distance scale (G. Stratta et al. 2007).
We processed the images using the High Energy As-
trophysics Software (HEASoft) tools version 6.33. In our
re-analysis, we checked each snapshot for common issues
that can be found in UVOT images, such as smeared
stars (D. Modiano et al. 2022) and small-scale sensitiv-
ity variations found on the detector (A. A. Breeveld et al.
2010). No smeared snapshots were detected, but many
of the UV frames are flagged as the target position lies
in detector regions affected by known small-scale sensi-
tivity variations. After excluding these flagged frames,
no UV detection is found in any filter.
Even setting aside instrumental systematics (option
sssfile=none), a uvw2 detection is difficult to recon-
cile with the constraints at other wavelengths. We re-
cover an excess in uvw2 at the 2.9 σ level (statistical
uncertainty, single trial). However, despite the longer
exposure and larger effective area of other filters, we do
not recover any other marginal detection, and instead
place the upper limits listed in Table 1.
A possible detection in wh has a higher nominal signif-
icance, ≈4 σ (statistical uncertainty, single trial). How-
ever, in the u , b, v and wh images, a read-out streak
from a nearby bright star crosses the target position (see
the left panel of Figure 5 in Appendix B), so these mea-
surements and their significance should be treated with
caution. Similar caution applies to the uvw1 observa-
tions in segments 000 and 001, which show scattered
light rings overlapping with the source position (Fig-
ure 5, right panel).
Our re-analysis therefore supports only a weak detec-
tion in the wh filter, with no convincing evidence for
an associated UV counterpart. Given the broad wave-
length coverage of the wh filter, the resulting constraints
from the Lyman limit is only z ≲5.6. For reference, the

2.2. Near-Infrared Imaging
2.2.1. SOAR
The target was observed on two consecutive nights
with the Ohio State Infrared Imager/Spectrometer
(OSIRIS), mounted on the 4.1 m Southern Astrophysical
Research (SOAR) Telescope. The first epoch, obtained
at T −T0 ≈0.48 d, consisted of J-band imaging (at
an average airmass of 1.9) followed by Ks-band imaging
(at an average airmass of 2.5). A second deeper J-band
sequence was obtained at T −T0 ≈1.43 d at an average
airmass of 1.8.
Science exposures and calibration files were retrieved
from the NOIRLab Astro Data Archive and reduced
with a custom Python-based pipeline following standard
image-processing steps, including bias subtraction, dark
correction, flat-fielding, and astrometric alignment. The
field was photometrically calibrated using two bright
stars from the VISTA Hemisphere Survey (VHS) cata-
log DR5 (R. G. McMahon et al. 2021) and converted
into the AB system using JAB = JVega + 0.916 and
Ks,AB = Ks,Vega + 1.839.
As the GRB counterpart is faint, we perform aper-
ture photometry using a circular region of radius
0.8×FHWM ≈0.8′′. Our analysis confirms the detec-
tion of a nIR counterpart (J. Haislip et al. 2006) with
J = 22.5 ± 0.2 AB mag that rapidly fades between the
two epochs (Table 1).

3. RESULTS
3.1. Host galaxy association
GRB 061201 has traditionally been associated with a
galaxy (G1 in Figure 1) at z ≈0.111 (G. Stratta et al.
2007), which is offset by ≈16.2′′(≈32.5 kpc in projec-
tion) from the GRB localization (W. Fong & E. Berger
2013). This association was supported by a relatively
low probability of chance coincidence, Pcc ≈11% in
r band (see Table 2), and by the UV constraints on
the afterglow redshift suggesting a low-redshift origin,
z ≲1.7 (G. Stratta et al. 2007).
Our re-analysis of the UVOT data significantly relaxes
this constraint. We find that the positive fluctuations
observed in the optical and UV filters are likely induced
by instrumental effects, including read-out streaks and
scattered-light rings (Appendix B, Figure 5). The de-
tection in the broad wh filter would then sets z ≲5.6.
A more stringent limit is derived from the VLT optical
spectroscopy (G. Stratta et al. 2007).
By inspecting
the 2D spectrum, a weak trace is clearly visible at the
GRB position down to ≈5100 ˚A (Appendix A). Below
this wavelength, the background noise becomes too high
to securely identify a source. The resulting upper bound
from the Lyman limit is z ≲4.6.
In light of these new findings, we consider three po-
tential GRB/galaxy associations: G1 at z ≈0.111, G2
at z ≈1.2, and G3 at z ≳2.
Based on the probabil-
ity of chance coincidence Pcc (J. S. Bloom et al. 2002),
no clear candidate emerges (Appendix C, Table 2): Pcc
ranges from 5% to 22%, and the most likely association
also depends on the selected filter. We therefore exam-
ine the GRB properties and assess how well they are
reproduced under each proposed association.
Several lines of evidence disfavor the association with
G1. First, the faint and rapidly fading optical emission
is not consistent with the behavior of an AT2017gfo-like
kilonova at z ∼0.1 (cf. Figure 4 of E. Troja 2023).
This conclusion is strengthened by the nIR constraints:
at z ∼0.1, an AT2017gfo-like kilonova would peak at
J ≈23 AB mag, above the limits derived in Table 1.
A further inconsistency arises from the large projected
offset of ≈32 kpc between G1 and the GRB position.
At such a distance from the putative host, the burst
would be expected to occur in a low-density environment
and produce a faint X-ray afterglow (P. Kumar & A.
Panaitescu 2000; B. O’Connor et al. 2020). Instead, the
observed X-ray flux is commensurate with the energy of
the explosion.
We derive an X-ray to prompt gamma-ray ratio
of log (FX,11hr/Sγ) ≈−6.1, where Sγ is the prompt
gamma-ray fluence in the 15-150 keV band and FX,11hr
is the X-ray flux in the 0.3-10 keV band evaluated at 11
hr. This quantity provides a useful proxy of the circum-
burst density (M. Nysewander et al. 2009; B. O’Connor
et al. 2022), and its value favors a modest physical offset
(≲10 kpc, cf. Figure 1 of Y.-H. Yang et al. 2024). For
comparison, two large-offset bursts, GRB 211211A and
GRB 230307A, have values of ≈−7.9 (E. Troja et al.
2022) and ≈−8.3 (Y.-H. Yang et al. 2024), respectively.
Y. Mao et al. (2026) argued in favor of an association
with G2 for which they derived a photometric redshift
z ≈1.2. This redshift lies close to the peak of the NS
merger rate distribution (P. Beniamini & T. Piran 2019),
where a substantial fraction of short GRBs are expected
to occur. However, this association would still imply a
large projected physical offset (≈17 kpc) which, as for
G1, is not supported by the X-ray afterglow brightness.
Instead, we propose G3 as the most viable candidate.
Based on chance coincidence probability Pcc (see Ta-
ble 2), G3 is broadly equivalent to G1 and G2 in a sta-
tistical sense. An association with G3 remains consistent
with the revised UV/optical constraints, does not imply
a large separation or a high kick velocity, and alleviates
the tension with the X-ray afterglow brightness.
Al-
though G3 is extremely faint in apparent magnitude, its
brightness would fall within the typical distribution of
short GRB host galaxies if G3 lies at z ≳3 (W. Fong &
E. Berger 2013; S. Dichiara et al. 2026), a redshift range
also consistent with its red color (Sect. 2.2.2).
Addi-
tionally, we argue that its possible higher redshift would
better account for the observed afterglow spectral en-
ergy distribution (SED).

4. CONCLUSION
We have revisited the distance scale and host galaxy
association of GRB 061201.
Our re-analysis of the
Swift/UVOT data shows that the source position is af-
fected by instrumental systematics and no secure UV
counterpart is recovered. The remaining optical limits
allow for a substantially higher redshift.
Deep JWST/NIRCam imaging reveals a faint red
source, G3, nearly coincident with the afterglow posi-
tion. We derive a modest chance coincidence probabil-
ity, and present the observational evidence supporting a
physical link between GRB 061201 and G3. Although
the available two-band photometry does not yield a
unique photometric redshift for G3, its color and bright-
ness are consistent with a galaxy at z ≳2.
A high
redshift of z ≈3 – 4 is also supported by the afterglow
SED. A secure redshift for G3, either from multi-color
imaging with narrower bandpass filters, or, ideally, from
spectroscopy, is needed to confirm this scenario. If es-
tablished, GRB 061201 would be among the most dis-
tant short GRBs known and would provide an important
observational anchor for the evolution of compact-object
mergers and their contribution to r-process enrichment
in the early Universe.

APPENDIX
A. VLT SPECTRUM
VLT/FORS2 spectroscopy of the GRB afterglow, presented in G. Stratta et al. (2007), sets a direct constraint on
the GRB distance scale. We therefore reanalyzed these observations to search for redshift-dependent spectral features.
The observations were carried out on 2006 December 2, starting at 01:18:55 UT (T0 +0.4 d) with 2×1800 s exposure
time, using a 1′′ wide slit. The instrumental setup consisted of the 300V grism combined with the GG375 filter, covering
approximately 3800–7600 ˚A. After cosmic-ray removal from the raw frames, the archival data were reduced with the
FORS spectroscopic pipeline through EsoReflex (W. Freudling et al. 2013). The reduced, stacked 2D spectrum reveals
a faint trace at the GRB position, visible down to ≈5100 ˚A. At shorter wavelengths, the background noise prevents a
secure identification of the source. Although no clear absorption or emission features are visible, the detection of the
afterglow continuum sets an upper limit on the Lyman-limit wavelength and implies z ≲(5100/912 −1) ≃4.6.
B. UVOT IMAGES
The GRB position lies approximately 2′ from a bright (V ≃11 mag) star, which affects the local background.
Thus, the limiting uncertainty is not only set by the Poisson noise but also by the accuracy with which the stellar
contamination can be estimated.
Figure 5 (left panel) shows the stack of the optical u, b, and v images taken during the first epoch (segment 000).
All images were reprocessed using the standard HEASoft Swift tools and the relevant calibration files, corrected for
modulo-8 fixed-pattern noise, astrometrically aligned using uvotskycorr, then co-added with uvotimsum. The optical
stack shows that a read-out streak from the nearby bright source crosses the afterglow position. This artifact also
affects images taken with the wh filter, for which we report a detection in Table 1. However, our conclusions do not
depend on this detection, but rather on the faintness of the optical counterpart at blue wavelengths. Thus, they would
remain unchanged even if the source was treated as an upper limit.
Figure 5 (right panel) shows the stack of the UV w1 images taken during the second epoch (segment 001). The
location of the streak has moved.
However, scattered-light rings from nearby bright stars contaminate the GRB
position. These rings produce a higher and spatially variable background, which might affect the detection significance
and photometry of faint sources. Our re-analysis does not reproduce the marginal w1 detection reported by Y. Mao
et al. (2026).

C. CHANCE COINCIDENCE PROBABILITY
When a direct measurement of the distance scale is not available, a GRB is commonly associated with the host
galaxy that has the lowest chance coincidence probability Pcc. This quantity estimates the likelihood of finding, by
chance, a galaxy of comparable or greater brightness within a given angular distance from the GRB position (J. S.
Bloom et al. 2002). Low values of Pcc therefore indicate that the GRB-galaxy alignment is unlikely to be random.
In Table 2, we report the brightness, projected angular offsets, and resulting Pcc values for the three candidate host
galaxies G1, G2, and G3. For comparison, we also report the values previously derived in the literature by W. Fong
& E. Berger (2013) and Y. Mao et al. (2026).

To estimate the chance coincidence probability for G3, we calculate the probability of intercepting a source 28.5
mag or brighter in our F322W2 image. We use the segmentation map produced by Source Extractor (E. Bertin & S.
Arnouts 1996), reject stellar sources with CLASS STAR≥0.3, and manually mask residual saturated stars and diffraction
spikes. In the cleaned segmentation map, sources with MAG AUTO ≲28.5 AB mag occupy ≃11% of the total image
area. The probability that a random position lies within 0.4′′ of any of these sources is Pcc ≃13%. As an alternative
method, we treat the observed F150W2 and F322W2 magnitudes as approximate values for the F150W and F356W
bands, respectively, and compare them with the near-infrared galaxy number counts of R. A. Windhorst et al. (2023).
We obtain values of Pcc ranging from 13% to 5%, comparable to the segmentation map method.
D. BROADBAND SED WITH AN SMC EXTINCTION LAW
The typical extinction law of short GRB sight lines is not well constrained. Therefore, in addition to the MW
