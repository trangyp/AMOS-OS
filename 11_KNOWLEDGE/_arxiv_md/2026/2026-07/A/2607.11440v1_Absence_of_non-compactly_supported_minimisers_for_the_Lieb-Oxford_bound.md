This bound was originally proved by Lieb [13] and Lieb and Oxford [15] in

it is used to calibrate exchange-correlation functionals [9, 18, 19, 24]. As
such, the search for the optimal value of cLO(s, d) has retained a lot of
attention since the original work of E.H. Lieb and S. Oxford, see e.g. [23, 6,
21, 11, 12].
As already stressed above, in the inequality (LO) the constant cLO(s, d)
is universal in the number of particles in the system. One can nevertheless
consider the Lieb–Oxford bound for a fixed (and finite) number of particles
N ∈N∗and look for the smallest constant such that (LO) is verified for
all N-particle distributions P ∈P((Rd)N). We denote by cLO(s, d, N) this
constant – and later on simply by cLO for shortness if no ambiguity arises.
