A RESCALING PRINCIPLE FOR QUASIREGULAR CURVES
WITH APPLICATIONS TO HYPERBOLICITY
TONI IKONEN AND JONATHAN PIM
Abstract. We prove a Miniowitz–Zalcman rescaling principle for quasiregu-
lar curves into calibrated manifolds. We have two main applications.
First, we introduce Brody hyperbolicity adapted to our setting and prove
its equivalence to the normality of the family of quasiregular curves from the
Euclidean unit ball into the target. When normality holds, we quantify the
local modulus of continuity for quasiregular curves using an injectivity radius
lower bound and a sectional curvature upper bound of the target.
Second, in the special case of conformal curves into closed calibrated man-
ifolds, we prove the equivalence of Kobayashi and Brody hyperbolicity. This
answers a question posed by Broder–Iliashenko–Madnick. As an intermediate
result, we prove an analogue of Marty’s theorem from complex analysis in this
setting.
Additionally, we construct new examples of non-constant entire quasireg-
ular curves factoring through a Special Lagrangian submanifold of a closed
Calabi–Yau manifold, an associative submanifold of a closed G2 manifold, and
four-dimensional analogues thereof, providing obstructions to Brody hyperbol-
icity.

1. Introduction
1.1. Background and the main theorem. In this manuscript, we investigate
rescaling principles and their applications in the calibrated geometries of Harvey
and Lawson [HL82]. A classical instance of such a principle is Brody’s theorem
[Bro78] in complex geometry: for a closed complex manifold N, the family of holo-
morphic maps from the unit disk D ⊂C into N is normal if and only if there
exists no non-constant entire holomorphic map C →N. This connection between
normality and non-existence of entire holomorphic maps is sharpened by Zalcman’s
lemma [Zal75]; see also Royden’s work [Roy71] on Kobayashi hyperbolicity [Kob70].
The rescaling principle has also been influential in the theories of various gen-
eralizations of holomorphic mappings. These include quasiregular mappings and
the Miniowitz–Zalcman lemma [Min82], and Kobayashi hyperbolicity [KO99] for
Gromov’s pseudoholomorphic curves [Gro85]. More recently, Broder, Iliashenko,
and Madnick developed in [BIM25] a theory of hyperbolicity in the context of the
calibrated geometries of Harvey and Lawson [HL82].
In particular, they formu-
lated notions of Kobayashi and Brody hyperbolicity using Smith maps [Smi11] and
asked if they are equivalent for closed manifolds. In this manuscript, we give a
positive answer to their question.
For the proof and other applications, we es-
tablish a generalized Miniowitz–Zalcman rescaling principle for quasiregular curves
[Pan20], a class which includes quasiregular mappings, pseudoholomorphic curves,
Smith maps, and mappings satisfying Monge–Ampère type inequalities [IVV02] or
generalized Beltrami-type equations [Ros10].
To define quasiregular curves, we first need a definition for a calibrated manifold:
given integers 2 ≤n ≤m, we say that a pair (N, ω) is an n-calibrated m-manifold
if N is an m-dimensional (Riemannian) manifold and ω is a strongly non-vanishing
n-calibration, i.e. a closed differential n-form on N whose comass is bounded from
above by one and below by a positive constant.
Now, given an oriented (Riemannian) n-manifold M and K ≥1, a continuous
map F : M →(N, ω) is a K-quasiregular curve if F is in the local Sobolev space

Our proof of Theorem 1.1 is based on rescaling at points where the family con-

This differs from the standard proof of

The critical lower bound for the

Observe that the relevant profiles are in codimension m −n and m −n + 1 which
may be large and are therefore naturally formulated using the theory of integral
currents. These methods are flexible enough that we prove a version of Theorem 1.1
for non-compact targets which satisfy a sectional curvature upper bound and an
injectivity radius lower bound; see Theorem 6.2.
1.2. Normality, Brody hyperbolicity and quasiregular ellipticity. As one
of our main applications of Theorem 1.1, we prove the following:
Theorem 1.2 (Brody’s theorem for calibrated manifolds). Let (N, ω) be a closed
n-calibrated m-manifold and K ≥1. Then the following are equivalent.
(1) every K-quasiregular curve Rn →(N, ω) is constant;
(2) the family of K-quasiregular curves M →(N, ω) is a normal family, for
each oriented n-manifold M;
(3) the family of K-quasiregular curves Rn ⊃B →(N, ω) is a normal family
for the unit ball B.
Property (1) is associated with two complementary notions: if Theorem 1.2 (1)
holds, we say that (N, ω) is Brody K-hyperbolic in analogy to [Bro78].
Other-
wise (N, ω) is K-quasiregularly elliptic following [Hei24]. Quasiregular ellipticity
for mappings was introduced by Bonk–Heinonen in [BH01].
For mappings, the
complete classification of closed quasiregularly elliptic 3-manifolds is due to Jor-
makka [Jor88], following the resolution of Thurston’s Geometrization Conjecture
by Perelman. Recently, the classification for closed 4-manifolds was completed; see
Heikkilä–Pankka and Piergallini–Zuddas [HP25, PZ21] for the simply connected
case and Manin–Prywes [MP24] for the general case. In Section 8, we show that
many of these manifolds occur as calibrated submanifolds of closed manifolds with
special holonomy.
Moreover, by applying Bryant’s construction of Calabi–Yau
manifolds [Bry00], we show that every closed manifold in Jormakka’s classifica-
tion occurs as a Special Lagrangian in a non-compact Calabi–Yau manifold, cf.
Corollary 8.5. These provide new examples of quasiregular ellipticity for curves.

Recently, the second author and Pankka showed in [PP25] that Theorem 1.2 (2) is
closely related to non-trivial bubbling of quasiregular curves; see also the bubbling
phenomenon for harmonic maps by Sacks–Uhlenbeck [SU81], pseudoholomorphic
curves (see [Gro85] and [MS12, Section 4]), Smith maps [CKM20], and quasiregular
mappings [PS23]. Indeed, if there exists a family F of K-quasiregular curves M →
(N, ω) with bounded energy and which is not normal, then there exists a non-
constant K-quasiregular curve Sn →(N, ω) by [PP25]. Conversely, if there exists a
non-constant K-quasiregular curve Sn →(N, ω), such a family F is formed by the
orbit of the curve under the action of the conformal automorphism group of Sn.
We expect that families of non-constant quasiregular curves Sn →(N, ω) can
be used to define a non-trivial homological and geometric invariant of the target
(N, ω). Indeed, the Hurewicz homomorphism πn(N) →Hn(N, Z) maps the homo-
topy classes of such curves Sn →(N, ω) into a cone of non-trivial homology classes
of positive ω-currents of Harvey–Lawson [HL82], cf. Section 4.1. As a related in-
variant, Joyce proposed in [Joy02] a suitable weighted count of the homology classes
of Special Lagrangian rational homology 3-spheres on Calabi–Yau manifolds. To
see the relation between these concepts, we recall that if such a rational homology
sphere in (N, ωSL), where ωSL is the Special Lagrangian calibration, has a finite
fundamental group, its universal cover is diffeomorphic to S3 and thus the rational
homology sphere is the image of a quasiregular curve S3 →(N, ωSL). This dis-
cussion readily extends to associative rational homology spheres in G2 manifolds
[Joy18]. For the closely related theory of Gromov–Witten invariants for pseudo-
holomorphic curves, we refer to [MS12, Chapter 7].
In Theorem 6.3 below, we prove a version of Theorem 1.2 for non-compact targets
which have a sectional curvature upper bound and an injectivity radius lower bound.

Following [BIM25], we define three notions of hyperbolicity closely related to the
discussion so far:
(1) (N, ω) is ω-hyperbolic if every conformal curve Rn →(N, ω) is constant,
i.e. Brody 1-hyperbolicity holds for this subclass of quasiregular curves;
(2) (N, ω) is Kω-hyperbolic if d(N,ω)(x, y) > 0 for every x ̸= y;
(3) (N, ω) is Rω-hyperbolic if, for each x ∈N, there exist c > 0 and r > 0 such

of hyperbolicity coincide when N is a closed calibrated manifold. The following

1.4. Structure of the paper. We outline the structure of the paper.
In Section 2, we introduce our fairly standard notation and prerequisites on
Sobolev maps.
In Section 3, we formulate our basic setup: that of calibrated manifolds which
have a sectional curvature upper bound and an injectivity radius lower bound.
We also recall geometric consequences of these assumptions, including small mass
isoperimetric inequalities for integral currents and a quantitative Poincaré lemma.
In Section 4, we investigate the quantitative geometry of quasiregular curves in
our setting. Our results include homological non-triviality of non-constant quasireg-
ular curves M →(N, ω) for closed M, modulus of continuity estimates, weak reverse
Hölder inequalities, and a new non-linear Caccioppoli type inequality.
In Section 5, we analyse families of quasiregular curves. In particular, we give
equivalent conditions for a family of quasiregular curves to be equicontinuous or
normal.
In Section 6, we prove a version of the Miniowitz–Zalcman rescaling principle
using equicontinuity and normality, respectively. Moreover, for the proof of the
principle, we propose a notion of Zalcman sequences and study basic properties of
such sequences.
In the sections mentioned above, we also treat non-compact targets and formulate
corresponding versions of our main results for equibounded families of quasiregular
curves.
In Section 7, we establish the equivalence of the various notions of hyperbolicity
for conformal curves for closed targets. In fact, we connect the normality of con-
formal curves F1(ω) to the uniform boundedness of the derivatives of F1(ω) in a
neighbourhood of the origin, similar to Marty’s theorem from complex analysis.
In Section 8, we refine the known connections between the quasiregular ellipticity
for mappings and for curves by exhibiting a large collection of quasiregular curves

2.2. Manifolds, comass norm, and calibrations. Our manifolds are smooth,
equipped with a Riemannian metric, and we denote the induced distance function
by d.
Submanifolds are equipped with the Riemannian metric induced by the
ambient space, and M ⊂N denotes that M is a submanifold of N. When M is
isometrically embedded in N, we write M ,−→N. If M is oriented, the image of the
embedding is equipped with the induced orientation.
Given a manifold N and a differential n-form ω on N, the pointwise comass of
ω is the function ∥ω∥: N →R defined by
∥ω∥(x) = sup {ω(v1 ∧· · · ∧vn): {v1, . . . , vn} is an orthonormal frame on TxN} .

N is m-dimensional and ω is a strongly non-vanishing calibration of degree n.

present a normality criterion for families of quasiregular curves. Before that, we
recall some standard terminology. Here and below, a family F of maps f : X →Y
is equibounded in space if for every compact set E ⊂X, there exists a compact set
E′ ⊂Y such that f(E) ⊂E′ for every f ∈F. As is standard, an equibounded family
refers to any family that is equibounded in space. If Y is compact, this holds for
any family. Finally, a family F is normal if every sequence has a subsequence which
converges locally uniformly. We recall that by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, when X
and Y are separable and locally compact metric spaces, normality is equivalent to
the family being equicontinuous and equibounded in space.

8. Examples of quasiregular ellipticity
In this section, we describe examples of quasiregularly elliptic n-calibrated m-
manifolds (N, ω); here 2 ≤n ≤m. We mainly focus on the lower-dimensional cases:
2 ≤n ≤4 and 6 ≤m ≤8. To generate the examples below, we use the following
elementary lemma.
Lemma 8.1. Let (N, ω) be an n-calibrated m-manifold and let M be an oriented
n-manifold.
If Σ ⊂(N, ω) is ω-calibrated and f : M →Σ is a K-quasiregular
mapping, then ι ◦f : M →(N, ω) is a K-quasiregular curve where ι: Σ ,−→(N, ω)
is the inclusion. In particular, if (Σ, volΣ) is K-quasiregularly elliptic, then so is
(N, ω).
We recall that an n-dimensional submanifold Σ ⊂(N, ω) is ω-calibrated if, for
each x ∈Σ, the tangent space TxΣ ⊂TxN has an orthonormal basis v1, . . . , vn for
which ω(v1 ∧· · · ∧vn) = 1. Equivalently, the inclusion Σ ,−→(N, ω) is a conformal
curve.
The following theorem by Alexander from piecewise linear geometry gives im-
portant examples of quasiregular mappings [Ale20].
Theorem 8.2. Let Σ be a closed and oriented (Riemannian) n-manifold. Then
there exists a surjective quasiregular mapping Σ →Sn that has bounded length
distortion.
