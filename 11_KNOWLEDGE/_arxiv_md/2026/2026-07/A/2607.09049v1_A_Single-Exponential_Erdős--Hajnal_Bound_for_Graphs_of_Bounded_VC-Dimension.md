Abstract
A homogeneous set in a graph is a clique or a stable set. The Erdős–Hajnal conjecture states that, for
every graph H, there exists c > 0 such that every H-free graph on n vertices has a homogeneous set of size
at least nc. Nguyen, Scott and Seymour proved that for every d > 0, graphs of VC-dimension at most d
have the Erdős–Hajnal property, confirming a conjecture of Fox, Pach and Suk. In particular, they showed
that every such n-vertex graph contains a homogeneous set of size at least nηd for some ηd ≥2−2O(d). In
this paper, we give a sharper quantitative bound on the homogeneous sets in graphs of VC-dimension at
most d, showing that one may take ηd ≥(Cd)−d, where C is an absolute constant. Equivalently, every
graph G of VC-dimension at most d satisfies
max{ω(G), α(G)} ≥|G|(Cd)−d.
Our proof refines the iterative sparsification method of Nguyen, Scott and Seymour. The main enhancement
is to apply the VC-dimension assumption directly, which gives a more efficient induction and thus improves
the dependence on d. We also derive quantitative consequences for polynomial Rödl subgraphs, hypergraph
Ramsey bounds under bounded VC-dimension, induced-free and viral formulations, tournaments, NIP
and semi-algebraic graphs, Boolean combinations of relations of bounded VC-dimension, graphs whose
adjacency matrices have bounded rank, graphs of bounded sign-rank, and graphs defined by dot-product
threshold representations.

Introduction
All (hyper)graphs in this paper are finite and simple. A graph H is an induced subgraph of a graph G if H
can be obtained from G by removing vertices. A class C of graphs is hereditary if it is closed under taking
induced subgraphs and under isomorphism; and a hereditary class C is proper if it is not the class of all
graphs. The Erdős–Hajnal conjecture is a far-reaching strengthening of Ramsey’s theorem for hereditary
graph classes. We say that a class C of graphs has the Erdős–Hajnal property if there exists c = c(C) > 0 such
that every n-vertex graph G ∈C contains a clique or a stable set of size at least nc. Equivalently, for every
fixed graph H, the conjecture asserts that the hereditary class of graphs with no induced copy of H has this
property [19, 20].
Conjecture 1.1. Every proper hereditary class of graphs has the Erdős–Hajnal property.

This conjecture has become one of the central problems in induced Ramsey theory; see a survey of
Chudnovsky [13]. A basic structural principle was established by Alon, Pach and Solymosi, who proved that
the Erdős–Hajnal property is preserved under substitution operation, which reduces the general problem
to prime graphs [1]. Several difficult prime or near-prime cases have since been settled. Chudnovsky and
Safra proved the conjecture for bull-free graphs [14]. Chudnovsky, Fox, Scott, Seymour and Spirkl obtained
a strengthened lower bound for graphs with no induced 5-cycle [15], and Chudnovsky, Scott, Seymour and
Spirkl later proved the full Erdős–Hajnal conjecture for this class [16]. Nguyen, Scott and Seymour proved
the conjecture for P5-free graphs [39]; together with the C5-free case, this completes the conjecture for all
graphs on at most five vertices. Very recently, Huang, Ju and Zhou proved the Erdős–Hajnal property for two
six-vertex graphs, the E-graph obtained from P5 by adding a pendant edge to the middle vertex and the Bird
graph obtained from the bull by adding a pendant edge to one horn [32].
Other progress comes from geometric and low-complexity intersection patterns. A graph class is said
to have the strong Erdős–Hajnal property if there exists δ > 0 such that every n-vertex graph in the class
contains two disjoint vertex sets A and B, each of size at least δn, that are complete or anticomplete to
each other in G. Pach and Solymosi proved the strong Erdős–Hajnal property for intersection graphs of
segments [40], Alon, Pach, Pinchasi, Radoičić and Sharir proved an Erdős–Hajnal type result for semi-algebraic
intersection graphs [2], and Fox, Pach and Tóth obtained related results for intersection patterns of curves
[24]. Tomon then proved that every string graph has the Erdős–Hajnal property [44]. Recent work has also
produced new general mechanisms for induced Ramsey problems. Fox, Nguyen, Scott and Seymour proved
sparse-versus-dense density statements for cographs [28]; Nguyen, Scott and Seymour found infinitely many
new prime graphs with the Erdős–Hajnal property [37], and proved that all paths satisfy near-polynomial
Erdős–Hajnal bounds [38]. In a different direction, Bucić, Nguyen, Scott and Seymour improved the general
lower bound for every fixed forbidden induced subgraph H [7], while Bucić, Fox and Pham proved that the
Erdős–Hajnal conjecture is equivalent to polynomial versions of the Rödl and Nikiforov conjectures [6].
A particularly rich source of graphs satisfying the Erdős–Hajnal property arises from low-complexity
set-system conditions, most notably bounded Vapnik–Chervonenkis dimension (VC-dimension for short). For
a family F of subsets of a set X, a set S ⊆X is shattered by F if for every A ⊆S there exists F ∈F with
F ∩S = A. The VC-dimension of F is the largest size of a shattered set [45]. If S ⊆V (G) and v ∈V (G),
we call NG(v) ∩S the trace of v on S. For a graph G, its VC-dimension VCdim(G) is the VC-dimension of
the neighbourhood family {NG(v) : v ∈V (G)}, where NG(v) denotes the set of all neighbours of v in G, not
including v itself. This definition was linked to model theory by Laskowski’s characterization of NIP formulas
via finite VC-dimension [34]. When a graph is viewed through the set system of its vertex neighbourhoods,
bounded VC-dimension becomes a concrete combinatorial way of saying that the graph cannot encode
arbitrary set systems, and this viewpoint has led to stronger regularity lemmas and structural results for
hereditary graph classes [3, 4, 35]. The same principle also manifests in extremal graph theory: For graphs
and hypergraphs of bounded description complexity, including semi-algebraic and distal relations, one obtains
much sharper regularity lemmas and Zarankiewicz-type bounds than in the general setting [9, 11, 18, 25, 26],
and the Zarankiewicz problem has been studied directly for bipartite graphs of bounded VC-dimension [33].
A parallel line of work in extremal set theory, originating with the Frankl–Pach theorem on uniform set
systems of bounded VC-dimension [29], has recently been improved by Ge, Xu, Yip, Zhang and Zhao [30],
by Chao, Xu, Yip and Zhang [8], and by Yang and Yu [47], suggesting that bounded VC-dimension is not
only a regularity condition but also a robust extremal hypothesis connecting incidence geometry, Turán-type
problems and uniform set systems.
In induced Ramsey theory, bounded VC-dimension has been a subject of extensive investigation. Fox, Pach
and Suk proved strong near-polynomial Erdős–Hajnal type bounds for graphs of bounded VC-dimension [27],
building on a broader line of induced Ramsey-type and induced-density results [7, 23, 39]. This direction
culminated in the theorem of Nguyen, Scott and Seymour, who proved that for every fixed d, the class of
graphs with VCdim(G) ≤d has the Erdős–Hajnal property and, in fact, the polynomial Rödl property [36].
Let ηd be the supremum of all η > 0 such that every graph G with VCdim(G) ≤d contains a clique or a
stable set of size at least |G|η; their proof yields the explicit bound ηd ≥2−2O(d).
We write ω(G) for the clique number of G, α(G) for the stability number of G, and |G| for |V (G)|. The

hypothesis in standard settings. We now explain how the required VC-dimension bounds are obtained in these
applications. Boolean combinations of relations of bounded VC-dimension again have bounded VC-dimension,
by the Sauer–Shelah lemma [41, 42, 45]. NIP definable relations give a model-theoretic source of finite
VC-dimension [10, 12, 43]. For semi-algebraic graphs, the required bounds follow from sign-pattern estimates,
especially Warren’s theorem and its standard incidence-geometric consequences [17, 26, 46]. Graphs whose
adjacency matrices have bounded rank admit a direct linear-algebraic VC-dimension bound. Finally, graphs
of bounded sign-rank, as well as graphs defined by thresholding dot products of vectors, have bounded
VC-dimension through standard sign-rank estimates [5, 22]. Thus each of these settings falls under the main
theorem and satisfies an explicit Erdős–Hajnal bound.
We briefly indicate the quantitative mechanism behind the proof. For r ≥0, let Cr be the class of graphs
with no bi-induced copy of the universal shattering bigraph Ur+1; thus VCdim(G) ≤d implies G ∈Cd.
Starting from a graph in Cr, the regularity input produces a long ε-pure blockade, and the pattern graph
records which pairs of blocks are almost complete. The key point is the mixed-block dimension drop. If a
vertex is mixed on many blocks and the corresponding pattern graph contained Ur, then the mixed vertex
could be used as one more coordinate. Indeed, in each block corresponding to a subset of [r], we choose two
vertices, one adjacent and one nonadjacent to the mixed vertex. Purity then allows us to choose vertices
from the blocks corresponding to the coordinates 1, . . . , r. These vertices form a bi-induced copy of Ur+1, a
contradiction. Hence the pattern graph lies in Cr−1, so the induction hypothesis is used with the parameter
sr−1, where sr−1 is the reciprocal of the corresponding Erdős–Hajnal exponent. Iterating the resulting
sparsification step gives, for every scale η, either an η-restricted induced subgraph of size at least ηO(rsr−1)|G|,
or a complete or anticomplete blockade with polynomial width loss. A standard cograph induction converts
this global alternative into the recursion sr ≤Crsr−1. Since s0 = 1, this gives sr ≤(Cr)r, and taking r = d
yields max{ω(G), α(G)} ≥|G|(Cd)−d.

A useful way to prove Erdős–Hajnal-type results is to find large induced subgraphs whose edge structure
becomes easier to control. In this section we set up the local tools needed for this approach. We first use

2.1
Blockades and restricted graphs
A blockade in a graph G is a sequence B = (B1, . . . , Bk) of pairwise disjoint subsets of V (G), called blocks.
For a positive integer k, write [k] = {1, . . . , k}. Its length is k and its width is mini∈[k] |Bi|. If its length is at
least ℓand its width is at least w, we call it an (ℓ, w)-blockade.
For disjoint vertex sets A, B ⊆V (G) and ε > 0, say that A is ε-sparse to B in G if every vertex of A
has at most ε|B| neighbours in B. The pair (A, B) is ε-pure if A is ε-sparse to B and B is ε-sparse to A
either in G or in G. A blockade is ε-pure if every two distinct blocks form an ε-pure pair. For disjoint sets
A, B ⊆V (G), we say that A is complete to B if every vertex of A is adjacent to every vertex of B, and
anticomplete to B if there are no edges between A and B. A blockade (B1, . . . , Bk) is complete, respectively
anticomplete, if every two distinct blocks are complete, respectively anticomplete, to each other. We call a
pair (A, B) pure if it is either complete or anticomplete. A graph G is ε-sparse if its maximum degree is at
most ε|G|, and is ε-restricted if either G or G is ε-sparse.
Let B = (B1, . . . , Bℓ) be an ε-pure blockade in a graph G. Let J be the pattern graph of B with V (J) = [ℓ]
such that ij ∈E(J) if and only if Bi, Bj are ε-sparse to each other in G. Since B is ε-pure, every non-edge
of J corresponds to a pair of blocks that is ε-sparse to each other in G.

2.2
Universal bigraphs and external VC-dimension
A bigraph is a graph together with a specified bipartition (V1, V2). If H is a bigraph, a bi-induced copy of H
in a graph G is an injective map ϕ : V (H) →V (G) such that, for all x ∈V1(H) and y ∈V2(H), we have
xy ∈E(H) if and only if ϕ(x)ϕ(y) ∈E(G). No condition is imposed on pairs of vertices that lie on the same
side of the bigraph.
For a graph G, a set S ⊆V (G) is externally shattered if, for every A ⊆S, there exists a vertex vA ∈V (G)\S
such that NG(vA) ∩S = A. The external VC-dimension of G, denoted eVCdim(G), is the maximum size of
an externally shattered set.
For a set X, let P(X) denote the set of all subsets of X. For an integer r ≥0, let Ur be the bigraph with
bipartition ([r], P([r])) such that for i ∈[r] and A ∈P([r]), we have iA ∈E(Ur) if and only if i ∈A. Let Cr
be the class of graphs with no bi-induced copy of Ur+1. Then Cr is exactly the class of graphs with external
VC-dimension at most r. It follows from the definitions that if VCdim(G) ≤d, then G ∈Cd. In this paper,
log denotes the logarithm of base 2.
