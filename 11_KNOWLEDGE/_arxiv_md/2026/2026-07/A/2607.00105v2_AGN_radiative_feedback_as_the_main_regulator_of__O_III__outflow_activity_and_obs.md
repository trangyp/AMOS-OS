ABSTRACT
Context. Large-scale ionised outflows and nuclear obscuration are fundamental manifestations of Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs)
activity, yet direct observational evidence simultaneously linking these phenomena remains scarce.
Aims. We used the eROSITA Final Equatorial Depth Survey (eFEDS), among the largest uniform optical spectroscopic datasets of
X-ray–selected AGNs, to investigate how AGN accretion rate affects ionised outflow kinematics and X-ray obscuration.
Methods. Our sample comprises 2,840 eROSITA AGNs at z < 0.82 with high-quality Sloan Digital Sky Survey spectra. Through
detailed optical spectral fitting, we measured Eddington ratios (λEdd) and [O iii] emission-line kinematics, tracing ionised outflows. In
addition, we used a combination of archival eROSITA X-ray spectroscopy with X-ray stacking analyses to constrain the obscuration
of the sample by measuring the hydrogen column density, NH.
Results. We find that (1) ∼35% of the entire sample hosts [O iii] outflows (W80 > 600 km s−1), with the outflow incidence increasing
with the AGN luminosity from ≈15% at LAGN < 1044 erg s−1 up to ≈60% at LAGN > 1046 erg s−1, (2) the outflow incidence increases
with Eddington ratio from ∼29% at log λEdd < −2.3 to ∼50% at log λEdd > −1.7, and (3) AGN obscuration decreases with Eddington
ratio, as X-ray stacking shows that sources with log λEdd > −1.7 are approximately five times less obscured than lower-λEdd AGNs. In
addition, we find that ∼1% of the sample populates the ‘forbidden region’ of the NH −λEdd plane, where the outflow incidence peaks
at ∼52%, consistent with a short-lived feedback phase. Notably, when matching the λEdd samples in AGN luminosity, these trends
vanish, implying that radiation pressure drives changes in outflow activity and obscuration, while the black hole mass does not play a
significant role.
Conclusions. Our results are in agreement with AGN radiative feedback scenarios, where the Eddington ratio regulates the AGN
environment by driving powerful galaxy-wide outflows and shaping the amount of circumnuclear material.
Key words. Galaxies: active – X-rays: galaxies – techniques: optical spectroscopy
1. Introduction
Active galactic nuclei (AGNs) are powered by matter accreting
onto a supermassive black hole (SMBH) and are among the most
luminous objects in the Universe. During this accretion process,
a fraction of the released energy may couple with matter in the
host galaxy, regulating the growth of both the SMBH and the
galaxy itself in a process known as AGN feedback (e.g. Springel
et al. 2005; Somerville et al. 2008; King & Pounds 2015; Har-
rison & Ramos Almeida 2024). The discovery of tight scaling
relations between SMBH mass and host galaxy properties, such
as the bulge velocity dispersion (the MBH −σ relation), provided
some of the first compelling evidence of this co-evolutionary
link (e.g. Magorrian et al. 1998; Ferrarese & Merritt 2000; Geb-
hardt et al. 2000; Marconi & Hunt 2003; Kormendy & Ho 2013).
Theoretical models propose that this regulation is achieved as
the growing SMBH injects energy into its surroundings, regulat-
ing the star formation within the host galaxy (e.g. King 2003;
Springel et al. 2005; Di Matteo et al. 2005).
One of the most direct manifestations of this feedback is
the presence of kiloparsec-scale outflows that reach the galaxy,
which often alter the interstellar gas kinematics. In the ‘radiative’
or ‘quasar’ feedback mode, intense radiation from the accretion
disk is believed to drive these winds (e.g. Zakamska & Greene
2014; Ishibashi et al. 2018; Rakshit & Woo 2018). A primary
diagnostic for tracing the impact of this process on the warm
ionised interstellar medium (ISM) is the [O iii]λ5007 emission
line (hereafter, [O iii] ). While the core of the [O iii] profile typ-
ically traces the virial motion of gas in the galaxy (e.g. Bian
et al. 2006; Le et al. 2023), the presence of strong, non-virial
kinematic components, such as broad wings (velocity dispersion
≳500 −600 km s−1) or significant velocity shifts, is interpreted

as a clear signature of an AGN-driven outflow (e.g. Komossa
et al. 2008; Nesvadba et al. 2008; Alexander et al. 2010; Harri-
son et al. 2012). These outflows, with velocities reaching up to
3000 km/s, are far more powerful and faster than those driven
by star formation (Rupke et al. 2005; Cicone et al. 2014; Concas
et al. 2017) and are considered a signature of AGN activity (see
Harrison & Ramos Almeida 2024, for a review).
Previous studies have established a fundamental link be-
tween the properties of [O iii] outflows and the power of the cen-
tral engine, finding positive correlations between outflow veloc-
ity and AGN luminosity (e.g. Mullaney et al. 2013; Fiore et al.
2017; Perna et al. 2017; Wylezalek et al. 2020; Musiimenta et al.
2023). Several statistical studies have made significant progress
in characterising [O iii] outflow properties in AGN samples. For
example, Mullaney et al. (2013) presented [O iii] kinematics for
over 20,000 optically selected AGNs from the SDSS at z < 0.4,
finding that the outflow incidence is higher in more luminous
AGNs. More recently, Wylezalek et al. (2020) used MaNGA in-
tegral field spectroscopy of ∼2, 700 AGN host galaxies in the
local Universe (z ∼0.05) to investigate the spatially resolved
[O iii] kinematics, finding enhanced velocity dispersions in AGN
hosts compared to inactive galaxies. Moreover, outflows have
been found to be particularly common in dusty, reddened quasars
and at high redshifts (z ∼2), when galaxy and black hole growth
are most intense (e.g. Brusa et al. 2015; Kakkad et al. 2016; Za-
kamska et al. 2016; Kakkad et al. 2020). However, our under-
standing of the prevalence and energetics of these outflows, and
of how they depend on the fundamental properties of the accre-
tion flow, remains incomplete.
While large optical AGN samples have been transforma-
tive in establishing foundational statistical trends, they are in-
herently biased towards high-luminosity objects at low redshifts
(z ≲0.4), often missing low-luminosity AGNs that are diluted
by host galaxy starlight. X-ray selection effectively mitigates
this bias, enabling the identification of AGNs across a broader
range of luminosities, as demonstrated by previous X-ray sur-
veys (e.g. Menzel et al. 2016). Furthermore, studies investigating
the [O iii] kinematics in X-ray-selected AGNs have provided key
insights into feedback processes (e.g. Perna et al. 2017; Musi-
imenta et al. 2023); however, there remains a need for analy-
ses based on well-controlled AGN samples matched in redshift,
black hole mass, and luminosity, to robustly disentangle the in-
terplay between outflows and accretion across a broad range of
physical parameters.
Some observational and theoretical studies have also argued
that AGN radiation can regulate the immediate environment of
SMBHs (e.g. Springel et al. 2005; Di Matteo et al. 2005; Fabian
et al. 2009; Ricci et al. 2017). In this ‘radiation-regulated’ model,
the radiation pressure on dusty gas can blow away the circum-
nuclear obscuring material (e.g. Ricci et al. 2017), thus suc-
cessfully explaining the observed anti-correlation between the
fraction of obscured AGNs and their luminosity, where low-
accreting SMBHs are typically obscured, while high-accreting
ones are typically unobscured (e.g. Maiolino et al. 2007; Treis-
ter et al. 2008; Ueda et al. 2014; Ricci et al. 2017). Hence, in
the AGN radiative feedback model, the accretion rate should si-
multaneously regulate both the SMBH nuclear obscuration and
the large-scale [O iii] outflow activity. However, a simultaneous
investigation of both phenomena across a large, complete, and
homogeneously selected sample has yet to be conducted.
In this work, we investigate how the AGN accretion rate
shapes the circumnuclear environment across different physical
scales, from the inner regions probed by obscuring column den-
sity (NH) to the larger-scale interstellar medium traced by the
[O iii] kinematics. For this, we used the eROSITA Final Equa-
torial Depth Survey (eFEDS; Brunner et al. 2022), a 140 deg2
extragalactic survey with homogeneous X-ray coverage from
eROSITA down to 6.5 × 10−15erg cm−2 s−1 in the 0.5 −2.0 keV
energy band. The survey has a very rich multi-wavelength cov-
erage, with multi-wavelength counterparts of the X-ray sources
identified by Salvato et al. (2022), and it has also extensive, high-
quality optical spectroscopy from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey
(SDSS; Gunn et al. 2006) and the Baryon Oscillation Spectro-
scopic Survey (BOSS; Dawson et al. 2013; Smee et al. 2013)
spanning over two decades, providing high-quality optical spec-
troscopy for 12,011 sources (Aydar et al. 2025). eROSITA is a
soft X-ray observatory; hence, it mostly identifies unobscured,
broad-line AGNs (Liu et al. 2022; Aydar et al. 2025). A pi-
lot study by Musiimenta et al. (2023) using a subset of eFEDS
sources identified ionised outflows in ∼45% of luminous, ob-
scured AGNs. After extending this analysis to a larger sample
(z ∼0.5−3.5), they reported a weak correlation between outflow
velocity and bolometric luminosity. We aim to extend and com-
plement this work by using the entire eFEDS-SDSS AGN sam-
ple, thus enabling more robust statistical trends in the [O iii] kine-
matic and AGN obscuration properties across a wide range of
luminosities.
This paper is structured as follows. Section 2 details the sam-
ple selection and the X-ray and optical datasets. Section 3 de-
scribes our analysis methods, including optical and X-ray spec-
tral fitting, spectral stacking, and the measurement of [O iii] line
kinematics. Section 4 presents our findings on the incidence of
[O iii] outflows in X-ray AGNs as well as the impact of the Ed-
dington ratio on large-scale outflow activity and obscuration. In
Section 5, we discuss and interpret these results, and in Section 6,
we summarise our main conclusions.
2. Sample and datasets
In this section, we describe the datasets used in this work. In
Section 2.1, we present the optical spectra, and in Section 2.2,
we describe the X-ray data and the X-ray spectral constraints.
2.1. Optical spectra
The eFEDS sky area has been extensively observed by SDSS,
as part of the Black Hole Mapper program1 of SDSS-V, yield-
ing optical spectra for 13, 079 X-ray sources. The majority of
the observations were obtained as part of a dedicated target pro-
gram to observe eROSITA AGNs during SDSS-IV and SDSS-V.
Some eFEDS sources also have serendipitous observations from
earlier SDSS campaigns (SDSS-I, II, and III), and thus were ob-
tained prior to the launch of SRG/eROSITA (see Aydar et al.
(2025) for a detailed description of the SDSS observations in the
eFEDS field). The optical spectra were mostly obtained with the
SDSS spectrograph, which covers an observed wavelength range
of 3, 800−9, 200Å and has a spectral resolution of R ∼2, 000 at
6,000 Å. At the redshifts considered in this work (z < 0.82), the
rest-frame spectral coverage includes key diagnostic emission
lines such as [O iii] and Hβ, which are fundamental in this work
(see Section 4.2) for details). Aydar et al. (2025) performed a vi-
sual inspection of all SDSS spectra and identified 12,011 sources
with reliable spectroscopic redshifts. In this context, a reliable
redshift is one that has been estimated by the BOSS redshift
pipeline, optimised for broad-line AGNs and passive galaxies,

From this parent sample, we selected sources based on spec-

tions for extragalactic sources observed with the SDSS spectro-
graph, we selected all the sources with a median signal-to-noise
(S/N) such that (S/N)2 > 10, calculated over all valid pixels in
the observed wavelength range. This threshold ensures that the
spectra are of sufficient quality for reliable emission-line mea-
surements, particularly for the detection and characterisation of
the Hβ and [O iii] profile, which are the primary focus of this
work. Second, we selected all sources with a reliable spectro-
scopic redshift of z < 0.82, the redshift at which the [O iii] line
shifts out of the SDSS spectral range. Within this redshift range,
the Hβ line is always observed, which we used to estimate single-
epoch black hole masses (see Section 4.2) for details). Applying
these two selection criteria yielded a sample of 2,840 sources;
see Table 1. For further details on the collation of the optical
spectra and their visual inspection, we refer the reader to Aydar

2.2. X-ray data and spectral constraints
The primary X-ray catalogue in eFEDS was released by Brun-
ner et al. (2022) and contains 27, 910 sources detected in the
0.5 −2 keV band down to 6.5 × 10−15 erg cm−2 s−1. In this work,
we use the X-ray measurements from Liu et al. (2022), who
performed X-ray data reduction, spectral extraction, and spec-
tral fitting, adopting the optical counterparts presented in Sal-
vato et al. (2022). They presented the eFEDS AGN catalogue,
containing 22, 079 sources, all of which are eROSITA point-like
X-ray sources with a secure optical counterpart. These sources
were classified as extragalactic by Salvato et al. (2022), based on
their multiwavelength photometric properties, thereby excluding
Galactic X-ray sources and galaxy clusters. This parent sam-
ple spans the full redshift range of the eFEDS survey; of these,
7,444 sources lie at z < 0.82, where redshifts are taken from Ay-
dar et al. (2025) when spectroscopic redshifts are available, and
from Salvato et al. (2022) otherwise. However, the primary focus
of this work is the subset of X-ray AGNs with available optical
spectra, as described in Section 2.1 and summarised in Table 1.
Adopting a Bayesian approach, Liu et al. (2022) fitted the
spectra of all the X-ray AGNs using different X-ray models.
In this work, we adopted the results from the baseline model
(Model 1), which fits the spectra with a single absorbed power
law. Although simple, this model adequately reproduces the
range of X-ray spectral shapes observed in eFEDS sources. From
these results, we primarily used the intrinsic 0.5–2 keV luminos-
ity (L0.5−2 keV), the photon index (Γ), and NH. A key parame-
ter for our analysis is the AGN bolometric luminosity (LAGN),
which we estimated from the X-ray constraints. First, we con-
verted the 0.5–2 keV luminosity to the 2–10 keV band, assum-
ing the intrinsic X-ray emission can be described by a single
power law with Γ = 2. This choice reflects the median of
the photon-index distribution in eFEDS reported by Liu et al.
(2022), and avoids dependence on individual, highly uncertain Γ
measurements. We then applied the bolometric correction from
Duras et al. (2020) to obtain LAGN. The uncertainties in LAGN
range σ log LAGN ≈0.006 −0.35 dex, with a medium value
of ∼0.13 dex. Additionally, by analysing the entire NH poste-
rior distribution, they identified the sources where the absorp-
tion could not be constrained (class=uninformative in the cata-
logue), mostly due to low X-ray counts. We found that ∼95%
(2722 out of 2840) of our sample have an NH measurement.
The sources with class class=uninformative are not included in
the X-ray analysis based on single measurements. We note that
the initial paper used photometric redshifts from Salvato et al.
(2022) for the X-ray spectral fitting; however, the authors subse-
quently released a new X-ray spectral catalogue using spectro-
scopic redshifts from Aydar et al. (2025), which are the values
used in this work. For more details on the AGN selection, X-
ray data reduction and spectral fitting, see Liu et al. (2022) and
Salvato et al. (2022). Additionally, we performed X-ray stack-
ing analyses of our sources, using the extracted spectra from Liu
et al. (2022), which we describe in Section 3.4.
3. Optical and X-ray spectral analyses
In this section, we describe the approach used to model the opti-
cal spectra (Section 3.1) of our sample, the optical spectral stack-
ing procedure (Section 3.2), and the calculation of the [O iii] line
kinematics (Section 3.3). Then, in Section 3.4, we describe the
X-ray stacking and X-ray spectral fitting of the stacks.
3.1. Optical spectral fitting
To measure the [O iii] line kinematics and the accretion rate of
our sources, which we express in terms of the Eddington ratio,
λEdd, we first modelled the full optical spectrum to determine
key parameters, including the broad Hβ full width at half max-
imum (FWHM), the 5100Å luminosity, and the [O iii] line pro-
file. The broad Hβ and 5100Å luminosity are then used to es-
timate single-epoch black hole masses, from which we subse-
quently derived λEdd. Accurate modelling of optical spectra also
requires isolating the various components, including host-galaxy
and AGN emission. For this, we fit the optical spectra following
the methodology outlined in Aydar et al. (2026); which has been
successfully used to measure the optical spectral properties of

3.1.1. Host galaxy fitting with pPXF
Penalized PiXel-Fitting (pPXF2) is a general-purpose spectral
fitting algorithm originally presented in (Cappellari & Emsellem
2004) and subsequently updated in Cappellari (2017, 2023). It
is available as a Python package but has been implemented in
multiple programming languages. pPXF is designed to model the
optical spectra of galaxies and extract the properties of their stel-
lar populations, together with both the stellar and gas kinemat-
ics. The flexibility of pPXF allows the user to include additional
templates to the galaxy emission model, such as the AGN con-
tinuum, Fe II continuum and emission lines. In this work, how-
ever, we used pPXF solely to constrain the stellar emission, as it
is specifically optimised for modelling stellar populations rather
than AGN spectra. In particular, pPXF is not well suited for fit-
ting complex AGN emission lines that require multiple Gaussian
components, such as Hβ and [O iii] , and it does not provide built-
in routines for estimating parameter uncertainties, both of which
are essential for this work.
To fit the optical spectra with pPXF, we followed a similar
approach to Bernal et al. (2025) and Aydar et al. (2026). We first
corrected for the galactic extinction using the dust maps from
Schlegel et al. (1998) and the extinction curve from Cardelli
et al. (1989)3. To model our sources, we included the stellar
populations emission from the E-MILES library (Vazdekis et al.
2016), two sets of Gaussian profiles to model the narrow and
broad emission lines (see Table A.1), a set of templates that re-
produce the Fe II pseudo-continuum (Boroson & Green 1992;
Vestergaard & Wilkes 2001), and a set of powerlaws to model
the AGN continuum. The E-MILES template library used in
this work considers a Salpeter initial mass function (Salpeter
1955) and the set of theoretical isochrones ‘Padova0’ of Girardi
et al. (2000). We considered a wide range of ages, from 0.063 to
15.8 Gyr, and metallicities, from Z = −1.71 to Z = 0.22. The
Fe II templates cover a wide range of velocities, from 1000 to
11800 km s−1. Finally, we modelled the AGN continuum with a
set of powerlaws defined as fλ = (λ/N)α, where λ corresponds
to the wavelength, N is the normalisation wavelength which we
set to 5000Å, and α the powerlaw slope, which takes values in
the range −3 ≤α ≤0. We note that we do not use the AGN con-
tinuum, Fe II continuum, and emission lines constraints from
pPXF; we just included these components to properly constrain
the stellar emission.
Our routine fits the spectrum iteratively to ensure a self-
consistent decomposition of all spectral components. In the first
iteration, we fit the spectrum with pPXF, including all compo-
nents except the Fe II template (i.e., the stellar continuum, broad
and narrow emission lines, and the AGN power-law continuum),
yielding an initial estimate of the broad-line region (BLR) veloc-
ity. Since the Fe II emission and the broad lines are both expected
to arise in the BLR (Barth et al. 2013), we used this BLR veloc-
ity to select the Fe II template with the closest BLR velocity, to
then perform the fit again using the selected Fe II template. We
repeated this process until the Fe II template velocity and the

3.1.2. Quasar fitting with PyQSOFit
PyQSOFit4 is a python package that models the optical spectra
of quasars. The code includes the different components of the
quasar spectrum, such as the Fe II emission, AGN continuum,
and broad and narrow emission lines. PyQSOFit also includes
a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) routine to calculate the
parameter uncertainties.
We first subtracted the stellar emission calculated with
pPXF from the Galactic-extinction-dereddened optical spectrum.
Then, we fitted the subtracted spectrum with PyQSOFit, only in-
cluding the Fe II emission, the AGN continuum, and narrow and
broad emission lines. Similar to pPXF, the AGN continuum is
fitted with a set of powerlaws with shape fλ = (λ/3000)α with
α = [−5, 3] and the Fe II emission is modelled with a combi-
nation of templates from (Boroson & Green 1992; Vestergaard
& Wilkes 2001; Tsuzuki et al. 2006; Salviander et al. 2007). Al-
though in this paper we used only the constraints from Hβ and
[O iii] emission lines, we still included all emission lines in the
spectra in our fit, which are listed in Table A.1. The fitting of the
Hβ+[O iii] complex is explained below, while the fitting of the
rest of the lines is described in Appendix A.
We jointly fitted the Hβ line and the [O iii] λλ4960, 5008
doublet. As eROSITA is a soft X-ray observatory, it mostly se-
lects unobscured, broad-line AGNs (Liu et al. 2022; Aydar et al.
2025); hence, we expect to have a negligible amount of Type II
AGNs in our sample. Hβ is modelled with a narrow Gaussian
and three broad Gaussian components, each with a maximum
FWHM of 17 000 km s−1. Each [O iii] line is represented by a
core and a wing: the core’s FWHM is tied to that of the nar-
row Hβ, while the wing, capturing line asymmetry, is modelled
by a Gaussian with a FWHM ranging 500 −2000 km s−1, which
are the typical outflow velocities observed at these redshifts (e.g.
Perna et al. 2017). The flux ratio between [O iii] λ4960 and
[O iii] λ5007 is fixed at 1:2.99 (Osterbrock 1981). Careful fit-
ting of both the Hβ and [O iii] profiles is necessary, as over-
lap can occur in cases of very broad Hβ or a fast, blueshifted
[O iii] wing. Figure A.1 in Appendix A shows examples of the
Hβ+[O iii] complex fitting for two sources in our sample. Our fit-
ting approach for the Hβ+[O iii] complex follows the recommen-
dations of the developers of PyQSOFit, and has been success-
fully used to measure the broad Hβ FWHM and the [O iii] kine-
matics in AGNs (e.g. Shen et al. 2019; Rakshit et al. 2020; Wu &
Shen 2022; Musiimenta et al. 2023; Panda & ´Sniegowska 2024;
Aydar et al. 2026). To further demonstrate this, we compared the
performance of the two- versus three- Gaussian model to fit the
broad Hβ component, and we demonstrated that, for our sam-
ple, which is selected to have a median (S/N)2 > 10, the three-
Gaussian model performs better in ∼98% of the cases, and even
when the two-Gaussian model performs better, the measured
broad Hβ FWHMs are consistent. This analysis is described in
Appendix A.2, and further supports that modelling the Hβ profile
with one narrow and three broad Gaussian components provides
an effective compromise between accurately fitting individual
sources and maintaining a uniform methodology applicable to
thousands of spectra. We note that the [O iii] kinematics is ulti-
mately determined using the non-parametric method described
in Section 3.3, rather than from the Gaussian fit itself.

3.2. Optical spectral stacking
In our analysis, we use optical spectral stacking to further con-
firm the [O iii] line kinematics measured in single sources, as
identifying and constraining a line wing can sometimes be chal-
lenging, even at high spectral S/N, due to difficulties in disen-
tangling it from the continuum. Additionally, the stacked spectra
provide a visual confirmation of the systematic change in [O iii]
kinematics as a function of different parameters. To stack the
optical spectra in our sample, we followed a similar approach
to Comparat et al. (2020). First, each spectrum is shifted to the
rest frame using λRF = λ/(1 + z) and corrected for Galactic ex-
tinction. We then interpolate both the flux and its uncertainties
onto a common wavelength grid, evenly spaced in log10(λ) from
800 Å to 11,000 Å with a spacing of ∆log10(λ) = 0.0001. Since
our goal is to analyse the median [O iii] profile across the sample,
we next subtracted the stellar emission as estimated with pPXF,
along with the AGN continuum, Fe II continuum, and the ex-
tended broad Hβ component as calculated by PyQSOFit. The re-
sulting spectrum is then normalised by the AGN flux at 5100 Å.
Finally, we calculated the median and standard deviation for
each pixel across the sample, resulting in the final stacked spec-
trum. Figure 1 illustrates an example of a stacked [O iii] profile
for the sources in our sample with log LAGN/(erg s−1) = 45−45.5.
3.3. [O iii] line kinematics calculation
To quantify the line kinematics, we calculated the velocity (i.e.
line width) and asymmetry of the [O iii] λ5007 profile using the
parameters W80 and ∆v, standard methods used in the literature
(e.g. Liu et al. 2013; Zakamska & Greene 2014). These quanti-
ties require measuring the velocities at which the integrated line
flux reaches 5%, 10%, 90%, and 95% of the total, denoted v05,
v10, v90, and v95, respectively, which are illustrated in Fig. 1.
We defined the velocity: W80 = v90 −v10, and the asymmetry:
∆v = (v05 +v95)/2. For a perfectly symmetric line, ∆v = 0, while
a blueshifted profile yields ∆v < 0.
We calculated the parameters W80 and ∆v and their uncer-
tainties using only the emission coming from the [O iii] line. For

3.4. X-ray spectral stacking and modelling
Following the same approach of Section 3.2, we performed X-
ray spectral stacking to independently confirm the X-ray spec-
tral constraints derived from individual sources. In particular,
we focus on the hydrogen column density NH, which has a typi-
cal uncertainty of ∼0.5 dex when measured from single-source
fits. Stacking the X-ray spectra therefore allowed us to average
over these uncertainties and more robustly identify any system-
atic variation of NH as a function of key physical parameters,
as performed in Section 4.3. To stack the X-ray spectra of our
sources, we used Xstack5 (Chen et al. 2025), a publicly avail-
able python package to stack eROSITA spectra. In brief, Xstack
first sums all (rest-frame) X-ray spectra without applying any
scaling, and its associated full responses, preserving the overall
spectral shape and carefully correcting for Galactic extinction.
Xstack then produces the stacked spectrum, stacked response
files (ARF and RMF), and stacked background. For more details
on the stacking procedure, we refer the interested reader to Chen
et al. (2025).

ray Analysis (BXA) package6 (Buchner et al. 2014). Our imple-
mentation links the X-ray spectral analysis environment XSPEC

4. Results
In this section, we explore the incidence of [O iii] outflows in
eROSITA AGNs (Section 4.1), and the impact of the Edding-
ton ratio in the [O iii] outflows kinematics (Section 4.2) and the
AGN obscuration (Section 4.3). A discussion of the relation of
[O iii] outflows with obscuration and Eddington ratio follows in
Section 5.2.
4.1. [O iii] outflow incidence in eROSITA AGNs
In this section, we present the [O iii] line properties of our sam-
ple. We find that 1, 943 sources have an [O iii] detection, equiv-
alent to 68% of the spectroscopic sample; see Table 1. Follow-
ing Wu & Shen (2022), we define a reliable [O iii] line detec-
tion as EW([O III])/σEW([O III]) > 2. We find that the distribu-
tions of spectral S/N for the [O iii] -detected and undetected sub-
samples are similar, with median values of approximately 7.5
and 7.0, respectively, suggesting that the detection rate is not
strongly driven by spectral quality alone. A more significant
difference is found in the distribution of AGN bolometric lu-
minosity (LAGN; Section 2.2 for details) derived from the X-ray
luminosity. The [O iii] -detected sample is more luminous with
a median log LAGN/(erg s−1) ≈44.6, compared to the [O iii] -
undetected sample, which has a median AGN bolometric lumi-
nosity ≈0.4 dex lower. This luminosity dependence suggests
that more luminous AGNs are more likely to produce detectable
[O iii] emission. This is consistent with the observed correlation
between the [O iii] luminosity and the 2−10keV luminosity (e.g.
Ueda et al. 2015), which we present in the bottom panel of Fig-
ure A.4 in Appendix A.3.
The [O iii] line velocity width is characterised by the non-
parametric quantity W80, as described in Section 3.3. We de-
fine an [O iii] outflow as present when there is a significant
[O iii] detection and when W80 > 600 km s−1. That threshold
is based on previous studies who have found that the velocity
of [O iii] AGN-driven outflows is always larger than 600 km s−1
(e.g. Harrison et al. 2014; Kakkad et al. 2020), while [O iii] out-
flows driven by star-formation processes have velocities ranging
≈200 −500 km s−1 (e.g. Rupke et al. 2005; Cicone et al. 2016;
Concas et al. 2017).
We first look at the outflow properties of the sources with
[O iii] detections. Figure 2 shows W80 as a function of both

an important role driving [O iii] outflow activity.
4.2. Connection between [O iii] outflow activity and the
Eddington ratio

these two quantities. Second, it enables a more direct comparison
of our results with AGN radiative feedback models (e.g. Harri-
son & Ramos Almeida 2024), which often make predictions in
terms of the Eddington ratio rather than luminosity alone.

5. Discussion
In this work, we have examined the prevalence of large-scale
outflows in X-ray-selected AGNs and explored the relationship
between the Eddington Ratio, AGN obscuration, and outflow ac-
tivity. Our analysis used the eROSITA/eFEDS sample, which is
among the largest uniform optical spectroscopic follow-up of X-
ray AGNs to date. The final sample consists of 2,840 AGNs at
z < 0.82 with high-quality SDSS optical spectra (median con-
tinuum (S/N)2 > 10). Through parallel optical spectral and X-
ray analysis, we identified three key results: (1) The [O iii] out-
flow incidence systematically increases with the AGN luminos-
ity; (2) after controlling for SMBH mass and redshift, both the
outflow rate and the fraction of blueshifted [O iii] lines increase
with Eddington ratio; and (3) similarly, AGN obscuration (NH)
decreases with increasing Eddington ratio, result which is more
significant when controlling by SMBH mass and redshift. These
results suggest that AGN radiation plays a key role in regulating
obscuration and driving outflows. In this section, we discuss the
implications of these findings in the context of AGN feedback.

5.1. AGN radiation as the main driver of [O iii] outflow activity
The [O iii] outflow rate in our spectroscopic sample is approx-
imately 34%, with a conservative lower limit of 14% (see Fig-
ure 3), indicating that large-scale outflows are common among
X-ray-selected AGNs. This prevalence is consistent with re-
sults from AGNs selected using different methods (e.g. Mullaney
et al. 2013; Harrison et al. 2014, 2016; Woo et al. 2016; Perna
et al. 2017; Musiimenta et al. 2023; Rakshit & Woo 2018). For
example, Perna et al. (2017) analysed the SDSS spectra of a sam-
ple of ∼600 X-ray AGNs identified by different X-ray facili-
ties and found that 41% of the sample shows outflow signatures,
which is consistent with our findings.
It is possible that previous works and our own results are
biased againts obscuration, as most X-ray AGNs with optical
spectroscopy are optically bright. Based on the photometric red-
shifts from Salvato et al. (2022), we estimate that 52% of all
eROSITA AGNs at z < 0.82 have corresponding optical spec-
tra from SDSS. At z < 0.82, the spectroscopic completeness
reaches about 70% for sources brighter than rAB < 21.3 AB
mag, indicating that the majority of missing spectra correspond
to optically faint sources. By examining the intrinsic 2–10 keV
X-ray luminosity (see top histogram of Figure 2) and column
density (NH) of sources with and without optical spectra, we
find that those without spectra have a median L2−10 keV about
0.2 dex lower and a median NH about 0.4 dex higher. This sug-
gests that these optically fainter sources are primarily affected by
higher levels of absorption, rather than by lower intrinsic AGN
luminosities. Hence, we do not expect the [O iii] rate among X-
ray AGNs without optical spectroscopy to differ substantially.
In fact, previous works have found that obscured AGNs have
a higher outflow incidence than unobscured AGNs (e.g. Brusa
et al. 2015; Zakamska et al. 2016; Perrotta et al. 2019; Musi-
imenta et al. 2023; Tozzi et al. 2024); therefore, we may even
expect the population without optical spectra to host a higher
incidence of outflows.

Overall, our results provide compelling evidence that the
observed [O iii] outflows are predominantly driven by radiation
pressure from the central AGN (e.g. King 2005; Murray et al.
2005; Costa et al. 2014; Harrison et al. 2018). The positive cor-
relations between AGN luminosity, outflow incidence, and out-
flow velocity strongly support the conclusion that the AGN is the
primary power source behind the ionised outflow activity. More-
over, the finding that a strong positive relationship between the
Eddington ratio and outflow incidence emerges only when con-
trolling for SMBH mass further indicates that radiation pressure
is the key physical mechanism that launches and accelerates the
surrounding gas (Woo et al. 2016; Rakshit & Woo 2018). These
observational results are consistent with predictions from hydro-
dynamical simulations, which likewise identify AGN radiation
pressure as the dominant driver of large-scale outflows (Costa
et al. 2014, 2020; Ward et al. 2024). We therefore conclude that
AGN radiative feedback is the principal mechanism powering
outflow activity in our sample.
We note that at log λEdd < −2.3 or LAGN < 1044 erg s−1,
where the accretion flow is likely radiatively inefficient, we still
find an [O iii] outflow occurrence rate of ≈10 −30%, suggest-
ing that in this regime, the physical mechanisms driving outflow
activity are different. Previous works have shown that at low
accretion states, the AGNs are in ‘jet mode’, where relativistic
jets can efficiently propagate through the galaxy, creating shocks
in the ISM (Sijacki et al. 2007), and eventually driving ionised
outflows (e.g. Hardcastle & Croston 2020; Kukreti et al. 2023;
Harrison & Ramos Almeida 2024). Investigating the origin of
outflows in this low-accretion regime, perhaps using LOFAR
(Shimwell et al. 2017) radio data in eFEDS (Igo et al. 2024),
is an interesting avenue for future work.
5.2. Outflow incidence in the NH −λEdd plane
The NH−λEdd plane provides important information on the phys-
ical processes that shape the circumnuclear environment of the
central SMBH in AGNs, and it has been studied in several works
(e.g. Fabian et al. 2008; Ishibashi et al. 2018; Ricci et al. 2017;
Laloux et al. 2024). In the radiative feedback scenario, intense
radiation from the AGN exerts pressure on circumnuclear dusty
gas, potentially driving it outwards. As a result, AGNs with high
accretion rates are expected to be predominantly unobscured,
or if obscured, the obscuring material must reside at large dis-
tances where radiation pressure is insufficient to disrupt it. Con-
versely, low-accretion sources are more likely to remain ob-
scured, as radiation pressure is insufficient to clear the surround-
ing medium. The critical threshold at which radiation pressure
becomes strong enough to expel the gas is known as the effective
Eddington limit (λeff
Edd), which has been derived in various theo-
retical models (e.g. Hönig & Beckert 2007; Fabian et al. 2008;
Ishibashi et al. 2018). In this work, we adopt the λeff
Edd curve from
Fabian et al. (2008), based on a Milky Way-like interstellar dust
composition (solid line in Figure 11).
Given that at the redshift of our sources the intrinsic obscura-
tion from host galaxies exceed log NH/(cm−2) ≳22 in a minority
of AGNs (e.g. Buchner et al. 2017), models predict a ‘forbidden
region’ in the NH–λEdd plane where nuclear obscuring clouds
are short-lived due to rapid destruction by radiation pressure. In
wind-driven AGN feedback scenarios, the timescale for clear-
ing circumnuclear material is typically 1–10 Myr (King 2010;
Costa et al. 2014; Hopkins et al. 2016), comparable to the pe-
riod over which SMBHs can sustain high accretion rates (e.g.
Hopkins et al. 2016). For sources in our sample within the for-
bidden region, which have a median log LAGN/(erg s−1) ≈44.9,
the quasar lifetime is estimated to be ∼100–300 Myr (Hopkins
et al. 2006), implying an expected fraction of ∼1–3% in this
region. We measure that ∼1.1% of our sample lies in this re-
gion, fully consistent with theoretical expectations and previous
observational results (e.g. Ricci et al. 2017; Toba et al. 2022;
Laloux et al. 2024). This agreement supports the view that the
immediate vicinity of the SMBH is dynamically unstable under

itly target these objects (e.g. Brusa et al. 2015; Musiimenta et al.
2023). Consequently, these samples are directly comparable to
the AGNs in our own forbidden region (see Figure 11), which
exhibit the highest outflow occurrence rates and blueshifted frac-

that the forbidden region represents a short-lived evolutionary
phase: a transition from obscured, slow accretion to rapid ac-
cretion, where powerful outflows are driven into the host galaxy
while the SMBH is still embedded in gas and dust. Subsequently,
radiation pressure clears this material, leading to a decrease in
column density (e.g. Ricci et al. 2017).
Overall, our results align with a scenario in which the SMBH
regulates its immediate surroundings and the host galaxy, with
AGN radiation regulating the amount of circumnuclear material
and driving powerful outflows into the ISM (e.g. Di Matteo et al.
2005; Hopkins et al. 2016).
6. Conclusions
We have investigated the prevalence of large-scale outflows in X-
ray-selected AGNs and explored how the Eddington ratio shapes
both ionised outflow activity and AGN obscuration. Our analy-
ses are performed in eROSITA/eFEDS, a 140 deg2 field which
has been extensively observed by SDSS, offering one of the
largest uniform optical spectroscopic follow-up of X-ray AGNs
to date. Specifically, we study 2,840 AGN detected by eROSITA
at z < 0.82 with good quality ((S/N)2 > 10) SDSS optical spec-
tra. Through optical spectral fitting, we extract key properties
including the FWHM of the broad Hβ emission line (used to
estimate MBH under the assumption of virialised motion) and
AGN luminosity at 5100 Å. We further determine the velocity
and asymmetry of the [O iii] emission using the non-parametric
parameters W80 and ∆v. Finally, we constrain the obscuration
properties of the sample by combining archival X-ray NH mea-
surements with results from X-ray spectral stacking. Our main
results are as follows:
1. Approximately 34% of the sample exhibit [O iii] outflows
with velocities W80 > 600 km s−1 (see Figures 2 and 3). We
find a weak but statistically significant correlation between
the [O iii] outflow velocity and AGN luminosity. Addition-
ally, we find that the outflow occurrence rate is ≈15% for
log LAGN/(erg s−1) < 44, and then systematically increases
with the AGN bolometric luminosity, reaching a maximum
of ∼60% at log LAGN/(erg s−1) > 45.5 (see Section 4.1).
2. When controlling for MBH and redshift, we find that the out-
flow and blueshifted fractions (fout, fblue) progressively in-
crease with λEdd (see Fig. 5, 6, and 7), going from fout = 0.29
and fblue = 0.27 at log λEdd < −2.3 to fout = 0.49 and
fblue = 0.4 at log λEdd > −1.7. This trend is also seen in
the stacked [O iii] spectra (see Section 4.2). Notably, when
controlled by LAGN instead (i.e., when the black hole mass
drives the changes in Eddington ratio, we do not observe
any change in the average [O iii] outflow kinematic proper-
ties with Eddington ratio, indicating that the increase in the
outflow occurrence rate is radiatively driven, while MBH does
not impact the outflow activity.

We also investigate the impact of including a soft-excess
component. The soft excess refers to an enhancement of flux
in the soft X-ray regime (E < 2 keV) in addition to the hard
X-ray power-law emission (E > 2 keV) produced by the hot
corona (e.g. Haardt & Maraschi 1991). Its physical origin re-
mains debated, but previous studies have found it to be more
prominent in unobscured, high–Eddington ratio AGN (Boissay
et al. 2016; Waddell & Gallo 2020; Chen et al. 2025). In addi-
tion, eROSITA-selected AGNs often show a strong soft-excess
component (Waddell et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2025).
To test whether the soft excess influences the NH values ob-
tained with the absorbed power-law model, we re-fit the λEdd
stacks including a soft-excess component, following the mod-
elling approach of Chen et al. (2025). They modelled the soft ex-
cess with a power law including a high-energy exponential cut-
off, and find average parameters of ΓSE ≈2.5 and Ecut ≈0.5 keV.
We therefore adopt the model, which in XSPEC is
nhdist × (powerlaw + const × cutoffpl),
where the primary power law has a fixed Gaussian prior on Γ
(Γ = 2, σΓ = 0.05), the soft-excess cutoff power law has fixed
parameters ΓSE = 2.5 and Ecut = 0.5 keV, and the normalisa-
tions of the intrinsic and soft-excess components are tied via the
constant const, which is allowed to vary freely.
