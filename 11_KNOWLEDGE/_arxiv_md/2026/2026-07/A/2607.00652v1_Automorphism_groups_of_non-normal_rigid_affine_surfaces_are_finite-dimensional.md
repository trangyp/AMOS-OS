AUTOMORPHISM GROUPS OF NON-NORMAL RIGID AFFINE
SURFACES ARE FINITE-DIMENSIONAL
IVAN BELDIEV AND ALEXANDER PEREPECHKO
Abstract. It was recently established by Perepechko and Zaidenberg that the
automorphism group of a normal affine surface is finite-dimensional if and only
if the surface admits no non-trivial action of the additive group of the base field.
We extend this result to non-normal affine surfaces.
1. Introduction
We work over an algebraically closed field K of characteristic zero. By an affine
variety, we mean an integral affine scheme of finite type over K; an affine surface is
an affine variety of dimension 2. We denote by Ga the additive group (K, +) of the
base field.
Let Y be an affine variety. The group Aut(Y ) of regular automorphisms of Y
carries a natural structure of an ind-group; see [6, Theorem 5.1.1]. In particular,
its neutral component Aut◦(Y ), i.e., the union of all irreducible algebraic subsets
of Aut(Y ) containing the identity, is well defined.
In contrast to the case of a
projective variety, the ind-group Aut(Y ) is in general infinite-dimensional; in this
case the neutral component Aut◦(Y ) is not an algebraic group.
The question whether Aut◦(Y ) is algebraic is related to the existence of Ga-
actions on Y , i.e., regular actions of the algebraic group Ga on Y . It is known (see
[4, Corollary 1.2]) that Aut◦(Y ) is not algebraic if Y admits at least one non-trivial
Ga-action and dim Y ≥2. Conversely, suppose that Y admits no non-trivial Ga-
actions; such varieties Y are called rigid. It is conjectured that in this case Aut◦(Y )
is algebraic; cf. [10, 12]. Indeed, if Y ≇A1, then Aut◦(Y ) is algebraic if and only
if it is an algebraic torus; see [7, Proposition 5] and [8, Theorem 1.3]. The precise
statement of the conjecture is the following.
Conjecture 1.1. Let Y be an affine algebraic variety over K. If Y is rigid, then
the group Aut◦(Y ) is an algebraic torus of rank at most dim Y .
If Conjecture 1.1 holds, then so does the following one; cf. the list of open problems
in [9].
Conjecture 1.2. If an affine variety Y admits no effective Ga- or Gm-actions, then
Aut(Y ) is a discrete group.
Conjecture 1.1 is known to hold, for instance, for rigid trinomial hypersurfaces
([1, Theorem 3]), for toric affine varieties ([2, Theorem 3]), and for rational affine
varieties with a torus action of complexity one ([3, Theorem 6.4]). Some partial
results in the general setting are obtained in [10, 11].
For normal affine surfaces, the conjecture is settled in [12], as follows.

Theorem 1.3 ([12, Theorem 1.0.3(a)]). Let Y be a normal affine surface over K.
Then the neutral component of the automorphism group Aut◦(Y ) is an algebraic
group if and only if Y admits no effective Ga-action. In this case, Aut◦(Y ) is an
algebraic torus of rank ≤2.
In the present paper we remove the normality assumption, establishing Conjec-
ture 1.1 for all affine surfaces.
Theorem 1.4. Let Y be a rigid, not necessarily normal, affine surface. Then the
neutral component Aut◦(Y ) is an algebraic torus of rank ≤2.
This completes the proof of Conjecture 1.1 in the case dim Y = 2. The main in-
gredient of our proof is to pass to the normalization bY of a non-normal affine surface
Y and to apply the birational techniques of [12] to the normal affine surface bY .
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we pass to the normalization
π: bY →Y : we embed Aut(Y ) into the group Aut(bY , bE) of automorphisms pre-
serving the exceptional set (Proposition 2.2) and describe bE as the vanishing set
of the conductor ideal (Proposition 2.4). In Section 3 we show that, although an
bE-preserving Ga-action on bY need not descend to Y (Example 3.1), rigidity of Y
nonetheless forbids such actions: bY admits no non-trivial Ga-action preserving bE
(Proposition 3.2). In Section 4 we recall normal-crossing completions and the rigidity
criterion of [12] (Theorem 4.2) and adapt its proof to a relative setting: for a proper
closed subset E of a normal affine surface X, if Aut(X, E) contains no Ga-subgroup,
then Aut◦(X, E) is algebraic (Proposition 4.3). Applying this with (X, E) = (bY , bE)
in Section 5 shows that Aut◦(bY , bE) is algebraic; hence so is Aut◦(Y ) via Proposi-
tion 2.2, and rigidity forces it to be an algebraic torus (Theorem 1.4).
2. Normalization
Let Y be a non-normal affine variety. Recall that its normalization is the unique
normal affine variety bY together with a finite birational morphism π: bY →Y
through which every dominant morphism from a normal variety to Y factors uniquely.
We denote by E ⊆Y and bE ⊆bY the exceptional sets of Y and bY under π, respec-
tively: they are the smallest closed subsets such that π: bY \ bE →Y \ E is an
isomorphism.
In particular, E is the closed set of non-normal points of Y , and
bE is its preimage under π. Algebraically, the algebra of regular functions K[bY ] is

3. Lifting Ga-actions
A Ga-action on an affine variety Y is a regular group homomorphism Ga →
Aut(Y ). For an affine variety Y , such actions correspond bijectively to locally nilpo-
tent derivations (LNDs) of K[Y ], i.e., K-derivations ∂: K[Y ] →K[Y ] such that for
every f ∈K[Y ] there exists n ≥0 with ∂n(f) = 0. The bijection sends ∂to the
action given on functions by (t, f) 7→exp(t∂)(f); see [5]. We write LND(K[Y ]) for
the set of all LNDs of K[Y ]. An affine variety Y is called rigid if LND(K[Y ]) = 0,
i.e. if Y admits no non-trivial Ga-action; see [1].
We now assume that Y is a rigid non-normal affine surface and keep the notation
of Section 2 for its normalization. It can happen that the normalization bY is not itself
rigid, i.e., admits a non-trivial Ga-action. Moreover, a Ga-action on bY preserving
bE need not descend to a Ga-action on Y via the embedding of Proposition 2.2;
we illustrate this explicitly in Example 3.1. Nevertheless, rigidity of Y implies the
absence of non-trivial Ga-actions in Aut(bY , bE), see Proposition 3.2.
Example 3.1. Let Y ⊆A3 be the surface given by the equation x3 −y3t = 0. The
algebra K[Y ] of regular functions on Y is the quotient K[x, y, t]/(x3 −y3t). Since
the element q := x/y ∈K(Y ) satisfies the equation q3 −t = 0, the intergral closure
of K[Y ] in K(Y ) is the polynomial algebra K[q, y]. Hence the normalization of Y is
the affine plane A2 with coordinates (q, y), and the normalization map π: A2 →Y
is given by
π : (q, y) 7→(qy, y, q3).
We see that the inverse map π−1 is well defined at a point (x, y, t) ∈Y if and only
if y ̸= 0.
So, the exceptional set E ⊆Y is given by the equation y = 0, and
bE = π−1(E) ⊆A2 is given by the same equation and is isomorphic to the affine line
A1.
The map bφ: Ga × A2 →A2 given by (s, (q, y)) 7→(q + s, y) defines a Ga-action on
A2 that preserves the exceptional set bE. However, this Ga-action cannot be obtained
by lifting a Ga-action on Y . Indeed, the three points (1, 0), (ε, 0), and (ε2, 0), where
ε ∈K is a primitive cube root of unity, are sent to the same point (0, 0, 1) ∈Y
by the map π. At the same time, the images of the points (1 + s, 0), (ε + s, 0),
and (ε2 + s, 0) under the map π are pairwise distinct for a general s. This means
that a general element of the considered Ga-subgroup of Aut(A2) does not belong
to Aut(Y ), and the embedding Aut(Y ) ,→Aut(A2, bE) from Proposition 2.2 is not
an isomorphism.
Proposition 3.2. Let Y be a rigid non-normal affine surface and let π: bY →Y
be its normalization. Then there is no non-trivial Ga-action on bY preserving the
exceptional set bE = π−1(E).

by ∂the locally nilpotent derivation corresponding to this action. Then the vanishing

4. NC-completions
Now we are ready to adapt the proof of Theorem 1.3 to non-normal surfaces in
Proposition 4.3. We will need the following notation for its proof.
Notation 4.1. A normal-crossing completion (an NC-completion for short) ( e
X, D)
of a normal affine surface X is a projective surface e
X together with a divisor D
such that X = e
X \ D and the only singularities of D are nodes.
For an NC-
completion ( e
X, D), the associated weighted dual graph Γ(D) is the weighted graph
whose vertices are in bijection with the irreducible components of D and whose
edges are in bijection with the nodes of D. The loops of Γ(D) at a vertex C are in
bijection with the self-intersection points of the component C of D, and the edges
joining two distinct vertices C1 and C2 of Γ(D) are in bijection with the points of
C1 ∩C2. The weight of C in Γ(D) is the self-intersection index C2 of the component
C in e
X. The vertices of Γ(D) corresponding to the rational components of D are
also called rational.
A vertex of a weighted graph Γ of valence ≥3 is called a branching vertex, and
a vertex of valence 1 is called a tip. An extremal linear segment of Γ is a maximal
connected subgraph of Γ without branching vertices that contains a tip. It is called
admissible if all its weights are at most −2. For more details and results on these
notions, we refer to [12].
Theorem 1.3 is based on the following rigidity criterion.
Theorem 4.2 ([12, Theorem 1.0.3(b)]). Let Y be a normal affine surface over K and
(X, D) be a minimal completion of Y by a normal-crossing divisor. Then Aut◦(Y )
is an algebraic group if and only if every extremal linear segment of the weighted
dual graph Γ(D) is admissible.

5. Proof of the main result
Proof of Theorem 1.4. The case of a normal surface Y is covered by Theorem 1.3.
Assume that Y is not normal, and let π: bY →Y be its normalization.
In the notation of Proposition 3.2, the subgroup Aut(bY , bE) of automorphisms
of bY preserving bE does not contain a Ga-subgroup. By Proposition 4.3, applied to
X = bY and E = bE, its neutral component Aut◦(bY , bE) is an algebraic group. Finally,
by Proposition 2.2, the neutral component Aut◦(Y ) is embedded as a subgroup of
Aut◦(bY , bE). So, Aut◦(Y ) is an affine algebraic group. Moreover, since Y is rigid,
Aut◦(Y ) contains no Ga-subgroups and hence no unipotent elements. Therefore,
Aut◦(Y ) is an algebraic torus.
□
