A FAMILY OF GENERALIZED GELFAND PAIRS
ATTACHED TO 3-STEP NILPOTENT LIE GROUPS
MAI KATADA AND CORNELIE MITCHA MALANDA
Abstract. It is well known that if (K ⋉N, K) is a Gelfand pair with N a
nilpotent Lie group and K a compact subgroup of the automorphism group of
N, then N is at most 2-step. The notion of Gelfand pairs is extended to the
notion of generalized Gelfand pairs, where K is not necessarily compact. Gallo
and Saal constructed an example of a generalized Gelfand pair (K ⋉N, K)
with N 3-step and K non-compact. In this work, we construct a family of
generalized Gelfand pairs (Kd ⋉Nd, Kd)d≥1, where Nd is 3-step and Kd is
isomorphic to Rd+1; the case of d = 1 recovers the example of Gallo and Saal.
1. Introduction
Let G be a unimodular Lie group and K a compact subgroup. We say that
(G, K) is a Gelfand pair if the convolution algebra of K-bi-invariant functions on
G is commutative, or equivalently, if every irreducible representation of K occurs
at most once in the regular representation of G on L2(G/K). Gelfand pairs play a
central role in non-commutative harmonic analysis, providing a natural framework
for extending harmonic analytical methods to the study of commutative Banach
algebras. Gelfand pairs are also crucial in the representation theory of non-compact
semisimple Lie groups [12, 9, 10].
There is a substantial body of literature on Gelfand pairs [6, 10, 18]. In partic-
ular, a great deal of attention has been paid to nilpotent Gelfand pairs (K ⋉N, K)
(often denoted by (K, N)), where N is a nilpotent Lie group and K is a com-
pact subgroup of the automorphism group of N [1, 2, 3, 13, 7]. In [1], Benson,
Jenkins, and Ratcliff showed that if (K, N) is a nilpotent Gelfand pair, then N
is at most 2-step. In particular, they studied Gelfand pairs associated with the
(2d + 1)-dimensional Heisenberg group Hd, which is a 2-step nilpotent Lie group.
In the 1980s, the notion of Gelfand pairs was extended to that of generalized
Gelfand pairs, in which K is allowed to be non-compact. The theory of generalized
Gelfand pairs was substantially developed and popularized through the work of
Gerrit van Dijk [15, 16, 17, 18].
The study of generalized Gelfand pairs associated with nilpotent Lie groups
has recently been advanced [8, 4, 5]. In [8], Gallo and Saal constructed the first
example of a generalized Gelfand pair associated with a 3-step nilpotent Lie group.
Subsequently in [4], Campos, Garc´ıa, and Saal constructed a family of generalized
Gelfand pairs associated with m-step nilpotent Lie groups with m ≥3, where
the case of m = 3 recovers the example of Gallo and Saal. In [5], they provided
another family of generalized Gelfand pairs associated with m-step nilpotent Lie

Proposition 3 (Cf. [8, Theorem (C)]). In the above setting, the pair (K ⋉N, K) is
a generalized Gelfand pair if and only if for any ρ ∈ˆN, the unitary representation
ωρ of K is multiplicity free.
Proof. It follows from Mackey theory that the representations
πσ,ρ(k, n) = σ(k) ⊗ωρ(k)ρ(n)
with σ ∈ˆK and ρ ∈ˆN form the complete set of irreducible unitary representations
of K ⋉N. Therefore, by definition, (K ⋉N, K) is a generalized Gelfand pair if
and only if for any πσ,ρ, the space of distribution vectors fixed by K is at most
one dimensional. Note that the representation πσ,ρ of K ⋉N has a distribution
vector fixed by K if and only if the representation σ ⊗ωρ of K has a distribution
vector fixed by K. By the result of Mokni and Thomas (see [8, Theorem (B)]),
the representation σ ⊗ωρ has a distribution vector fixed by K if and only if the
dual representation σ∗of K appears in the decomposition of ωρ into irreducibles.
Therefore, the statement follows.
