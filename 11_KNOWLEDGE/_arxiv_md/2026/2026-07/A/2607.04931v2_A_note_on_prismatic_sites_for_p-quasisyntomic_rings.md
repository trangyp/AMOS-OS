A NOTE ON PRISMATIC SITES FOR P-QUASISYNTOMIC RINGS
JINGBANG GUO
Abstract. Let p be a fixed prime number and let R be a p-quasisyntomic ring. In this note,
we provide conditions for objects in the absolute prismatic site R∆to cover the final object
in Shv(R∆). More precisely, we introduce in R∆the so-called transversal objects, with which
coproducts exist in R∆. Immediately generalizing this, we introduce the so-called relatively
quasiregular semiperfectoid covers of R, whose prismatic cohomology (of δ-pairs, in the sense
of Antieau-Krause-Nikolaus) would produce in R∆objects with which coproducts exist.

1.3. Motivation. This note arises from an attempt to compute the prismatic cohomology
(as well as the syntomic cohomology) by choosing suitable covers and dealing with the
corresponding descent. There have been many great works related to this line of thought. For
example, in [15] there is a computation of TC(OK; Fp) using essentially Breuil-Kisin prisms
in the form of relative topological Hochschild homology; in [2] there is a delicate algorithm
that computes the syntomic cohomology of Z/pn (in order to compute K(Z/pn)) by descent;
in [8], [9], and [10], etc, there are studies of prismatic F-crystals and Hodge-Tate crystals by
descent technique. In future work, we would use Examples 5.4 and 5.6 (and their variants)
to compute the prismatic cohomology (as well as the syntomic cohomology) and study the
corresponding crystals; similar considerations can be found in [12].

2. A Reminder on Absolute Prismatic Sites
Let p be a fixed prime number. In this section, we recall the standard definitions concerning
absolute prismatic sites for p-quasisyntomic rings. For more details on prismatic cohomology,
refer to [7] and [5].
Definition 2.1 (p-Quasisyntomic Rings, [5, Definition C.6]). We say that a commutative
ring R is p-quasisyntomic if it satisfies the following conditions:
(1) The commutative ring R has bounded p-power torsion.
(2) The object LΩ1
R ∈D(R) has p-complete Tor-amplitude concentrated in homological
degree [0, 1].
Example 2.2. The following commutative rings are p-quasisyntomic:
(1) Let k be a perfect ring of characteristic p, then W(k) is p-quasisyntomic.
(2) A polynomial ring W(k)[x1, . . . , xn] is p-quasisyntomic.
(3) Let f1, . . . , fr ∈W(k)[x1, . . . , xn] be a regular sequence, then W(k)[x1, . . . , xn]/(f1, . . . , fr)
is p-quasisyntomic.
(4) Let K be a finite extension of Qp, then the ring of integers OK is p-quasisyntomic.
In order to introduce the prismatic sites, let us first recall the notion of prisms.
Definition 2.3 (Prisms, [7, Definition 3.2]). A prism is a pair (A, I), where A is a δ-ring
and I ⊂A an ideal, such that the following conditions are satisfied:
(1) I ⊂A defines a Cartier divisor on Spec(A).
(2) A is derived (p, I)-complete.
(3) p ∈I + ϕ(I)A, where ϕ is the Frobenius associated to the δ-ring A.
A prism (A, I) is called bounded if A/I has bounded p-power torsion. A map (A, I) →(B, J)
of prisms is (faithfully) flat if the map A →B is (p, I)-completely (faithfully) flat.
We now introduce the absolute prismatic site for a p-quasisyntomic R. In fact, the absolute
prismatic site can be defined for any bounded p-adic formal scheme. However, only for
p-quasisyntomic p-adic formal schemes, the derived prismatic cohomology coincides with the
site-theorectic prismatic cohomology, see for example [5, Theorem 4.4.30].

ring. We define a category R∆as follows:
• The objects of R∆are triples (A, I, u), where (A, I) is a bounded prism and u : R →
A/I is a ring homomorphism.
