APPLICATIONS OF RELATIVE MULTISYMPLECTIC GEOMETRY
DINAMO DJOUNVOUNA
Abstract. Relative multisymplectic geometry studies smooth maps F : M →N equipped with
a closed, nondegenerate relative (n + 1)-form ϖ in the mapping-cone complex of F, together
with the associated Lie n-algebras of relative observables and relative homotopy moment maps
developed by the author in earlier work.
The purpose of this article is to demonstrate the
scope of this framework through a systematic collection of applications, each developed with
complete statements and proofs. We construct a relative integration theory (relative cycles, a
relative Stokes theorem, and periods) and use it to prove: (i) that closed relative forms are
precisely the topological terms of action functionals – in particular, Wess–Zumino terms are
canonically relative objects, well defined modulo relative periods; (ii) a relative Weil–Kostant
theorem characterizing prequantizability of relative 2-forms by integrality of the relative class,
which for quasi-Hamiltonian G-spaces recovers the level quantization of group-valued moment
map theory via relative gerbes; (iii) a relative Noether theorem producing conserved charges
supported on relative cycles, with bulk–boundary charge splitting; (iv) a rigidity theorem in the
relative symplectic case: comoment maps, when they exist, are unique, automatically strict, and
automatically equivariant – the Kostant–Souriau cocycle vanishes; (v) canonical functoriality
of the moment map theory of quasi-Hamiltonian G-spaces under fusion, with applications to
moduli spaces of flat connections on surfaces with boundary. We conclude with an outlook on
hydrodynamics, Dirac geometry, and reduction.

1. Introduction
A differential form which is closed and nondegenerate relative to a smooth map is a surprisingly
common object. The Wess–Zumino term of a sigma model, a prequantum line bundle together
with a trivialization along a subsystem, a group-valued moment map in the sense of Alekseev–
Malkin–Meinrenken [1], a bulk action functional together with its boundary counterterm, a
symplectic form together with a primitive along a constraint locus – each of these is, structurally,
a single cocycle
ϖ = (ω, η) ∈Ωk(F) := Ωk(N) ⊕Ωk−1(M),
dF (ω, η) = (dω, F ∗ω −dη) = 0,
in the mapping-cone complex of a smooth map F : M →N: a closed form ω on a target N,
together with a coherent trivialization η of its pullback to a source M.
In [6, 7] the author developed the differential geometry of such objects systematically: the
mapping cone Ω•(F) carries a full relative Cartan calculus in which all classical identities hold
on the nose; closed nondegenerate relative (n + 1)-forms (relative n-plectic structures) possess
Lie n-algebras of relative observables L∞(F, ϖ) generalizing the construction of Rogers [17];
and quasi-Hamiltonian G-spaces fit the framework as relative 2-plectic maps. The companion
paper [8] developed the accompanying moment map theory – relative homotopy moment maps,
generalizing Callies–Fr´egier–Rogers–Zambon [5] – including component equations, a relative
Cartan model, explicit extension-to-moment-map formulas, and an existence theory in which
the Lie-algebra cohomology obstruction of the absolute theory vanishes identically.
The present article is devoted to applications. Our aim is to show that the relative framework
is not a formal generalization but a working tool: it isolates mechanisms that are invisible,
or must be treated ad hoc, in the absolute theory. Each application below is developed with
complete definitions, theorems and proofs, resting on a small amount of shared infrastructure –
a relative integration theory – which we also develop in full.

B. Relative prequantization (Section 5). For n = 1 we prove a relative Weil–Kostant theorem
(Theorem 5.2): a closed relative 2-form (ω, η) admits a relative prequantization – a Hermitian line
bundle with connection (L, ∇) on N of curvature −2πi ω together with a unit section s of F ∗L
with F ∗∇s = −2πi η⊗s – if and only if the class [ϖ] ∈H2(Ω(F)) is integral. Necessity is proved
by a holonomy argument over relative cycles; sufficiency by an explicit ˇCech construction on the
mapping cone. In degree 3 the same integrality governs relative gerbes and the prequantization
of quasi-Hamiltonian G-spaces [19, 11, 15], which we explain (Section 5.2).
C. Conserved quantities and boundary charges (Section 6). We prove a relative Noether theorem
(Theorem 6.1): if a Hamiltonian F-pair vσ generates the dynamics and vx is a Hamiltonian
symmetry preserving σ, then Lvσf1(x) is dF -exact with explicit primitive; consequently the
charges Qx(Z) = ⟨f1(x), Z⟩over relative (n−1)-cycles Z are conserved (Corollary 6.2). The
splitting of a relative cycle into an N-chain and an M-chain realizes the familiar bulk-minus-
boundary structure of physical charges.
D. Rigidity of relative comoment maps (Section 7). In the relative symplectic case (n = 1) we
prove (Theorem 7.1) that when N is connected and M ̸= ∅, a comoment map for a preserving
action, if it exists, is unique, automatically a strict Lie algebra morphism, and automatically
infinitesimally equivariant: the Kostant–Souriau cocycle vanishes because the relative complex
has no constants, H0(Ω(F)) = 0. As an illustration we show that the canonical equivariant
moment map of a cotangent-lifted action is an instance of this rigidity (Example 7.3).
E. Quasi-Hamiltonian geometry, fusion, and moduli of flat connections (Section 8). Every quasi-
Hamiltonian G-space carries a canonical relative homotopy moment map, whose data live entirely
on the group [8]. We prove that this canonical structure is natural under fusion (Proposition 8.2)
and under equivariant maps of quasi-Hamiltonian spaces, and we spell out the consequence for
the moduli spaces of flat G-connections on compact surfaces with boundary (Corollary 8.4),
where the boundary holonomy is the group-valued moment map [1, 2].
1.2. Relation to the literature. The integration theory of Section 3 is classical homological
algebra adapted to the smooth mapping cone; its specializations recover Poincar´e–Lefschetz-
type pairings [3]. The observation that Wess–Zumino terms are governed by relative (co)cycles
is implicit in the physics literature and in the prequantization results of [19, 11, 12, 15]; our
contribution is to derive these mechanisms uniformly from the relative multisymplectic calculus
of [7, 8], at arbitrary degree, with moment maps included. The rigidity theorem of Section 7
sharpens, in the relative setting, classical facts about moment map cocycles [10]; its higher
analogue is the obstruction-vanishing theorem of [8].

Remark 8.5 (Reduction). The reduction theorem of [1] – smoothness of µ−1(e)/G at good
levels, with induced symplectic form – consumes exactly the two relative ingredients: closedness
dω = µ∗η restricts to dω = 0 on µ−1(e) (where µ is constant), and relative nondegeneracy
descends to nondegeneracy of the reduced form. It is natural to expect a homotopy reduction of
the full Lie 2-algebra L∞(µ, ϖ) along the canonical relative homotopy moment map, generalizing
recent multisymplectic reduction schemes; we leave this to future work (see also Section 9).
9. Outlook
We close by indicating three directions in which the applications developed here invite con-
tinuation.
Hydrodynamics and linking numbers. For an ideal fluid on a 3-manifold, the volume form
and the vorticity define multisymplectic data whose homotopy comoment maps encode helicity
and higher linking numbers [16]. Knots and linked configurations are naturally relative cycles
for inclusions of tubular domains, and the conserved charges of Section 6 suggest a systematic
relative treatment of boundary vorticity and of invariants of links relative to domain boundaries.

Dirac-geometric interpretation. Quasi-Hamiltonian spaces are equivalently Dirac morphisms
into the Cartan–Dirac structure on G. The relative 2-plectic description of Section 8 suggests
a higher analogue: relative n-plectic maps as morphisms into “Cartan–Dirac” objects of higher
standard Courant algebroids, with L∞(F, ϖ) as the algebra of admissible observables (compare
[20]). The rigidity phenomena of Section 7 should then acquire a Dirac-theoretic explanation.
Reduction and quantization. Sections 5 and 8 assemble the two halves of a geometric quan-
tization program for relative n-plectic maps: relative integrality provides the prequantum data
(line bundles for n = 1, relative gerbes for n = 2), and the canonical relative homotopy moment
maps provide the symmetry data. Carrying out reduction and polarization in this setting – for
instance, recovering the Verlinde formulas from the relative 2-plectic geometry of D(G)⊛h along
the lines of [15] – is a natural and, we believe, tractable objective.
