A measurable equivariant Weierstrass theorem
Konstantin Slutsky, Mikhail Sodin, Aron Wennman
To Nick Makarov, a friend, colleague, and remarkable mathematician
Abstract
This paper is a prequel to our recent work, “Equivariant Borel liftings in complex analysis and
PDE” (arXiv:2507.12058). While the results presented here were established in that work in
a more general and abstract setting, the purpose of this paper is to provide a direct proof of
the equivariant Weierstrass theorem. It states that there exists a Borel map assigning to each
non-periodic positive divisor Λ an entire function FΛ such that the divisor of zeroes of FΛ is Λ
and such that FΛ−w(z) = FΛ(z + w), w ∈C. In general, non-periodicity cannot be omitted, and
Borel measurability cannot be strengthened to continuity. The two key ingredients are the Runge
approximation theorem and the existence of “Borel toasts”, which are Borel counterparts of Rokhlin
towers from ergodic theory.
We do not assume prior knowledge of descriptive set theory and have aimed to make the
exposition self-contained, aside from several results taken from graduate textbooks.

Question. Does there exist an equivariant Weierstrass map, i.e., a function W : D →E such that
Z ◦W = idD and TwW = W Tw
for all w ∈C?
An evident obstacle is that there exist doubly periodic divisors in D, while there are no non-
constant doubly periodic entire functions. So, for the time being, we restrict ourselves to the free
part D0 of D, which consists of non-periodic divisors. Then the equivariant map W : D0 →E can
be readily constructed using the axiom of choice, which allows us to select a representative ΛO in
each C-orbit O ⊂D0. Since the action C ↷D0 is free, for any Λ ∈O, there is a unique w ∈C
such that Λ = TwΛO. Then, we apply the classical Weierstrass map W to ΛO, and set
WΛ = TwWΛO.
The spaces E and D are endowed with natural topologies, namely, the topology of uniform
convergence on compact sets and the topology of vague convergence of Radon measures, which
turn these spaces into Polish spaces. The actions C ↷E and C ↷D are continuous and the map
Z is also continuous (by Hurwitz’ theorem). Answering a question by Eremenko, Remling [18]
constructed a continuous right inverse to Z. It is therefore natural to ask whether there exists a
continuous equivariant Weierstrass map from D0 to E. A brief reflection shows that such a map
cannot exist. Indeed, D0 contains non-trivial invariant compact sets1; let K be one of them. Then,
by continuity of W , the image W (K) is a compact subset of E, and by equivariance of W , it is
also invariant. This contradicts the Liouville theorem since E cannot contain non-trivial (that is,
containing non-constant entire functions) translation-invariant compact subsets.
We therefore abandon continuity and pass to Borel measurability. The spaces E and D are
equipped with the Borel σ-algebras generated by their open sets. Our main result asserts the
existence of a measurable equivariant Weierstrass map:
Theorem 1. There exists a Borel measurable equivariant map W : D0 →E such that
Z ◦W = idD0.
An immediate corollary to Theorem 1 is that the map W lifts any translation-invariant non-
periodic point process on C to a random entire function with a translation-invariant distribution2.

Related work
The study of measurable entire functions began with a theorem of Benjamin Weiss [23]:
Theorem (Weiss). Let (X, L , P) be a standard probability space, and τ : C ↷X a measure-
preserving free action. Then, there exists a measurable map f : X →C such that, for P-a.e. x ∈X,
the function fx(z) = f(τzx) is non-constant entire.
Weiss calls the function f a measurable entire function. Pushing forward the measure P under the
map x 7→fx yields a non-trivial translation-invariant probability measure on the space E of entire
functions. The proof in [23] adapts to the Borel category4. The only essential modification is that
instead of using Rokhlin towers to cover orbits by compact sets with connected complements, one
employs the notion of Borel toasts (see Section 3.2):
Theorem (Borel version of the Weiss theorem). Let (X, B) be a standard Borel space and τ : C ↷X
be a free action. Then there exists a measurable map f : X →C such that, for every x ∈X, the
function fx(z) = f(τzx) is non-constant entire.
Buhovsky, Gl¨ucksam, Logunov and Sodin [4] and Gl¨ucksam [7] studied the growth properties of
measurable entire functions. Recently, Gl¨ucksam and Weiss [8] extended Weiss’ construction to
measurable entire functions of several complex variables.
In [21], a “little theory” is developed, providing further equivariant counterparts of classical
results from complex analysis and partial differential equations. That work also contains several
examples of operators on entire functions that, similarly to the differentiation operator D, admit
no measurable equivariant right inverse.

Preliminaries
Given a topological space X, its Borel σ-algebra is the σ-algebra of subsets of X generated by
the open sets. Main references for the theory of Borel sets are the textbooks by Kechris [11] and
Srivastava [22].
A Polish space is a separable completely metrizable topological space. A standard Borel space
is a pair (X, B), where B = BX is a σ-algebra on X that coincides with the Borel σ-algebra
associated with some Polish topology on X.
It is worth keeping in mind that if (X, B) is standard Borel and Y ⊂X is in B, then (Y, B|Y )
is also standard Borel [11, 13.4].
What everyone wanted to know about standard Borel spaces
By the Kuratowski theorem, all uncountable standard Borel spaces are Borel isomorphic, see [22,
Section 3.3], [11, Section 15B]. In this work, we always tacitly assume that the standard Borel
space is uncountable. Another useful fact, which is also worth keeping in mind:
Theorem (Kuratowski). Let X be standard Borel, Y be Polish, and f : X →Y be a Borel
map. Then there exists a Polish topology τf on X generating the same Borel σ-algebra, so that
f : (X, τf) →Y is continuous.
If (X, τ) was a Polish space, then the topology τf can be chosen to be stronger than the original
one, τf ⊇τ.
Two other fundamental results on Borel spaces, which we will be using, are the following ones:
Theorem (Luzin–Suslin). Let X, Y be standard Borel spaces, and f : X →Y be Borel. If A ⊆X
is Borel and f|A is injective, then f(A) is Borel and f is a Borel isomorphism A →f(A).
Theorem (Luzin–Novikov Uniformization). Let X, Y be standard Borel spaces, and B ⊆X × Y
be Borel.
If every section Bx = {y ∈Y : (x, y) ∈B} is countable, then there exists a Borel
uniformization f : projX(B) →Y such that {(x, f(x)): x ∈projX(B)} ⊂B (the graph of f is in
B). In particular, the projection projX(B) = {x ∈X : ∃y ∈Y (x, y) ∈B} is Borel.
Moreover, B = F

Free Borel actions
Fix a standard Borel space X and a free Borel action T : Rd ↷X. Borelness means that the map
Rd × X ∋(w, x) 7→Twx ∈X
is Borel, and freeness means that Twx ̸= x, for each x ∈X and w ̸= 0. It is worth mentioning that
by a deep equivariant generalization of the Kuratowski theorem due to Becker and Kechris any
Borel action of a Polish group G on a standard Borel space X can be made continuous by choosing
an appropriate Polish topology on X that generates its Borel structure; see [1, Chapter 5] and [12,
Lecture II]. So, without loss of generality, we may assume that the action is continuous.
Each orbit of the action can be viewed as an affine copy of Rd with no distinguished origin,
carrying all translation-invariant structures of Rd, such as the Euclidean distance and the Lebesgue
measure. For any two x, y ∈X belonging to the same orbit, denote by ρ(x, y) the cocycle of the
action T, namely, the unique w ∈Rd such that y = Twx. One can think about ρ(x, y) as the
position of the point y viewed from x. Then |ρ(x, y)| (the length of the vector ρ(x, y)) coincides
with the Euclidean distance between x and y viewed as points of the affine copy of Rd.
By ET = {(x, y) ∈X × X : ∃w ∈Rd Twx = y} we denote the orbit equivalence relation. The
map
Φ: X × Rd ∋(x, w) 7→(x, Twx) ∈X × X
is injective and Borel.
Hence, by the Luzin–Suslin theorem, ET is Borel as the range of Φ.
Furthermore,
Φ−1 : ET ∋(x, y) 7→(x, ρ(x, y)) ∈X × Rd
is Borel. Hence, the cocycle is a Borel function ρ: ET →Rd.
In this work, T mostly will be a free continuous action of C by translations on the space D0
of non-periodic divisors or on the space E0 of non-periodic entire functions. We will also need an
action of the multiplicative group of non-vanishing entire functions E× on E by multiplication:
(THF)(z) = H(z)F(z),

Existence of a recurrent sequence of cross-sections (proof of Theorem 4)
We will provide a self-contained compilation of the proof of Theorem 4. We assume that X is a
standard Borel space and that T : Rd ↷X is a free Borel flow, which, whenever needed, we may
assume continuous (as we have already mentioned, by the Becker–Kechris theorem, the continuity
is no restriction here).
Here is an outline of the proof.
• Our starting point is a theorem of Kechris [10, 12], which we accept without proof: For any
free Borel action Rd ↷X, there exists a Borel cross-section C.
Note that Kechris’ theorem holds in greater generality: the group Rd may be replaced by an
arbitrary locally compact Polish group, and the action need not be free.
• The first step is, given r > 0, to make the cross-section C r-separated. The cross-sections con-
structed in Kechris’ work [10] are already r-separated. However, for the reader’s convenience,
we explain how an r-separated cross-section can be found.
• The second step is due to Kechris, Solecki, and Todorcevic [13]. Using a version of the greedy
algorithm, they showed that retaining uniform separation, one can fill the “holes” in the
cross-section, achieving 2r-density.
Note that almost the same argument yields R-density with any R > r. We will not pursue this,
since we do not need that precision; for our purposes 2r-density is as good as 100r-density.
• The next step is due to Boykin and Jackson [3]. They proved existence of a sequence (Ci)i of
ri-separated and 2ri-dense cross-sections, ri →∞, with the following recurrence property
(property 2 in Theorem 4): for every x ∈X and every ε > 0, there are infinitely many i for
which inf
ci∈Ci |ρ(x, ci)| < εri.
• The last step is the rationality property of the sequence of cross-sections (Ci)i, that is,
property 3 in Theorem 4. It is due to Slutsky [19, Lemma 2.3]. The main difficulty of that
step is the proof of existence of a rational grid for the action T, that is, a Borel set Y ⊂X
which is invariant under the action of Qd and which intersects each orbit of the flow in a
unique Qd-orbit. In turn, this construction is based on (a special case of) the hyperfiniteness
of the equivalence relation EC established by Jackson, Kechris, and Louveau [9] in the context
of actions of groups of polynomial growth. Here EC is the restriction of the orbit equivalence
relation ET to the cross-section C. The hyperfiniteness means that the equivalence relation
EC is an increasing union of finite Borel equivalence relations.

Recall that, for a standard Borel space X, a Borel graph is a Borel irreflexive and symmetric relation
G ⊂X × X (i.e., (x, x) /∈G for any x ∈X, and (x, y) ∈G, x, y ∈X yields (y, x) ∈G).
We say that a Borel graph is locally countable if each vertex has only countably many neighbours.
