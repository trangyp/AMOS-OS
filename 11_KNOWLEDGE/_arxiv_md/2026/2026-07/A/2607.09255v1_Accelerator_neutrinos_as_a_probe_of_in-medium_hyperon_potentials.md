Accelerator neutrinos as a probe of in-medium hyperon potentials
J. A. Nowak1, ∗
1School of Physics and Astronomy, Lancaster University, Lancaster LA1 4YB, United Kingdom
(Dated: July 13, 2026)
Charged-current (anti)neutrino interactions create Λ and Σ hyperons inside the nucleus, making
hyperon final-state interactions a terrestrial probe of the in-medium potentials that govern hyperon
onset in neutron stars.
In the StrangeMC simulation the trapped-Λ fraction, escaping hyperon
momenta, and a kaon-vetoed Σ+ tag respond monotonically to UΛ and UΣ at SBND and DUNE.
Marginalised over the low-density slope, a forecast gives δUΛ ≈6 MeV with systematics distinct
from hypernuclear data; UΣ is limited by hyperon–nucleon cross sections.
Introduction.—Pulsar timing has
established
neu-
tron stars at or above 2 M⊙(PSR J1614−2230 [1],
J0348+0432 [2], J0740+6620 [3]), requiring a stiff EOS at
several times nuclear saturation density ρ0 ≃0.16 fm−3.
Yet microscopic calculations find that for ρ ≳2ρ0 it be-
comes favourable to convert energetic neutrons into hy-
perons (Λ, Σ−); the new degrees of freedom relieve the
Fermi pressure and soften the EOS, generically lower-
ing the maximum mass below the observed value—the
hyperon puzzle [4–6]. The resolution hinges on the in-
medium single-particle potential UY (ρ) felt by a hyperon
Y in matter of density ρ: its depth at saturation sets
how favourable the hyperon is, and its high-density slope
controls how much the species that do appear soften the
matter. These potentials are sharply species-dependent:
UΛ(ρ0) ≈−28 MeV, attractive, from Λ hypernuclei [7],
while UΣ(ρ0) ≈+30 MeV, repulsive, from Σ−atoms and
(π−, K+) data [8].
A sufficiently repulsive Σ removes
Σ−from the star, one of the cleaner ways to stiffen the
hyperonic EOS [9].
The strange sector is the least-constrained part of
the dense-matter EOS, and the three established ter-
restrial handles—hypernuclear spectroscopy for UΛ, Σ−
atoms for UΣ, and heavy-ion Λ flow for the high-density
slope [10–12]—each carry their own model dependence.
We argue, and quantify, that accelerator (anti)neutrinos
provide a fourth, independent handle: they create Λ and
Σ inside a nucleus and let them propagate through nu-
clear matter, so the in-medium potential leaves imprints
on the measured hyperon kinematics. Like hypernuclei
and Σ−atoms this handle probes ρ ≲ρ0: it anchors the
input to a hyperon-onset calculation, but does not by it-
self determine a neutron-star core’s composition, which
the supra-saturation continuation of UY decides.
The neutrino handle.—Charged-current (anti)neutrino
interactions produce hyperons through the Cabibbo-
suppressed (∆S = 1) quasi-elastic reaction ¯νµN →µ+Y
(Y ∈{Λ, Σ0, Σ−}), which dominates antineutrino beams
(∼85% of the strange final states for SBND RHC), and
the Cabibbo-favoured (∆S = 0) associated production
νN →µ−Y K on neutrino beams. The hyperon is born
inside the nucleus and feels UY (ρ) on the way out, with
four measurable consequences: (i) an energy/momentum
shift on escape (attractive Λ lowers, repulsive Σ raises
the momentum of a given hyperon); (ii) trapping of slow
Λ’s into a hypernucleus, whose bound fraction measures
UΛ; (iii) in-medium Σ →Λ conversion; and (iv) a Σ+
“fake-CCQE” tag: an event whose topology—one µ+,
one hyperon, no kaon—is quasi-elastic, but whose hy-
peron charge the weak vertex cannot make, since ∆S =
∆Q forbids ¯νµN →µ+Σ+.
Any Σ+ there is a pure
final-state-interaction (FSI) product—a low-background,
kaon-vetoed probe of in-medium dynamics (its only com-
petitor, secondary πN →Σ+K, carries a taggable kaon).
Elementary and nuclear hyperon production and hyperon
FSI have been developed extensively [13, 14]; a recent
calculation of antineutrino-induced hyperon production
off nuclei [15] already includes a Λ-nucleus FSI potential
and the Σ→Λ conversion, and MicroBooNE has reported
the first antineutrino quasi-elastic Λ-on-argon measure-
ment [16].

solute cross sections and the hadronic final-state cascade
are described in the companion paper [21]. The relevant
layer here is the hyperon potential, written in a turn-over,

Sensitivity of the neutrino observables.—We scan the
potential around the physical baseline (UΛ = −28 MeV,
UΣ = +30 MeV, γ = 1) for the dominant hyperon chan-
nel of each beam.
The response (Fig. 1) is monotone
and sign-correct. Deepening the attractive Λ well from
−5 to −60 MeV raises the trapped fraction from 0.02
to 0.24, while the mean momentum of the surviving Λ’s
rises slightly (⟨pΛ⟩: 0.465 →0.485 GeV): the exit shift
(i) lowers every momentum, but the slowest Λ’s are pref-
erentially captured and the survivor selection overcom-
pensates. ⟨pΛ⟩is thus a composite of two large opposing
effects; the trapped fraction, not it, is the UΛ observable.
Making the Σ well more repulsive from 0 to +60 MeV
raises ⟨pΣ⟩: 0.471 →0.581 GeV while the Σ/Λ yield
ratio stays fixed—so the momentum is the UΣ observ-
able. Each hyperon’s potential maps onto a distinct kine-
matic handle, and the differing ν/¯ν and SBND/DUNE
responses break the UΛ/UΣ degeneracy. The associated
channel at DUNE energies adds an independent handle
on the Σ+, Σ0 charge states the antineutrino quasi-elastic
channel cannot reach, and the FSI-Σ+ tag probes the po-
tential difference UΣ −UΛ that controls the Σ−onset in
the star.

Projected reach and a joint fit.—We decay each es-
caped hyperon weakly, reconstruct it in a schematic
liquid-argon response (a Λ →pπ−“V 0” tag, momentum
smearing, tracking thresholds and efficiency), and build
a Fisher forecast from the gradients of the reconstructed
observables over the (UΛ, UΣ) plane (Fig. 3). The two
handles sit on a very different footing, and we state the
hierarchy up front: UΛ is robust both statistically and
systematically, whereas UΣ is statistically promising but

quasi-elastic beams carry the UΛ constraint: at fixed γ,
DUNE-ND RHC alone reaches δUΛ ≈0.3 MeV and the
combined beams give δUΛ ≈0.3, δUΣ ≈3 MeV (the UΣ
information is split across the Λ momentum spectrum,
the Λ-reco fraction and the Σ+ rate; the neutrino-beam
yields inherit the factor ∼3–5 associated-production
model spread). The γ degeneracy is common to all four
beams (dUΛ/dγ ≈−6 MeV each), so combining them—
which does separate UΛ from UΣ—does not separate
UΛ from γ. Marginalising over γ (these data give only
δγ ≈0.8) yields δUΛ ≈5.6 MeV, or 1.3 MeV under an ex-
ternal ±0.2 prior, while δUΣ is unchanged. The reach is
otherwise robust to the decay asymmetry, detector model
and exposure (O(104) reconstructed Λ at SBND-RHC,
O(105) at each DUNE-ND polarity). Two comparable
systematics act on UΛ: the hyperon-FSI cross section
(−5/+2 MeV) and the transport prescription, since ap-
plying UY as an exit shift rather than by force-integrated
gradient transport—which traps more strongly—biases
UΛ by −6 MeV.
For UΣ the same O(50%) YN uncer-
tainty biases the extraction by O(150) MeV, and this is
not a defect of the Σ+ tag: dropping that observable
leaves the bias unchanged, because the Λ momentum
spectrum carrying most of the UΣ information is itself
shaped by Σ→Λ conversion. A competitive UΣ needs ex-
ternal YN-scattering input; UΛ, resting on the YN-robust
trapped-Λ fraction, is limited at the ∼10 MeV level by
the slope degeneracy and the transport model, and re-
mains far the more secure handle. The kaon-less FSI-Σ+
tag is 1.0–1.4% of the antineutrino quasi-elastic hyper-
ons, adding O(102) reconstructed events at SBND-RHC
and O(103) at DUNE-RHC—robust at DUNE, viable at
SBND. An SBND antineutrino (reverse-horn) run is not
yet planned, but the soft BNB ¯ν beam gives the cleanest
quasi-elastic hyperon sample and the differently-oriented
degeneracy that separates UΛ from UΣ, so these results
motivate one.
A dense-grid Bayesian fit over (UΛ, UΣ, cΛ) combines
the projected neutrino constraint with the established
terrestrial inputs as priors (hypernuclei UΛ = −30 ±
4 MeV [7]; Σ-atoms UΣ = +30 ± 20 MeV [8]; heavy-ion
flow cΛ = 15 ± 15 MeV [10, 11]), each grid point carry-
ing a GM1 RMF Mmax. With the γ-marginalised likeli-
hood the neutrino data tighten UΣ(ρ0) statistically from
±20 to ±3.1 MeV and give UΛ(ρ0) = −29.3 ± 3.2 MeV—
a genuine combination with the ±4 MeV hypernuclear
prior, of comparable weight and different systematics,
rather than a neutrino-dominated result (the YN-FSI
systematic above dominates the UΣ budget). The high-
density cΛ is not constrained by the fit—the neutrino like-
lihood depends only on (UΛ, UΣ)—so its marginal is the
15±15 MeV heavy-ion prior truncated to the [0, 30] MeV
range of the RMF Mmax table; the neutrino probe, con-
fined to ρ ≲ρ0, carries no information on the high-
density slope.

than the terrestrial forecast.
The accelerator-neutrino
probe accesses a different production-density and mo-
mentum regime and provides a largely independent pull
on UY —a complementary fourth leg of the constraint un-
derpinning the hyperonic neutron-star EOS, though its
leading UΣ systematic (the Y N cross section) is shared
with the hypernuclear, Σ-atom and heavy-ion extrac-
tions. The simulation, calibration, EOS implementation
and detailed forecasts are given in the companion pa-
per [21].
