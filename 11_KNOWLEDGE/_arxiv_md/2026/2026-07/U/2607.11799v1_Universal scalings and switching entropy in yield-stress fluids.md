Abstract
Yield-stress fluids exhibit a singular transition from solid-like to liquid-like behav-
ior at a critical stress threshold. While predominantly investigated under steady
shear, large-amplitude oscillatory tests force these materials to cyclically navi-
gate between arrested and fluidized states. Here, we uncover a hidden universality
in the non-linear oscillatory response of these fluids: in the plastic regime, when
the frequency of applied shear is sufficiently low, the first-harmonic viscoelas-
tic moduli collapse onto master curves against strain amplitude. This collapse
reflects an invariant intra-cycle stress plateau, revealing that these materials can
rearrange almost instantaneously to maintain a constant stress state. Crucially,
we argue that this steady dynamic state is governed by a characteristic temporal
trajectory of the material’s relaxation time. We entirely capture the emerging
phenomenology using a new fluidity model derived from a Lyapunov function
showing symmetry breaking, where stress and shear rate act as quasi-static con-
trol parameters. Our framework reveals that recoverable elastic energy, fragility,
and the entropy produced during stress inversion are fundamentally intertwined.
This connection defines a single viscoplastic parameter that governs yielding
abruptness and entropy production, and provides a novel thermomechanical
foundation for the dynamic yield stress.

Yield-stress fluids, ranging from colloidal suspensions to molecular glasses, are defined
by their dual nature: they behave as elastic solids at rest but flow as liquids when sub-
jected to stresses exceeding a characteristic threshold [1–3]. Specifically, this transition
is governed by the dynamic yield stress, below which the material inevitably under-
goes structural arrest [1]. Numerous efforts [4–10] have attempted to couple elastic
and dissipative phenomena to describe the yielding transition. Despite such work, the
interplay governing both the magnitude of this threshold and how abruptly an arrested
state transitions to full strain-induced fluidization has yet to be established, limiting
our ability to treat the yield stress as a true thermomechanical property rather than
a mere fit parameter [11].
In this context, Large-Amplitude Oscillatory Shear (LAOS) effectively probes this
transition, forcing the material to cyclically navigate yielding by probing both solid-like
recovery and flow-induced rejuvenation within a single period [12, 13]. Crucially, in the
low-frequency, large-amplitude limit, the response becomes asymptotically equivalent
to steady shear, mapping the distinct steady states characteristic of the material’s
flow curve.
Here, we show that the yielding of soft glassy materials is governed by a universal
scaling of the first-harmonic viscoelastic moduli, unveiling that yield-stress fluids act
as bistable oscillators transitioning between non-equilibrium steady states.
We performed dynamic strain sweep experiments under oscillatory strain γ(t) =
γ0 sin(ωt), alongside steady-state flow curve measurements, across a diverse set of
glassy Ludox TM50 silica suspensions and jammed PNIPAM microgels (coded as
MCr1 and MCr5) showing yield stress behavior (extended data in SI). The stress
waveforms were extracted and analysed in detail for two of these systems: jammed
microgels (MCr5) at an effective volume fraction [14] φ = 1.3 and a glassy Ludox
TM50 suspension at φ = 0.432. The first-harmonic moduli, G′(γ0) and G′′(γ0), for the

Inspired by the observed universal behavior of the moduli and the existence of a steady
stress under oscillatory shear, we develop a model based on the minimization of a
Lyapunov function — treated in a frozen-time formalism [22, 23] — which generates
the equation of motion for the material’s relaxation rate, fits the full intracycle stress
response of yield stress fluids, recovers the observed experimental scaling and unveil a
novel relationship between yield stress, entropy production and abruptness of yielding
transition.
We adopt a generalized Maxwellian framework to capture the universal mechanical
response of yield stress fluids. This constitutive backbone allows us to isolate the
fundamental competition between elastic stress accumulation and complex relaxation
dynamics. Under oscillatory shear, this competition arises from the balance between
the elastic recoil associated with structural recovery and plastic fluidization, effectively
mediated by a time-dependent relaxation rate—historically termed “fluidity”, F(t)
[4, 25, 26].

The BFF developed here establishes a unifying paradigm for the rheology of yield-
stress materials under oscillatory shear, with a phenomenology emerging from
universal elastoviscoplastic scaling laws. By identifying fluidity as a fast variable,
we have transcended the limitations of classical constitutive descriptions, revealing a
hidden non-equilibrium architecture where the steady-state stress is dictated by the
symmetry breaking of the system.
Central to this framework is the derivation of an unprecedented thermomechanical
identity for the dynamic yield stress: equations (13) and (14) establish the existence
of a stress threshold as the direct consequence of the interplay between the energy

Methods
Sample synthesis and preparation
The synthesis of MCr5 microgels (with 5.4 mol% of crosslinker) performed via emulsion
polymerization and the preparation of their suspensions are described in detail in
Ref. [45]. The synthesis and sample preparation protocols for the MCr1 microgels
(with 1.4 mol% of crosslinker) are reported in Refs. [14, 48]. Ludox TM50 particles
were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received without further purification.
Glassy ludox suspensions were prepared by centrifugation as in Refs. [14, 44]. The
effective volume fraction φ of the microgel suspensions was determined by matching
the zero-shear viscosity of dilute samples with the Einstein-Batchelor equation, as
comprehensively detailed in Refs. [14, 45]. Conversely, the volume fraction of the Ludox
suspensions was determined by measuring the dry weight residual of each sample
alongside the known mass density of the silica particles [44].
Rheological characterization and LAOS signal extraction
Nonlinear rheology tests
All rheological measurements were performed using a strain-controlled ARES RFS
1KFRT rheometer (TA Instruments) equipped with a torque transducer with a work-
ing range of 0.002 g·cm to 20 g·cm. For all the tests we employed a cone-plate geometry
(cone angle 1◦and diameter 25 mm). All measurements were carried out at a temper-
ature of 20.00◦C ± 0.01◦C controlled via a Peltier element. To prevent evaporation
during measurements, the sample gap was sealed with silicone oil. Prior to each mea-
surement, a pre-shear was applied for 180 s at a strain amplitude of 250% and a
frequency of ω = 1 rad/s, in order to erase any loading history.
Dynamic strain sweep (DSS) measurements were performed at a fixed frequency,
with the strain amplitude γ0 spanning 3 order of magnitudes. This allowed a com-
prehensive characterization the initial solid-like response (linear regime), the yielding
transition and the fully fluidized state. Flow curves were obtained by imposing
descending ramps of steady shear rates from 300 s−1 to 0.01 s−1 for PNIPAM systems
and from 100 s−1 to 0.01 s−1 for Ludox suspensions, holding the shear rate constant
for 30 s at each point along the curve. Prior to each ramp, step-rate experiments were
performed at selected shear rates to evaluate the time required for the samples to
reach a stable steady-state stress, which was found to be shorter than 30 s.
Stress signal acquisition
The acquisition of the raw torque signal has been obtained through a National Instru-
ments NI USB-6008 acquisition board. The system is controlled by software developed
in Python 3.0, utilizing the NI-DAQmx driver software for National Instruments
boards. Using an integrated system directly connected to the ARES RFS rheometer
is not possible. Indeed, the low input impedance of the acquisition board affects the
rheometer’s electrical signals, which distorts the results. It was necessary to build inter-
face electronics between the rheometer and the acquisition board to achieve an input

SI16 Bifurcation diagrams
To uncover the mathematical origin of the ductile-to-brittle transition in the BFF model, we
analyze the fixed points of the dynamical system governed by the effective potential V(θ) =
1
2αθ2−|σθ|λ, where the stationary points θm are determined by the condition ∂V/∂θ = 0. From
a dynamical systems perspective, the parameter λ acts as a structural control variable that
profoundly alters the topology of the phase space, as illustrated in the bifurcation diagrams of
Fig. S11a).
In the regime 0 < λ < 2, the system undergoes a continuous, supercritical pitchfork-like
bifurcation at σ = 0 (Fig. S11a,b). For σ = 0, the origin θm = 0 represents the sole stable
minimum of the potential. Upon increasing σ, the central fixed point undergoes a continuous
loss of stability, giving rise to two symmetric, stable branches that smoothly emerge from
zero. This continuous state-selection describes a gradual plastic yielding, which macroscopically
manifests as a ductile rheological response.

the potential reduces to V(θ) = 1

SI17 Emergence of overshoots and ringing in intra-cycle
stress and fluidity responses
In Fig. S12 (comprising panels a–f), we report the intra-cycle stress-strain (σ(γ)) Lissajous-
Bowditch curves for three representative values of the exponent λ, mapped across a wide range
of the damping factor r. While the choice of r does not significantly alter the macroscopic
steady-state stress attained at large deformations, it exerts a profound influence on the non-
linear transient dynamics, specifically governing the onset of intra-cycle stress overshoots and
inertial-like ringing phenomena.
As λ is progressively increased, we observe a clear evolution characterized by the emergence
of highly localized stress overshoots alongside the development of distinct, high-frequency stress
ringing before the steady state is reached. The overarching physical behavior can be summarized
as follows:
• Low plasticity production exponents (small λ): The yielding transition is charac-
terized by a smooth crossover from elastic recoil to the plastic steady-state regime. This
manifestation takes the form of broad stress overshoots. Upon increasing r, the width of
these overshoots progressively narrows, while the overshoot peak displays a non-monotonic
dependence on damping, eventually vanishing in the strictly over-damped limit (r →∞).
• High plasticity production exponents (large λ): Concomitant with a marked increase
in the amplitude of the overshoot peaks, a well-defined stress ringing emerges. This oscillatory
signature acts as a precursor that layout the rapid attainment of the stationary stress plateau.
We point out that a detailed exploration of the exact mathematical origin of this intra-cycle
ringing—while remaining secondary to the main scope of the present work—is currently under
investigation within a dedicated theoretical study.
