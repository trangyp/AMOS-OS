from a unified N ¯N-interaction perspective

Near-threshold structures have been observed in the cross sections for e+e−→p¯p, e+e−→n¯n,
and several non-baryonic final states in the vicinity of the N ¯
N thresholds. We investigate whether
these structures can be understood as manifestations of a common N ¯
N final-state interaction. The
strong N ¯
N interaction is taken from the chiral EFT description of the coupled 3S1-3D1 system
constrained by low-energy N ¯
N scattering data. With this interaction fixed, the p¯p and n¯n cross
sections are described by fitting only short-distance electromagnetic production sources, which are
assumed to vary slowly over the near-threshold region. The resulting N ¯
N production amplitudes
are then used as input for five inelastic hadronic channels. A simultaneous description of the near-
threshold cross sections is obtained, indicating that the observed structures can be consistently
interpreted as consequences of the same underlying N ¯
N dynamics, without introducing separate
narrow resonances in individual channels.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Near-threshold structures associated with the nucleon-
antinucleon thresholds have been observed in a vari-
ety of processes and have become a recurrent topic in
hadron spectroscopy.
In J/ψ decays, pronounced en-
hancements close to the p¯p threshold have been reported
in the p¯p invariant-mass spectrum [1–4], together with
several other threshold structures in light-meson pro-
duction channels [5–9]. Similar enhancements in the p¯p
invariant-mass distribution have also been observed in
B decays [10–14]. In the timelike electromagnetic sec-
tor, the effective form factors of the proton and neu-
tron show strong rises close to the corresponding thresh-
olds [15–19].
Moreover, nontrivial structures are visi-
ble near the N ¯N thresholds in a number of hadronic
cross sections measured in e+e−annihilation, includ-
ing 3(π+π−), 2(π+π−π0), 2(π+π−)π0, ωπ+π−π0, and
K+K−π+π−[17, 20–23]. The occurrence of such struc-
tures in both baryonic and non-baryonic final states sug-
gests that the opening of the p¯p and n¯n channels may
leave observable imprints beyond direct baryon-pair pro-
duction. This is a generic possibility for near-threshold
phenomena, as required by unitarity and analyticity [24–
26].
This observation raises a natural question: can the
near-threshold structures seen in different channels be
understood as manifestations of a common N ¯N dynam-
ics?
One particularly relevant mechanism is that the
opening of the p¯p and n¯n channels generates nonanalytic
threshold effects, which are then transmitted to other
hadronic final states through the strong interaction.

Several theoretical explanations have been proposed
for some of these near-threshold structures. One class
of interpretations attributes the observed anomalies to
near-threshold baryonium states [27–30] or to N ¯N final-
state interactions [31–52]. Within these approaches, the
N ¯N interaction has been described using phenomeno-
logical potentials [53–57] and, more recently, chiral ef-
fective field theory (EFT) [58, 59].
Such a framework
naturally connects the strong threshold enhancement in
e+e−→N ¯N with possible two-step mechanisms of the
type e+e−→N ¯N →hadrons.
It also allows one to
include effects that are essential for resolving the fine
structure within a few MeV of threshold, such as the
Coulomb interaction, the proton-neutron mass differ-
ence, and the coupled-channel p¯p-n¯n dynamics [43, 51].
Alternative descriptions have also been put forward.
For example, vector-meson interference, in particular in-
volving ρ(1900), has been proposed to describe several
hadronic channels in e+e−annihilation [60], while the
near-threshold structures in J/ψ decays have been inter-
preted in terms of pseudoscalar glueballs or excited η′
states [61–68].
In this work, we study the near-threshold structures
associated with N ¯N production in e+e−annihilation in
the JP C = 1−−sector. We examine whether the thresh-
old behavior observed in both baryonic and non-baryonic
final states can be understood within a unified descrip-
tion based on a single dynamical input for the coupled
3S1-3D1 N ¯N system.
The baryonic channels consid-
ered are p¯p and n¯n, while the non-baryonic channels
are 3(π+π−), 2(π+π−π0), 2(π+π−)π0, ωπ+π−π0, and
K+K−π+π−. Chiral EFT provides a suitable framework
for such an analysis. It has been successfully applied to
the low-energy NN interaction [69–73] and has subse-
quently been extended to the N ¯N sector [37, 58, 59, 74].
Here we use the chiral EFT N ¯N interaction constructed

II.
FRAMEWORK
To describe the near-threshold structures in a uni-
fied manner, we proceed in three steps.
First, the
strong N ¯N interaction is fixed by the chiral EFT de-
scription of the coupled 3S1-3D1 system. Second, keep-
ing this interaction unchanged, we determine the elec-
tromagnetic production amplitudes from the p¯p and n¯n
cross sections.
Third, the non-baryonic channels are
described by channel-dependent short-range production
terms, parametrized as low-order polynomials in energy
to account for slow varying background contributions,
together with the same N ¯N rescattering response.

III.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A.
Cross sections for e+e−→N ¯
N
Using the N ¯N scattering amplitudes obtained from the
chiral EFT potential, we fit the e+e−→p¯p and e+e−→
n¯n cross sections evaluated according to Eq. (11). For the
charged channel, we use the data from Refs. [15–18, 80],
while for the neutral channel we use those from Refs. [19,
81]. The data set contains 54 cross-section points in the
energy range 2mp ≤√s ≤2.0 GeV, which covers the
near-threshold region where the chiral EFT description
of the N ¯N interaction is expected to be applicable.
The fit contains seven real free parameters, correspond-
ing to the four complex particle-basis source coefficients
cα defined in Eq. (6), after fixing the overall phase by tak-
ing c(S,p) to be real and positive. The resulting best-fit
values and uncertainties are listed in Table I. The un-
certainties here quoted for these parameters are statisti-
cal fit errors only and should not be interpreted as EFT
truncation uncertainties. In particular, the source coeffi-
cients absorb order-dependent changes in the N ¯N final-
state interaction. Consequently, the fitted values of the
production parameters can vary substantially from one
chiral order to another. The best-fit curves are shown
in Fig. 1, where the shaded bands indicate the trunca-
tion uncertainties estimated from the order-by-order chi-
ral EFT interactions following Ref. [59]. Notice that the
broadening of the bands at higher energies reflects the
decreasing applicability of the chiral EFT description as
the energy moves farther away from the N ¯N thresholds.
We obtain χ2 = 45.9 for 47 degrees of freedom, corre-
sponding to χ2/dof = 1.0, with individual contributions
χ2
p¯p = 41.2 and χ2
n¯n = 4.7. Both the p¯p and n¯n cross
sections are well reproduced in the fitted energy region.
These results demonstrate that the chiral EFT de-
scription of the N ¯N interaction captures the main near-
threshold enhancements in both the p¯p and n¯n channels.
We note, however, that the CMD-3 points closest to the
p¯p threshold require some care. As for all measured cross
sections, the quoted Born cross sections are extracted
from visible cross sections through experiment-specific
corrections. This extraction becomes particularly sensi-
tive in the immediate threshold region, where the cross
section varies rapidly on the MeV scale and is affected
by initial-state radiation, the beam-energy spread, and
the reconstruction efficiency for slow antiprotons. Since
our theory curves are evaluated at the Born level and
do not implement the CMD-3 smearing and extraction
procedure [17], we do not attempt a point-by-point de-
scription of these fine-scan points.
In particular, no additional narrow resonance is re-
quired to account for the rapid rise of the cross sections
just above thresholds. This conclusion is supported by
the fact that the strong N ¯N input is fixed from low-
energy N ¯N scattering and is not adjusted to reproduce
the threshold structures in the production data.

IV.
CONCLUSION
We have studied the near-threshold structures ob-
served in e+e−annihilation into N ¯N and multi-hadron
final states within a common N ¯N final-state-interaction
framework. The analysis proceeds in three steps. First,
the strong N ¯N interaction is taken from the chiral EFT
description of the coupled 3S1-3D1 system, constrained
by low-energy N ¯N scattering data. Second, the p¯p and
n¯n cross sections are used to determine the short-distance
electromagnetic production of N ¯N, dressed by this fixed
final-state interaction. Third, several inelastic channels
are described by channel-dependent production terms
together with the same universal N ¯N rescattering re-
sponse.
With this setup, the near-threshold behaviors of the
p¯p and n¯n cross sections, as well as the structures ob-
served in several inelastic channels, can be described
consistently by the same coupled-channel N ¯N dynam-
ics. The nearby poles generated by the strong N ¯N in-
teraction play an essential role in shaping the line shapes
around the p¯p and n¯n thresholds.
These
results
indicate
that
the
prominent
near-
threshold structures need not be attributed to separate
narrow resonances introduced independently in different
channels. Rather, they arise naturally from a common
N ¯N final-state interaction, showing that a naive Breit–
Wigner interpretation may be misleading in the near-
threshold region if the underlying rescattering dynamics
is not taken into account. This interpretation is further
supported by the truncation-uncertainty analysis based
on the order-by-order chiral EFT interactions of Ref. [59].
After refitting the short-distance production parameters
