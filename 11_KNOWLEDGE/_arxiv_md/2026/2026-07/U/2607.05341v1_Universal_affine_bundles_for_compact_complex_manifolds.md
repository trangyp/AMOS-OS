Abstract. We construct a universal affine bundle E over a com-
pact complex manifold X equipped with a probability measure µ,
which is affine over the vector space H1,1(X; R), and satisfies a
number of natural properties. The bundle E carries a natural ac-
tion of the automorphism group of (X, µ), and provides potentials
for all ddc-closed (1, 1)-forms on X. We relate our construction
to lifts of the action of the automorphism group of a Calabi–Yau
manifold to its universal torsor.

Universal torsors have been studied extensively, mainly in the con-

In this note, we develop some tools to study the case when X is a
Calabi–Yau manifold, equipped with an action of an infinite group Γ
of automorphisms.
Our main result is Theorem 2.1.10 which constructs, for any compact
complex manifold X equipped with a probability measure µ, a smooth
bundle E →X which is affine over the vector space H1,1(X; R), and
which has a number of natural properties described therein. A key fea-
ture of E is that it carries potentials for all ddc-closed (1, 1)-forms on X.
Additionally, the affine bundles E behave naturally under morphisms of
varieties and push-forward of measures, as described in Theorem 2.1.13.
With this in mind, we expect the following conjecture to hold:
Conjecture (Lift in Calabi–Yau case). Suppose that X is a projective
Calabi–Yau manifold (i.e. the canonical bundle is torsion, and assume
H1(X, OX) = 0), and U is a universal torsor for X.
The action of Aut(X) on X lifts to an action on U, after possibly
passing to a finite index subgroup. More generally, the same should
hold for the group of birational automorphisms of X.
It is clear that if Aut(X) fixes a point, then the action lifts to the
universal torsor. So in a way, the conjecture says that the volume form
on a Calabi–Yau manifold can serve as a substitute for a rational point.
In general, the action of a group Γ on X leads to the action of a
TX-extension of Γ on the universal torsor U, and the lift of the action
is equivalent to the splitting of the short exact sequence:
1 →TX →eΓ →Γ →1.
Our main result towards the above Conjecture is Theorem 3.2.6, which
states that if Γ is a group of automorphisms of a compact complex
manifold X preserving a probability measure, then the above short
exact sequence splits after quotienting by the compact subgroup of
TX(C). This would suffice to establish the Conjecture if we knew that
the associated cohomology class in H2(Γ, TX(C)) is integral. Note that
Theorem 2.1.10 and Theorem 3.2.6 do not require X to be Calabi–Yau,
or even simply-connected.

Remark.
(i) Constructing a universal torsor U, or an affine bundle E which
provides potentials for all (1, 1)-forms, can always be done by
choosing an explicit basis of the Néron–Severi group or H1,1(X).
The difficulty is in extending the group action to the resulting
space, and this is where our universal construction in Theo-
rem 2.1.10 provides a natural solution.
(ii) Are there examples of varieties X with actions of groups Γ on
X where it is not possible to lift the action to the universal
torsor, even after passing to a finite index subgroup in Γ?
In §3.3 we provide some examples of Calabi–Yau manifolds
with actions of finite groups Γ which do not lift to the universal
torsor, as well as some examples of group actions that do not
preserve a measure but nonetheless lift to the universal torsor.
(iii) In [FT23], we constructed canonical currents on the boundary
of the ample cone of a K3 surface X, which are equivariant
under the action of Aut(X).
The affine bundle E that we
construct in this paper carries potentials for these currents,
that behave equivariantly under the action of Aut(X).
(iv) It would be of interest to perform the above constructions in
the situation when Pic0(X) ̸= 0. In this case the universal
torsor should be replaced by a universal semi-abelian variety.

3.3.4. Projective linear virtual lifts. We conclude with a final ob-
