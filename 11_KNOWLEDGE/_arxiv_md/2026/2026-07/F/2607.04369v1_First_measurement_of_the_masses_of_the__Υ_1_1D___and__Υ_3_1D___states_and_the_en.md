We study the processes e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η and e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−at center-of-mass energies
√s=(10.73 – 11.02) GeV using a 142.5 fb−1 data sample, including 122 fb−1 near the Υ(10860) peak
(√s = 10.866 GeV), collected with the Belle detector at the KEKB asymmetric-energy e+e−collider.
From the peak sample, the products of Born cross section times branching fraction are obtained for
σBorn(e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η) or σBorn(e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−) and B(ΥJ(1D) →χb1γ) or B(ΥJ(1D) →
χb2γ) for each ΥJ(1D) state. The corresponding branching fractions for Υ(10860) decays are also
obtained.
The significances of the Υ1(1D), Υ2(1D), and Υ3(1D) signals are 4.8σ, >10σ, and
3.0σ, respectively, including systematic uncertainties.
The mass for Υ2(1D) is measured to be
(10167.0±1.0±0.2) MeV/c2, where the first and second uncertainties are statistical and systematic.
The mass splittings ∆m12 = m(Υ2(1D)) −m(Υ1(1D)) and ∆m23 = m(Υ3(1D)) −m(Υ2(1D)) are
(11.8 ± 1.5 ± 0.4) MeV/c2 and (7.6 ± 2.4 ± 0.6) MeV/c2, respectively. We determine the energy
dependence of the cross sections for e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η and e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−for the Υ1(1D),
Υ2(1D), and Υ3(1D) states, combined.
As a prominent member of the heavy quarkonium fam-
ily, bottomonium serves as an exemplary probe for eluci-
dating the properties of non-perturbative quantum chro-
modynamics [1–4]. To date, only one D-wave bottomo-
nium state, Υ2(1D), has been observed, by CLEO and
BaBar [5, 6]. The relativized quark model, nonrelativistic
constituent quark model, screened potential model, and
the modified relativized quark model with a screening ef-
fect have predicted the mass splittings of the ΥJ(1D) [7–
10]. However, the states Υ1(1D) and Υ3(1D) have not
yet been observed.
Within the hadronic loop mechanism, the branch-
ing fractions for Υ(10860) →ΥJ(1D)η, Υ(11020) →
ΥJ(1D)η, and Υ(10860) →ΥJ(1D)π+π−range in mag-
nitude from 10−4 to 10−3 [11–13]. In a 121.4 fb−1 sample
at √s = 10.866 GeV, Belle studied e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η
and e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−in the recoil mass spectra
of η and π+π−[14, 15], where the Υ1(1D), Υ2(1D),
and Υ3(1D) states were treated as a single component
or with fixed mass splittings. In this work, we separate
Υ1(1D), Υ2(1D), and Υ3(1D) states through exclusive
reconstruction, applying a kinematic constraint, and per-
forming a three-dimensional (3D) fit. To date, there are
no reported experimental results for e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η
and e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−in the energy region around
the Υ(11020) resonance.
In this Letter, we measure the cross sections for
e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η and e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−using
Belle data at √s from 10.73 to 11.02 GeV. We report
first evidence for the Υ1(1D) and Υ3(1D) states, as
well as measurements of the ΥJ(1D) masses.
For the
energy point at √s = 10.866 GeV, we determine the
product of the Born cross section for e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η
(e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−) and the branching fraction of
ΥJ(1D) →χb1,b2γ. The corresponding branching frac-
tions for the Υ(10860) decays are also found.
The data reported here were recorded by the Belle de-
tector [16, 17] at the KEKB asymmetric-energy e+e−
collider [18, 19].
We use ≈1 fb−1 taken at each of 18
center-of-mass (c.m.) energies in the range 10.73 GeV –
11.02 GeV (energy scan data) and a 122 fb−1 sample col-
lected at the Υ(10860), √s=10865.8 MeV (Υ(10860) on-
resonance data). Thus, there are 19 energy points in total
at which we measure cross sections for e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η
and ΥJ(1D)π+π−. A detailed description of the Belle
detector can be found in Refs. [16, 17].
We generate signal Monte Carlo (MC) events with c.m.
energies from 10.73 to 11.02 GeV using the evtgen gen-
erator [20] to determine the reconstruction efficiencies
and signal shapes. Initial-state radiation (ISR) at next-
to-leading order precision in quantum electrodynamics is
simulated with phokhara [21]. For e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η,
the polar angle of the η in the e+e−c.m. system (θη)
is distributed according to (1 + cos2θη), (1 −1
7cos2θη),
and (1 + cos2θη) for Υ1(1D)η, Υ2(1D)η, and Υ3(1D)η
production, respectively. For e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−, the
polar angle of the π+π−system in the e+e−c.m. system
(θπ+π−) is distributed uniformly. Generic MC samples
of e+e−→q¯q (q = u, d, s, c) with the kkmc [22] and
pythia packages and Υ(10860) →B(∗)
(s) ¯B(∗)

with 4 times the luminosity of the data are used to esti-
mate backgrounds. The simulated events are processed
with a detector simulation based on geant3 [23].
We reconstruct the decay chains [7–10]
e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η, e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−,
η →γγ, η →π+π−π0,
Υ1(1D) →χb1γ, Υ2(1D) →χb1,b2γ, Υ3(1D) →χb2γ,
χb1,b2 →Υ(1S)γ, and Υ(1S) →ℓ+ℓ−(ℓ= µ or e).
Event selection and criteria for tracks and particle iden-
tification follow the methods used in Refs. [24, 25].

Photon pairs remaining in the event are used to recon-
struct χb1,b2 and ΥJ(1D) candidates. The photon with
the higher energy (γH) in the e+e−c.m. frame is used

To calibrate the pho-

D∗0 →D0γ, π0 →γγ, and η →γγ are used [26].
We require the numbers of charged tracks to be two,
four, and four in the channels e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η(→
γγ), e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η(→π+π−π0), and e+e−→

background from γ →e+e−conversions is removed by
requiring cosθπ+π−< 0.95, where θπ+π−is the angle be-
tween π+ and π−in the laboratory frame.
To reduce backgrounds with additional particles in the
final state, a 5C kinematic fit using kfit [27] is per-
formed, where the four-momentum of the final state sys-

and the invariant mass of the lepton pair is constrained to
the Υ(1S) nominal mass [28]. The χ2
5C/ndof value of the

the signal. In events with multiple candidates, only the
candidate with the smallest value of χ2
5C is retained. The
fractions of selected events with multiple candidates are

ΥJ(1D)η(→γγ), e+e−→ΥJ(1D)η(→π+π−π0), and
e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−, respectively.
These values are
consistent with the multiple candidate rates observed in
the data. The fractions of correctly reconstructed candi-

π+π−π0), and e+e−→ΥJ(1D)π+π−are found to be
94%, 97%, and 98%, respectively.

The mass of Υ2(1D) is found to be (10167.0±1.0±0.2)
MeV/c2. The first and second uncertainties are statisti-

nosity, and branching fractions of intermediate states.
The additive systematic uncertainties are those from the
photon energy calibration and fit model.
The other
sources of systematic uncertainties are multiplicative.
We study the additive systematic uncertainties from
the photon energy calibration and fitting procedure as
follows. The resulting uncertainties of the peak positions
are 1.1 MeV/c2 in both the M ′(ℓ+ℓ−γH) and ∆M dis-
tributions; the uncertainties in the resolutions are 0.9
and 1.1 MeV/c2 in the M ′(ℓ+ℓ−γH) and ∆M distribu-
tions. For the results obtained with the fitting method,
we change the signal peak positions and resolutions by
±1σ. We increase the order of the polynomial describing
the background by one and change the fit interval. We
vary the ratio N sig(η →γγ)/N sig(η →π+π−π0) from
4.2 to 4.1 and 4.3 to account for the uncertainty of the η
decay branching fractions, and take the difference in the
yield as a systematic uncertainty. For each fitted quan-
tity, we perform all possible combinations of the above
variations and conservatively consider the largest devia-
tion of fit results compared to the nominal result as the
additive systematic uncertainty for the corresponding en-
ergy point.
