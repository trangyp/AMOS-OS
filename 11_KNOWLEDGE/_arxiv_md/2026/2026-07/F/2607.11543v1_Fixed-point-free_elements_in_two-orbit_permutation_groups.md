FIXED-POINT-FREE ELEMENTS IN TWO-ORBIT
PERMUTATION GROUPS
JESSICA ANZANELLO AND SEAN EBERHARD
Abstract. Let G be a two-orbit permutation group on n > 2 points.
We show that G contains either a derangement or an element of prime-
power order with a unique fixed point. As a corollary, if the orbits of
G have length n1 and n2 and gcd(n1, n2 −1) = gcd(n1 −1, n2) = 1,
then G contains a derangement. The special case n1 = n2 was recently
conjectured by Ellis and Harper and proved under various restrictive
hypotheses. We prove our result by reducing to the case of simple groups
and leveraging the classification of normal 2-coverings of simple groups
due to Bubboloni, Spiga, and Weigel.
1. Introduction
If G ≤Sym(Ω) is a permutation group acting on a finite set Ω, we write
fix(g) for the number of fixed points on Ωof an element g ∈G, and we say g
is a derangement if fix(g) = 0.
A classical theorem of Jordan states that every finite transitive group
G ≤Sym(Ω) with |Ω| > 1 contains at least one derangement. A deep theorem
of Fein–Kantor–Schacher [3] adds that some derangement has prime-power
order.
In this note we obtain the following generalization to two-orbit permutation
groups. The theorem of Fein–Kantor–Schacher coincides with the special
case in which one of the orbits is trivial.
Theorem 1. Let G ≤Sym(Ω) be a two-orbit permutation group with |Ω| > 2.
Then G contains either
(1) a derangement, or
(2) an element of prime-power order with a unique fixed point.
Note that, in conclusion (1), we cannot require the derangement to have
prime-power order. Indeed, C6 has a two-orbit action of degree 5, with point
stabilizers C2 and C3, in which every derangement has order 6.
Two-orbit permutation groups arise naturally in several contexts. For
instance, they occur in graph theory as automorphism groups of graphs that
are edge-transitive but not vertex-transitive. They also lead naturally to
normal 2-coverings of finite groups, whose motivations and applications are

Our original motivation for formulating Theorem 1 is a recent conjecture
of Ellis and Harper [2] (generalizing [8, Proposition 1]), which predicts that
every two-orbit permutation group with equal-sized nontrivial orbits contains
a derangement. Ellis and Harper proved their conjecture under various
restrictive hypotheses, for example if G is soluble, or almost simple, or
primitive on at least one of the orbits. Further results were obtained by
Lee–Popiel–Verret [5].
The Ellis–Harper conjecture follows easily from Theorem 1, and indeed
the following corollary is more general. From now on, we say that a two-orbit
permutation group has orbit type (n1, n2) if its orbits have lengths n1 and n2.
Corollary 2. Let G ≤Sym(n) be a two-orbit permutation group of orbit
type (n1, n2), where n = n1 +n2 > 2. If gcd(n1, n2 −1) = gcd(n1 −1, n2) = 1,
then G contains a derangement.
Proof. Let Ω1 and Ω2 be the orbits, where |Ωi| = ni. Suppose g ∈G is a p-
element with a unique fixed point x. If, say, x ∈Ω1 then p divides n1 −1 and
n2, and similarly if x ∈Ω2. Therefore if gcd(n1, n2 −1) = gcd(n1 −1, n2) = 1
then G cannot contain an element of prime-power order with a unique fixed
point, so Theorem 1 implies that G contains a derangement.
□
We record the following graph-theoretic consequence of Corollary 2, which
was already observed as a consequence of the Ellis–Harper conjecture in
[2, Remark 8].
Corollary 3. Let Γ be a finite edge-transitive regular graph of degree n > 1.
Then Aut(Γ) contains a derangement in its action on the vertices of Γ.
Proof. If Γ is vertex-transitive then the result follows from Jordan’s theorem.
Otherwise, since Γ is regular and edge-transitive, Aut(Γ) has exactly two
orbits on the vertices, and Γ is bipartite.
Moreover, regularity implies
that these two orbits have the same size. The result therefore follows from
Corollary 2.
□
The coprimality conditions in Corollary 2 are sufficient but not necessary
for the existence of a derangement in a two-orbit permutation group. For
instance, every two-orbit permutation group of orbit type (3, 55) contains a
derangement, although this pair does not satisfy the hypotheses of Corollary 2.
This suggests the following natural question.
Question 4. Which pairs (n1, n2) of integers have the property that every
two-orbit permutation group of orbit type (n1, n2) contains a derangement?
The proof of Theorem 1 is easy to outline.
