Abstract
A numerical investigation of steady forced convection heat transfer from an isoflux circu-
lar cylinder immersed in the liquid metal Galinstan is presented. The governing stream-
function, vorticity, and energy equations are solved using a fourth-order compact finite
difference scheme in cylindrical coordinates (FOCS–CC) coupled with a stable pseudo-
time iteration (PTI) technique. The influence of the Reynolds number (1 ≤Re ≤600) on
the flow and heat transfer characteristics is systematically investigated for Galinstan with
a Prandtl number of Pr = 0.025. The performance and accuracy of the proposed scheme
are first established through grid independence studies and validation against previously
published numerical results for the average Nusselt number and total drag coeﬀicient
over a range of Reynolds numbers. Excellent agreement with the available literature con-
firms the reliability and robustness of the present formulation. The effects of Reynolds
number (1 ≤Re ≤600) on the flow and thermal fields are examined through stream-
line patterns, isotherm distributions, and local Nusselt number variations. The results
reveal that increasing the Reynolds number promotes flow separation, enlarges the wake
region, intensifies downstream thermal transport, and significantly enhances convective
heat transfer from the cylinder surface. Furthermore, a new empirical correlation for the
average Nusselt number is proposed for Galinstan fluid over the Reynolds number range
1 ≤Re ≤600, exhibiting excellent agreement with the numerical data with a coeﬀicient
of determination of R2 = 0.99939.
Keywords —Forced convection, Circular cylinder, FOCS–CC, isoflux thermal boundary
condition, PTI

Introduction
The study of forced convection heat transfer around circular cylinders is of considerable
practical importance because of its widespread use in thermal engineering systems.

Heat transfer from circular cylinders has been investigated through both analytical and
experimental studies by Khan et al. (2006), Grosh and Cess (1958), and Ishiguro et al.
(1976). Subsequently, numerous numerical and experimental investigations have focused
on forced convection heat transfer from an isothermal circular cylinder. Notable contri-
butions include the works of Dennis and Chang (1970), Fornberg (1980), Tritton (1959),
Dennis et al. (1968), Baranyi (2003), Erturk and Gokcol (2018), and Abdelhady and
Wood (2019), who examined the problem over a broad range of Reynolds numbers and
flow conditions.
For the isoflux thermal boundary condition, Ahmad (1996) employed a finite difference
method with a hybrid differencing scheme to study forced convection heat transfer for
100 ≤Re ≤500 at Pr = 0.7. Later, Khan et al. (2005) applied the Von Kármán–
Pohlhausen integral boundary layer approximation to analyse heat transfer from circular
cylinders subjected to both isothermal and isoflux boundary conditions by solving the
coupled momentum and energy equations. This problem was subsequently revisited by
Bharti et al. (2007), who utilised the QUICK discretization scheme to investigate the
ranges 10 ≤Re ≤45 and 0.7 ≤Pr ≤400. Furthermore, Paramane and Sharma (2010)
investigated forced convection heat transfer from an isoflux circular cylinder using the
finite volume method for 20 ≤Re ≤160 at Pr = 0.7. Sufyan et al. (2015) analysed flow
and forced convection heat transfer past a circular cylinder with both isothermal and
isoflux thermal boundary conditions using the finite volume method based on the Semi-
Implicit Method for Pressure-Linked Equations (SIMPLE) algorithm for 5 ≤Re ≤40
and 0.7 ≤Pr ≤67.
Although considerable progress has been made in the numerical simulation of forced
convection around bluff bodies, most existing studies employ second-order accurate dis-
cretization techniques. Fourth-order compact formulations provide an attractive alterna-
tive by achieving higher spatial accuracy while retaining a compact computational stencil.
In this direction, fourth-order compact schemes have been successfully developed for the
Navier–Stokes and energy equations in spherical coordinates by Raju et al. (2018), Nath
et al. (2019), and Nath and Hema Sundar Raju (2022). Subsequently, the applicabil-
ity of the fourth-order compact approach was extended to forced convection of liquid
metals over a flat plate in elliptical cylindrical coordinates using a pseudo-time iteration
technique by Hema Sundar Raju (2021). Galinstan (Pr=0.025), a low-Prandtl-number
liquid metal alloy with exceptionally high thermal conductivity, has attracted consider-
able attention for advanced thermal management applications, including electronic cool-
ing, compact heat exchangers, and liquid-metal-based energy systems Cai et al. (2026).
Although the physical flow past a circular cylinder becomes unsteady beyond the crit-
ical Reynolds number, steady solutions of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations
have been successfully computed by Dennis and Chang (1970) and Fornberg (1985) up
to Re =100 and Re = 600, respectively, demonstrating that the steady solution branch

Results and Discussions
To assess the sensitivity of the proposed FOCS–CC scheme to grid resolution, a grid
independence study was conducted using uniformly refined computational grids, namely
17 × 17, 33 × 33, 65 × 65 and 129 × 129, for Reynolds numbers of Re = 100, 300, 500
and 600. The corresponding Nu values are presented in Table 1. It is observed that the
computed values obtained on the 65 × 65 and 129 × 129 grids are in close agreement,
indicating that the numerical solution is essentially grid independent. Therefore, the
129 × 129 grid is adopted for all simulations to ensure high numerical accuracy.
The streamline contours in the vicinity of the circular cylinder are presented in Figure 2
for Reynolds numbers Re = 1, 200, and 600 at Pr = 0.025. As shown in Figure 2, the flow
remains fully attached to the cylinder surface at the low Reynolds number (Re = 1), and
no flow separation is observed due to the dominance of viscous forces. As the Reynolds
number increases to 200 and 600, flow separation occurs behind the cylinder, leading to
the formation of a wake region. Furthermore, the separated wake becomes progressively
larger with increasing Reynolds number, indicating the increasing influence of inertial
forces over viscous effects.
The temperature distribution in the vicinity of the circular cylinder is presented in Fig-
ure 3 for Re = 1, 200, and 600 at a fixed Prandtl number of Pr = 0.025. At the low
Reynolds number (Re = 1), the isotherms exhibit nearly concentric ring-shaped contours
around the cylinder, indicating that heat transfer is predominantly governed by thermal
diffusion. As the Reynolds number increases to 200 and 600, the flow velocity increases,
thereby enhancing the convective transport of heat. Consequently, the isotherms become
more closely packed near the upstream stagnation point, indicating larger temperature
gradients at the cylinder surface. Furthermore, the isotherms are progressively stretched
and convected downstream with increasing Reynolds number, resulting in a more pro-
nounced thermal plume and enhanced convective heat transfer. Despite this downstream
stretching, the thermal field remains relatively diffuse compared with conventional fluids
because of the exceptionally high thermal diffusivity of Galinstan (Pr = 0.025). As a re-

even at higher Reynolds numbers. This interplay between enhanced convective transport
and dominant thermal diffusion is a characteristic feature of low-Prandtl-number liquid
metals and distinguishes their thermal behaviour from that of moderate- and high Prandtl
number fluids, where the thermal boundary layer remains comparatively thin.
Figure 4 illustrates the angular variation of the local Nusselt number for different Reynolds
numbers at a fixed Prandtl number of Pr = 0.025. As the Reynolds number increases,
flow separation becomes more pronounced, leading to the development of a larger wake
behind the cylinder. This significantly influences the temperature field and the local heat
transfer characteristics. Consequently, the angular variation of the local Nusselt num-
ber becomes increasingly pronounced with increasing Reynolds number. While the local
Nusselt number varies only slightly with the angular position at low Reynolds numbers,
a much steeper variation is observed at higher Reynolds numbers due to the stronger
convective effects associated with the enlarged separated wake.
Tables 2 and 3 summarize the numerical predictions of the average Nusselt number (Nu)
and total drag coeﬀicient (CD), respectively, for a range of Reynolds numbers at Pr = 0.7.
The computed results are validated against the published numerical data reported by
Dennis and Chang (1970), Fornberg (1980), Ahmad (1996), and Morgan (1975). The
close agreement between the present and reported values demonstrates the accuracy and
robustness of the proposed FOCS–CC in predicting both the hydrodynamic and thermal
characteristics of the flow.

Conclusions
A fourth-order compact finite difference scheme in cylindrical coordinates (FOCS–CC)
integrated with a pseudo-time iteration (PTI) technique has been successfully developed
to investigate steady forced convection heat transfer from an isoflux circular cylinder
immersed in the liquid metal Galinstan. The numerical formulation was validated through
comparisons of the average Nusselt number and total drag coeﬀicient with previously
published results, demonstrating excellent agreement and confirming the accuracy and
reliability of the proposed method.
• A grid independence study established the adequacy of the selected computational
grid.
• The flow and thermal characteristics were examined for Reynolds numbers in the
range 1 ≤Re ≤600 at a fixed Prandtl number of Pr = 0.025.
The results
showed that the flow remains attached to the cylinder at low Reynolds numbers,
whereas increasing the Reynolds number promotes flow separation and enlarges the
wake region behind the cylinder. The isotherms become increasingly distorted and
convected downstream with increasing Reynolds number, indicating enhanced con-
vective heat transfer. In addition, the angular variation of the local Nusselt number
becomes more pronounced at higher Reynolds numbers owing to the stronger in-
fluence of flow separation on the thermal field.
• The present results reveal that increasing the Reynolds number enhances the aver-
age Nusselt number throughout the investigated range. However, the enhancement
becomes progressively smaller at higher Reynolds numbers, indicating diminishing
returns in convective heat transfer. This behaviour is a characteristic feature of
low-Prandtl-number liquid metals, where the high thermal diffusivity of Galinstan
continues to play a significant role even under stronger convective conditions.
• An empirical correlation for the average Nusselt number was proposed for Galinstan
over the Reynolds number range 1 ≤Re ≤600. The proposed correlation predicts
the numerical results with excellent accuracy, yielding a coeﬀicient of determination
of R2 = 0.99939. Overall, the present FOCS–CC provides an accurate numerical
framework for simulating forced convection heat transfer around circular cylinders
involving low-Prandtl-number liquid metals.
