Finite-frequency magnetic common baths in ferromagnetic planar cavities
O. J. Kwon1
1Independent Institute for Theoretical Studies, Yongin, Republic of Korea∗
We formulate the finite-frequency magnetic common bath of two spin probes in a ferromagnetic
planar cavity.
The probes couple to the retarded magnetic Green tensor of the cavity, whose
imaginary and real parts determine the collective decay kernel γ12 and the Lamb-shift kernel Ω12.
We evaluate these kernels for a finite-thickness scalar TE channel formed by a ferromagnetic film on
a nonmagnetic conducting substrate, using the transverse diagonal Polder permeability µ⊥(ω, B)
as the magnetic input. The normalization is fixed by the free-space magnetic-dipole decay rate.
In the ω →0 limit, the constant-reflection benchmark reproduces the static image-series reference,
while the finite slab retains the corresponding static TE reflection amplitude. For a t = 200 nm
film described by a representative Ni-like parameter set in a micron-scale mid-gap cavity, the GHz
probe transition samples the positive-frequency response of the body-assisted magnetic reservoir at
millikelvin temperature. On resonance, the off-diagonal linewidth splitting reaches about two thirds
of the single-spin scattering linewidth at ρ = 3 µm. The resulting linewidth splitting and collective
Lamb shift provide finite-frequency counterparts of the static TE coupling-frequency shift.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Planar magnetic cavities provide a controlled reser-
voir for spin probes because their boundary response
reshapes magnetic-field fluctuations in the gap.
At fi-
nite frequency, the reservoir is described by the retarded
magnetic Green tensor. The off-diagonal projections of
this tensor determine whether two separated probes de-
cay independently or through a common bath. They also
generate a coherent exchange shift between the probes.
Physically, the same body-assisted magnetic-field fluctu-
ations couple to both probes, producing a correlated off-
diagonal decay channel whose strength is tuned by the
ferromagnetic bias.
The zero-frequency limit of the same TE boundary
channel gives the static Ising coupling-frequency shift
studied in Ref. [1]. This shift appears between two lo-
calized Zeeman probes as the magnetic state of the films
is changed.
The present work addresses the real-frequency sector
of the same planar scattering geometry.
A GHz spin
probe at millikelvin temperature samples the positive-
frequency response of the body-assisted magnetic reser-
voir, whose spectral density is set by the imaginary part
of the retarded magnetic Green tensor. We separate this
tensor into a free-space part and a scattering correction,
and the benchmark figures evaluate the cavity-induced
scattering contribution. The ON/OFF signal is defined
as the difference between resonant and detuned ferro-
magnetic bias settings; it cancels bias-independent off-
sets and, in the fixed-frequency benchmark, isolates the
bias dependence of the scattering correction. The rele-
vant open-system quantities are the collective decay ma-
trix and the coherent exchange matrix. For two magnetic
two-level systems, the off-diagonal elements of these ma-
trices are the common-bath kernels γ12 and Ω12 evalu-

surfaces have been studied in the atom-chip setting [2–

In mag-

probe’s relaxation rate to the imaginary part of the dy-

γ12 and Ω12 studied here are the corresponding common-

This formulation uses the macroscopic-QED Green-
tensor description of emitters in absorbing and dispersive
finite geometries [6–9]. The collective-emission structure
follows the Dicke–Lehmberg framework [10–16]; in struc-
tured environments, the coherent exchange and collective
decay are determined by the real and imaginary parts of
the retarded Green tensor [17].
For planar interfaces,
the Green tensor is expressed through Fresnel scattering
amplitudes [18–20]. We apply this construction to mag-
netic transition moments using the curl-defined magnetic
Green tensor [7, 8]. Related Green-tensor treatments of
surface-modified collective emission and resonant dipole–
dipole interactions appear in other structured electro-
magnetic environments [21–25].
This work places the static coupling-frequency shift
∆f and the real-frequency open-system kernels γ12 and
Ω12 within a common magnetic Green-tensor normaliza-
tion. We then use the same finite-frequency framework
to evaluate the off-diagonal decay and exchange kernels
for two probes in the mid-gap region of a finite-thickness
ferromagnetic planar cavity, with the ferromagnetic res-
onance (FMR) bias serving as a spectroscopic tuning pa-
rameter. The resulting linewidth splitting and collective
Lamb shift provide differential magnetic-kernel observ-
ables and fix the normalization and extraction protocol
for a corresponding gyrotropic extension.

tensor is evaluated. In an NV implementation, however,
the same magnetic field used to tune the ferromagnet

GBB[ωη(B), B].
(18)
The benchmark figures fix ωpr, corresponding to the com-
pensated or frequency-tracked interpretation. This iso-

direct Zeeman shift of the probe transition. Experimen-
tally, this can be approached with either a transverse-
bias geometry or compensation of the longitudinal field
component at the selected NV axis, using the standard
NV ground-state spin structure [34]. As alternatives to
explicit field compensation, dressed-state sensing reduces
the first-order field sensitivity of the probe transition [36],
and optimized-control protocols make the magnetometry
readout robust against drifts in the driving field [37].

BENCHMARK SCOPE
Using Γ+ −Γ−= 2γ12, the differential common-bath

These observables are off-diagonal elements of the collec-

removes bias-independent spectral offsets, while the diag-

A practical extraction protocol compares the collective
spectra at Bon and Boff, repeats the measurement un-
der B →−B when allowed by the geometry, tracks the
probe transition frequency during the bias sweep, and
measures the local linewidths at the same bias settings,
since these linewidths set the background against which
the off-diagonal splitting is resolved.
At T = 20 mK the thermal factor is nth ≃10−3. In NV
ensembles, the Raman and Orbach channels are strongly
suppressed on cooling, while the low-temperature relax-
ation is set by sample-dependent cross-relaxation and
environmental noise [39]; this residual linewidth must
be treated as an experimentally measured background.
Residual surface- and film-noise contributions are then
quantified through the measured diagonal linewidths and
the cavity-induced local correction δγ(zz)
11 .

VI.
CONCLUSION
We have formulated the finite-frequency magnetic
common-bath response of a ferromagnetic planar cavity
in terms of real-frequency magnetic Green tensors, with
a normalization directly comparable to the static TE
coupling-frequency shift. The finite-thickness reciprocal
TE benchmark, built from the transverse diagonal Polder
permeability, gives bias-tunable off-diagonal decay and
collective Lamb-shift kernels for two mid-gap spin probes.
In the constant-reflection limit, the ω →0 kernel reduces
to the magnetostatic image-series reference; for the fi-
nite slab, the same limit retains the static slab reflection
amplitude and fixes the normalization shared with the
companion static calculation.
In the millikelvin GHz regime, the linewidth split-
ting, collective Lamb shift, and local linewidths are ob-
servables of the body-assisted magnetic reservoir, whose
fluctuation spectrum is shaped by the ferromagnetic
films and the cavity geometry. Together with the zero-
frequency TE Ising coupling-frequency shift ∆f, the
pair {γ12, Ω12} provides the dynamic counterpart of the
static magnetic response.
The scalar reciprocal calcu-
lation establishes the finite-frequency normalization and
ON/OFF extraction protocol for magnetic common-bath
measurements in ferromagnetic planar cavities.
The
same formalism extends to biased gyrotropic films by re-
placing the scalar TE amplitude with the full TE/TM
reflection matrix.
