FUNCTION SPACES ON SEPARABLE COMPACT LINES
KACPER KUCHARSKI
ABSTRACT. In this paper, we provide a complete isomorphism classification of
the spaces Cp(K) of real-valued continuous functions endowed with the topology
of pointwise convergence for separable compact lines K of weight ω1, under the
assumption of Baumgartner’s Axiom BA. More specifically, we show that, up to
linear homeomorphism, there are exactly two function spaces Cp(K) for such K.
We also construct an example of a separable compact line K of weight 2ω neither
of whose spaces of continuous functions, Cp(K) and Cw(K), is homeomorphic to
its square.
1. INTRODUCTION.
The study of function spaces of the form C(X), equipped with various natural
topologies, is a central theme both in topology and functional analysis. A recur-
ring question in this area is to what extent the structure of the underlying space X
determines the topological or linear–topological properties of the corresponding
function space. In particular, classification problems for spaces C(X) and Cp(X),
as well as questions concerning their factorization properties (e.g. whether they
are homeomorphic or isomorphic to their squares) have attracted considerable at-
tention.
A compact line is any linearly ordered compact topological space. In their recent
work, Korpalski, Koszmider and Marciszewski [10, Theorem 1.3] proved that, as-
suming the Baumgartner’s Axiom BA, all Banach spaces C(K), where K is a sep-
arable compact line of weight ω1, are isomorphic. In particular, it is easy to see,
that C(K) is isomorphic to C(K) × C(K), for all such K (see [10, Corollary 1.4]).
The aim of this paper is to investigate to what extent analogous results hold for
the spaces Cp(K), that is, the spaces of real–valued continuous functions equipped
with the topology of pointwise convergence. It turns out that this situation some-
what differs from that of Banach spaces C(K).
First, we consider separable compact lines of weight ω1. Under the assumption
of BA, we obtain a complete classification of the spaces Cp(K) for such K. More
precisely, we show that there exist exactly two separable compact lines L0 and L1

2. NOTATION.
All spaces considered in this paper are assumed to be Tychonoff topological
spaces. For a space X, by C(X) we denote the set of all real-valued continuous
functions on X, and by Cp(X) the space C(X) endowed with the topology of point-
wise convergence. If X is compact, then Cw(X) denotes the space C(X) equipped
with the weak topology.
As usual, ω denotes the first infinite ordinal, identified with the set of natural
numbers {0, 1, 2, . . . }, and ω1 denotes the first uncountable ordinal. A set is called
countable if it is either finite or countably infinite. For sets X and Y, by X ⊂Y we
mean that X is contained in Y or that X is equal to Y.
For spaces X and Y, we write X ≃Y whenever X and Y are homeomorphic.
If the spaces X and Y additionally carry linear structures, then by an isomorphism
we mean a linear homeomorphism, and we write X ∼Y. In particular, this termi-
nology is used both for Banach spaces of the form C(K) and for spaces Cw(K) and
Cp(K). The symbol X ⊕Y denotes the topological disjoint union of spaces X and
Y. By dimension, we always mean the topological covering dimension dim (see
[4]).

3. THE LINEAR–TOPOLOGICAL STRUCTURE OF Cp(K) FOR SEPARABLE COMPACT
LINES K OF WEIGHT ω1 UNDER BA.
Let us recall that a subset A of a space X is called ω1–dense, if |A ∩U| = ω1 for
any nonempty open U ⊂X. The Baumgartner’s Axiom is the following statement:
BA: Every two ω1–dense subsets of reals are order isomorphic.
It was proved by Baumgartner (cf. [2]), that BA is consistent with ZFC. Below,
we will investigate the linear and topological structure of the spaces Cp(K) for
separable compact lines K of weight ω1. In particular, we aim at providing the
following isomorphic classification of such spaces, under the assumption of BA.
Theorem 3.1 (BA). Let K be a separable compact line of weight ω1. Fix an ω1–dense set
B ⊂(0, 1) and let L0 = IB and L1 = IB ⊕I. Then Cp(K) ∼Cp(Li), if dim(K) = i.
As a consequence we will obtain that all such spaces are isomorphic with their
square, which shows that the assumption of BA is critical. Indeed, below in Section
4 we provide an example (see Theorem 4.6) of a separable compact line K of weight
continuum, such that the space Cp(K) is not even homeomorphic to the space
Cp(K) × Cp(K). This example, together with the aforementioned Theorem 3.1,
will show, that the sentence
If K is a separable compact line of weight ω1, then the space Cp(K) is isomorphic to its
square Cp(K) × Cp(K)
is independent of the axioms of Set Theory ZFC.

acknowledge the similarities in the proof below, to the proof of the "Banach space

first provide a series of lemmas, from which the proof of Theorem 3.1 will follow.
