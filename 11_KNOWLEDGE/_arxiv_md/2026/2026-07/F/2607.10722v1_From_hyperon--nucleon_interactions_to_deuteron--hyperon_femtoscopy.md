From hyperon–nucleon interactions to deuteron–hyperon femtoscopy

We investigate the low-energy scattering and femtoscopic correlation functions of the d −Λ,
d −Σ, and d −Ξ systems within a microscopic folding approach. The effective deuteron–hyperon
interactions are constructed by folding the HAL-QCD hyperon–nucleon potentials with the deuteron
wave function, while the spin and isospin structures are treated through Wigner-6j recoupling
coefficients. Using the resulting interactions, we calculate the scattering parameters and momentum
correlation functions for all spin channels. No bound states are found for the dΛ, dΣ, or dΞ systems.
Nevertheless, the dΛ correlation exhibits a pronounced low-momentum enhancement associated with
a large scattering length and a near-threshold pole, whereas the dΣ correlation is suppressed by its
predominantly repulsive interaction. The neutral dΞ0 system shows only a moderate enhancement,
while the charged dΞ−correlation is strongly amplified by the attractive Coulomb interaction. We
further investigate feed-down effects from Σ0, Σ(1385), and Ξ decays using Monte Carlo response
matrices and demonstrate that these decays clearly modify the observable dΛ correlation.
Our
results provide quantitative predictions for future femtoscopic measurements and establish deuteron–
hyperon correlations as a sensitive probe of hyperon–nucleus interactions.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The interaction between hyperons and nucleons plays
a fundamental role in nuclear and hadronic physics. It
governs the formation and structure of hypernuclei, de-
termines the properties of strange nuclear matter, and
provides essential input for understanding the composi-
tion and equation of state of neutron stars [1, 2]. While
nucleon–nucleon interactions have been determined with
high precision through abundant scattering data, direct
experimental information on the hyperon–nucleon (Y N,
with Y = Λ, Σ, Ξ) interaction remains rather limited be-
cause of the short lifetime of hyperons [3, 4].
Conse-
quently, considerable efforts have been devoted to con-
straining the Y N interaction using phenomenological
meson-exchange models [5–7], chiral effective field the-
ory [8, 9], and lattice QCD [10, 11].
Since hypernuclei are formed by binding one or more
hyperons to ordinary nuclei, their binding energies, ex-
citation spectra, and decay properties are determined
by the underlying hyperon–nucleus interaction. There-
fore, hypernuclei provide a unique laboratory for prob-
ing the Y N interaction, especially its spin-dependent
components [12, 13]. The first step beyond the elemen-
tary Y N system is the interaction between a hyperon
and the lightest bound nucleus, the deuteron.
As the
simplest hyperon–nucleus system, the deuteron–hyperon
(dY ) interaction provides a direct connection between
the underlying Y N force and the structure of hypernu-
clei [14]. The hypertriton, 3
ΛH, has long been discussed
as a weakly bound dΛ cluster, although its microscopic

structure is more accurately described within a three-
body ΛNN framework. Its extremely small Λ separation
energy nevertheless makes it highly sensitive to the low-
energy Λd interaction. Similarly, the existence and prop-
erties of light Σ and Ξ hypernuclei are governed by the
corresponding dΣ and dΞ interactions.
Precise knowl-
edge of deuteron–hyperon interactions is therefore indis-
pensable for developing a quantitative description of light
hypernuclei.
Femtoscopic correlation has emerged as a powerful
experimental tool for probing hadron–hadron interac-
tions [15–20]. The correlation function at small relative
momentum is determined jointly by the emission source
and the final-state interaction, enabling the extraction of
scattering parameters even when direct scattering exper-
iments are impractical [15, 21]. This technique has been
successfully applied to nucleon–nucleon [22], hyperon–
nucleon [22–26], and hyperon–hyperon [22, 27, 28] sys-
tems in high-energy collisions. Extending femtoscopy to
deuteron–hyperon pairs offers a complementary approach
for investigating effective hyperon–nucleus interactions.
Since the deuteron serves as the lightest composite nu-
cleus, the measured d −Y correlation functions are di-
rectly sensitive to the low-energy d −Y scattering am-
plitude and can provide independent constraints on the
existence of shallow bound or virtual states, as well as on
the corresponding scattering length and effective range.
Experimentally, deuterons and strange baryons are
abundantly produced in heavy-ion collisions over a broad
energy range.
d −Λ correlation has been measured
in Au+Au collisions at √sNN = 3 GeV by the STAR
experiment at RHIC [29].
It will be also particularly
feasible at STAR BES-II, CBM@FAIR [30], HIAF [31],
and future NICA experiments, where large hyperon and
deuteron yields and excellent particle identification are

FOLDING POTENTIALS FOR THE dY

The folding potential is a well-established microscopic
approach for constructing effective interactions between
a composite nucleus and an external hadron [32]. The
basic idea is that the interaction between the projectile
and the nucleus arises from the coherent sum of the el-
ementary two-body interactions between the projectile
and each constituent nucleon. Assuming that the inter-
nal structure of the nucleus remains unchanged during
the scattering process, the many-body interaction can
be reduced to an effective one-body potential by averag-
ing the underlying hadron–nucleon interaction over the
nuclear wave function. As a result, the effective nucleus–
hadron potential naturally incorporates both the spatial
structure of the nucleus and the microscopic dynamics of
the elementary interaction.

tential of A −Y system can generally be written as

IV.
FEED-DOWN CONTRIBUTIONS
Besides primordial produced dΛ pairs, the experimen-
tally measured d −Λ correlation function also receives
contributions from the decays of heavier hyperons, in-
cluding mostly Σ0 →Λγ, Σ(1385) →Λπ, and Ξ →
Λπ [43].
Since the lifetimes of these parent hyperons
are much longer than the timescale of the strong final-
state interaction, the parent dY (Y = Σ0, Σ(1385), Ξ)
pair first develops its femtoscopic correlation before the
hyperon decays.
The decay subsequently changes the
relative momentum of the daughter dΛ pair, thereby re-
distributing the parent correlation over the measured dΛ
momentum.
The feed-down contribution from Σ0 can be treated
explicitly, since the dΣ0 interaction is calculated in the
present work and the subsequent electromagnetic decay
Σ0 →Λγ is well understood. The corresponding response
matrix can be obtained by Monte Carlo simulation of the
decay kinematics.
The treatment of the Σ(1385) feed-down is less
straightforward. At present, no reliable Σ(1385)N inter-
action is available from either lattice QCD or phenomeno-
logical models, preventing the construction of a realistic
dΣ(1385) potential. Therefore, our baseline calculation
neglects the corresponding final-state interaction and as-
sumes CdΣ(1385)(k) = 1.
To estimate the uncertainty
associated with this approximation, we also consider an
alternative scenario in which the unknown dΣ(1385) in-
teraction is approximated by the J = 3/2 dΣ correla-
tion, CdΣ(1385)(k) ≃CJ=3/2
dΣ
(k), motivated by the fact
that the Σ(1385) is a spin-3/2 resonance. The difference
between these two prescriptions is taken as a systematic
uncertainty associated with the unknown Σ(1385)N in-
teraction.
Finally, the contribution from Ξ feed-down depends
on the experimental reconstruction procedure. Since the
weak decay Ξ →Λπ occurs far outside the femtoscopic
region, its contribution should be included only if sec-
ondary Λ hyperons from Ξ decays are retained in the
experimental sample. Otherwise, it is naturally removed
by the selections used to reject weak-decay backgrounds.

V.
SUMMARY
In this paper, we constructed microscopic d–Y folding
potentials from HAL-QCD hyperon–nucleon interactions
by explicitly incorporating spin and isospin recoupling.
The resulting interactions were used to calculate low-
energy scattering observables and femtoscopic correlation
functions for the dΛ, dΣ, and dΞ systems. Although no
two-body bound states were found, the dΛ system ex-
hibits a pronounced low-momentum enhancement aris-
ing from its large scattering length and the proximity
of a near-threshold pole. In contrast, the d −Σ corre-
lation is suppressed by the predominantly repulsive in-
teraction, while the dΞ0 and dΞ−systems are charac-
terized by moderate strong-interaction effects and a sub-
stantial Coulomb enhancement, respectively. We further
developed a complete feed-down framework including Σ0,
Σ(1385), and Ξ decays through Monte Carlo response
matrices. Feed-down modifies the observable d −Λ cor-
relation, but the interaction signal remains clearly visi-
ble after reconstruction. These results provide quantita-
tive predictions for future femtoscopic measurements and
establish deuteron–hyperon correlations as a promising
probe of hyperon–nucleus interactions.
