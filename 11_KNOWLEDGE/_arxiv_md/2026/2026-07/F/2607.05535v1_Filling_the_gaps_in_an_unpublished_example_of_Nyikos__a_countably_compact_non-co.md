Filling the gaps in an unpublished example of Nyikos: a
countably compact non-compact manifold under ♣C
Mathieu Baillif
July 8, 2026
Abstract
We provide details for a Theorem which is only available on a unﬁnished preliminary draft of P.
Nyikos: the existence under ♣C of a hereditarily collectionwise normal countably compact non-compact
manifold which does not contain a copy of ω1. This shows in particular that MA + ¬CH does not
imply the existence of a copy of ω1 in a countably compact non-compact manifold (it is known that
PFA does imply it). The said manifold is obtained from a principal S1-bundle over the long ray, and
some structural Theorems for these spaces (due to Nyikos as well) are also proved. We show that the
same type of theorems hold for n-to-1 closed preimages of ω1 and Zn-“bundles” over ω1, where Zn is the
additive group of integers modulo n. A small generalization of ♣C is also quickly investigated.

Introduction
In this note, a manifold is a connected Hausdorﬀspace locally homeomorphic to Rn for some n
(which is ﬁxed by connectedness). We use standard terminology, see the end of this introduction if
in need of deﬁnitions. The two following theorems were proved quite some time ago.
Theorem 1.1 (Countable compactness in manifolds).
(a) (Z. Balogh, 1989 [1, Corollary 2.7]) Under PFA, any countably compact non-compact manifold
contains a copy of ω1.
(b) (M.E. Rudin, 1976 [8, Corollary 2]) Under ♦, there is a perfectly normal non-compact countably
compact manifold which does not contain any copy of ω1.
Countably compact metrizable manifolds are compact, hence the manifolds above are non-
metrizable. Here, PFA is the proper forcing axiom and ♦is Jensen’s diamond axiom. Since then,
many variants of Rudin’s construction have been given, but to our knowledge all published ones use
at least some ﬂavor of ♦incompatible with MA + ¬CH, that is, Martin’s axiom plus the negation
of the continuum hypothesis. Since PFA implies MA + ¬CH, the following question is natural:
Questions 1.2. Is MA + ¬CH enough for (a) ?
Nyikos answered this question in the negative.
Theorem 1.3 (Nyikos, circa 2010 [4]).
There is a countably compact non-compact manifold without any copy of ω1 under ♣C. The manifold
is moreover hereditarily collectionwise normal.
The axiom ♣C is not killed by any ccc forcing, and thus may coexist with MA + ¬CH. This con-
struction also sheds some light on other results concerning strengthenings of normality in manifolds,
such as those ones (see the references for deﬁnitions and details).

Nyikos’ Theorem 1.3 appears in Sections 4 and 5 of an unﬁnished draft [4] which he made available
on his webpage. There is a sketch of a proof through a series of lemmas (which are given without
proof), while the construction itself of the manifold is described in some more detail. Since P. Nyikos
unfortunately passed away before completing his draft, we thought that it might be a good idea to
try to ﬁll the gaps and this note is the result of our eﬀorts. Most of our presentation follows Nyikos’
roadmap, we only add some lemmas of our own when we were not able to cut directly through the
arguments, and adapt a bit his notations or conventions to our taste.
There are two notable exceptions to our loyalty to Nyikos’ plan. The ﬁrst one is Section 3, where we
isolate a kind of combinatorial result on ladder systems which makes Nyikos’ construction more easily
translatable in other settings. (Another reason is that we struggled to ﬁnd the correct deﬁnition for
making the crucial Lemma 5.1 work, as none is given in Nyikos’ notes. We thus felt the need to
temporarily focus on the combinatorial aspects of our problem, away from the more geometrical
ones.)
The other exception is Section 6. What happened is that during the course of writing these notes,
we thought that it would be nice to say something about a related class of spaces that Nyikos also
investigated, in the same or in other papers and drafts (e.g. in [6]): the ﬁnite-to-1 closed preimages
of ω1. As is often the case, the small set of remarks we wanted to add grew up taking almost a third
of the note and might contain new results, at least ones that we could not spot in the literature.
Some of them are immediate consequences of the combinatorial results of Section 3. These possible
novelties have the advantage of giving us the sensation of a little original contribution and a small
boost to our self-esteem. This gave us enough momentum to add a short last section (Section 7)
where we investigate a variation of ♣C (which might also be new, although a ﬁrst proof that it is
consistent was provided by G. Goldberg and A. Lietz, see below). Due to a misunderstanding of a
deﬁnition in Nyikos’ draft, we thought for some time that this variation was needed in the proof of
Theorem 1.3.
This note is organized as follows. Nyikos’ manifold is actually a principal S1-bundle over the long
ray L+. Section 2 contains generalities about these spaces and a structure theorem for them. This
enables us to deﬁne the order of such a bundle, which is an integer or ∞, and to prove that bundles
of order > 1 do not possess a copy of ω1. Our goal then becomes to show that under ♣C there are
bundles of any order. Section 3 contains the aforementioned combinatorial results on (arbitrary)
ladder systems, and Section 4 uses these results to construct “arcs” aα on the set L+ × S1 which
engender (in a precise sense given in this section) the topology of a principal S1-bundle on this set.
This section is probably ﬁlled with way too many details for a lot of readers’ taste, but we were
not conﬁdent enough to sprint to conclusions more quickly. Then, Nyikos’s theorem (or rather, the
fact that there are bundles of any order under ♣C) is proved in Section 5. As said above, Section 6
contains some musing about ﬁnite-to-1 closed preimages of ω1. In particular, we investigate “discrete”
equivalents of principal S1-bundle over the long ray L+: principal Zn-bundles over ω1 (where Zn is
the group of integers modulo n). Of course, there is something a bit silly in the idea of looking at
bundles above a (highly) disconnected space, but ω1 has a kind of “rigidity” that makes some things

Our terminology for topological terms is standard and follows e.g. [3]. We denote ordered pairs
by ⟨·, ·⟩, reserving parenthesis (·, ·) for open intervals in totally ordered spaces. We use the symbol
↾for restrictions (of a function or a topology to a subspace, etc). We recall that the word club
is a shorthand for “closed and unbounded”. The greak letters α, β, γ, η, ξ are used exclusively for
ordinals. Any ordinal is the set of its predecessors, hence β < α and β ∈α means the same thing.
Similarly, as sets, α is the same as the interval [0, α) taken in ω1. We generally favor the notations
β < α and [0, α) to avoid unnecessary confusions between variables and domains of some functions.
The long ray L+ is the space ω1 × [0, 1) with lexicographic order topology, with the smallest point
removed. It is a 1-dimensional manifold. It will be convenient to write the members of L+ as α + r,
for r ∈[0, 1) and α ∈ω1, and when needed we see ω1 as a subset of L+ (all the points with r = 0).
We let Λ be the club subset of limit ordinals in ω1, and Λ2 be that of limits of limits. If E ⊂ω1, we
write E′ for the subset of its limit points. A ladder system on ω1 is a sequence ⟨Lα : α ∈Λ⟩where
each Lα is a strictly increasing sequence of ordinals with limit α. We recall that ♣C is the following
axiom:
♣C : There is a ladder system ⟨Lα : α ∈Λ⟩such that for each club C ⊂ω1, the set
{α ∈Λ : Lα ⊂C} is stationary.
Such a ladder system is called a ♣C-sequence. The following classical lemma will be used many times
in the proofs.
Lemma 1.5 (Fodor’s lemma). If S ⊂ω1 is stationary and f : S →ω1 satisﬁes f(α) < α, then
there is a sationary S0 ⊂S and β ∈ω1 such that f(α) = β for each α ∈S0.
We also use several times (without acknowledging it) the fact that a ﬁnite or countable union of
non-stationary sets is non-stationary.

Generalities on principal S1-bundles over L+
Nyikos’ example is built as a principal S1-bundle over L+, with S1 the circle. A quick reminder on
principal S1-bundles E over a space X:
• It is a ﬁber bundle, i.e. there is a continuous bundle map π : E →X such that for each x ∈X
there is an open U ∋x and a ﬁber preserving homeomorphism fU : π−1(U) →U × S1, i.e.
fU sends {y} × S1 to π−1({y}) for each y ∈U. (This is sometimes summarized by the phrase
“π : E →X is locally trivial”.)
• There is a free transitive continuous group action by S1 on E which leaves ﬁbers π−1({x})
invariant.
We sometimes abuse the terminology and say that a ﬁber (or principal) bundle π : M →L+ has a
topological property P whenever the total space M has P, or say “the (ﬁber or principal) bundle”
instead of “the total space of the (ﬁber or principal) bundle”. We see S1 as the reals modulo 2π,
with the group action given by the addition modulo 2π. Recall that any ﬁber bundle or principal
S1-bundle on R is trivial, that is, there is a ﬁber preserving homeomorphism onto R × S1.
Notation:
If π : M →L+ is an S1-principal bundle and p ∈L+, we write Mp = π−1({p}). If x ∈M and
θ ∈[0, 2π), we denote by x + θ the point in Mπ(x) that is θ radians from x in the positive direction.

6.5
