First Search for Kaluza-Klein Gravitons and Radion Using Planck Data
Alexander P. Cassem1 and Soubhik Kumar1
1Institute of Cosmology, Department of Physics and Astronomy, Tufts University, Medford, MA 02155, USA
Heavy moduli and Kaluza-Klein (KK) gravitons from extra dimensions may evade terrestrial
probes but can be produced during inflation, generating primordial non-Gaussianity (NG) through
unavoidable couplings to density perturbations. In a warped five-dimensional (5D) model, we com-
pute the full radion- and KK-graviton-mediated bispectra and perform the first search for these
signals using Planck 2018 temperature and polarization data. We find no significant evidence for
NG, with the maximum significance being 1.8σ for mKK ≈1.6H. We also identify a 5D setup which
naturally generates NG with fNL ∼1 −50, within the reach of future surveys.
I.
INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY
Extra spatial dimensions often arise in efforts to go
beyond the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics. In
string theory, extra dimensions are required to have a
mathematically consistent description of quantum grav-
ity [1, 2].
Extra dimensions could also play central
roles in explaining a number of key puzzles beyond the
SM, such as the origin of the Higgs mass [3–6], gauge
coupling unification [7–9], the origin of flavor hierar-
chies and neutrino masses [10, 11], and the identity of
dark matter [12, 13].
Accordingly, extensive searches
for signatures of extra dimensions have been performed
in astrophysics, cosmology, and especially particle col-
liders [14].
In general, these searches show that the
scale of higher-dimensional quantum gravity (for flat
extra dimensions [3, 4]) or Kaluza-Klein (KK) masses
(for warped extra dimensions [5, 6]) have to be above
O(1 −100) TeV, depending on the specific set up. How-
ever, these searches lose their sensitivity if the extra
dimensions are much smaller, or equivalently the KK
masses are much larger, simply because the associated en-
ergy scales cannot be reached in terrestrial experiments.
The early Universe provides a natural way around
this challenge since the energy scales then could have
been much higher than the TeV scale. This is especially
true during cosmic inflation when the Hubble scale H,
that governs the spacetime expansion rate, could have
been as high as 4 × 1013 GeV [15]. Due to the rapidly
expanding spacetime, states with masses ∼H can be
produced via vacuum fluctuations, without needing any
special coupling in the theory.
Therefore, if some of
the KK gravitons or moduli—always present in extra-
dimensional theories—have masses ∼H, they could al-
ready have been produced during inflation, despite being
too heavy to be accessed in terrestrial experiments today.
Importantly, since KK gravitons and moduli are grav-
itational degrees of freedom, they have irreducible cou-
plings to the source responsible for generating the scalar
density perturbations ζ observed in the cosmic mi-
crowave background (CMB) and large-scale structure
(LSS). Thus, following their production during inflation,
KK gravitons and moduli can decay into ζ, leading to pri-
mordial non-Gaussianity (NG). The simplest such NG is
the bispectrum ⟨ζ(k1)ζ(k2)ζ(k3)⟩as a function of (co-
moving) three-momenta k1,2,3. The momentum depen-
dence of such bispectra encodes both the mass and the
spin of the heavy particle. For example, a KK graviton-
mediated bispectrum contains oscillations, with a fre-
quency determined by the KK graviton mass, and a dis-
tinctive angular dependence that is characteristic of its
spin-2 nature. Thus, searches for such NG let us do mass-
spin spectroscopy at very high energies, potentially at
1013 −1014 GeV, with the early Universe functioning as
a ‘cosmological collider’ [16–20]. This provides a direct
probe of ultraviolet (UV) physics far beyond the reach of
terrestrial experiments.
This opportunity has motivated a number of recent
searches for cosmological collider signals, from both
scalar and spinning massive particles, using CMB and
LSS data [21–33].
In particular, the existing searches
for spin-2 particles have considered only dimension-6 ef-
fective field theory (EFT) operators that describe the
(cubic) interaction between ζ and spin-2 particles. How-
ever, KK gravitons generate the leading NG already
at dimension-5.
Within EFT power counting, these
dimension-5 signals are parametrically larger than the
dimension-6 contributions previously considered.
Ad-
ditionally, the dimension-5 and dimension-6 operators
generate distinct bispectrum shapes.
Although these
dimension-5 operators for KK gravitons were identified
in [34, 35], their full bispectrum shapes have not previ-
ously been computed, and existing cosmological collider
searches therefore miss the leading NG signatures of KK
gravitons. Here we compute these shapes and perform
the first dedicated search using Planck 2018 temperature
and polarization data.
The results of our search for the radion modulus and
the KK graviton are shown in Fig. 1, where we have
parametrized the overall strength of the bispectrum us-
ing the fNL parameter, defined in Eq. (4). We do not
find any evidence for these shapes at 95% CL. To inter-
pret these values of fNL, as well as derive an explicit tar-
get for future searches, particularly with LSS data, we
outline a warped extra-dimensional construction where
the spacetime is a slice of five-dimensional anti-de Sitter
space (AdS5), with UV and infrared (IR) boundaries [5].
In this setup, the wave functions of the radion and KK
gravitons peak towards the IR boundary. Thus, if den-
sity perturbations ζ originate from a field σ that is also

II.
FOUR DIMENSIONAL EFFECTIVE
THEORY
To keep our discussion model independent, we start
with an effective 4D description, postponing the detailed
5D construction. This will be sufficient for computing
the shapes of the bispectrum, which are determined by
the structure of the operators coupling the KK gravi-
tons and the radion to σ. These structures are entirely
fixed by symmetries, as we discuss shortly. On the other
hand, computing the magnitude of the bispectra requires
a knowledge of the actual KK graviton and radion cou-
plings, which we will determine from the 5D setup.
Our 4D description follows from the AdS/CFT dual-
ity [37–39] which relates a 5D theory in AdS5 in terms
of a large-N, 4D strongly coupled conformal field theory
(CFT4) [40, 41]. This 4D theory is characterized by two
dimensionful scales, the Planck scale Mpl and a confine-
ment scale Λc. The interactions among the composite
states, dual to IR-localized 5D fields, are controlled by
N and Λc. Cosmology introduces two additional scales:
the inflationary Hubble scale H and the mass of σ, mσ.
We are interested in describing the confining phase of
this theory during inflation and thus we require H <
Λc ≲mKK, where the last relation follows from the du-
ality between the KK modes and the composite states.
An additional restriction comes from the fact that the
distinctive oscillatory NG mediated by KK gravitons be-
come exponentially suppressed if their masses mKK ≫H,
owing to the fact that vacuum fluctuations cannot effi-
ciently produce on-shell KK gravitons for mKK ≫H.
Therefore, in this work we focus on scenarios where the
lightest KK gravitons have mKK ≃Λc ≃H. However,
this is not a fundamental restriction since there are mech-
anisms with sources of energy injection, such as a chemi-
cal potential [42–46] or a primordial feature [47], that can
produce on-shell particles parametrically heavier than H.
Our focus on Λc ≃H also implies that the inflation-
ary energy density Vinf ∼H2M 2

ite states (dual to radion and KK graviton) to mediate
observable NG, density perturbations ζ should also be
sourced by some composite state, which we label as σ.
This feature of one (elementary) source driving the ho-
mogeneous inflationary expansion and another (compos-
ite) source giving rise to ζ is naturally present in sev-
eral mechanisms, e.g., involving curvaton [48–50], mod-
ulated reheating [36, 51], and inhomogeneous end of in-
flation [52, 53]. For concreteness, we will focus on the
modulated reheating scenario to derive fNL.
Since ζ is controlled by σ, its mass mσ should be
smaller than H, and hence Λc, such that it can obtain
(approximately) scale-invariant, long-wavelength fluctu-
ations. This can happen naturally if σ arises as a com-
posite pseudo-Nambu Goldstone boson, similar to pions
in the chiral Lagrangian, and respects a shift symmetry,
σ →σ + constant, that is softly broken only by mσ.

V.
INTERPRETATION OF THE RESULT AND
OBSERVATIONAL TARGETS
Having derived the radion and KK graviton couplings,
we can compute fNL via Eq. (4), once we quantify the
relation ζ(k) = fδσ(k) discussed earlier. To this end,
we focus on the modulated reheating scenario [36]. In
this scenario, the perturbations of inflaton ϕ, and hence
ζ generated during inflation, are negligible.
However,
the decay rate Γ of ϕ is dependent on another light field
σ, which obtains large-scale fluctuations during inflation.
Thus, Γ also fluctuates on large scales and different parts
of the Universe get reheated at different times. These
modulations of the surface of reheating generate ζ.
To make this explicit, we consider couplings yϕqqc
and yϕσqqc/M, where q, qc are fermionic decay prod-
ucts of ϕ and M is some UV scale.
In our 5D con-
struction, these couplings can originate via some heavy
bulk mediator with mass ∼M that connect the IR-
localized σ with the UV-localized ϕ. These couplings give
Γ ∝y2mϕ(1 + σ/M)2, with mϕ the inflaton mass. Ow-
ing to fluctuations δσ(t, x), Γ becomes location depen-
dent. In the limit when σ0 ≪M and to the linear order,
δΓ/Γ ≈2δσ/M. To relate ζ to δΓ/Γ, we model the end of
inflation through ϕ decay. We solve the associated Boltz-
mann equations in Appendix D, finding ζ ≈(−1/6)δΓ/Γ,
consistent with [36].
This gives the desired relation,

VI.
DISCUSSION
In this work, we have performed the first observational
search for extra-dimensional states: the radion modulus
and KK gravitons, with the Planck 2018 temperature and
polarization data. Our search is consistent with fNL = 0
at 95% CL, with the maximum local significance of 1.8σ
for mKK ≈1.6H. Our search differs qualitatively from
previous searches for generic spin-2 particles, which fo-
cused on dimension-6 interactions, whereas KK graviton-
mediated NG arises already through dimension-5 opera-
tors. It would be interesting to search for the radion and
KK gravitons both using the CMB trispectrum (four-
point function) and especially future LSS data, which
are expected to provide significantly improved sensitiv-
ity.
For the bispectra considered here, our sensitivity
to oscillatory NG signals decreases when mKK ≫H
because vacuum fluctuations cannot efficiently produce
these heavier KK gravitons on shell.
It is worth con-
structing explicit mechanisms that evade this suppression
and generate observable signals from heavier on-shell KK
gravitons. While we have explored one example 5D con-
struction, it would be useful to explore other set ups with
different stabilization mechanisms and brane configura-
tions. Our results demonstrate that cosmological obser-
vations already probe higher-dimensional physics, with
future surveys potentially capable of revealing direct ev-
idence for the higher-dimensional origin of our Universe.
