I.
INTRODUCTION
Investigations of multiquark hadrons which cannot be
classiﬁed as conventional mesons or baryons have a long
history.
Thus assumption about four-quark nature of
light mesons from the lowest scalar nonet was made al-
ready in Ref. [1]. Another interesting result is connected
with a state built of six light quarks which if exists would
be strong-interaction stable particle [2].
Exotic mesons composed of heavy and light quarks
were also objects for theoretical studies. Structures con-
taining heavy QQ diquarks (Q = c or b ) and light
antidiquarks qq′ were considered as real candidates to
multiquark hadrons stable against strong and/or electro-
magnetic decays. In pioneering publications [3–5] it was
shown that structures QQqq′ may be stable particles if
the ratio mQ/mq is large. Recent explicit calculations
conﬁrmed this assumption and led to conclusions that
the axial-vector tetraquark bbud is probably such a state
[6]. Strong-interaction stable nature of the fully-heavy
tetraquarks with diﬀerent contents and spin-parities were
revealed in Ref. [7] as well.
Naturally, these tetraquarks can decay through weak
processes. In our articles [8–14] we evaluated full widths
of the scalar and axial-vector tetraquarks bbud, bbus and
bcud
by exploring their various semileptonic and non-
leptonic decay channels.
These exotic mesons may be
observed soon in ongoing and future experiments.
Experimental situation with particles QQQQ is more
promising.
In fact, the LHCb-ATLAS-CMS collabora-
tions discovered four X resonances with masses 6.2 −
7.2 GeV in the di-J/ψ and J/ψψ′ mass distributions.
Measured parameters of these resonances provide valu-

able information on fully-heavy exotic mesons [15–19].
This is connected with the fact that X structures are
supposedly tetraquarks cccc and their studies can shed
light on features of fully-heavy systems. There are ob-
served processes with b-mesons at the ﬁnal states as well.
In fact, decays to J/ψΥ and ΥΥ mesons were seen and
studied by D0 and CMS experiments [20, 21]. Such ﬁnal
products imply generation of intermediate cccc, bcbc and
bbbb structures followed by transformations to conven-
tional heavy mesons. In other words, there are data con-
ﬁrming existence of fully-beauty tetraquarks bbbb, which
deserve detailed analysis.
Theoretical examinations of fully-heavy exotic mesons
were actually performed in numerous publications [22–
28] in which their parameters were investigated by means
of diﬀerent methods and schemes. Discoveries of LHCb-
ATLAS-CMS collaborations triggered appearance of new
interesting articles where authors addressed various prob-
lems of
fully-heavy tetraquarks [29–37]. In Refs. [38–
41] the X resonances were investigated in the context of
QCD sum rule (SR) method [42, 43].
In these works,
we considered these new structures as scalar particles
using both diquark-antidiquark and hadronic molecule
models by calculating masses and decay widths of these
states.
But new measurements of CMS experiment
demonstrated that these structures are tensor states
JPC = 2++.
As tensor particles they were studied in
our articles [44, 45] as well.
Structures bbbb were objects of interesting investiga-
tions in which authors applied diﬀerent methods.
For
instance, the masses of fully- heavy tetraquarks may
be found by solving nonrelativistic Schrodinger equa-
tion [22]. In accordance with this article all fully-beauty
(i.e., ones with J = 0, 1, 2) tetraquarks reside below ΥΥ
threshold, and cannot be seen in this mass distribution.
The scalar tetraquark bbbb was explored also in Ref. [23].
Prediction for m4b = (18826 ± 25) MeV permitted the
authors to conclude that this particle does not decay to

Predictions for the mass m diﬀer from each other not
only quantitatively, but imply also diﬀerent mechanisms
for decays of this particle. Thus, there are two impor-
tant thresholds for fully-beauty tetraquarks, i.e., 2ηb and
2Υ thresholds 18798 MeV and 18921 MeV, respectively.
Possible decay modes of the tensor tetraquark T to ordi-
nary mesons are limited by its position in this mass scale.
Even in the maximally allowed value m = 18616 MeV it
is below 2ηb and 2Υ thresholds and stable against strong
decays to conventional bb mesons (see, Fig. 3).
Such
structures transform to known particles owing to anni-
hilation of bb to light quark-antiquark pairs which later
convert to heavy-light mesons [47].

V.
CONCLUDING NOTES
The fully-beauty diquark-antidiquark tensor state T =
bbbb with the structure Cγµ⊗γνC+Cγν⊗γµC considered
in the present article is an interesting object for investi-
gations. First of all, one should theoretically compute
physical parameters of T by employing one of known
approaches of high energy physics.
In our article, we
have calculated the mass and full decay width of this
tetraquark in the framework of QCD sum rule method.
Our prediction m = (18530±86) MeV for its mass proves
that T does not decay to pairs of ηbηb and ΥΥ mesons.
In other words, the tensor tetraquark T cannot be seen
as a peak in mass distributions of these particles. Similar
conclusions were made in publications of other authors,
though there are works which predict considerably larger
masses for T. In some scenarios, the tensor state T can
decay to ηbηb mesons, whereas in other ones it is unstable
against dissociation to both ηbηb and ΥΥ mesons. But
in all options, it is important to ﬁnd not only the mass
of a particle under discussion but also evaluate its width:
Conclusions drawn solely from the masses of these struc-
tures are not entirely convincing.
To obtain the width of the tetraquark T we have ex-
plored six decay modes of this state.
These channels
appear owing to annihilation of b-quarks in the tensor
tetraquark.
Our prediction Γ [T] = (48 ± 6) MeV for
width of T is not large, therefore it may be classiﬁed
as a state with relatively moderate full width. Qualita-
tively this means that the tensor structure bbbb may be
observed in the mass distribution, for instance, of the
meson pairs B+B−. But here one has to take into ac-
count and estimate possible eﬀects arising from disso-
ciations of the scalar and tensor tetraquarks bubu and
hadronic molecules B+B−.
In other words, there are
various strong background noises to observe decays of T.
The tetraquarks bbbb till now are hypothetical parti-
cles. But they can be observed in ongoing LHC exper-
iments which have certain potential to discover these
structures [52].
The Tera-Z factory would have also
such a potential [53].
Therefore, theoretical studies
of tetraquarks bbbb with diﬀerent spin-parities remain
among important problems of particle physics. This is
especially true in light of the existing contradictory pre-
dictions for their parameters. In the present article we
have tried to illuminate one of essential aspects of this
problem.
