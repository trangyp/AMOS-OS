FINITENESS OF PSEUDO-ANOSOV FLOWS WITHOUT PERFECT FITS
THOMAS BARTHELM´E, CHI CHEUK TSANG, AND JONATHAN ZUNG
Abstract. We show that a fixed closed 3-manifold admits at most finitely many pseudo-Anosov
flows without perfect fits and deduce finiteness of veering triangulations in a fixed 3-manifold.
1. Introduction
The problem of classifying (pseudo-)Anosov flows on 3-manifolds up to orbit-equivalence, as introduced
by Smale [Sma67], can be split into three main steps: Determining which 3-manifolds admit pseudo-
Anosov flow (the existence question), determining how many distinct pseudo-Anosov flows a given
3-manifold admits (the abundance question), and finally giving algebraic/numerical complete invariants
to classify pseudo-Anosov flows on a given manifold (the classification question).
In this article, we are concerned with the abundance question. There are now many known examples of
3-manifolds admitting arbritrarily many Anosov flows [BBY17, BM22, CP25b, BY24, BSZ25]. Here we
consider the last main open part of that question, which is the so-called Finiteness Conjecture:
Conjecture 1.1. Let M be a fixed closed 3-manifold. Then M admits at most finitely many pseudo-
Anosov flows up to orbit equivalence.
This conjecture has seen a lot of development in the past few years thanks in large part to the
incorporation of techniques coming from contact geometry, together with the complete invariant of
transitive pseudo-Anosov flows introduced in [BM24, BFM25]. More precisely, in [BM24], Barthelm´e–
Bowden–Mann proved the finiteness conjecture for the class of Anosov Reeb flows. This was later
extended by Zung [Zun25] and Baldwin–Sivek–Zung [BSZ25] to pseudo-Anosov flows admitting positive
Birkhoff sections1 in rational homology spheres, and finally by Chaidez–Pan [CP25a] to a class that
they introduce and call pseudo-Anosov Reeb flows.
There are further works in progress regarding this conjecture of which we are aware, and which do
not use contact geometry: Landry–Taylor [LT] prove that every finite-depth foliation in a hyperbolic
3-manifold admit finitely many almost transverse pseudo-Anosov flows, while Barthelm´e–Paulet [BP]
prove finiteness of transitive pseudo-Anosov flows in graph-manifolds up to finite covers.
Our main result in this article is an extension of the finiteness conjecture to the class of pseudo-
Anosov flows on atoroidal manifolds that admit a positive Birkhoff section away from singularities, i.e.
flows that admit a Birkhoff section such that all the negative boundaries are on singular orbits (see
Definition 2.13). By definition, this class contains the cases treated in [Zun25] and [BSZ25], and by
Asaoka–Bonatti–Marty [ABM24] it also contains all Anosov Reeb flows on atoroidal manifolds. The
intersection between Chaidez–Pan’s pseudo-Anosov Reeb flows and the class discussed here is more
conjectural and discussed in section 6.
The interest of our extension resides in the following consequence:
Theorem 1.2. Let M be a closed 3-manifold. Then there are at most finitely many pseudo-Anosov
flows without perfect fits on M up to isotopy equivalence.

There are several reasons why the class of pseudo-Anosov flows without perfect fits is of particular
importance, but one of them is the correspondence, introduced by Agol and Gu´eritaud, between
pseudo-Anosov flows without perfect fits and veering triangulations. The finiteness conjecture for
pseudo-Anosov flows admit a veering triangulation counterpart, which we can now deduce:
Theorem 1.3. Let M ˝ be an orientable compact 3-manifold with torus boundary components. Then
there are at most finitely many veering triangulations on M ˝ up to isotopy.
The strategy to prove Theorem 1.2 is in spirit similar to those of [BM24, Zun25, BSZ25, CP25a]: One
wants to translate the problem of distinguishing pseudo-Anosov flows up to isotopy equivalence into a
problem of distinguishing contact structures of Reeb flows up to isotopy. Morally, the key is to show
that one can replace a pseudo-Anosov flow by a Reeb flow with the same spectrum, or free homotopy
data. The spectrum is a complete invariant of (most) transitive pseudo-Anosov flows by [BFM25], and
on the Reeb side, cylindrical contact homology gives that it is an invariant of contact structures up
to isotopy. Finiteness then follows from Colin–Giroux–Honda’s finiteness theorem for tight contact
structures [CGH09].
This strategy works directly in the case of Anosov Reeb flows, but since a typical pseudo-Anosov flow
is not the Reeb flow of a contact structure, going from pseudo-Anosov to Reeb dynamics becomes
one of the main sticking points in the works extending [BM24]. The new observation in our work
is that, after drilling out the singular orbits, it is possible to realize a pseudo-Anosov flow without
perfect fits as (a modification of) the Reeb flow of a tight contact structure on the drilled manifold.
This observation leads us to Theorem 1.3. For Theorem 1.2, the fact that it is enough to consider
the drilled manifold follows from a result of Li [Li], based on Gabai’s Kneser normal form argument,
which shows that there are only finitely many possibilities for the isotopy classes of singular orbits of a
pseudo-Anosov flow in a hyperbolic manifold.
Thus, our proof of Theorem 1.2 ultimately rests on two previously established finiteness results:
Colin–Giroux–Honda’s finiteness result for tight contact structures without Giroux torsion, and
Gabai’s finiteness result for a collection of branched surfaces carrying all essential laminations. Both
finiteness theorems are proven by pulling the relevant structure into good position relative to a chosen
triangulation of M. It remains a challenge to prove finiteness of pseudo-Anosov flows by a similar
direct approach.
As mentioned above, our strategy works in more generality than for just pseudo-Anosov flows without
perfect fits: it works as long as the manifold is atoroidal and, up to drilling out the singular orbits, the
pseudo-Anosov flow admits a positive Birkhoff section (see Theorem 5.1 for the precise statement).
It seems likely that such flows have a nice description in terms of their orbit space (see Section 6 for
more details). In particular, we record the following case of the Finiteness Conjecture that may be
approachable via the technique in this article:
Conjecture 1.4. Let M be a closed 3-manifold. Then there are finitely many pseudo-Anosov flows
without negative lozenges on M up to orbit equivalence.

A pseudo-Anosov flow on M is a continuous flow φ : M ˆ R Ñ M for which there exists a pair of
transverse singular 2-dimensional foliations pFs, Fuq such that
‚ Fs-leaves and Fu-leaves intersect in flow lines,
‚ flow lines along each Fs-leaf converge in forward time and diverge in backward time, and
‚ flow lines along each Fu-leaf diverge in forward time and converge in backward time.
For more details, we refer the reader to [BM25].
The singularity locus of Fs and Fu must coincide and equal to a collection of closed orbits. We call
these closed orbits the singular orbits of φ, and denote it by singpφq. See Figure 1 for an illustration
of the dynamics of φ near a singular orbit.
Two flows φ1 and φ2 on M are orbit equivalent if there exists a homeomorphism h: M Ñ M sending
the flow lines of φ1 to the flow lines of φ2 in a way that preserves orientation but not necessarily
preserving parametrization. Furthermore, φ1 and φ2 are isotopically equivalent if h can be chosen
to be isotopic to identity.

2.3. Birkhoff sections. Let φ be a flow on an oriented closed 3-manifold M. A partial Birkhoff
section for φ is an immersed cooriented surface with boundary S in M where
‚ the interior of S is embedded and positively transverse to φ, and
‚ the boundary components of S cover closed orbits of φ.

6. Discussion and further questions
6.1. Orbit space characterization of BAS. Recall from Non-example 2.17 that a pseudo-Anosov
flow with both positive and negative lozenges disjoint from singular orbits cannot be BAS. We conjecture
that the converse is also true.
Conjecture 6.1. Let φ be a transitive pseudo-Anosov flow on an oriented closed 3-manifold. If φ
does not admit both positive and negative lozenges disjoint from singpφq, then φ is BAS.
Remark 6.2. Examples of transitive pseudo-Anosov flows that do not admit both positive and
negative lozenges disjoint from singpφq, and are not yet known to be BAS, can be constructed for
instance in the following two ways:
‚ Following the construction developed in [BF13, section 8], one may build totally periodic
pseudo-Anosov flows for which all lozenges have corners on singpφq. To do that, it suffices to
build the examples from admissible fat graphs all of whose vertices have valence ą 4. Note
that this family of examples do admit both positive and negative lozenges. See [BF15] or
[BM25, Section 5.5] for more details.
‚ Taking branched covers of geodesic flows (or more generally contact Anosov flows) as in [BF21,
Remark 5.10] or [CP25a, Example 2.12] yields a different family of examples. Up to changing
the orientation of the manifold, all lozenges of flows in this family are positive.

Note that the finiteness conjecture is already known for both of these families, by [BF15] for the totally
periodic case, and by [CP25a] for the branched covers of contact Anosov flows.
♢
It would also be interesting to have a better understanding on the relationship between pseudo-Anosov
flows with positive sections, pseudo-Anosov Reeb flows in the sense of [CP25a] and BAS pseudo-Anosov
flows. There exist flows admitting both a positive Birkhoff section and a transverse surface; such a
flow is BAS, but not pseudo-Anosov Reeb. Finally, there exist examples of BAS pseudo-Anosov flows
that are neither pseudo-Anosov Reeb nor admit a strictly positive Birkhoff section. For instance, one
can construct many pseudo-Anosov flows without perfect fits that admit a transverse surface (e.g., a
suspension of a pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphism), and therefore cannot be pseudo-Anosov Reeb ([CP25a,
Lemma 2.13]). Similarly suspensions of pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphisms cannot have a strictly positive
Birkhoff section.
6.2. Other rigid orbits. Morally, our main result builds on [BM24] by using the fact that singular
orbits of a pseudo-Anosov flow are rigid, in the sense that on a given 3-manifold, there are at most
finitely many possibilities for the isotopy class of the singular orbits and their degeneracy curves.
A natural question is then: Are there other rigid types of orbits for a pseudo-Anosov flow? More
specifically, we make the following conjecture.
Conjecture 6.3. A branching orbit of a pseudo-Anosov flow φ is a closed orbit that lies on a leaf of
Λs or Λu that is part of a cataclysm in the leaf space. The set of branching orbits of a pseudo-Anosov
flow is rigid, i.e. on a given 3-manifold, there are at most finitely many possibilities for the isotopy
class of the branching orbits and their degeneracy curves.
If Conjecture 6.3 is true, then our strategy would generalize to show finiteness of pseudo-Anosov flows
that admit a Birkhoff section all of whose negative boundary components lie along branching and
singular orbits. A possible acronym of such flows is `BABS.
More precisely, extending the proof of Theorem 5.1, one can show:
Theorem 6.4. Let M be a closed atoroidal 3-manifold and L be a link. There are at most finitely
many pseudo-Anosov flows on M that are BABS and such that their branching periodic orbits are in
the isotopy class of L.
6.3. Effective bounds. It might be interesting to have effective bounds on the number of (orbit
equivalence classes of) pseudo-Anosov flows on a given 3-manifold M. As mentioned in the introduction,
our finiteness result comes from finiteness of tight contact structures [CGH09] and finiteness of isotopy
classes of singular orbits [Li]. Since the proof of both results involve some sort of ‘normal form’ with
respect to some triangulation ∆on M, one might expect to get a bound in terms on the number of
tetrahedra in ∆. More specifically, we ask:
Question 6.5. Can one give an explicit exponential bound on the number of (orbit equivalence classes
of) pseudo-Anosov flows without perfect fit on a closed 3-manifold M in terms of the number of
tetrahedra in a triangulation of M?
