FUNCTIONS IN L1(µ, Y ) WITH OPTIMAL TENSOR
REPRESENTATIONS
LUIS C. GARC´IA-LIROLA AND JUAN GUERRERO-VIU
Abstract. We study the existence and characterization of optimal tensor rep-
resentations of elements in the space L1(µ, Y ) of Bochner integrable functions.
We completely describe the set of norm-attaining elements in two settings.
First, when the Banach space Y is strictly convex, and second, when Y = L1(ν)
and K = R. In both situations, our analysis yields the existence of non-norm-
attaining tensors whenever the underlying measures are not purely atomic.
Finally, we introduce a geometric property over Y ensuring that every element
in L1(µ, Y ) admits an optimal representation.
In particular, this holds for
Lipschitz-free spaces over complete scattered metric spaces, for C(K) spaces
when K is a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected space, and for c0(Γ) where
Γ is any index set. As a byproduct, we settle two open questions regarding
projective norm-attainment.
1. Introduction
The connection between the existence of norm-attaining functions and the geo-
metric structure of Banach spaces has been extensively studied and has generated
numerous contributions (e.g.
for bounded linear operators [12, 28, 39, 43], for
bounded bilinear mappings [1, 7, 17, 36], and for Lipschitz functions [10, 16, 24, 26]).
Similarly, the search of optimal representations has played a significant role in the
geometry of Banach spaces, giving rise to a substantial body of literature in differ-
ent contexts (e.g. integrals and convex series of molecules in Lipschitz-free spaces
[4, 5, 6, 44], and the λ-property or CSRP [8, 9, 30, 42]).
Bridging the gap between those perspectives, projective tensor products offer a
natural setting where optimal representations and norm-attainment meet together.
Namely, given u ∈X b⊗πY , it is said that u is norm-attaining if the infimum from the
definition of its projective norm turns out to be a minimum. In other words, if there
exists an optimal representation u = P∞

Returning to tensor products, we recall that the notion of projective norm-
attainment was introduced in [20], motivated by the development of nuclear oper-
ator theory. However, in the finite-dimensional case, the study of norm-attaining
tensors goes back to Pelczy´nsky and Tomczak-Jaegermann, who study it in [32]
under the name of faithful operator. After that, new results have appeared in the
literature. Writing NAπ(X b⊗πY ) for the set of norm-attaining tensors, we collect
here the main results in this line:
(1) NAπ(X b⊗πY ) = X b⊗πY if
• X and Y are finite dimensional [20, Proposition 3.5];
• X = Y is a Hilbert space (based on a diagonalization argument, see
[20, Proposition 3.8]);
• X is a finite-dimensional polyhedral space and Y is 1-complemented
in its bidual [19, Theorem 4.1] and [23, Corollary 3.2];
• X = ℓ1(Γ) for some set Γ and Y is any space [20, Proposition 3.6];
• X and Y are dual spaces, one of them with the approximation property,
and X = W ∗where W ⊆Z and Z∗= ℓ1(Γ) [23, Theorem 3.4].
(2) NAπ(X b⊗πY ) is dense in X b⊗πY if X has the metric π-property and Y
either has the metric π-property or it is a dual space with the RNP [20,
Theorem 4.8] and [23, Corollary 4.3], if X is a polyhedral Banach space
with the metric π-property and Y is a dual Banach space [19, Theorem 4.2]
or if X and Y are dual spaces with the RNP and either X∗or Y ∗has the
approximation property [19, Corollary 4.6].
(3) There are Banach spaces X and Y such that NAπ(X b⊗πY ) is not dense in
X b⊗πY [20, Theorem 5.1] (in particular, in such space there are non-norm-
attaining tensors which are expressible as finite sum of basic tensors).
(4) X b⊗πY \ NAπ(X b⊗πY ) is dense if X = c0(Γ) and Y is a Hilbert space [34,
Theorem 3.3]. The same happens, for instance, if X = c0 and both Y and
Y ∗are strictly convex (in the real setting) [3, Remark 4.8]. In particular, it
is possible that both NAπ(X b⊗πY ) and its complement are simultaneously
dense in X b⊗πY .
Nevertheless, we remark that there is no known description of the set NAπ(X b⊗πY )
in the afore-mentioned results, except that in the case it coincides with the whole
space X b⊗πY . As a result, the task of characterizing elements that admit an opti-
mal representation becomes even more appealing. From this perspective, our study
is intended to provide a framework for finding examples and counterexamples of
various phenomena, since it offers a setting where one can work conveniently (see,
for instance, Remarks 3.4 and 3.11). In particular, the former resolves an open
question raised in [3].

2. Notation and Preliminaries
Throughout the paper X, Y , and Z denote a Banach space over the scalar field
K, which can be either R or C. We reserve the notation BX and SX for the closed
unit ball and the unit sphere of a Banach space X, respectively. We write L(X, Y )
for the space of bounded linear operators from X into Y . If Y = K, then L(X, K)
is denoted by X∗and is known as the topological dual of X.
2.1. Measure theory. We understand a measure space (Ω, Σ, µ) as a triple where
Σ is a σ-algebra over a set Ωand µ is a (non-negative, countably additive) mea-
sure. For every measurable subsets A, B ∈Σ we consider the equivalence relation
A ∼B if µ(A∆B) = 0, where A∆B := (A ∪B) \ (A ∩B) is the symmetric differ-
ence. Throughout the text, measurable sets will be identified with their equivalence
classes. This abuse of notation is harmless, since all statements are considered mod-
ulo null sets.
A measurable set A ∈Σ is an atom if 0 < µ(A) < ∞, and for every measurable
set B ⊆A, either µ(B) = 0 or µ(B) = µ(A). Moreover, given E ∈Σ with 0 <
µ(E) < ∞, we say that E is purely atomic if E ∼S{A ⊆E : A is an atom} (note
that since atoms are considered modulo ∼, µ is countably additive and µ(E) < ∞,

This motivates the following question. Recall that a Banach space is polyhedral
if the unit ball of every finite-dimensional subspace is a polytope (e.g. c0(Γ)).
Question 5.13. Let (Ω, Σ, µ) be a measure space and Y be a polyhedral space. Is
it true that
NAπ(L1(µ)b⊗πY ) = L1(µ)b⊗πY ?
Note that if Y is polyhedral then L1(µ) ⊗Y ⊆NAπ(L1(µ)b⊗πY ) (that is, finite
rank tensors attain their norm).

such that u ∈L1(µ) ⊗E. Since BE is a polytope and L1(µ) is 1-complemented in
the bidual, we have that u ∈NAπ(L1(µ)b⊗πE), in virtue of [19, Theorem 4.1] and
[23, Corollary 3.2]. Thus, the conclusion follows from the fact that L1(µ)b⊗πE =
L1(µ, E) is a closed subspace of L1(µ)b⊗πY = L1(µ, Y ).
