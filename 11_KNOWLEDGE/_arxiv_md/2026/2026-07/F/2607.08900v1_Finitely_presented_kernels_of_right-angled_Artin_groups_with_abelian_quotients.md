FINITELY PRESENTED KERNELS OF RIGHT-ANGLED ARTIN GROUPS
WITH ABELIAN QUOTIENTS.
MALLIKA ROY
Abstract. In this article, we characterise when the kernels of right-angled Artin groups with
abelian quotients are finitely generated and finitely presented — exhibiting an explicit finite
generating set and finite presentation. As an application, we deduce the finite presentation of the
kernel of any character of a right-angled Artin group. These results generalise the presentation of
the kernel of a rational character of a right-angled Artin group given by Casals–Kazachkov–Roy
and Dicks and Leary’s presentations of Bestina–Brady groups.
1. Introduction
Let Γ be a finite simplicial graph. The associated right-angled Artin group GΓ is the group having
the presentation as follows. The set of generators of GΓ is the vertex set V (Γ) = {a1, a2, . . . as},
and GΓ has only the commuting relations stating that two generators commute if and only if their
associated vertices are adjacent in Γ, that is, the relators of GΓ are the commutators [ai, aj] for each
pair of adjacent vertices (ai, aj) in Γ. Let △Γ be a non-empty finite flag complex with 1-skeleton Γ
— a finite simplicial complex that contains a simplex bounding every complete subgraph of Γ.
Right-angled Artin groups contain many interesting subgroups, for example Bestvina–Brady
groups. The Bestvina–Brady groups were first introduced in the seminal work of Bestvina and
Brady [3] as an answer to a long-standing open question regarding the existence of non-finitely
presented groups of type FP. If one considers the kernels of the homomorphisms from GΓ to Z
that map every standard generator of GΓ to 1, then these kernels, denoted by HΓ, are known
as Bestvina–Brady groups in the literature.
Bestvina and Brady [3] described the homological
finiteness properties of the kernels HΓ in terms of the topology of the flag complex △Γ. In particular,
they proved that HΓ is finitely presented if and only if △Γ is simply connected and HΓ is of type
FPn+1 if and only if △Γ is n-acyclic.
In this present article, we consider the kernel Hψ of an epimorphism ψ: GΓ →Zk and study the
combinatorial presentation of the kernels when they are finitely presented.
In [14], Dicks and Leary provided an explicit presentation for HΓ. The generators in the presen-
tation correspond to the edges ei of Γ and in the case when △Γ is simply connected, it is shown
that the relations are of the form eϵ

We show that if GΓ has a free abelian quotient with integral rank k and △Γ is (k−1)-0-connected

to the geometry of the underlying flag complex. Our results generalise [14] and [9] recovering both
the presentations of HΓ and Hφ as it deal with finitely generated free abelian groups as codomains
instead of infinite cyclic groups.

2. Preliminaries and Notations
2.1. Graph theory. We recall and set up some basic notation and terminologies in graph theory.
Throughout this article, we assume finite graphs which have no loops and multi-edges, i.e., all the
graphs are finite simplicial. Given a graph Γ, we denote the set of its vertices and edges by V (Γ) and
E(Γ), respectively. We denote e = (ai, aj) to be an edge connecting vertices ai and aj. ι(e), τ(e)
respectively denotes the initial point and the terminal point of the edge e, i.e., e = (ι(e), τ(e)). Two
vertices are adjacent if they are connected by an edge. Given any subset V ′ of V (Γ), the induced
subgraph (in some literature, it is also called as full subgraph) on V ′ is a graph Γ′ whose vertex set
is V ′, and two vertices are adjacent in Γ′ if and only if they are adjacent in Γ.
A simplicial complex L is called flag complex (also known as clique complex in the literature) if
every complete subgraph in the 1-skeleton spans a simplex in L. In other words, L is completely
determined by its 1-skeleton. Thus, the flag complex on Γ is denoted by △Γ, contains an (n)-simplex
bounding each complete induced subgraph with (n + 1)-vertices of its 1-skeleton Γ.
We first recall that a graph Γ is m-connected if Γ \ {v1, . . . , vℓ} (ℓ< |V (Γ)|) is connected for any
collection of ℓ< m vertices. In this article we use a notion of connectivity adopted from [20] in
the context of simplicial complexes. A simplicial complex L is m-n-connected if for any ℓvertices
{v1, . . . , vℓ} with 0 ⩽ℓ⩽m and ℓ< |V (L)|, the complex L \ {v1, . . . , vℓ} is n-connected. Hence,
the notion of ‘m-connected’ in graph theory is equivalent to the notion of (m −1)-0-connected.
Let R be a commutative ring with unity. A complex is n-acyclic if its reduced homology groups
(over R), up to and including dimension n, are trivial. Throughout this note we use the ring of
integers Z for homological properties of the groups and their associated complexes.
2.2. Homological finiteness properties. Finiteness properties Fn were introduced by C.T.C.
Wall in [25]. A group G is said to be of type Fn if it has an Eilenberg-MacLane complex K(G, 1)
with finite n-skeleton. Equivalently, a group is of type Fn if it acts freely, faithfully, properly,
cellularly, and cocompactly on an (n −1)-connected cell complex. A group is finitely generated if
and only if it is of type F1, and is finitely presented if and only if it is of type F2.
The finiteness properties FPn were introduced by Bieri [5]. The properties FPn are weaker than
the properties Fn — if a group is of type Fn, then it is of type FPn. The group G is said to be
of type FPn if there exists a resolution of the trivial ZG–module Z such that the first n terms are
finitely generated projective ZG–modules. One can easily deduce that the finitely presented groups
are of type FP2 by using the chain complex of the universal cover of a presentation 2-complex.
And the converse is not true as FPn is weaker than Fn.

3. Main results
Meier–Meinert–VanWyk [20] established the finiteness properties of kernels of maps from RAAGs
to abelian groups. In particular Meier–Meinert–VanWyk [20] determined the Fn (and FPn) prop-
erties of the kernels of rational characters of RAAGs and the kernels Hψ, where ψ: GΓ ↠Zk is an
epimorphism. In [20] the authors described the homotopical and homological Σ-invariants of GΓ in
terms of the topology of subcomplexes of △Γ (see also [8] for a different reinterpretation). We refer
the reader to [6] and [7] for background on the Σ-invariants.
Casals–Kazachkov–Roy [9] studied the kernel Hφ of a rational character of a RAAG. Any rational
character from a RAAG can be viewed as an epimorphism φ: GΓ ↠Z sending each generator ai
of GΓ to an integer ni ∈Z. Let Γφ < Γ be the induced subgraph of Γ spanned by the vertices that
are mapped nontrivially by the rational character and let △φ be the corresponding induced flag
complex in △Γ.
In [9, Theorems 3.11, 3.16] Casals–Kazachkov–Roy generalised Dicks–Leary presentation of
Bestvina–Brady groups and provide an algebraic proof for the result proven by Meier–Meinert–
VanWyk [20, Corollary A]. More precisely, In [9, Theorem 3.11] the authors proved that Hφ is

Note that 0-acyclic-domination requires that each vertex in △Γ that is not in △φ be adjacent
to a vertex in △φ. Similarly, 1-acyclic-domination requires that for each edge (ai, aj) ∈△Γ, there
is a vertex adjacent to ai and aj that belongs to △φ, i.e. either ai or aj are vertices in △φ or there
exists ak such that (ai, ak), (aj, ak) are edges in △Γ and ak is a vertex in △φ. The reader is referred
to [20, Definition 1] for the definition of an n-acyclic-dominating subcomplex.
Motivated by the results of [9] and [20] and using the technique developed in [9], in this note,
we further generalise the result of [9] and we characterised when the kernel of an epimorphism
ψ: GΓ ↠Zk is finitely generated and finitely presented. Our theorems algebraically demonstrate
the result of Meier–Meinert–VanWyk [20, Corollary B’] stated below.
Theorem 3.1 ([20], Corollary B’). A right-angled Artin group GΓ has an abelian quotient of integral
rank k with Fn kernel if and only if △Γ is (k −1)-(n −1)-connected.
In this section, we give an algebraic proof of the fact that △Γ is (k−1)-0-connected (equivalently,
Γ is k-connected) if and only if the kernel Hψ is finitely generated and we exhibit an explicit (possibly
infinite) presentation (see Theorem 3.15). Furthermore, if △Γ is (k −1)-1-connected, then we show
that the Hψ is finitely presented and we exhibit an explicit presentation (see Theorem 3.16).

Theorem 4.1. Let Hψ be the kernel of an epimorphism ψ : GΓ →Zk. If Hψ is of type FPk, then
GΓ ≃Hψ ⋊Zk.
