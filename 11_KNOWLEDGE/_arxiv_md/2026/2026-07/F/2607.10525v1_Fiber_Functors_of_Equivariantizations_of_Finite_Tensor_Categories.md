FIBER FUNCTORS OF EQUIVARIANTIZATIONS OF FINITE
TENSOR CATEGORIES
CÉSAR GALINDO, CLAUDIA GALLEGO, AND YIBY MORALES
Abstract. Let G be a finite group acting on a finite tensor category C. We classify
fiber functors on the equivariantization CG in terms of equivariant exact module
categories over C, indexed by subgroups of G. The data are a subgroup H ⊆G
and an H-equivariant C-module category M whose underlying C-module category is
indecomposable, exact, and semisimple; they give a fiber functor precisely when H
acts transitively on the simple objects of M and the stabilizer cocycle of one, hence
every, simple object is non-degenerate. Through Tannaka–Krein reconstruction this
describes realizations of CG as the representation category of a finite-dimensional
Hopf algebra, with no semisimplicity hypothesis on C.
As applications, for odd
primes p we determine the fiber functors on Rep(Hp), where Hp denotes Nikshych’s
semisimple Hopf algebra of dimension 4p2 [Nik08]: there is one equivalence class if
p ≡3 (mod 4) and two if p ≡1 (mod 4). We also use the classification for gaugings
to determine which non-pointed entries in the small-dimensional list of [GN25] are
representation categories of semisimple factorizable Hopf algebras.
1. Introduction
Let C be a finite tensor category over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic
zero. A fiber functor on C is a k-linear exact faithful tensor functor F : C →Veck.
Classical neutral Tannakian duality reconstructs affine group schemes from symmetric
tensor categories equipped with a fiber functor [SR72, DM82]. In the non-symmetric
setting relevant here, Tannaka–Krein reconstruction instead produces a Hopf alge-
bra [JS91, Sch92, BV07]: for every fiber functor F on a finite tensor category C, there
is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra AF with C ≃Rep(AF), under which F becomes
the forgetful functor [EGNO15, Theorem 5.3.12]. The fiber-functor problem is thus
the problem of Hopf realization: deciding when a finite tensor category is the repre-
sentation category of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra, and counting the realizations
arising from inequivalent forgetful functors.
For group-theoretical fusion categories this problem has a concrete answer in terms
of finite groups and cohomology.

module categories [EGNO15, Proposition 7.3.3 and Example 7.4.6]. Exact module
categories over equivariantizations come from equivariant module categories over the
original category [GM12]. Finally, simple objects in equivariant semisimple categories
are governed by projective representations of stabilizers. Combining these facts gives
Theorem 1.1.

We use [EGNO15, EO04] as our main references for finite tensor categories and

2.1. Finite tensor categories and their module categories. A finite tensor cat-
egory is a finite abelian category equipped with a rigid monoidal structure, bilinear
tensor product, and simple unit [EO04, Section 2.2]; a fusion category is a semisimple
finite tensor category.
