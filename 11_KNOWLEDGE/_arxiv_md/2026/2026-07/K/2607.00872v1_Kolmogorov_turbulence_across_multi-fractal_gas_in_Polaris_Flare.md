ABSTRACT
We reveal a pristine, scale-invariant 3D Kolmogorov velocity cascade (α3D
V
∼2/3) spanning 0.05–20 pc in the Polaris Flare using
PPCOS 12CO data. A transition scale at ∼0.5 pc marks a bifurcation in the structure functions’ exponents, below which the degree
of intermittency is also saturated. By deriving an analytical mapping relation (α3D
V
= αV −1
3αI), we obtain the scale-invariant value
of α3D
V , proving that the apparent transition stems from geometric projection and a changing density fractal dimension rather than a
turbulent mode shift. Kolmogorov turbulence is smoothly inherited from the large-scale cold neutral medium, remaining uninterrupted
by compression or gravity below 0.1 pc.
Key words. ISM: kinematics and dynamics — Turbulence — ISM: clouds — ISM: evolution
1. Introduction
Interstellar turbulence fundamentally regulates the lifecycle of
gas clouds by providing macroscopic structural support, driving
multi-scale energy dissipation, and compressing gas into highly
localized structures (e.g., Larson 1981; Elmegreen & Scalo
2004; Federrath et al. 2010; Liu et al. 2025; Liu 2025). These
dynamics serve as a critical gateway for understanding general
cloud physics and large-scale Galactic architectures (e.g., Spitzer
1978; Kennicutt 1998; Krumholz et al. 2009; Kalberla & Kerp
2016). Characterizing this turbulence requires measuring the
spatial scaling behavior of the gas fields via the power-law expo-
nent (α) of the second-order structure function, S 2(L) = ⟨|f(x +
L)−f(x)|2⟩∝Lα (e.g., Kolmogorov 1941; Heyer & Brunt 2004).
Discerning the true three-dimensional exponent α3D
V for the ve-
locity field (V) is crucial to identifying the dominant turbulent
pattern, separating classic incompressible cascades (α3D
V
= 2/3;
Kolmogorov 1941, hereafter K41) from highly compressible,
shock-dominated environments (α3D
V
= 1; Burgers 1948), as
well as smooth laminar flows governed by stable velocity gra-
dients (α3D
V
= 2). However, recovering these intrinsic three-
dimensional scaling properties from astronomical observations
is historically constrained by line-of-sight projection effects and
a limited spatial map size relative to the achievable resolution
(e.g., Ossenkopf et al. 2008; Schneider et al. 2011).
The Polaris Flare, located at a high Galactic latitude (b >
20◦) with a distance of ∼150 pc (Falgarone et al. 1998), provides
an ideal cosmic laboratory for studying interstellar turbulence
(e.g., Ossenkopf et al. 2008; Miville-Deschênes et al. 2010). The
PMO Polaris CO survey (PPCOS; Liu et al. 2026b, hereafter Pa-
per I) delivers a 100 deg2 footprint (∼10◦in size, correspond-
ing to a physical scale of ∼26 pc) fully Nyquist-sampled at sub-
arcminute (50′′) resolution towards the Polaris Flare, capturing
large-scale energy injection at cloud boundaries while resolving
the inertial cascade down to ∼0.04 pc (approximately 10 000 au).

The diffuse outskirts of the Polaris Flare display a highly co-
herent dynamical coupling with the surrounding Cold Neutral
Medium (CNM) (Liu et al. 2026a, hereafter Paper II). Crucially,
the cloud remains entirely untouched by active star formation
(André et al. 2010), carrying cold dust temperatures, optically
thin gas envelopes, and a simple energy injection profile (e.g.,
Lagache et al. 1998; Bernard et al. 1999, see also Paper I). Thus,
PPCOS provides an unprecedented window into these turbulence-
related physics.
In this letter, we apply ∆-variance (σ2
∆) analysis to the 12CO
(J = 1 −0) integrated intensity map (I) and centroid veloc-
ity map (V) of the Polaris Flare. The measured spectrum of σ2
∆
serves as a robust proxy for the second-order structure function
slope α when α > 0 (Stutzki et al. 1998; Ossenkopf & Mac Low
2002). By co-analyzing the two-dimensional indices α2D
V
and
α2D
I , we break the degeneracy between projection effects and
spatial geometry to recover the true three-dimensional velocity
scaling α3D
v
(with the method detailed in Sect. 2). Remarkably,
this dual-index framework uncovers a classic Kolmogorov scal-
ing (α3D
V
∼2/3) extending across a vast spatial dynamic range
enabled by PPCOS. We report this finding and its physical impli-
cations in Sect. 3, validate it by analyzing the high-order features
of the velocity structure in Sect. 4, and present our discussion in
Sect. 5. A breif summary is presented in Sect. 6.
2. Method
The TurbuStat package (Koch et al. 2019) implements the
∆-variance framework following Stutzki et al. (1998) and
Ossenkopf et al. (2008). A two-dimensional map is convolved
with an azimuthally symmetric, zero-mean Mexican-hat wavelet
kernel ψL across spatial lag scales L, with the spectrum σ2
∆(L)
computed as the mean square of the transformed map. This
method is robust against irregular boundaries and observational
noise (through smoothing).

3. Transition of turbulence in Polaris Flare
We apply the standard ∆-variance fitting framework (Sect. 2)
to the 12CO (1–0) integrated intensity (I, as a tracer of Σ) and
centroid velocity (V) maps of the Polaris Flare delivered by the
PPCOS survey (with a pixel size of 0.5′, Paper I). It is worth
noting that the physical component traced by 12CO (1–0) cor-
responds to the 12CO-excited molecular gas phase.
3.1. Intrinsic transition of velocity field?
As shown in Figure 1, both σ2
∆(I) and σ2
∆(V) display power-law
behaviors as a function of the spatial lag (L) on log-log scales.
Both spectra exhibit a steeper linear growth at small spatial
scales (L < 0.5 pc), which transitions smoothly into a shallower,
flatter linear growth regime at larger spatial scales (L > 0.5 pc).
A broken power-law fit to σ2
∆(V) yields scaling exponents of
αV = 1.18 ± 0.03 in the small-scale regime and αV = 0.70 ± 0.02
in the large-scale regime. For σ2
∆(I), a linear fit at low L yields
αI = 1.35±0.06, whereas αI = 0 provides a solid approximation
at high L. Notably, the transition scale (Ltrans) of 0.5 pc (∼11.5′)
is comparable to the spatial resolution of the CfA CO survey.
Based on these data, Stutzki et al. (1998) reported a single αV
value of 0.8, which closely aligns with our measured large-scale
exponent. This agreement implies that the transition is physi-
cal—whether reflecting a change in the intrinsic Hurst mode or

5. Limitations and discussions
Although our framework does not strictly require the 12CO emis-
sion to maintain a constant excitation temperature, we assume
that the line is optically thin to preserve geometric symmetry be-
tween the line-of-sight and plane-of-sky dimensions. The highly
diffuse nature of the Polaris Flare satisfies this criterion excep-
tionally well compared to more evolved, star-forming molecu-
lar clouds. However, this optically thin assumption may break
down within the densest structures of the cloud core (see Pa-
per I), potentially contributing to the observed scaling turnover
in σ2
∆around Ltrans.
Another underlying assumption is that Dc ≳2, which is
physically justified at our spatial resolution and consistent with
the lack of active star formation in the Polaris Flare (Paper II).
For sparse systems with Dc < 2, projection effects are statis-
tically minimal due to negligible line-of-sight overlap. While
our 0.05 pc resolution resolves typical filament widths, higher-
resolution observations are needed to trace the lower bound-
ary of the K41 cascade. Limitations remain: first, the projection
mapping for the log-variance σ2
s is currently unquantified, re-
stricting high-order analysis to a qualitative assessment. Second,
our model omits the interstellar magnetic field. Strong magnetic
fields break isotropic symmetry via anisotropic turbulent chan-
neling and induce Alfvénic coherence, causing the velocity field
to deviate locally from an isotropic stochastic process.
6. Summary
Using wide-field 12CO (J = 1 −0) data from the PPCOS sur-
vey, we show that the Polaris Flare exhibits a remarkably scale-
invariant, intrinsic 3D velocity scaling (α3D
V
∼0.62–0.64) that
matches Kolmogorov turbulence across more than two orders of
magnitude (0.05–20 pc). While localized mass restructuring al-
ters the cloud’s fractal geometry and saturates the intermittency
log-variance (σ2
s ∼1.3) below 0.5 pc, the fundamental kinetic
cascade remains uninterrupted. We conclude that the internal
turbulence of the Polaris Flare is cleanly inherited from the sur-
rounding large-scale CNM, maintaining a stable, scale-invariant
energy cascade despite localized mass restructuring.
