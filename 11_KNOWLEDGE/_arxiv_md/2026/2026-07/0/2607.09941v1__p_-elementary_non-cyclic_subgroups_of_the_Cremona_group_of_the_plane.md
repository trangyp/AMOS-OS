p-ELEMENTARY NON-CYCLIC SUBGROUPS OF THE CREMONA
GROUP OF THE PLANE
MANI ESNA ASHARI
Abstract. We classify, up to conjugacy, the subgroups of the Cremona group of the
plane isomorphic to (Z/pZ)r, where p is prime and r ≥2, over an algebraically closed
field k of characteristic not equal to p. In particular, we show that:
• If p ≥5, then r ≤2.
• If p = 3, then r ≤3.
• If p = 2, then r ≤4.
Furthermore, we give an explicit list of representatives via a set of 20 families consisting
of subgroups of the de Jonqui`eres group and subgroups of automorphisms of del Pezzo
surfaces, and we study the possible conjugacies by birational maps between these families.
Finally, we give some results on subgroups of the Cremona group of the plane isomorphic
to (Z/pZ)r, where p is prime and r is an integer, over an algebraically closed field k of
characteristic equal to p.
Keywords: algebraic geometry, birational geometry, Cremona group, de Jonqui`eres
group, del Pezzo surfaces, Sarkisov program.

1.2. Context of the article
This article belongs to the field of algebraic geometry, specifically focusing on birational geometry
and the study of the Cremona group. We provide a state of the art on p-elementary subgroups
of the Cremona group to identify current limitations, before introducing the specific goals of this
text.
1.2.1. First historical results on finite subgroups of the Cremona group.
The study of finite subgroups of the Cremona group was initiated by Seligmann Kantor in the late
nineteenth century. Later, Igor Dolgachev and Vasily Iskovskikh established a systematic approach
to the problem by linking these subgroups to automorphisms of del Pezzo surfaces (introduced by
Pasquale del Pezzo) and to de Jonqui`eres maps (first studied by Ernest de Jonqui`eres).
1.2.2. Previous results on p-elementary subgroups of the Cremona group.
Among the recent results on p-elementary subgroups of the Cremona group, we can cite the
thesis of J´er´emy Blanc [Bla06], where, among other results, he determined the isomorphism classes
of finite abelian subgroups of the Cremona group over the field of complex numbers. In particular,
he showed that the isomorphism class of maximal p-elementary subgroups are:
(Z/2)4,
(Z/3)3,
(Z/p)2
when p > 3.
Arnaud Beauville studied specifically p-elementary subgroups of the Cremona group over the
field of complex numbers and was able to give a complete classification of maximal p-elementary
subgroups up to birational map (see [Bea07]).
Finally, J´er´emy Blanc did an exhaustive study of cyclic subgroups of the Cremona group over
the field of complex numbers (see [Bla11]) up to birational conjugacy.
1.2.3. Limitations of previous results.
Hence the question of classifying up to conjugation by birational the ”intermediate” p-elementary
subgroups of the Cremona group, i.e. not cyclic and not maximal, in practice:
(Z/2)2,
(Z/2)3,
(Z/3)2,
was still open before this article.
Furthermore, we seek a classification that extends beyond the field of complex numbers to more
general fields.
1.2.4. Goal of the article.
The goal of this article is to provide a classification up to conjugation of p-elementary non-cyclic
subgroups of the Cremona group Cr over an algebraically closed field k in the case where the
characteristic of k is not equal to p. This is done in Theorem A and Theorem B:
• In Theorem A we give an exhaustive list of the p-elementary non-cyclic subgroups of Cr
when char(k) ̸= p.
• In Theorem B we give further information about the conjugation of p-elementary subgroups
in Cr when char(k) ̸= p.
We give also some results about the case char(k) = p in Theorem C: We explore the p-elementary
subgroups of the de Jonqui`eres group, and give a complete study of the Klein subgroups of Aut(P2).
1.2.5. Open problems and perspectives.
A natural next step would be to extend these results to more general settings:
• Study of the case where char(k) = p.
While Theorem C provides some results for p-
elementary subgroups, a comprehensive classification for the full Cremona group in char-
acteristic p remains an open challenge.
• Generalization to perfect fields. The results in this article are established over an alge-
braically closed field k. It would be of interest to extend this classification to the broader
class of perfect fields.

1.4. Fundamental lemmas on subgroups of the Cremona group
The study of finite subgroups of the Cremona group relies on the ability to realize these purely
algebraic objects as groups of automorphisms of smooth rational surfaces.
In this section, we
recall the fundamental definitions and results regarding minimal pairs and finite subgroups of the
Cremona group. In particular we state the important Proposition 1.4.4 which justifies the split of
our study of finite subgroups between Part 3 and Part 4.
1.4.1. Minimality of finite subgroups with Picard rank invariant 1.
Definition 1.4.1.
Let S be a rational surface and let G be a subgroup of the automorphism
group Aut(S). We say that the pair (G, S) is minimal if any G-equivariant birational morphism
ϕ: S →S′ such that ϕGϕ−1 ⊂Aut(S′) is an isomorphism.

(1)
Part 1 is dedicated to presenting the main results of this article and providing some
preliminary lemmas that we will use at various places in the text. In particular, we state
Proposition 1.4.4 that allows us to reduce the work to the study of subgroups of the de

Part 2 serves as a technical preamble, gathering essential results on subgroups of PGL(n, K).
By isolating these preliminary results, we establish a robust framework that simplifies the clas-

for the proofs in futures parts.
(3)
Part 3 is devoted to the study of p-elementary subgroups of the de Jonqui`eres group up
to conjugation in this group. This requires various tools including cohomology and linear
algebra. A part of this study is also done in the case where the characteristic of the field is
equal to p.
(4)
Part 4 explores the p-elementary subgroups of automorphisms of some del Pezzo surfaces.
The reader can observe that we don’t study all del Pezzo surfaces, rather we focus on del
Pezzo surfaces of degree lower or equal than 4, and on the Hirzebruch surface P1 ×P1. That is
because we don’t need the results about the other del Pezzo surfaces for our study. Examples
of tools used in this part are the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, the Picard rank formula, and
group actions.
(5)
Part 5 investigates potential conjugations between the representatives established in Parts
3 and 4, primarily through the Iskovskikh-Sarkisov program. Our analysis demonstrates that
this list is remarkably robust, as we identify only one instance of conjugation between distinct
families; this corresponds to the amazing result (see Subsection 1.3.2)
(6)
Part 6 is entirely dedicated to the case where char(k) = p. More specifically, we study
some 2-elementary subgroups of Aut(P2) when the characteristic of the field is 2. This allows
us to have a first impression of what can happen when char(k) = p, and observe some major
differences with the case where char(k) ̸= p. This part is independent of Parts 3, 4 and 5.
(7)
In Part 7 we summarize all intermediate steps from previous parts in order to prove the

(8)

result used in our classification. Appendix B provides some results on automorphisms of del
Pezzo surfaces; it notably covers the embedding of automorphism group for degrees 1, 2 and

Appendix C establishes a formula for the rank of the invariant Picard group, a recurring tool
in our study.

Part 5. Conjugation by birational maps of p-elementary subgroups
The work in the previous parts has allowed us to give a list of representatives of p-elementary
non-cyclic subgroups of the Cremona group. Now we would like to study the possible conjugations
by birational maps between these representatives. The goal of this part is to give the necessary
results to prove Theorem B. The four sections of this part are almost independent:
• In Section 5.1 we study a first easy case: The possible conjugations of a subgroup of
automorphisms of a del Pezzo surface of degree 1, 2 or 3 with Picard rank invariant equal to
1. We show that a conjugacy to a minimal pair can always be replaced by an isomorphism.
• In Section 5.2 and 5.3 we do a similar study as in Section 5.1 but for quartic surfaces.
This requires more work, and the conclusion differs: Klein subgroups of automorphisms
of quartic del Pezzo surface with Picard rank invariant equal to 1 are conjugate to de
Jonqui`eres subgroups (Proposition 5.3.7).
• Finally in Section 5.4 we study the conjugation between de Jonqui`eres subgroups. This is
used to show the amazing result (see Subsection 1.3.2)
We start our study by defining links of type (II, D) and (II, C):
Definition 5.0.1.
(1) A link of type (II, D) is a link of type II between two del Pezzo surfaces.
(2) A link of type (II, C) is a link of type II between two rational surfaces with conic bundle
structures.
The reader can refer to [Isk96, Theorem 2.6] for more details about these links.
