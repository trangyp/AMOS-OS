In the conventional Z′ portal dark matter scenario, the prediction of detectable dark matter χ
typically relies on the collider sensitivities of Z′ and direct detection, where the Majorana type right-
handed neutrinos are usually assumed. However, if the right-handed neutrinos νR are Dirac type,
they will contribute to the additional effective number of relativistic species ∆Neff, which brings
different detectable predictions for Z′ portal dark matter. In light of the great improvement of ∆Neff
for the upcoming experiments, we investigate the Z′ portal dark matter with Dirac type νR. Under the
U(1)B−L symmetry, this model includes νR with U(1)B−L charge QνR = −1 and χ with arbitrary
Qχ beyond the SM. Based on the relation in the production of χ and νR, both the WIMP and FIMP
dark matter through the Z′ portal scenario are considered. We perform a comprehensive exploration
of the viable parameter space under the constraints from ∆Neff induced by thermal and non-thermal
νR, perturbative limit, dark matter direct and indirect detection, and collider searches of Z′.

I.
INTRODUCTION
The mainstream opinion holds that the Standard Model (SM) does not contain any viable dark matter
candidate and predicts massless neutrinos 1. Currently, the cosmological and astrophysical observations
provide robust evidence for the existence of dark matter (DM)[2]. Meanwhile, the neutrino oscillation ob-
servations indicate that neutrinos have sub-eV masses[3–6]. However, the fundamental natures of DM and
neutrinos remain unclear. DM may consist of particles[7], in which the weakly interacting massive particle
(WIMP)[8, 9] and feebly interacting massive particle (FIMP)[10, 11] are the popular DM candidates. The
experiments searching for neutrinoless double-beta decay [12] do not yield a definitive signal for Majorana
neutrinos. Another alternative is Dirac neutrinos, which considerably contribute to the effective number of
relativistic neutrino species Neff via new interactions in new physics models[13–20]. The induced cosmo-
logical constraints impose stringent limits on these models incorporating dark matter[21–34].
The gauged B −L extension of the SM is a popular new physics framework [35–40], in which any
anomalies could be resolved by introducing three right-handed neutrinos νR, then producing the tiny neu-
trino masses via the seesaw mechanism [41–44]. Continuing to extend the dark sector enables connections
with DM, the cosmological observations of Neff induced by Dirac neutrinos νR could achieve the detection
of DM and the Z′ boson [45–51]. Focus on the WIMP dark matter in the resonant scenario, the minimal
extension involving only Dirac fermionic DM χ investigates the constraints from ∆Neff on the U(1)B−L
charge Qχ of DM [52]. Besides the resonance scenario [53], DM could be produced via the secluded and
FIMP scenarios [54] in this minimal U(1)B−L model, which has not been considered with Dirac neutrino
νR and viable region under ∆Neff.

candidate. In the WIMP scenario with resonance and secluded cases, χ has the U(1)B−L charge Qχ.

V.
CONCLUSION
In the minimal U(1)B−L model containing dark matter χ and Dirac neutrinos νR, the light Dirac
neutrinos have considerable contribution to the ∆Neff, which is constrained by the current experiments
DESI (requiring ∆Neff ≲0.4) and P-ACT (∆Neff ≲0.17), as well as the future experiments CMB-S4
(∆Neff ≲0.06) and CMB-HD (∆Neff ≲0.027). Since there is a close relationship between the generation
of dark matter χ and Dirac neutrino νR through Z′ portal, these ∆Neff constraints further restrict the al-
lowed parameter space of dark matter. Building upon the dark matter detection constraints and the collider
searches of Z′, this model predicts promising dark matter regions that can be tested by the future ∆Neff
experiments. Based on the production mechanism of dark matter, detailed discussions are carried out in
both the WIMP and the FIMP scenarios.
In the WIMP scenario, the observation of the dark matter relic density appears at the resonant (mZ′ ≃
2mχ) and secluded (mZ′ < mχ ) positions. In the resonance scenario, we fix rZ′ = 2.001 to avoid the
fate of being excluded by indirect detection constraints with extreme enhanced annihilation cross section.
Under various constraints including P-ACT, the surviving parameter space satisfies 400 GeV ≲mχ ≲
1.54 × 105 GeV with 0.7 ≲Qχ ≲3400. Meanwhile the corresponding gauge coupling is O(10−4) ≲
g′ ≲O(10−1). Hence dark matter always lies in the thermal region of νR, which indicates that the allowed
region has ∆Neff no less than 0.14. In the secluded scenario, we fix rZ′ = 0.5 to illustrate the allowed
region, which meets 20 GeV ≲mχ ≲4.9 × 104 GeV with 30 ≲Qχ ≲O(107). The corresponding gauge
coupling extends to O(10−9) ≲g′ ≲O(10−1). When Qχ ≳5 × 106, νR is non-thermally generated,
resulting in ∆Neff being less than 0.14. Future CMB-S4 and CMB-HD are expected to verify the finally

obtained allowed parameter spaces in both cases.
In the FIMP scenario, dark matter could transition from the non-thermal regime of νR to the thermal
regime as Qχ decreases. Eventually, the promising parameter space lies in the region enclosed by the CMB-
HD and P-ACT constraints, which requires 1.6×10−11 ≲Qχ ≲O(10−3) in the range of 0.1 GeV ≲mχ ≲
104 GeV with rZ′ = 10 and corresponding gauge coupling O(10−9) ≲g′ ≲O(10−1). Among this region,
Qχ ≲2 × 10−3 is mainly constrained by P-ACT, while CMB-HD at O(10−5) ≲Qχ ≲O(10−3) provides
a detectable upper limit.
In summary, dark matter in both the secluded and FIMP scenarios could be generated in the non-thermal
region of νR, thereby yielding ∆Neff smaller than 0.14, which corresponds to the minimum ∆Neff con-
tributed by thermal νR. In contrast, dark matter in the resonance scenario never leaves the thermal region of
νR, resulting in a minimum ∆Neff larger than 0.14. If the future experiments CMB-S4 and CMB-HD do not
observe a deviation of Neff compared to the SM, the resonance scenario will be completely excluded, while
the secluded and FIMP scenarios still have surviving parameter space. Meanwhile, the secluded scenario
can be further tested by the indirect detection experiments.
