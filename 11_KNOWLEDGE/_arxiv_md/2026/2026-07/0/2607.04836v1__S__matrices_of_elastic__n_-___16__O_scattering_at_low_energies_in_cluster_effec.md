Elastic n-16O scattering at low energies is studied in the framework of cluster eﬀective
ﬁeld theory. An evaluated data set of the total cross section of elastic n-16O scattering
at neutron energy, 0 ≤En ≤4 MeV, is adopted from Evaluated Nuclear Data File
(ENDF/B-VIII.0). We derive an expression for the S matrices of the elastic scattering for
seven spin-partial wave channels, lj = s1/2, p1/2, p3/2, d3/2, d5/2, f5/2, f7/2, including one
excited state and nineteen resonant states of 17O. Thirty-four parameters of the theory are
ﬁtted to the ENDF data, and we ﬁnd that a plotted line, by using the ﬁtted parameters,
reproduces the ENDF data well. We discuss the uncertainties that may appear in the
present approach from the ﬁtted parameters of the resonant states with widths, larger
than Γ = 90 keV. We also discuss the implications of the ﬁtted values of energies and
widths of the resonant states in the estimate of the astrophysical S factor of 13C(α,n)16O
reaction at stellar energies.

1. Introduction
Estimates of astrophysical S factors of nuclear reactions at low energies are essen-
tial for the study of stellar evolutions and explosions and nucleosynthesis in stars [1, 2].
13C(α,n)16O reaction plays an important role as a neutron source to create heavy ele-
ments through slow neutron captures, the so-called s-process, in low-mass asymptotic
giant branch (AGB) stars [3]. Recently, accurate measurements of 13C(α,n)16O at low
energies were reported from deep underground laboratories, the LUNA collaboration [4]
and the JUNA collaboration [5]. In the previous work [6], we constructed an eﬀective ﬁeld
theory (EFT) for the 13C(α,n)16O reaction and extrapolated the S factor to the stellar
energies after ﬁtting the parameters to the data reported by the LUNA and JUNA col-
laborations. We found that an uncertainty in the extrapolation appeared from a resonant
state of 17O around the α-13C breakup energy of 17O. The resonant states of 17O near
the α-13C breakup threshold can be investigated through elastic neutron-16O scattering
at low energies.
Early study of the elastic n-16O scattering can be found in the 1950s, e.g., in Refs [7,
8, 9]. This reaction is essential and selected as one of the highest-priority isotopes for the
CIELO collaboration [10], and the evaluated nuclear reaction data are compiled in the
evaluated nuclear data ﬁles, ENDF/B-VIII.0 [11]. Accurate knowledge of cross-section
data and related uncertainties is also crucial for the reactor analysis and design, and
nuclear criticality safety as well as for the processing and disposal of nuclear waste [12].
In this work, we adopt an evaluated data set of elastic n-16O scattering at low energies
from ENDF/B-VIII.0 as experimental data to ﬁx parameters of the theory. One may
refer to the compilation of resonant energies and widths of 17O in the TUNL nuclear data
evaluation [13] as well.
To construct an EFT for the study of a reaction is a popular theoretical method in
hadron physics and few-body physics [14, 15]. When constructing an EFT, one needs to
introduce a scale to separate the relevant degrees of freedom at low energy from irrelevant
degrees of freedom at high energy. One constructs an eﬀective Lagrangian with the ﬁelds of
the relevant degrees of freedom, satisfying required symmetries, and perturbatively expand
it in terms of the number of derivatives on the ﬁelds. Then, the reaction amplitudes can
be calculated from the eﬀective Lagrangian order by order, in powers of Q/ΛH, where Q
is a typical momentum scale of a reaction in question, and ΛH is the separation scale of
the theory. The irrelevant degrees of freedom at high energy are integrated out, and their
eﬀects are embedded in the coeﬃcients of the terms of the eﬀective Lagrangian. Those
coeﬃcients, in principle, can be ﬁxed from their mother theory, but they are practically
ﬁtted to experimental data. The theory is also easy to include external electromagnetic
and weak probes and to extend to the study of three-body systems.
In the previous
works, we constructed an EFT for α-12C system and studied elastic α-12C scattering [16,
17, 18, 19], β delayed α emission from 16N [20], and E1 and E2 transitions of 12C(α,γ)16O
reaction [21, 22, 23].
In this work, we study elastic n-16O scattering at 0 ≤En ≤4 MeV, where En is
the neutron energy in the laboratory frame, within the framework of an EFT. As we
will discuss in the next section, we introduce one excited and nineteen resonant states of

2. Overview of the construction of the theory
In the previous work [6], as mentioned, we studied the calculation of the S factor of
13C(α,n)16O at the energy up to 1 MeV, where the energy is of α-13C system in the
center-of-mass frame, by constructing an EFT. Parameters of the theory were ﬁtted to
the experimental data of the S factor of 13C(α,n)16O, reported from the LUNA and JUNA
collaborations, in an energy range from 0.23 MeV to 1 MeV. In the study, we included
three resonant states of 17O; s1/2(2), f5/2(3), p3/2(3) (lj(ith)) states at resonant energies,
Ex = 6.3623, 7.16424, 7.215 MeV, respectively [13]. Because the resonant energy of the
s1/2(2) state is very close to the α-13C breakup energy of 17O, the resonant peak of s1/2(2)
is not covered by the experimental data of the S factor. It leads to a major uncertainty
in the extrapolation of the S factor to the stellar energies.
In the present work, we study elastic n-16O scattering at neutron energies, 0 ≤En ≤
4 MeV in laboratory frame. 2 In this energy range of En, one has two open channels, n-16O
and α-13C, for 17O, where the breakup energy of the α-13C channel is En = 2.2156 MeV.
In the present explorative study, we ignore the α-13C channel. The contribution of the
α-13C channel is suppressed due to the penetration factor of the Coulomb interaction
between α and 13C. One may notice other contributions to the elastic n-16O scattering
such as the breakup to the n-12C system of 13C and the excited states of 13C and 16O.
Those contributions are open at higher energies than En = 4 MeV. We regard them as
irrelevant degrees of freedom at high energy.

6. Results and discussion
In this work, the elastic n-16O scattering at the neutron energy, 0 ≤En ≤4 MeV,
for the seven channels, lj = {s1/2, p1/2, p3/2, d3/2, d5/2, f5/2, f7/2}, is studied within the
framework of an EFT. We included one excited state and nineteen resonant states of 17O,
as relevant degrees of freedom for the present study, and derive the expression of the S
matrices of the elastic scattering for the seven channels from the eﬀective Lagrangian.
Thirty-four parameters of the theory are ﬁtted to the evaluated data of ENDF/B-VIII.0,
and we ﬁnd that the evaluated data are well reproduced by using the ﬁtted values of
the parameters. We note that with the parameters in the ﬁrst step of the ﬁt, the ﬁtted
line can reproduce the data of the resonances with small widths, but the discrepancies
between the plotted line and the data appear at the regions between the resonances with
large widths. (We did not show it in the ﬁgure.) Those discrepancies due to the broad
resonant tails are completely ﬁtted by the shape parameters, alj(i) and blj(i), in the second
step of the ﬁt, and the whole range of the data is reproduced well. We also plot the phase
shifts for the s1/2, p1/2, p3/2, d3/2 channels and ﬁnd that the plotted lines well reproduce

The evaluated data of the elastic n-16O scattering at the neutron energy, 0 ≤En ≤
8 MeV in ENDF/B-VIII.0 are calculated by an R-matrix analysis [11]. Our result indicates
that the formalism of EFT can be an alternative framework, along with the R-matrix
analysis, for the study of elastic n-16O scattering at low energies. Notable diﬀerences
between the EFT and the R-matrix analysis are as follows: 1) two parameters, a matching
radius a and a boundary parameter Bc in the R matrix analysis [35] do not exist in the
EFT, 2) the expression of the S matrices in the EFT is simple and transparent, and the
energies and widths of the resonant states appearing in the data are directly ﬁtted by the
parameters of the EFT, and 3) the EFT needs to introduce the shape parameters, alj(i)
and blj(i), for the resonances with large widths: those shape parameters do not exist in
the R-matrix theory [36]. Because of those diﬀerences, even though the two methods can
equally well ﬁt the same data set, the values of the energies and widths of the resonant
states can be diﬀerent; a model dependence (or a systematic uncertainty) between the
two methods may appear from the resonance states with large widths. As seen in Table 1,
the four resonant s1/2(2), p3/2(3), d3/2(1), d3/2(3) states, to which we needed to introduce the
shape parameters, have the widths larger than Γ = 90 keV.
Especially, the resonant s1/2(2) and d3/2(3) states of 17O may bring the uncertainties in
the extrapolation of the S factor of 13C(α,n)16O to the stellar energies. One may ﬁnd
some scattered values of the resonant energies and widths of those states in Table 1 in
Ref. [37] and Table I in Ref. [38]. Thus, one should be cautious when choosing one of
those evaluated values of the parameters for his or her study. To avoid such a systematic
uncertainty, one may need to analyze simultaneously the two data sets, the elastic n-16O
scattering and the 13C(α,n)16O reaction, for the study of the S factor of 13C(α,n)16O at
the stellar energies.
