NATHANIEL BANNISTER
JUSTIN TATCH MOORE
Abstract. We develop methods for forcing lim1 A ̸= 0, where A is a partic-
ular inverse system of abelian groups introduced by Mardeˇsi´c and Prasolov in
their computation of certain strong homology groups. These methods allow
us to extend previous nonvanishing results of Casarosa and Lambie-Hanson
for limk A for k ≥2. Specifically we show that, for a given n, it is relatively
consistent with ZFC that b = d = ωn and limk A ̸= 0 whenever 1 ≤k ≤n (pre-
viously established with 2 ≤k ≤n). We also show it is relatively consistent
with ZFC that b = d = ωω+2 and limk A ̸= 0 for all k ≥1 (previously estab-
lished with k ≥2). We also adapt proofs of Kamo to show that lim1 A = 0
holds in many finite support iterated forcing extensions.
1. Introduction
This paper contributes to a growing body of research on the interactions of set
theory and homological algebra, and in particular to the derived functors of the
inverse limit functor. We isolate several situations in which the first derived limit
of a particular system A does and does not vanish. The derived limits of this system
play a role in several fields of mathematical research; to give two examples:
• In [24], Mardeˇsi´c and Prasolov isolate the system A and show that if strong
homology is additive on the class of closed subspaces of Rn, then limn A = 0
for all n > 0;
• Clausen and Scholze have shown that limn A[H] = 0 for all n > 0 is equiv-
alent to a statement about derived functors in condensed abelian groups
(see, for instance, [11, Lecture 4]).
The focus of the early research in the late 1980s and 1990s was on the first derived
limit of A; see, for instance, [24, 13, 33, 38]. The past decade has seen renewed
interest in the system A, with a particular focus on the derived limits limn A for
n ≥2, including [5, 6, 2, 7, 39, 10, 1]. A number of questions have remained open
