Abstract. We completely determine the magnitude homology of tope graphs of real hyperplane ar-
rangements. Their ranks can be described as the Hilbert functions of the Stanley–Reisner rings of certain
simplicial complexes naturally associated with the arrangements. For Coxeter arrangements, this gives
a computation of the magnitude homology of the Cayley graph of the corresponding Coxeter group. We
also prove the homological reciprocity for central arrangements conjectured by Koizumi–Liu. The proof
combines poset combinatorics, the Edelman–Walker theorem, and Alexander duality.

1. Introduction
Magnitude is an invariant of metric spaces, or more generally of enriched categories, introduced by
Leinster [Lei13; Lei19]. For a finite connected graph, its magnitude is a formal power series in q whose
constant term is the number of vertices.
Magnitude has various cardinality-like properties, such as
additivity and multiplicativity, and may be regarded as an extension of cardinality that reflects metric
structure. Hepworth and Willerton [HW17] constructed a homology theory called magnitude homology
and showed that the coefficient of qℓin the magnitude is the Euler characteristic of the length-ℓmagnitude
homology. Since then, the magnitude homology of graphs has been studied using various tools from
combinatorics and topology, including order complexes [KY21], simplicial complex models [AI21], and
Morse-theoretic methods [Gu18; TY23]. These studies have not only developed various computational
methods but also revealed structural properties such as the existence of torsion [KY21; SS21] and the
relationship between diagonality and girth [AHK24].
Because magnitude refines cardinality, it is natural to seek magnitude refinements of enumeration
problems involving objects equipped with metrics or graph structures.
In joint work with Liu, the
author studied such a “magnitude refinement” of the classical problem of counting the chambers of a
real hyperplane arrangement [KL26]. For a real hyperplane arrangement A, the tope graph T (A) has
the chambers as vertices, with edges joining adjacent chambers. The author and Liu proved that when

7.4. n-diagonal graphs that are not diagonal. Let G be a finite connected graph and n ≥1. We
say that G is n-diagonal if
∀k ≤n,
∀ℓ> k,
MHk,ℓ(G) = 0.
This means that the magnitude homology concentrates on the diagonal k = ℓin the range 0 ≤k ≤n.
All finite graphs are 1-diagonal [LS21, Example 4.6]. Here, we construct, for every n ≥1, a graph whose
magnitude homology is concentrated on the diagonal for k ≤n, but is not diagonal in all degrees.
Consider the generic central hyperplane arrangement Un+1,n+2 in Rn+1 (Example 2.5). Its tope graph
Gn = T (Un+1,n+2) is isomorphic to the two-punctured hypercube {+, −}n+2 \ {(+, . . . , +), (−, . . . , −)}.
The face {0} of Un+1,n+2 is of bidegree (n + 1, n + 2), and all other faces are of bidegree (k, k) for some
k ≤n.
Therefore, for any face flag F, we have either rank(F) = ℓ(F) or rank(F) ≥n + 1.
By
Corollary 6.6, this shows that Gn is n-diagonal. On the other hand, the face flag ({0}) is of bidegree
(n + 1, n + 2), so we have MHn+1,n+2(Gn) ̸= 0. In particular, Gn is not diagonal.
