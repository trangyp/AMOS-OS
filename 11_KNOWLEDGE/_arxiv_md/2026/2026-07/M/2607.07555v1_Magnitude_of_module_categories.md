ERLEND D. BØRVE, DANIEL HORIATAKIS, AND MARTIN KALCK
In memory of Idun Reiten.
Abstract. We define an invariant of the module category of a representation-finite algebra by the
magnitude of its Auslander algebra. This invariant will be called the magnitude of the module category.
For bound path algebras, it can be computed as the Euler characteristic of the Auslander–Reiten quiver,
in a suitable sense. To aid the computation of our invariant, we define the Auslander–Reiten–Euler
characteristic of a translation quiver.
We build on classical results in Auslander–Reiten theory to
determine the magnitude of module categories of biserial algebras, hereditary path algebras, radical
square zero bound path algebras, and self-injective bound path algebras. In these cases, we express
our invariant in terms of other known quantities, notably the rank of the Grothendieck group and
Coxeter numbers of Dynkin quivers. Based on our calculations and results, we obtain a conjectural
characterisation of representation-finite biserial algebras in terms of the magnitude of the module category
and the rank of the Grothendieck group.

0. Introduction
Over the last two decades, a notion of magnitude has been introduced and studied in various settings.
It generalises the Euler characteristic of topological spaces to a wide range of other mathematical objects,
like metric spaces, graphs, groupoids, and orbifolds [Lei08,Lei13]. Before it was mathematically developed,
the magnitude of metric spaces appeared implicitly in the study of ecology and diversity [SP94]. A
mathematically rigorous definition [Lei08,Lei13] later paved the way for interdisciplinary insights between
mathematics and ecology [Lei09,LC12,Mec15,LM16,Lei21].
The most general setting where one considers magnitude is in the framework of enriched categories
with an appropriate notion of “size” of objects [Lei13,NT16]. In familiar instances, it is clear that one
obtains very natural invariants. Indeed, for finite metric spaces, the magnitude is to be regarded as the
“effective number of points” [Lei13, §2.1]. For finite skeletal categories whose classifying space is a finite
simplicial complex, one recovers the Euler characteristic of the classifying space [Lei08, Proposition 2.11].
Another suitable setting is formed by Hom-finite linear categories, which lead Chuang–King–Leinster to
define the magnitude of a finite-dimensional algebra [CKL16]. It is analogous to the Euler characteristic
of topological spaces (see Proposition 1.4).

The purpose of this text is to develop a theory of magnitude for categories of finitely generated modules
over finite-dimensional algebras. At present, our constructions work for representation-finite algebras,
i.e. finite-dimensional algebras admitting only finitely many indecomposable modules up to isomorphism.
Given a representation-finite algebra Λ, we will achieve our aim by investigating the magnitude of its
Auslander algebra. This quantity will be called the magnitude of the module category of Λ, and will be
denoted χ(Λ-mod). It only depends on the category of indecomposable left Λ-modules (see Remark 1.7(1)).
If Λ is a bound path k-algebra, Lemma 1.9 lets us compute our invariant as N −A + E, where the terms
are the numbers of vertices, arrows, and meshes, respectively, in the Auslander–Reiten quiver of Λ-mod.
Here, Λ-mod denotes the category of finitely generated left Λ-modules.
Our main results determine the magnitude of the module categories of several important classes of
representation-finite algebras Λ. Recall that the rank of Λ is defined as the rank of the Grothendieck
group K0(Λ-mod). The rank of Λ is equal to the number of finitely generated indecomposable projective
left Λ-modules up to isomorphism. Below, Λ is a bound path algebra, so the rank is also equal to the
number of vertices in the Gabriel quiver of Λ.
Theorem (Theorem 3.5, Corollary 3.6, and Corollary 3.8). Let Λ be a representation-finite (special)
biserial bound path algebra of rank n. Then χ(Λ-mod) = n. In particular, this holds when Λ is a string
algebra, a Nakayama algebra, or a representation-finite local bound path algebra.

Structure of the paper. We begin by arguing that the magnitude of an Auslander algebra is
an invariant under Morita equivalence. It is not, however, invariant under derived Morita equivalence,
see Remark 4.5. The rest of Section 1 is dedicated to developing tools which will be used to calculate
magnitudes of specific Auslander algebras in later sections.
In Section 2, we recall the notion of a translation quiver, and define the Auslander–Reiten–Euler
characteristic of such. One specific type of translation quivers are repetitive quivers. Since many Auslander–
Reiten quivers are closely related to them, they provide a useful tool for magnitude computations in later
sections. In Sections 3, 4, and 5, we prove our main results listed above.
Notation and conventions. Throughout the text, we fix a field k. Given a finite-dimensional
k-algebra Λ, the word module will mean finitely generated left Λ-module. If a and b are arrows in a quiver,
such that the target of a is the source of b, we denote the concatenated path by ba.

5. Self-injective algebras
Definition 5.1. A k-algebra Λ is self-injective if Λ is injective as a left Λ-module.
Representation-finite self-injective bound path algebras have a well understood Auslander–Reiten theory.
This will enable us to compute the magnitude of their module categories. To give a sufficient description
of the Auslander–Reiten quiver of the module category of a self-injective algebra, we need to recall some
terminology.
Definition 5.2 ([Rie80]). Let (T, τ) be a translation quiver.
(1) A vertex x in T is stable if τ ℓx is non-projective for all ℓ≥0.
(2) The translation quiver (T, τ) is stable if every vertex in T is stable. Equivalently, every vertex in
T is non-projective.
(3) Given a translation quiver (T, τ), we define its stable part to be the full sub-translation quiver of
(T, τ) consisting of the stable vertices in T.
For example, repetitive quivers (as defined in Definition 2.5) are stable translation quivers. Another
important class of stable translation quivers are given by stable Auslander–Reiten quivers. These are by
definition the stable parts of Auslander–Reiten quivers.
Remark 5.3. The case of self-injective bound path algebras is of particular interest because the stable
Auslander–Reiten quiver contains all vertices of the Auslander–Reiten quiver except the indecomposable
projectives. Indeed, if M is a non-projective indecomposable, then τ ℓM cannot possibly be projective for
any ℓ≥0, because there would otherwise exist a non-injective indecomposable projective.
Before proving Theorem 5.8, we follow Riedtmann’s approach to characterising the stable Auslander–
Reiten quivers of self-injective algebras [Rie80] over algebraically closed fields. By Remark 1.8(2), the
Auslander–Reiten quiver of a bound path algebra is unaltered under extension of scalars. We may therefore
consider self-injective bound path algebras over an arbitrary field.
Definition 5.4. Let G be a group acting on a translation quiver (T, τ) by automorphisms (see Defini-
tion 2.1(6)), i.e. a subgroup of the automorphism group of (T, τ). We say that the action of G is admissible
if for every x in T0, each orbit of G meets {x} ∪x−in at most one vertex, and meets {x} ∪x+ in at most
one vertex.
The translation τ of a stable translation quiver (T, τ) determines an automorphism of (T, τ). By
Definition 2.1(6), the automorphism τ is central in the automorphism group of (T, τ).
If a group G acts admissibly on a translation quiver (T, τ), then the quotient T/G again admits the
structure of a translation quiver. We abuse notation by writing τ for the induced translation on T/G.
We call (T/G, τ) the orbit translation quiver of T with respect to G. If (T, τ) is stable, so is (T/G, τ). In
particular, an admissible quotient ZT/G of a repetitive quiver ZT is a stable translation quiver. We now
recall how Riedtmann characterises the stable Auslander–Reiten quivers of connected representation-finite
bound path k-algebras.
Lemma 5.5 ([Rie80, Hauptsatz and Anhang 2]). Let Λ be a representation-finite bound path k-algebra.
(1) For every connected component (L, τ) of the stable Auslander–Reiten quiver of Λ-mod, there
exists a Dynkin quiver T and a non-trivial group G acting admissibly on ZT such that (L, τ) is
isomorphic to ZT/G as a translation quiver.
(2) Let T be a Dynkin quiver. Then any non-trivial group acting admissibly on ZT is an infinite
cyclic group. All possible generators are of the form στ −r, where r ≥1 and σ is an automorphism
of ZT which fixes a vertex x ∈ZT.
(3) In particular, if Λ is self-injective, connected, and not semisimple, then the stable Auslander–Reiten
quiver of Λ-mod is connected (see Remark 5.3) and isomorphic to ZT/⟨στ −r⟩. Here, T is a Dynkin
quiver, r ≥1, and σ is an automorphism of ZT with a fixed vertex.
Let T be a directed tree. Recall that a section of ZT is a full subquiver of ZT containing exactly one
vertex in each τ-orbit. It is clear that automorphisms of ZT send sections to sections, and that only the
identity automorphism sends a given section to itself. Let σ be as in Lemma 5.5(2), and let x be a fixed
vertex of σ. Since x is contained in at least one section S of ZT, σ sends S to a (possibly different) section
containing x. There are only finitely many sections of ZT containing x. Hence, there must exist positive
integers s ̸= t such that σsS = σtS. This shows that σ must be of finite order.
To prove Theorem 5.8, we need a result on the Auslander–Reiten–Euler characteristic of certain finite
stable translation quivers. An illustrative example will follow in Example 5.7.
