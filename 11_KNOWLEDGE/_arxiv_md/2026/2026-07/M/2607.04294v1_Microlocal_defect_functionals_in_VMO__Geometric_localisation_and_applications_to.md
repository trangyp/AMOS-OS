M. MIˇSUR
Abstract. We extend the concept of microlocal defect functionals to test
functions belonging to the space L∞∩VMOc. Following L. Tartar’s remark
that an extension of such concepts to VMO spaces should be possible, we es-
tablish a functional-analytic framework for this extension within the Lp −Lq
setting. Because the topological dual of VMO is the Hardy space H1, the re-
sulting object takes the form of an H-distribution rather than a non-negative
Radon measure.
By assuming strict H¨older conjugate inequalities, we uti-
lize the John-Nirenberg inequality over localized domains to construct these
functionals. We demonstrate that the functional acts as a distribution on the
inductive limit topology of the test spaces, enabling exact geometric localisa-
tion principles for equations with rough VMO coefficients, that is, coefficients
admitting sharp transitions of vanishing mean oscillation. To demonstrate the
versatility of this framework across diverse physical environments, we deploy it
to determine the exact geometric structure and support of macroscopic energy
defects in three distinct settings: the characteristic support of stratified trans-
port, zero-order non-local cross-phase energies, and the microlocal trapping of
sub-critical acoustic scattering in high-contrast media.
1. Introduction
The theory of H-measures, introduced independently by Tartar [22] and G´erard
[11], has proven to be an indispensable tool in studying the oscillations and concen-
tration effects of weakly converging sequences in L2. A fundamental requirement
of this classical construction is the commutation of multiplication and Fourier mul-
tiplier operators. Tartar noted in his introductory text [23] that because the com-
mutator [Mb, Aψ] remains compact when the multiplication symbol b belongs to
the space of functions of vanishing mean oscillation (VMO) [25], an extension of
microlocal defect functionals to VMO test functions should be possible.
In this note, we establish the mathematical framework required for such an exten-
sion. Because the topological dual of global VMO(Rd) is the Hardy space H1(Rd)
[20], and elements of H1(Rd) must satisfy a zero-integral moment condition, any
non-negative measure acting continuously on global VMO(Rd) is necessarily trivial.
While restricting the test space to compactly supported functions shifts the dual
to a local Hardy space (bypassing this strict moment condition), the strict integra-
bility gap of the Lp −Lq framework fundamentally necessitates that the proposed
extension takes the form of an H-distribution, joining other significant generaliza-
tions of the classical theory, such as the ultraparabolic H-measures developed by
Panov [19]. The H-distribution framework was introduced by Antoni´c and Mitrovi´c

This extrinsic topological construction is a deliberate and necessary choice. If one
were to define the space intrinsically—evaluating the mean oscillation exclusively
over balls strictly contained within the compact set K—extending such functions
by zero to the whole of Rd would introduce sharp boundary jumps. Due to the
non-local nature of the John-Nirenberg mean oscillation, evaluating the BMO norm
across these artificial boundaries causes the integrals to blow up, perfectly mirroring
the well-known zero-extension pathologies of fractional Sobolev spaces on bounded
domains.
By defining VMOc(Rd) extrinsically, via the strict inductive limit of global func-
tions restricted to compact supports, the functions inherently possess a controlled
decay to zero. We entirely bypass these boundary singularities, guaranteeing that
non-local operators—such as global Fourier multipliers and fractional derivatives—
can act on the space without restriction.
The ambient test space is the full strict LF-space VMOc(Rd), endowed with
the inductive limit topology generated by the BMO norms of its Banach steps
VMOKn(Rd); the space L∞(Rd) never enters the topology. The essential bound-
edness of the test functions is instead imposed as a set restriction on the subclass
over which the microlocal functionals are directly evaluated. Concretely, the bi-
linear functional D of Section 3 is defined by an explicit integral formula on the
dense subspace L∞(Rd) ∩VMOc(Rd)—where boundedness is genuinely required,
see Remark 1—and is subsequently extended to all of VMOc(Rd) by continuity,
using the fact that the localized BMO seminorm is a genuine norm on compactly
supported functions (a function of vanishing mean oscillation with support in a
proper compact set is constant only if it is zero).
The intersection space L∞(Rd)∩VMO(Rd) additionally carries a Banach algebra
structure that is exploited when absorbing variable coefficients into the test slot. By
themselves, BMO(Rd) and VMO(Rd) are linear spaces but fail to be algebras under
pointwise multiplication, as elements can exhibit localized logarithmic singularities.
Intersecting with L∞(Rd) provides the necessary truncation, establishing a Banach

10. Future Directions and Possible Applications
The theoretical framework for VMO H-distributions formally established in this
note opens several pathways for further mathematical analysis. The utility of this
framework is the capacity to handle rough, sharply varying coefficients that classical
H-measures and distributions cannot process, provided their oscillation vanishes in
the mean, i.e. they are of vanishing mean oscillation. Natural future applications
include the following.
10.1. Fractional Derivatives and Non-Local Dirichlet Forms. While frac-

preserve and amplify microlocal oscillations. If a sequence is bounded in a fractional

10.2. Variable-Exponent Nemytskii Operators. The sub-critical polynomial

is rigorously identified as a single, continuous linear entity belonging to the dual
space (X ˆ⊗πY )′.
Additionally, this joint continuity guarantees that the partial evaluation mapping
φ 7→D(φ, ·) is a well-defined, continuous linear operator from X into the strong
topological dual Y ′.
Because Y = Cκ(Sd−1) with κ > d/2, its dual space Y ′
precisely constitutes the space of spherical distributions of order κ on the unit
sphere.
□
Remark 35. This operator representation establishes the H-distribution as a precise
physical measuring device. By inputting a localized spatial test function φ ∈X,
which isolates a specific geometric region of highly oscillatory VMO concentration,
the continuous linear operator outputs exactly the angular spectrum of the trapped
macroscopic energy as a spherical distribution. This mathematical decomposition
confirms that the defect measure inherently separates the spatial location of the
trapped energy from its corresponding directional scattering signature.
A.9. Separability and Sequential Weak-∗Compactness. In classical func-
tional analysis, the space L∞(Rd) is strictly non-separable. Consequently, bounded
sets in its topological dual are not metrizable in the weak-∗topology, preventing
the extraction of weakly-∗convergent subsequences and forcing the reliance on gen-
eralized nets. However, the topological architecture of our specific test space fully
resolves this obstruction.
Proposition 36. The strict LF-space VMOc(Rd) is separable, and so is its bounded
subspace L∞(Rd)∩VMOc(Rd). Consequently, every bounded sequence in the Fr´echet
dual space admits a weakly-∗convergent subsequence.
Proof. Each Banach step VMOKn(Rd) is separable: the global space VMO(Rd)
is separable, being the closure of Cc(Rd) in the BMO norm, and separability
passes to its closed subspaces. By the density proposition above, D(Rd) is dense
in VMOc(Rd) in the inductive limit topology; since D(Rd) is itself separable it
contains a countable dense subset M, and the continuity of the inclusion D(Rd) ,→
VMOc(Rd) forces M to be dense in VMOc(Rd) as well. Thus VMOc(Rd) is a sep-
arable topological vector space, and its subspace L∞(Rd)∩VMOc(Rd) is separable
in the induced (BMO-based) topology.
By the generalized Banach-Alaoglu theorem, the polar of any neighborhood of
the origin in a separable locally convex space is a weakly-∗compact, metrizable
subset of the dual space. Therefore, bounded sets in the Fr´echet dual space are
sequentially compact in the weak-∗topology, guaranteeing that any bounded se-
quence of linear functionals admits a weakly-∗convergent subsequence.
□
Remark 37. This topological separability provides the rigorous mathematical jus-
tification for the Cantor diagonal extraction utilized in the proof of Theorem 3.
Crucially, the argument does not require separability of L∞(Rd): it is the BMO-
based inductive limit topology of VMOc(Rd) that is separable, and the essential
supremum enters only as a set restriction on the functions being evaluated, never
as part of the topology.
