Abstract
We study the low-temperature expansion of the disk partition function Zpβq of the double-
scaled SYK model (DSSYK) at fixed coupling λ “ 2p2{N, where N is the number of Majorana
fermions and p is the number of fermions in each interaction term, both taken to infinity.
We show that the exact Bessel-function representation of Zpβq, expanded at large argument
(corresponding to low temperature), can be organized in terms of the classical ring of quasi-
modular Eisenstein series E2, E4, E6 and their differential identities. Exploiting the modular S-
duality properties of this ring, we derive the semiclassical (small λ) low-temperature expansion
of Zpβq, splitting it into a perturbative tower and a non-perturbative sector controlled by rq “
e´4π2{λ. At each order in rq, we determine the non-perturbative correction in closed form up to
second order in λ; the resulting series resums into a compact expression in the same Eisenstein
series, extending previous semiclassical results beyond their strict β Ñ 8 limit. We further
show that this entire structure follows from a single, exact differential equation coupling a
modular derivative to derivatives with respect to temperature. Finally, we prove that the non-
perturbative sector of Zpβq is exactly supported, to all orders in λ, on the same exponents as
the on-shell actions of known bilocal-Liouville saddles of the DSSYK Schwarzian limit, pointing
to a well-defined bulk origin for these non-perturbative corrections.
Keywords: Non-perturbative effects, 2D gravity, AdS/CFT correspondence

Introduction and summary of results
Since its introduction, the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model of N strongly-coupled, randomly-interacting
Majorana fermions [1] has been a rare example of a quantum-mechanical system that is simultane-
ously maximally chaotic and exactly solvable at large N [2]. Keeping the number p of fermions per
interaction term fixed as N Ñ 8, the model flows in the infrared to Schwarzian quantum mechanics,
the boundary dynamics of nearly-AdS2 JT gravity [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]. Instead, a different and equally
tractable regime is reached by sending p Ñ 8 together with N, at fixed ratio λ “ 2p2{N [8]. In
this double-scaled SYK (DSSYK) model, the disorder average can be performed exactly, reducing
every observable to a combinatorial sum over intersecting chord diagrams [5, 9], or equivalently
to matrix elements of simple operators built from q-deformed oscillators acting on an auxiliary
chord Hilbert space, with q “ e´λ [10, 11]. This exact control at all values of λ, well beyond the
Schwarzian/JT corner of parameter space, is what makes DSSYK a valuable testing ground for
holography. At generic λ, this holographic role has been made precise: the DSSYK transfer matrix

dilaton gravity, identifying the latter as the exact holographic dual of the model [12, 13, 14, 15], as

In this paper we reconsider the disk partition function Zpβq, and specifically its behaviour at low
temperature, β Ñ 8, at fixed double-scaling parameter λ. The high-temperature (β Ñ 0) expansion
of Zpβq is a convergent power series in β with λ-dependent coefficients, reflecting the analyticity
of the exact partition function at β “ 0 [18, 19]. The low-temperature regime is comparatively
less studied and qualitatively different: the semiclassical (λ Ñ 0) saddle-point expansion of Zpβq
at generic β [20, 21] is only asymptotic in λ, and matching it order by order onto the small-β
result shows that a genuine non-perturbative completion, invisible to this saddle-point expansion,
is required once β becomes large.
An initial treatment of the non-perturbative low-temperature completion was given in [22].

partition function is exactly multiplied, to all orders in rq “ e´4π2{λ, by the cube of the Dedekind eta
function ηprqq3, with no residual dependence on β at the order of approximation considered there.
A possible holographic interpretation of this correction was also proposed there.

Conclusions
Starting from the exact Bessel-function representation of Zpβq, we found that its expansion at large
argument can be organized entirely in terms of the classical ring of quasi-modular Eisenstein series
E2, E4, E6, eq. (4.22). Exploiting the modular (S-duality) properties of this ring, we obtained the
semiclassical expansion of log Zpβq at small λ, splitting it into a perturbative tower, systematic to
all orders in both 1{β and λ, and a non-perturbative sector controlled by rq “ e´4π2{λ.
At each order rqn, keeping systematically the subleading large-β corrections neglected by the
leading Bessel approximation of [22], we found that the non-perturbative correction resums, up to
second order in λ, into the closed forms fn,0pyq, fn,1pyq and fn,2pyq, eq. (5.35), with coefficients built
from the divisor functions σ1pnq and σ3pnq – extending the β-independent, leading-order result of
[22], Cn “ ´3σ1pnq{n, to a genuine function of y “ 24π2{pλβq. Using the generating functions of
σ1pnq and σ3pnq in terms of the quasi-modular Eisenstein series E2, E4, the entire non-perturbative
series up to this order resums into the closed expression (5.38). We then showed that this structure –
perturbative and non-perturbative alike – follows from a single, exact differential equation, eq. (6.3),
relating a modular derivative and x-derivatives of the Bessel-function representation of Zpβq; both
towers are recovered from it as solutions in the appropriate limits, with the divisor-sum coefficients
entering as boundary data fixed by matching onto the quasi-modular expansion, rather than being
derived from the differential equation itself.
Beyond providing a systematic, all-order completion of the semiclassical expansion, these re-
sults illustrate a broader point: the low-temperature behaviour of the DSSYK partition function,
non-perturbative sector included, is controlled by essentially number-theoretic data – Eisenstein
series, divisor sums, and their modular transformation properties – reflecting general modular-form
technology rather than features specific to the detailed structure of the DSSYK Hamiltonian. The
next natural question to ask is whether this structure admits a direct bulk interpretation.
To this end, it is tempting to compare the powers of Λ defined in (5.36) with the additional sad-
dles of the bilocal-Liouville theory found in [24] in the triple-scaling limit discussed in Section 5.1.1.
Besides the primary Schwarzian saddle discussed there – whose free energy underlies their eq. (2.14),
matched against (5.9)–(5.11) in Section 5.1.1 – one finds in [24] a tower of further saddles labelled
by k “ 1, 2, . . . , corresponding to reparametrizations winding 2k`1 times around the thermal circle
