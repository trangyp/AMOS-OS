Modified Cosmology from Mass-to-Horizon Relation: Background Evolution
Pranav Prasanthan,1, ∗Hussain Gohar,1, † and Vincenzo Salzano1, ‡
1Institute of Physics, University of Szczecin, Wielkopolska 15, 70-451 Szczecin, Poland
(Dated: July 2, 2026)
We investigate the cosmological implications of the mass-to-horizon relation, which provides a
unified framework for thermodynamically consistent generalized horizon-entropy functionals. Using
the Cai–Kim formulation of the first law of thermodynamics, we derive the corresponding modified
Friedmann equations and examine the resulting background evolution. We find that cosmological
viability sharply restricts admissible deviations from the Bekenstein–Hawking area law: phenomeno-
logically acceptable scenarios are confined to a narrow neighborhood of the standard entropy, while
more pronounced deviations generically spoil the standard radiation–matter–dark-energy sequence.
Power-law entanglement corrections can give rise to a moderate early-dark-energy component, but
only within a tightly constrained region of parameter space, whereas quantum-gravity corrections
are suppressed by the Planck scale and remain observationally irrelevant.
Consequently, all vi-
able models predict a ΛCDM-like cosmological background at the present epoch. These findings
demonstrate that background cosmology alone imposes stringent constraints on thermodynamically
consistent generalized entropy constructions of this class.
I.
INTRODUCTION
A wide range of cosmological observations indicates
that the Universe is undergoing a phase of accelerated
expansion [1–8]. Within General Relativity (GR), this
phenomenon is commonly attributed to dark energy, of-
ten modeled as a cosmological constant Λ with equation
of state ω = −1. Although the resulting Λ cold dark mat-
ter (ΛCDM) model successfully describes a broad range
of observations [9], it faces well-known theoretical chal-
lenges, including the fine-tuning and coincidence prob-
lems [10–13], as well as observational tensions such as
the H0 and S8 discrepancies [14]. These issues have moti-
vated the exploration of alternative cosmological scenar-
ios based on modified gravity and dynamical dark energy
[15–17].
An alternative route is provided by the gravity–
thermodynamics correspondence, which relates gravita-
tional dynamics to horizon thermodynamics [18].
Fol-
lowing the pioneering works of Bekenstein and Hawk-
ing [19–21], Jacobson showed that Einstein’s equations
can be derived from the Clausius relation applied to lo-
cal causal horizons [22]. This thermodynamic perspec-
tive was subsequently extended to Friedmann–Lemaître–
Robertson–Walker (FLRW) spacetimes with any spatial
curvature by Cai and Kim [18], who derived the Fried-
mann equations from the first law of thermodynamics
applied to the apparent horizon.
Within this frame-
work, the standard Bekenstein–Hawking entropy repro-
duces the standard ΛCDM equations, while modifications
of the entropy-area relation generate modified Friedmann
equations; the resulting corrections can be reinterpreted
as an effective dark energy sector [23].

The Bekenstein definition of horizon entropy is in-
trinsically nonextensive and nonadditive, as it scales
with the area of the horizon rather than with the en-
closed volume [24–26]. This nonextensive character has
motivated the development and systematic investiga-
tion of generalized entropy frameworks—such as Tsal-
lis, Rényi, Sharma–Mittal, Kaniadakis, Tsallis–Cirto,
Tsallis–Zamora, and Barrow entropies [27–35]—which
have been extensively employed in gravitational and
cosmological settings [36–55].
In addition, quantum-
gravitational and entanglement-induced corrections to
the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, which yield logarith-
mic and power-law modifications to the Bekenstein en-
tropy, have also been the subject of extensive analysis
[56–61].
Recent studies have demonstrated that employing gen-
eralized entropies on black hole and cosmological hori-
zons in conjunction with the Hawking temperature leads
to thermodynamic and holographic inconsistencies [62–
67].
To restore consistency, the notion of a mass-to-
horizon relation was introduced in [65] to maintain holo-
graphic thermodynamic consistency, resulting in a gener-
alized entropy functional that encompasses several non-
extensive and phenomenological entropy frameworks.
More recently, this construction has been extended to
incorporate quantum-gravity and entanglement correc-
tions, thereby providing a unified treatment of both non-
extensive and quantum-gravity/entanglement-corrected
entropies while preserving the Clausius relation and en-
suring holographic thermodynamic consistency [66, 67].
In this work, we investigate the cosmological conse-
quences of this generalized mass-to-horizon entropy in
[66].
Applying the Cai–Kim formulation [18] of the
first law of thermodynamics to the apparent horizon, we
derive the corresponding modified Friedmann equations
and reinterpret the resulting corrections as an effective
dark energy component. We then solve the cosmologi-
cal equations numerically and study the viability of the
model across the parameter space.

III.
MASS-TO-HORIZON ENTROPY AND
MODIFIED COSMOLOGY
Within holographic frameworks applied to black hole
thermodynamics and cosmology, thermodynamic consis-
tency constitutes a fundamental requirement.
In this
context, thermodynamic consistency refers to the self-
consistent definition of horizon thermodynamic quanti-
ties, namely the entropy, temperature, and associated
mass (or energy), such that they satisfy the Clausius re-
lation. This condition ensures that the mass and energy
assigned to the horizon are thermodynamically equiva-
lent for the chosen entropy and temperature.
Recent investigations have demonstrated that gener-
alized horizon entropy formalisms, when combined with
the Cai–Kim temperature or Hawking temperature, gen-
erally fail to satisfy the Clausius relation [62, 63] unless
an appropriate mass–to–horizon relation is introduced
[64, 65]. The MHR provides the additional thermody-
namic structure required to restore equilibrium consis-
tency and establish a well-defined correspondence be-
tween horizon thermodynamics and gravitational dynam-
ics.

Cai–Kim temperature, thermodynamic consistency is
achieved through the linear MHR

The background evolution is obtained by numerically
solving the dimensionless Friedmann equation (3.9) si-
multaneously with Eq. (3.10). Results for the three phys-
ically motivated parameter configurations of the gen-
eralized mass-to-horizon entropy (3.3) are presented in
Figs. 1 and 2.
In each figure, the left panel displays
the energy densities εi(a) and the right panel the effec-
tive equation of state ωde(a). The fiducial cosmological
parameters are fixed to Ωm0 = 0.31, Ωr0 = 9 × 10−5,
and H0 = 73 km s−1 Mpc−1, with the cosmological con-
stant determined self-consistently from Eq. (3.12) for
each model.
The ΛCDM limit (m = γ = 1, β = 0)
is indicated by the solid blue curve throughout.
The
effective dark-energy density (3.13) decomposes into a
constant contribution εc0ΩΛ0 and a dynamical correc-
tion proportional to εc(a) = εc0E2(a). Depending on the
powers of E(a) and the coefficients A and B, this correc-
tion can grow substantially or become negative at early

impose Ωde < 0.045 at Big Bang nucleosynthesis and
matter–radiation equality [83, 85], alongside Ωde < 0.02
at a ≃0.02 from CMB constraints [86].

and study the cosmological dynamics as a function of α,
together with γ and m. The corresponding results are
presented in Fig. 2. Throughout this section we adopt
the upper sign in Eq. (3.10), for which the entanglement

C.
QG Corrections (α = 4)
We now examine the quantum-gravity–corrected sec-
tor introduced in Sec. III C, corresponding to α = 4
(equivalently, σ = m −1). The entropy expression (3.21)
includes a quantum-gravity (QG) correction whose sign
distinguishes the canonical (+) from the microcanonical
(−) branch. Since this correction is predominantly gov-
erned by the parameter β, we fix β = 0.1 and vary m and
γ. The resulting cosmological dynamics are displayed in
Fig. 2.
For m = γ = 1, the leading correction term 1 −
AE1−m vanishes identically, and Eq. (3.11) implies B ∝
(ℓpH0/c)2 ∼10−122. Accordingly, the QG contribution is
completely negligible, independent of the value of β and
of the choice of canonical or microcanonical branch. The
background evolution is thus exactly that of ΛCDM, as
corroborated by Fig. 2. In this regime, the effective dark-
energy density is strictly constant, εde = εc0ΩΛ0, and the
dark-energy equation-of-state parameter remains fixed at
ωde = −1 throughout cosmic history.
When either γ ̸= 1 or m ̸= 1, the cosmological evo-
lution continues to be dominated by the term AE1−m,
while the genuine QG contribution remains suppressed
by the Planck-scale factor 10−122. Consequently, the de-
viations from ΛCDM, illustrated by the dashed curves
in Fig. 2, are effectively indistinguishable from those
analyzed previously in Secs. IV A 2 and IV A 1.
In
particular, the parameter choices (1, 0.985, 0.1, 4) and
(1.001, 0.871, 0.1, 4) reproduce, respectively, the same
quintessence-like and marginally phantom behaviors
identified in those earlier sections.
Although the QG correction induces a formal struc-
ture analogous to that of the entanglement sector, its
phenomenological impact is negligible because observa-
tionally admissible values of m remain extremely close to
unity and the coefficient B is suppressed at the Planck
scale. As a result, neither the canonical nor the micro-
canonical branch gives rise to observable modifications of
the dark-energy sector. Within the present framework,
the cosmological dynamics are dictated almost entirely
by the MHR coupling parameter γ and its interplay with
the entropy exponent m, while the QG correction itself
remains observationally irrelevant.
V.
CONCLUSIONS
We have examined the cosmological consequences
of the generalized four-parameter mass-to-horizon en-
tropy (3.3) within the Cai–Kim thermodynamic frame-
work.
The corresponding modified Friedmann equa-
tions depend on the entropy exponent m, the MHR cou-
pling parameter γ, and the quantum-correction param-
eters β and α.
The combined effect of these parame-
ters can be recast as an effective dark-energy contribu-
tion. By solving the background equations for scale fac-
tors in the range a ∈[10−10, 1], we constrained the pa-
rameter space using bounds on the dark-energy density
parameter Ωde derived from Big Bang Nucleosynthesis,
matter–radiation equality, and Cosmic Microwave Back-
ground observations.
In the case of the standard area-law extension (β = 0),
the cosmological dynamics exhibit a pronounced sensitiv-
ity to deviations from the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy.
For fixed γ = 1, the entropy exponent is confined to a
narrow interval around unity (0.99986 ≤m ≤1), be-
yond which even small departures lead to either nega-
tive or excessively large effective dark-energy densities,
thereby disrupting the standard radiation–matter–dark-
energy sequence.
For the γ-rescaled Bekenstein–Hawking entropy (m =
1), observational consistency is achieved only in the
sub-scaled regime (0.981 ≤γ ≤1), within which the
effective dark-energy sector is positive and displays a
quintessence-like behavior. The super-scaled regime (γ >
1) produces negative dark-energy densities and is conse-
quently ruled out.
Entanglement-induced corrections (β = 1, 2 < α <
m + 2) generate an early-time dark-energy contribution
that naturally decays toward late times.
Values of α
near the lower bound give rise to an excessively large
early dark-energy fraction and are excluded, whereas
values close to the upper bound effectively reproduce
ΛCDM. Admissible solutions therefore require only mod-
erate entanglement corrections and remain close to a
cosmological-constant behavior at the present epoch. By
contrast, the quantum-gravity corrections controlled by
β are suppressed by the Planck-scale factor (ℓpH0/c)3−m
and are entirely negligible on cosmological scales, irre-
spective of the underlying statistical origin.
Overall, our analysis confines the entropy exponent
and MHR coupling parameter to a narrow region
around the standard area-law values.
Non-extensive
Tsallis–Cirto and Barrow-type generalizations are thus
tightly constrained, with the viable parameter space re-
stricted to values close to the Bekenstein–Hawking en-
tropy (δ ≃1, ∆≃0).
For all viable models, the
present-day dark-energy equation-of-state parameter re-
mains close to ωde = −1, and the deceleration parameter
is nearly indistinguishable from its ΛCDM counterpart.
These conclusions are based exclusively on background
evolution and early-universe constraints. A comprehen-
sive statistical analysis incorporating cosmological data
to obtain quantitative parameter bounds will be pre-
sented in a forthcoming companion work.
