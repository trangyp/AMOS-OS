Modal logics of conjunctively closed provability
predicates
Haruka Kogure∗†and Taishi Kurahashi‡§
Abstract
We investigate non-normal modal logics corresponding to provability
predicates PrT (x) satisfying the derivability condition C: T ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝)∧
PrT (⌜ψ⌝) →PrT (⌜φ ∧ψ⌝). The modal counterpart of this condition is
the axiom scheme C: □A∧□B →□(A∧B). First, we introduce a new se-
mantics based on closure operators for non-normal modal logics including
logics adopting C as an axiom scheme. We prove modal completeness for
several non-normal modal logics studied in this paper with respect to this
semantics. Second, we prove the arithmetical completeness theorems for
the logics CN, CNP, CN4, CNP4, and CND by using our new semantics.

Introduction
Let T be a primitive recursively axiomatized consistent LA-theory extending
Peano Arithmetic PA, where LA is the language of first-order arithmetic. In
the usual proof of G¨odel’s incompleteness theorems, provability predicates of a
theory T play important roles. The theorem states that if a provability predicate
PrT (x) of T satisfies the following Hilbert–Bernays–L¨ob derivability conditions
D2 and D3, then T cannot prove the consistency statement ConT :≡¬PrT (⌜0 =
1⌝) of T.
• D2: T ⊢PrT (⌜φ →ψ⌝) →(PrT (⌜φ⌝) →PrT (⌜ψ⌝)).
• D3: T ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝) →PrT (⌜PrT (⌜φ⌝)⌝).
These conditions naturally correspond to the following modal principles K and
4 respectively by interpreting the modal operator □as PrT (x).
• K: □(A →B) →(□A →□B).
• 4: □A →□□A.

For each provability predicate PrT (x) of T, let PL(PrT ) be the set of all T-
verifiable modal principles of PrT (x). Solovay’s arithmetical completeness the-
orem [15] states that if T is Σ1-sound, then for the canonical Σ1 provability
predicate ProvT (x) of T, PL(ProvT ) is exactly the G¨odel–L¨ob modal logic GL.
On the other hand, not all provability predicates satisfy the full Hilbert–
Bernays–L¨ob derivability conditions. For instance, it is known that the sec-
ond incompleteness theorem does not hold for Rosser provability predicates.
Hence every Rosser provability predicate does not satisfy at least one of D2
and D3. Later, Mostowski [13] introduced a particularly simple example of a
Σ1 provability predicate for which the second incompleteness theorem fails. Let
PrM
T (x) :≡PrT (x) ∧x ̸= ⌜0 = 1⌝. Then T proves the corresponding consistency
statement ¬PrM
T (⌜0 = 1⌝). On the other hand, PrM
T (x) still satisfies the deriv-
ability condition D3, and hence it cannot satisfy D2. Then, the corresponding
provability logic PL(PrM
T ) is a non-normal modal logic.
The pure logic of necessitation N, which was introduced by Fitting, Marek,
and Truszczy´nski [3], is obtained from classical propositional logic by adding
only the necessitation rule
A
□A in the language of modal propositional logic.
The rule corresponds to the derivability condition D1 : T ⊢φ ⇒T ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝),
which is common to all provability predicates. In fact, the second author proved
in [11] that N is exactly the provability logic of all provability predicates. More-
over, the arithmetical completeness of several extensions of N obtained by adding
modal principles corresponding to derivability conditions and consistency prin-
ciples has been established in our previous works [7, 6, 9, 8, 11]. An overview
of this line of research is given in [8].
The purpose of the present paper is to continue the research by studying
provability predicates satisfying the derivability condition C: T ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝) ∧
PrT (⌜ψ⌝) →PrT (⌜φ∧ψ⌝). Its modal counterpart is the axiom scheme C: □A∧
□B →□(A∧B). The significance of the condition C in the context of the second
incompleteness theorem was investigated in [12]. In particular, the combination
of C with D3 yields a version of the second incompleteness theorem on the
unprovability of the schematic consistency statement ConS
T := {¬(PrT (⌜φ⌝) ∧
PrT (⌜¬φ⌝)) | φ is an LA-sentence}. Mostowski’s predicate PrM
T (x) is an example
satisfying both C and D3. Hence, it satisfies T ⊢¬PrM
T (⌜0 = 1⌝), but T ⊬
¬(PrM
T (⌜φ⌝) ∧PrM
T (⌜¬φ⌝)) for some LA-sentence φ.
Thus, while the modal
principles P : ¬□⊥and D : ¬(□A ∧□¬A) are equivalent in normal modal
logics, they are not equivalent in general in the non-normal setting. Indeed,
by the above mentioned version of the second incompleteness theorem, there
is no provability predicate PrT (x) such that PL(PrT ) contains CND4, whereas,
PL(PrM
T ) contains CNP4.
One obstacle to studying the principle C over the logic N is that the relational
semantics introduced by Fitting, Marek, and Truszczy´nski does not seem to
handle C satisfactorily. Therefore, in the present paper, we introduce a simple
new semantics suitable for C. Our semantics consists of points, but it does not
employ an accessibility relation. Instead, each point x is assigned a set G(x) of
modal formulas, and a fixed closure operator cl determines the set cl(G(x)) of

modal formulas. The formula □A is true at x exactly when A ∈cl(G(x)). This
separation between the generator G(x) and its closure cl(G(x)) is useful for finite
countermodel constructions. Even when the set of points and all generators
G(x) are finite, the set cl(G(x)) may be infinite. The modal principles are then
reflected by conditions on the closure cl(G(x)). For example, C is true at x if
and only if cl(G(x)) is closed under taking conjunction.
In Section 3, we prove completeness theorems with respect to our new seman-
tics for non-normal modal logics having combinations of the modal principles N,
C, P, D, and 4. For logics in which N and D are not both contained, we actually
prove the finite model property. For logics containing both N and D, we prove
completeness by a canonical model construction. However, the countermodels
obtained in this way are not primitive recursively presented. For arithmetical
completeness, in order to embed countermodels into arithmetic, we need a prim-
itive recursive presentation of the countermodels. We therefore prove separately
that the set of all theorems of CND is primitive recursive, and we then prove that
if CND ⊬A, then we can find a primitive recursively presented countermodel
for A.
In Section 4, we then turn to arithmetical completeness. We prove the arith-
metical completeness theorems for CN, CNP, CN4, CNP4, and CND based on
our new semantics. The proof method is based on the Solovay-style construc-
tions for non-normal provability logics developed in our previous studies (see
[7, 6, 9, 8, 11]).

Preliminaries
In this section, we introduce basic notions and notation used throughout the
paper.
2.1
Provability predicates and derivability conditions
Throughout the present paper, T denotes a primitive recursively axiomatized
consistent extension of Peano Arithmetic PA in the language LA of first-order
arithmetic (cf. H´ajek and Pudl´ak [4]). Let ω denote the set of all natural num-
bers. For each n ∈ω, the numeral for n is denoted by n. We fix a standard
G¨odel numbering such that if α is a proper sub-expression of a finite sequence
β of LA-symbols, then the G¨odel number of α is less than that of β. For each
LA-formula φ, let ⌜φ⌝denote the numeral for the G¨odel number of φ.
A Σ1 formula PrT (x) is called a provability predicate of T if for every LA-
sentence φ, T ⊢φ if and only if PA ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝).
Definition 2.1. Let PrT (x) be a provability predicate of T.
1. PrT (x) is said to satisfy D2 if T ⊢PrT (⌜φ →ψ⌝) →(PrT (⌜φ⌝) →
PrT (⌜ψ⌝)) for any LA-sentences φ and ψ.
2. PrT (x) is said to satisfy D3 if PA ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝) →PrT (⌜PrT (⌜φ⌝)⌝) for
any LA-sentence φ.

3. PrT (x) is said to satisfy C if PA ⊢PrT (⌜φ⌝) ∧PrT (⌜ψ⌝) →PrT (⌜φ ∧ψ⌝)
for any LA-sentences φ and ψ.
It is easily shown that if PrT (x) satisfies D2, then it also satisfies C.

2.2
Modal logics
The language of modal propositional logic consists of countably many proposi-
tional variables p0, p1, . . ., the logical constant ⊥, the logical connectives ¬, ∧,
∨, →, and the modal operator □. The symbols ⊤and ↔are introduced as
abbreviations in the usual way. Let MF be the set of all modal formulas. For a
modal formula A, let Sub(A) be the set of all subformulas of A.
A modal logic is a set L ⊆MF containing all classical tautologies and closed
under Modus Ponens and uniform substitution. The pure logic of necessitation
N is the smallest modal logic closed under Nec:
A
□A.
Let C and 4 denote the axiom schemata □A ∧□B →□(A ∧B) and □A →
□□A, respectively. These axiom schemata correspond to the derivability condi-
tions C and D3, respectively. Let P be the axiom ¬□⊥, and let D be the axiom
scheme ¬(□A ∧□¬A). These modal principles correspond to the consistency
statements ConL
T and ConS
T , respectively. It is easily shown that D is stronger
than P over the logic N.
For Λ ⊆{N, C, P, D, 4}, let LΛ be the smallest modal logic satisfying the
following requirements: it is closed under Nec if N ∈Λ, and it contains each
of the axiom schemata among C, P, D, and 4 if their names belong to Λ. In
particular, L{N} = N. Although we defined a wider class of non-normal modal
logics, we mainly study the following logics:
CN = L{N,C},
CNP = L{N,C,P},
CND = L{N,C,D},
CN4 = L{N,C,4},
CNP4 = L{N,C,P,4},
CND4 = L{N,C,D,4}.

2.3
Arithmetical interpretations and provability logics
Let PrT (x) be a provability predicate of T. A mapping f from modal formulas
to LA-sentences is called an arithmetical interpretation based on PrT (x) if it
satisfies the following conditions:
1. f(⊥) is 0 = 1,
2. f(A ◦B) is f(A) ◦f(B) for ◦∈{∧, ∨, →},
3. f(¬A) is ¬f(A),
4. f(□A) is PrT (⌜f(A)⌝).
Definition 2.4. The provability logic PL(PrT ) of PrT (x) is the set of all modal
formulas A such that T ⊢f(A) for all arithmetical interpretations f based on
PrT (x).
It is obvious that N ⊆PL(PrT ) for any provability predicate PrT (x).
Proposition 2.5. Let PrT (x) be a provability predicate of T.
1. PrT (x) satisfies C if and only if CN = L{N,C} ⊆PL(PrT ).
2. PrT (x) satisfies D3 if and only if L{N,4} ⊆PL(PrT ).
3. T ⊢ConL
T if and only if L{N,P} ⊆PL(PrT ).
4. T ⊢ConS
T if and only if L{N,D} ⊆PL(PrT ).
We have CNP4 ⊆PL(PrM
T ) for Mostowski’s provability predicate PrM
T (x).
From Fact 2.3.2, CND4 is not arithmetically sound, that is, there is no Σ1
provability predicate PrT (x) such that CND4 ⊆PL(PrT ). The main purpose
of Section 4 is to prove the arithmetical completeness theorems for CN, CNP,
CN4, CNP4, and CND. That is, for each logic L among them, there exists a Σ1
provability predicate PrT (x) of T such that PL(PrT ) = L.
For textbooks on provability logic, we refer to Boolos [2] and Smory´nski
[14]. Comprehensive surveys on provability logic are given by Japaridze and de
Jongh [5] and Artemov and Beklemishev [1].
3
A new semantics
In this section, we introduce a new semantics for non-normal modal logics. In
ordinary Kripke semantics, the truth of □A at a world is defined in terms of an
accessibility relation. In our semantics, each point x is assigned a set G(x) of
formulas and a closure operator cl is fixed on sets of formulas. A formula □A
5


## Page 6


is true at x exactly when A ∈cl(G(x)). The distinction between the generator
G(x) and its closure cl(G(x)) is important below, since G(x) may be finite even
if cl(G(x)) is infinite.
Definition 3.1. A closure operator is a mapping cl : P(MF) →P(MF) satisfy-
ing the following conditions for all X, Y ⊆MF:
1. X ⊆cl(X),
2. If X ⊆Y , then cl(X) ⊆cl(Y ),
3. cl(cl(X)) = cl(X).
Definition 3.2. A closure frame is a triple F = (W, G, cl) where W is a non-
empty set, G : W →P(MF), and cl is a closure operator.
A closure frame is said to be finite if W is finite and G(x) is finite for every
x ∈W.
Definition 3.3. A closure model is a quadruple M = (W, G, cl, ⊩) where F =
(W, G, cl) is a closure frame and ⊩is a satisfaction relation between W and MF
satisfying the following clauses:
1. x ⊮⊥.
2. x ⊩¬A ⇐⇒x ⊮A.
3. x ⊩A ∧B ⇐⇒(x ⊩A and x ⊩B).
4. x ⊩A ∨B ⇐⇒(x ⊩A or x ⊩B).
5. x ⊩A →B ⇐⇒(x ⊮A or x ⊩B).
6. x ⊩□A ⇐⇒A ∈cl(G(x)).
A modal formula A is said to be valid in a closure model M (in symbols,
M |= A) if x ⊩A for all x ∈W. Let Th(M) := {A ∈MF | M |= A}.
In the proofs of modal completeness below, countermodels are constructed
in different ways depending on Λ ⊆{N, C, P, D, 4}. Some of these countermodels
are finite. Even if a closure frame is finite, the set cl(G(x)) may be infinite. For
the proofs of arithmetical completeness in Section 4, we need an effective presen-
tation of the relation B ∈cl(G(x)) so that arithmetical formulas representing
the model can be obtained.
We therefore use the following notion. A closure model M = (W, G, cl, ⊩)
is said to be primitive recursively presented if the relations x ∈W, x ⊩p for
propositional variables p, and B ∈cl(G(x)) are primitive recursive. Then the
full satisfaction relation x ⊩B is also primitive recursive, by recursion on the
construction of B.
Definition 3.4. Let M = (W, G, cl, ⊩) be a closure model. We say that M
satisfies:

Proposition 3.6. Let Λ ⊆{N, C, P, D, 4}. If a closure model M satisfies (X)
for all X ∈Λ, then LΛ ⊆Th(M).
In the following subsections, we prove completeness results with respect to
our new semantics. We first study logics in which N and D are not both con-
tained. In this case, we develop methods for constructing primitive recursively
presented finite countermodels. We then study logics containing both N and
D. We prove the completeness theorems for these logics by using a canonical
model construction with a different closure operator. The models obtained in
this way are not primitive recursively presented. Therefore, for the arithmetical
completeness theorem for CND, we separately prove that CND has a primitive
recursively presented countermodel.

Concluding remarks
In this paper, we investigated modal logics corresponding to provability pred-
icates satisfying the derivability condition C. We introduced a new semantics
in which □A is interpreted by membership of A in a set of formulas associated
with each point. Closure under conjunction in these sets corresponds to the
modal axiom C.
We proved completeness results with respect to our new semantics for the
logics determined by combinations of the principles N, C, P, D, and 4. If N and
27


## Page 28


D are not both contained, then our construction yields primitive recursively
presented finite countermodels. For logics containing both N and D, we proved
completeness by using a canonical model construction. In the case of CND, we
further obtained primitive recursively presented countermodels.
The semantics introduced in this paper seems to be a useful tool for analyzing
other weak non-normal logics not discussed in this paper.
It would also be
natural to extend the decidability analysis developed in Subsection 3.3 for CND
to other logics, such as CND4.
We also proved the arithmetical completeness theorems for CN, CNP, CN4,
CNP4, and CND.
The problem of proving the arithmetical completeness of
these logics was suggested in our overview presented in [8]. The present paper
solves it by introducing the new semantics and by developing the construc-
tion of primitive recursively presented countermodels. In particular, the arith-
metical completeness of these logics shows that the corresponding combinations
of derivability conditions do not produce additional modal principles through
arithmetical principles such as the Fixed Point Theorem.
It remains to clarify a significance of these results from the viewpoint of the
second incompleteness theorem. By contrast with the arithmetical completeness
results above, CND4 is not arithmetically sound. Thus it would be interesting
to investigate arithmetically complete extensions of CNP4. One example in this
direction is given by Mostowski’s provability predicate PrM
T (x). Its provability
logic PL(PrM
T ) is studied in a forthcoming paper by the second author.
