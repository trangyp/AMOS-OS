Multiband topological group-velocity control from slow light to light stopping
Junhao Yang (杨俊豪),1 Jiarui Wang (王佳瑞),1 Jingyu Liu (刘
靖宇),2 Shirong Lin (林仕容),2, 3, ∗and Xinyuan Qi (齐新元)1, †

We introduce next-nearest-neighbor (NNN) couplings into a Harper–Hofstadter photonic lattice
to establish a long-range topological photonic platform for group-velocity engineering. We show that
the NNN couplings not only open a previously closed band gap but also flatten the dispersion of
the edge states, thereby offering a potential route toward topological slow-light control. Theoretical
calculations reveal that the band gaps support band-gap Chern numbers with opposite signs. Propa-
gation simulations demonstrate robust, topologically protected slow-light transport of counter-chiral
edge states, while the presence of slow-light edge states in all three topological band gaps enables
broadband topological slow light. By further tuning the NNN coupling parameter, multiple topo-
logical light-stopping states can be realized. These results establish long-range NNN coupling as an
effective mechanism for topological group-velocity engineering and provide new design principles for
topological slow-light devices, optical delay lines, and integrated multiband photonic systems.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The group velocity, defined as the propagation speed of a wave-packet envelope, is a fundamental physical quantity
governing the dynamics of light pulses [1, 2]. Precise control of the group velocity is central to numerous applications
in modern photonics, including optical information processing, signal buffering, delay lines, and enhanced light–matter
interactions for quantum technologies [3–5]. Consequently, considerable efforts have been devoted to manipulating
the group velocity of light in a variety of platforms, including atomic media [6], engineered photonic structures [7],
and optomechanical systems [8]. Phenomena such as electromagnetically induced transparency [9, 10] and coherent
population oscillations [11, 12] have enabled dramatic reductions in group velocity in atomic media, even approaching
zero group velocity [6].
However, these approaches are often limited by stringent experimental requirements [9],
narrow operational bandwidths [6], poor compatibility with integrated photonic circuits [13], and limited dynamic
tunability [14, 15]. Such limitations hinder their practical implementation in robust, scalable, and reconfigurable
integrated photonic systems [16].
Artificial photonic microstructures, particularly periodic photonic structures, provide a powerful platform for disper-
sion engineering through structural design [7, 17]. By tailoring coupling strengths [18, 19], lattice geometries [20], and
spatial symmetries [21], the photonic band structure—and consequently the group velocity of supported modes—can
be precisely engineered. These capabilities have enabled the realization of slow-light waveguides [22], stopped-light
cavities [23], and tunable delay lines on chip-scale platforms [24]. However, transport in conventional photonic lat-
tices typically relies on bulk states or topologically trivial edge states [22, 25], which remain highly susceptible to
backscattering induced by disorder, fabrication imperfections, and sharp bends [26]. This lack of robustness poses
a major obstacle to reliable device operation in practical, non-ideal environments. Recently, topological photonics
has emerged as a paradigm-shifting framework for realizing chiral edge states protected by bulk topological invari-
ants through broken time-reversal symmetry [27, 28]. Such states support robust unidirectional boundary transport
that is highly resistant to disorder and structural imperfections [29–33]. Recent studies have begun to investigate
group-velocity engineering within these topological edge channels [34–40], for example through dispersion engineering
in Floquet topological insulators [36] and valley photonic crystals [38, 40]. However, existing studies have primarily
focused on group-velocity tuning of a single topological edge state [41–43]. Group-velocity engineering of multiple
coexisting edge states, particularly the simultaneous realization of slow-light transport in edge states with opposite
chiralities, remains largely unexplored and constitutes a significant open challenge in topological photonics.
As a paradigmatic model in topological physics, the Harper–Hofstadter (HH) model provides an ideal platform for
investigating the transport dynamics of multiple topological edge states associated with multiple band gaps [31, 44, 45].
In photonic implementations, the effective magnetic flux gives rise to multiple topological band gaps, each supporting
topologically protected chiral edge states, thereby offering a natural platform for studying multiband topological light

magnetic fields and the characterization of topological properties [50–53], the development of topological lasers [54, 55],
and the exploration of exotic phases in non-Hermitian regimes [56, 57]. By contrast, the dispersion engineering of

A major limitation originates from the conventional HH model, which incorporates only nearest-neighbor (NN) cou-
plings, severely restricting the tunability of the band structure and edge-state dispersion. This limitation motivates
the exploration of extended coupling mechanisms. Recently, long-range next-nearest-neighbor (NNN) couplings have
been introduced into HH lattices, enabling additional topologically protected edge channels and robust interface
states between regions with different Chern numbers [60]. These advances provide new opportunities for topological
band-structure engineering and group-velocity control of topological light transport.
In this work, we introduce long-range reciprocal NNN couplings [61–67] into a Harper–Hofstadter (HH) photonic
lattice, establishing a long-range topological photonic platform for group-velocity engineering. We demonstrate that
the vertical long-range coupling opens a previously closed central topological band gap, while the horizontal long-range
coupling flattens the edge-state dispersion, providing an effective route for tuning topological slow-light dynamics. By
calculating the band-gap Chern numbers, we reveal the coexistence of positive and negative topological invariants,
corresponding to edge states with opposite propagation chiralities. Under fully open boundary conditions, the system
supports edge-state transport in the first and third band gaps, while edge transport in the second band gap can be
realized within the ribbon approximation. Light-propagation simulations further demonstrate that these slow-light
edge states preserve unidirectional transport and robustly bypass defects, thereby realizing topologically protected
counter-chiral slow-light transport. Since slow-light edge states exist in all three topological band gaps, the proposed
system enables broadband counter-chiral topological slow light. Furthermore, by tuning the horizontal long-range
coupling, the edge-state group velocity can be continuously reduced to zero, resulting in a topological light-stopping
effect confirmed by dynamical simulations. These results establish long-range NNN couplings as an effective mechanism
for topological group-velocity engineering, enabling broadband counter-chiral topological slow light and topological
light stopping, and providing new design principles for robust slow-light devices, topological delay lines, and integrated
multiband photonic platforms.

VI.
ANALYSIS OF FEASIBILITY OPTIONS
In femtosecond-laser-written photonic waveguide arrays, periodic modulation of the propagation trajectories—such
as helical bending or segmented transverse displacement—provides a natural route to Floquet engineering of HH
lattices [36, 71, 72]. The longitudinal modulation introduces an effective synthetic gauge field along the propagation
direction, leading to complex Peierls phases in the NN couplings and realizing a time-averaged tight-binding Hamil-
tonian equivalent to the HH model. Building on this Floquet NN platform, the introduction of detuned auxiliary
waveguides in a three-dimensional photonic architecture provides an effective route to engineer long-range couplings
via virtual tunneling processes. In this scheme, the auxiliary channels act as intermediate states with controllable
propagation-constant detuning relative to the main lattice [73], mediating second-order hopping between non-adjacent
sites. This mechanism has been extensively explored in photonic waveguide systems for effective Hamiltonian engi-
neering based on auxiliary-state-mediated coupling. In the large-detuning regime, the intermediate modes can be
adiabatically eliminated [74], leading to an effective second-order coupling between the outer sites obtained from
perturbation theory.
The resulting hopping amplitude scales as the product of the two evanescent couplings di-
vided by the detuning, thereby generating effective NNN and longer-range tunneling terms under multi-site auxiliary
configurations. When generalized to both transverse and longitudinal directions, this mechanism yields anisotropic
long-range couplings within a Floquet-averaged framework. Importantly, the Floquet modulation and the auxiliary-
state-mediated coupling act on different length and energy scales, allowing their effects to be incorporated indepen-
dently within the effective Hamiltonian. The resulting model incorporates both Floquet-induced complex NN hopping
and bridge-mediated long-range tunneling, providing a unified and fully programmable tight-binding description of
the system.
VII.
CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we have introduced long-range NNN couplings into a HH photonic lattice, establishing a long-range
topological photonic platform for group-velocity engineering. We have demonstrated that the long-range coupling
η opens a previously closed central topological band gap, while the coupling δ reshapes and flattens the edge-state
dispersion, providing an effective mechanism for controlling topological slow-light dynamics. By calculating the band-
gap Chern numbers, we reveal the coexistence of positive and negative topological invariants, corresponding to edge
states with opposite propagation chiralities. Under fully open boundary conditions, the system supports edge-mode
transport only in the first and third band gaps, whereas edge transport in the second band gap can be realized within
the ribbon approximation. Light-propagation simulations further show that these counter-chiral slow-light edge states
maintain robust unidirectional transport and efficiently bypass defects under topological protection. Since slow-light
edge states are supported in all three topological band gaps, the proposed lattice enables broadband counter-chiral
topological slow light. Furthermore, the additional degree of freedom provided by the long-range couplings allows
continuous tuning of the edge-state group velocity. By varying the coupling parameter δ, the group velocity can be
continuously reduced to zero, leading to a topological light-stopping effect verified by dynamical simulations. Moreover,
the proposed lattice can be experimentally implemented in femtosecond-laser-written waveguide arrays through spatial
modulation of waveguide trajectories, providing a feasible platform for observing the predicted topological slow-light

and topological light stopping, with potential applications in robust slow-light devices, topological delay lines, optical
buffering, and integrated multiband photonic systems.
