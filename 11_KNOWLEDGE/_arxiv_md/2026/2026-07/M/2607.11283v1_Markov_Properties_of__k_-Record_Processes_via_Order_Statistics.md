Abstract
The theory of k-record values (Type 2 k-records) plays an important role in
the study of partial extremes and in statistical inference based on record data. A
common approach in the literature reduces the analysis of k-records associated with
a distribution function F to that of ordinary record values from the transformed
distribution F1:k(x) = 1 −(1 −F(x))k.
This representation is widely used in
deriving distributional results and inferential procedures, often without an explicit
construction of the underlying stochastic mechanism, and relies on a structural
property of order statistics that, although classical, is typically invoked without
proof in the record literature.
In this paper we provide a direct derivation of the probabilistic structure of k-
record processes based on the sequence of running order statistics Un = Xn−k+1:n,
the k-th largest among the first n observations. We show that this process forms a
Markov chain with respect to its natural filtration, with an explicit transition ker-
nel. The key step is a conditional-independence property of upper order statistics,
which we isolate and prove in detail: conditionally on Un, the k −1 observations
exceeding Un are distributed as order statistics from the distribution truncated at
Un, independently of the whole trajectory of the running order-statistic process.
Under the continuity assumption on F, the usual Type 2 k-record times coincide
almost surely with the record times of (Un)n≥k.
This yields a transparent construction of the k-record process as the record
process of a Markov chain.
Classical distributional results, including the repre-
sentation through F1:k and the joint density of the first m k-record values, are
recovered in a unified framework. As additional results, we work out the expo-
nential case, in which the k-record values form a random walk with independent
exponential increments and Gamma-distributed marginals, and we record a corre-
sponding characterisation of the exponential distribution.
Keywords:
records; k-records; order statistics; Markov chains; record
times; exponential distribution.

Introduction
Record values constitute a classical topic in probability theory, going back
to early work of Chandler, 1952 [3] and R´enyi, 1962 [9]; see also Nevzorov,
2001 [8] for a comprehensive account.
The subject has a wide range of
applications in statistics, reliability theory and extreme value analysis. A
standard book-length reference is the monograph of Arnold, Balakrishnan
and Nagaraja [2].
More recently, increasing attention has been devoted
to generalisations of ordinary records, among which the so-called k-record
values, or Type 2 k-records, introduced by Dziubdziela and Kopoci´nski [5],
play a prominent role; see, for example, Hofmann and Balakrishnan, 2004
[6] and Ahmadi and Doostparast, 2008 [1].
Given a sequence of independent and identically distributed random vari-
ables, k-record values arise naturally when one considers the evolution of
the k-th largest observation among the first n observations. These gener-
alised records retain a number of the attractive features of ordinary records,
whilst providing a richer structure that is better suited for statistical infer-
ence based on partial extreme information. In particular, k-records have
been used in inferential problems involving likelihood methods, Bayesian
procedures and information measures; see [6, 1, 10].
A common approach in the literature consists in reducing the study of
k-record values associated with a distribution function F to the study of
ordinary record values from the transformed distribution
F1:k(x) = 1 −(1 −F(x))k.
This representation is widely used as a starting point for the derivation of
joint distributions and likelihood functions. For example, Arnold et al. [2,
p. 43] introduce Type 2 k-records in this framework, while later papers such
as Hofmann and Balakrishnan [6] and Ahmadi and Doostparast [1] use the
corresponding distributional formulas in subsequent developments. Related
ordinary-record arguments are also invoked in inferential work such as Wang
and Ye [10].
While this reduction is standard, it implicitly relies on a structural
property of order statistics that is often used without explicit justification.
Namely, one needs that, conditionally on the k-th largest observation, the
remaining upper order statistics are distributed as order statistics from the

truncated distribution above that value. Although a fixed-sample version
of this fact is classical in the theory of order statistics (see, e.g., Arnold et
al. [2, pp. 18–19] or David and Nagaraja [4, Sec. 2.4–2.5]), what the record-
setting framework actually requires is a stronger, dynamic form of it: the
conditional law of the upper order statistics must be insensitive to the whole
past trajectory of the running order-statistic process, and must remain valid
at suitable random times. To the best of our knowledge this stronger form
is not explicitly formulated and proved in the literature on k-records, where
it is typically invoked implicitly. One of the contributions of the present pa-
per is to isolate this property in the precise conditional-independence form
in which it is needed (Lemma 3.7 below) and to provide a complete proof
adapted to the record-setting framework.
The purpose of the present paper is thus to provide a direct and rigorous
derivation of the probabilistic structure underlying the k-record process.
Our approach is based on the analysis of the sequence of running order
statistics
Un = Xn−k+1:n,
n ≥k,
that is, the k-th largest observation among the first n observations.
We

natural filtration, with an explicit transition mechanism. We then compare,
in Section 4, two natural constructions of record times: the usual Type 2
k-record times and the ordinary record times associated with the process
(Un)n≥k. Under the continuity assumption on F, we prove that these two
constructions coincide almost surely.
This point of view yields a transparent construction of the k-record pro-
cess and provides a natural framework in which classical results may be
recovered as consequences. In particular, in Section 5 we identify the k-
record values with the record values of the Markov chain (Un) and derive
the transition kernel of the k-record chain, and in Section 6 we recover the
standard representation through F1:k together with the usual joint density
formulae for the first m k-records. Section 7 contains additional results: it
works out the exponential case, in which the k-record values form a random
walk with independent exponential increments, and records a corresponding
characterisation of the exponential law. Section 8 concludes.

Concluding remarks
In this paper we have provided a direct probabilistic construction of the
Type 2 k-record process based on the sequence of running order statis-
tics Un = Xn−k+1:n. We have shown that (Un)n≥k is a time-homogeneous

## Page 33

Markov chain with respect to its natural filtration, with an explicit tran-
sition kernel, and that, under the continuity assumption on F, the usual
k-record times coincide almost surely with the record times of this process.
The technical core of the paper is Lemma 3.7, which isolates, in the
conditional-independence form actually required by the record-setting frame-
work, a structural property of order statistics that is classical in its fixed-
sample formulation but is typically invoked without proof in the record
literature: conditionally on the running k-th maximum, the values above
it are distributed as order statistics from the truncated distribution, inde-
pendently of the whole past trajectory of the process. Remark 3.9 shows
in what sense this statement is sharp: the Markov property holds up to
the configuration filtration (Dn), but fails for the full data filtration (Fn) as
soon as k ≥2.
This identification yields a transparent description of the k-record values
as the record values of a Markov chain. As a consequence, the transition
kernel of the k-record process is obtained directly — with the conditioning
taken on the whole record past — and classical distributional results, in-
cluding the representation in terms of the transformed distribution F1:k and
the joint density of the first m k-record values, follow naturally from the un-
derlying stochastic structure. The regeneration mechanism of Remark 5.5,
whereby each new k-record value arises as the minimum of k independent
draws from the truncated distribution, explains the ubiquity of F1:k in this
theory. The additional results of Section 7 illustrate the framework in the
exponential case, where the k-record sequence becomes a random walk with
independent exponential increments, and record a converse characterisation.
Several directions for further investigation may be considered. It would
be of interest to extend the present analysis to settings where the continuity
assumption on F is relaxed; Remark 4.5 shows that for k ≥2 the two
natural constructions of record times then genuinely part ways, so that new
phenomena are to be expected. Extensions to other types of generalised
records form another natural direction.
Finally, the study of statistical
procedures based on k-records within the Markovian framework developed
here, with particular emphasis on the role of the transition kernel in inference
problems, appears promising.
