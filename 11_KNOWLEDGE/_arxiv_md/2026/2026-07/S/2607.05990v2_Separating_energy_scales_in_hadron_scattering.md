Separating energy scales in hadron scattering
S.M. Troshin, N.E. Tyurin
NRC “Kurchatov Institute”–IHEP
Protvino, 142281, Russian Federation,
Sergey.Troshin@ihep.ru
Abstract
We discuss energy scales in soft hadron scattering and possibility of their
separation: the one relevant to the region where the total cross–sections in-
crease begins and another one related to the asymptotic region. The latter
scale is most sensitive to the unitarity effects and former one — to the gross
features of absorption of the initial wave due to multiparticle production.
Transition from the shadow to reflective scattering sets a relation between
the two energy scales.

Introduction
It is a notorious statement that the study of soft hadron interactions in the ab-
sence of reliable theoretical approaches have to address to the general principles
such as unitarity and analyticity to provide a consistent description. The existing
models should take these principles into account but cannot pretend to be unique
theoretical schemes even being the QCD-inspired ones and despite of their of-
ten widespread use. Approach to the asymptotic regime depends on a particular
model and thus there is an energy scale where the asymptotics can become be
effective. Solution of this case in a general model–independent way is not trivial,
discussion of this problem in the model framework can be found in [1]
Currently, there are the experimental indications on transition to the reflective
scattering mode [2] with the energy increase. The energy value where this tran-
sition starts can be hopefully used as a reference point for determination of the
asymptotic region with the unitarity effects domination and the region of lower en-
ergies where hadron interaction dynamics responsible for the total cross–section
growth is activated. Indeed, this energy range determines a noticeable and impor-
tant scale in hadron interactions. Namely, the measurements at the LHC energy of
√s = 13 TeV have indicated [3] that the hadron interaction region responsible for
the inelastic processes is transforming from a black disk to a black ring. Transition
to such a picture has been discussed in [2] and references therein. The black ring
picture naturally emerges from the reflective scattering mode (antishadowing) [4]
being a higher energy consequence of that mode.

The unitarity effects with self-damping of inelastic channels as well as the collec-
tive effects are significant at very high energies. The elastic scattering amplitude
at the energies √s ≪√sr, say at √s < 500 GeV, has the shadow nature, i.e.
its magnitude reflects straightforwardly an increasing role of the newly opened
inelastic channels contributions1.
The model with independent emission of the secondary particles used for cal-
culation of the inelastic overlap function and subsequent elastic amplitude extrac-
tion under the shadow scattering mode [9] can be applied for illustration of this
point. The shadowing in the model could be be interpreted as a consequence of
absence of interactions under production of secondaries or their clusters in the
limited ranges of the longitudinal momenta (“uncorrelated jets”).
The respective transition between the two modes becomes particularly trans-
parent when one represents the scattering amplitude (pure imaginary) in the im-

U–matrix approach
Under model construction of the amplitude, partial restoration of the unitarity
occurs often only. The examples are adoption of a black disc picture or the use of
eikonal approximation.
The unitarity can be naturally and fully accounted in the form of the general-
ized reaction matrix approach representing the elastic scattering amplitude by the
equation:
F = U + iUDF
(13)
expressed here in the operator form [13] with the U being an input under model
construction. Eq. (13) is the relativistic generalization of the basic equation of
the quantum theory of radiation damping [14], which implies depletion of the
transient state with the energy increase and provides solution of the unitarity for
the amplitude in the rational form in impact parameter representation:
f(s, b) = u(s, b)/[1 −iu(s, b)],

We would like to make a comment here on the eikonal approximation for the
scattering amplitude4. It implies an existence of shadow scattering mode (weak
coupling regime) only. As it is known, this high energy approximation is based
on the assumption that high energy particle undergoes a small deflection during
scattering process due to a relative weakness of the interaction potential. This
assumption excludes reflective scattering mode of a nonperturbative nature. How-
ever, notion of a potential must be generalized for the case of high energy rela-
tivistic particle scattering when the potential should become an energy – depen-
dent quantity. The infinitely rising total cross-sections imply that the potential
rises with the energy too.Thereby assumption on a small deflection of high en-
ergy particle during the scattering cannot be justified at high energies and so is
the standard eikonal approximation itself (but, of course, not an exponential form
of the scattering amplitude valid in any case). This is correlated with result of
the paper [20], where it has been shown that the eikonal approximation is valid in

Conclusion
Following the above considerations one can infer that the shadow and reflective
scattering modes correspond to a weak and strong couplings of hadron interac-
tions theory, respectively, and appearance of the reflective scattering is a natural
consequence of transition to strong coupling implied by the unitarity and the in-
teraction dynamics. This conjecture finds justification in the U–matrix form of
the amplitude unitarization where, considered as a generalized potential, the input
function u(s, b) grows up with the energy. The growth is reflecting the key prop-
erties of hadron interactions. The ultimate asymptotic regime expressed by Eqs.
(5) and (6) as well as the total cross–section increase described by the universal
empirical dependence, Eq. (9), have been related to a single mechanism consider-
ing the scattering amplitude as solution of Eq. (13) which naturally accounts for
the unitarity and presence of the both scattering modes with different dynamical
appearances of the inelastic channels contribution.
The rational form of unitarization (U–matrix) can be treated as a way of tran-
sition to the strong coupling limit in hadron scattering. The scattering amplitude
being solution of Eq. (13) provides a continuous transition between the two scat-
tering modes. The energy range of transition between these two modes separates
the asymptotical region and the range of lower energies dominated by the dynam-
ics that determines the total cross–section universal increase linear in √s. This
interval sets a relation between the two respective energy scales.
It is to be noted that existence of the reflective scattering mode is in accord with
postulate of Chew and Frautchi on the maximal strength of hadron interactions: “
a characteristic in the strong interactions is a capacity to “saturate” the unitarity
conditions at high energies” [21].
The presence of reflective scattering mode along with the shadow scattering
provide the required consistency and completeness in hadron interactions at the
high energies. Ad hoc exclusion of the reflective scattering mode would mean an
unjustified assumption on neglect of the strong coupling in the interaction dynam-
ics.
This dynamics is considered by QCD theory with colored objects (quarks and
gluons) confined inside hadrons [22]. One can assume that the color conduct-
