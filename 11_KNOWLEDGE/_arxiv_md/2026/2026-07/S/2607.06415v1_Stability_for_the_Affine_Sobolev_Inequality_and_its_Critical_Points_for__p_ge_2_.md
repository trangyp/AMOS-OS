Abstract
We prove stability for the affine Sobolev inequality for exponents p ≥2 with
best possible norm and best possible stability exponent. We prove a corresponding results for
critical points of the functional in the absence of bubbling.

In passing we mention some other developments of affine Sobolev inequalities, for example, the
affine Sobolev–Zhang inequality on BV(Rn) [54], the asymmetric affine Sobolev inequalities [29],

There is also a natural spectral and PDE side of the affine theory. Haddad et al. [32] developed
affine Poincar´e inequalities and affine spectral inequalities. They introduced the affine p-Laplacian
associated with the affine p-energy. This operator arises as the Euler–Lagrange operator for the
affine p-Rayleigh quotient. It is affine invariant and coincides with the usual p-Laplacian on
radial functions. In the Hilbertian case p = 2, this operator coincides with the affine Laplacian
introduced by Schindler and Tintarev [48]. In this case, the affine Laplacian can be viewed as
the usual Laplace operator in a suitable coordinate system depending on the function. This gives
a natural PDE framework for extremals and eigenvalue problems related to affine Sobolev-type
inequalities.
Stability of the classical and affine Sobolev inequalities
Lions [39] has proved qualitative stability for the classical Sobolev inequality in the sense that
if the Sobolev deficit

Stability of critical points of the classical Sobolev inequalities
Another natural direction is to study stability at the level of critical points, rather than minimiz-
ers, of the Sobolev quotient. More precisely, one may ask whether a function that almost solves
the Euler–Lagrange equation associated with the sharp Sobolev inequality must be quantitatively
close to an Aubin–Talenti bubble. In the classical notation, this means estimating the distance
from u to the family of bubbles in terms of the ˙W−1,p′-norm of the residual
−div(|∇u|p−2∇u) −|u|p∗−2u .
The qualitative theory goes back to the compactness theorem of Struwe [50]. In the case p =
2, Struwe proved that a nonnegative Palais–Smale sequence for the critical Sobolev functional
decomposes, up to a small error in ˙W1,2, into a sum of weakly interacting Talenti bubbles. This
phenomenon is usually referred to as bubbling. The corresponding compactness theory for the
critical p-Laplacian was later developed by Alves [1] and Mercuri and Willem [42]. Thus, under
an a prior energy condition that excludes the appearance of more than one bubble, the qualitative
theory implies that an almost critical point is close to a single bubble.
The first sharp quantitative result in this direction was obtained, for p = 2 and in the single-
bubble regime, by Ciraolo et al. [10]. They proved a linear estimate of the ˙W1,2-distance from a
bubble in terms of the Euler–Lagrange residual. The multiple-bubble case was then studied by
Figalli and Glaudo [20], who proved that the same linear control holds in dimensions 3 ≤n ≤5,
while it fails in dimensions n ≥6. The sharp quantitative estimates in the higher dimensional
bubbling regime were later obtained by Deng et al. [14]. The bound is no longer linear due to the
interaction of the bubbles.
For general 1 < p < n, the problem is substantially more delicate and still not completely
solved. Recently, Liu and Zhang [40], following the sharp stability framework of Figalli and
Zhang [22], proved the sharp single-bubble estimate

Organization of this paper
Outline of the proof of Theorem 1.1
The overall strategy follows the sharp gradient stability argument of Figalli–Zhang [22] (see
also [4, 21]). We first use a compactness argument to reduce the problem to functions that are
close, up to affine transformations, to the manifold Maff in Section 2. Then the main new diffi-
culty is the expansion of the affine energy in the sense of [22, Section 2], which is the objective
of Section 3. Unlike the classical Dirichlet energy, Ep is obtained from the directional quantities
{∥∇ξ f∥p
p}ξ∈Sn−1 through a nonlinear negative-power average on the sphere. Therefore, after expand-
ing the directional energies, one also has to expand the outer affine functional. This produces an
additional second-order term of variance type on the sphere [see (3.16)]. Such a term is absent
in the classical Sobolev inequality. Its role is to detect the extra trace-free affine directions in the
tangent space of Maff.
A key point is to control this new variance term. Following the affine Hessian viewpoint used
in the affine fractional setting of Fan et al. [18], we establish an affine spectral gap inequality
involving this variance term in Section 4 (precisely, Theorems 4.2 and 4.11). A similar analysis has
appeared independently and concurrently in [19]. Combining these and the nonlinear expansion,
we obtain the desired lower bound for the affine deficit, and finish the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
Section 5. Some technical estimates are collected in two appendices.
Outline of the proof of Theorem 1.3
Theorem 1.3 is a local formulation and the overall strategy of proof follows Liu and Zhang [40,
Theorem 1.2]. Since we have already assumed that the function under consideration is close to the
normalized affine Talenti bubble, we expand the affine Laplacian around this bubble in Proposition
3.4. The expansion is parallel to the one used in the proof of Theorem 1.1: after expanding the
directional terms and the outer affine functional, one obtains the same variance operator on Sn−1.
Hence, we need to establish the corresponding affine spectral gap inequality in Theorem 4.11.
There is, however, an additional point in the critical point problem. The normalized affine bub-
bles solve the Euler–Lagrange equation with a fixed amplitude, while the spectral gap is naturally
formulated after removing the full tangent space generated by the amplitude, scaling, translation,
and trace-free affine directions. To handle this mismatch, we first perform the modulation on the
enlarged cone of affine bubbles with a free amplitude parameter. This gives the desired orthogo-
nality with respect to this full tangent space of directions. We then test the residual against the ap-
proximate bubble to prove that the optimal amplitude is close to one [see (5.12)]. This is the affine
