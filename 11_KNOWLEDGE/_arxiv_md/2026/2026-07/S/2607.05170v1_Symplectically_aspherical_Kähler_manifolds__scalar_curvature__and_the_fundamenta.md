SCALAR CURVATURE, AND THE FUNDAMENTAL GROUP
LUCA F. DI CERBO, ALEXANDER DRANISHNIKOV, AND EKANSH JAUHARI
Abstract. We present a detailed study of closed smooth manifolds having
K¨ahler forms that pullback to exact forms on the universal cover. We show
that these manifolds, which we call symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds,
exist in abundance, even outside the aspherical setting, and have interesting
topological and geometric features, such as large fundamental group ´a la Koll´ar
and the absence of K¨ahler metrics of positive scalar curvature.
Motivated
by the latter, we address an extension of the Gromov–Lawson Conjecture in
the symplectic setting for Riemannian metrics. We also study K¨ahler cones
on symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds and the realizability problem of
their fundamental group, and explore their other complex geometric properties.

1. Introduction
The study of closed K¨ahler manifolds having extra structures is ubiquitous in
complex geometry. For instance, there are Gromov’s K¨ahler hyperbolic manifolds
that are defined as those having a K¨ahler form whose pullback to the universal cover
is d-bounded (and include, for example, K¨ahler manifolds having negative sectional
curvature) [Gro91], and then there are Koll´ar’s manifolds having large fundamental
group (also called Koll´ar large manifolds) that are defined as those whose universal
cover does not contain any positive dimensional compact complex subvarieties (and
include, for example, K¨ahler manifolds having Stein universal cover) [Kol93, Kol95].

In this paper, we study symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds that are defined
as those having a K¨ahler form whose pullback to the universal cover is exact, or
equivalently, that vanishes on every real spherical homology class (such forms are
called aspherical in the literature). The class of these manifolds properly contains
the class of K¨ahler hyperbolic manifolds and is contained within the class of Koll´ar
large manifolds, and manifolds in this class have tame topological properties that
help obtain results of complex and Riemannian geometric flavors.
There are two primary motivations behind studying these K¨ahler manifolds.
The first motivation comes from symplectic topology and geometry, where imposing
the asphericity condition (first formulated by Floer in his study of the Arnol’d
conjecture [Flo89]) on symplectic forms is fruitful for various purposes. Indeed,
the Arnol’d conjecture holds true for symplectically aspherical manifolds [RO99],
and classical theories such as Floer homology, the Lusternik–Schnirelmann theory
for Lagrangian intersections, and the theory of J-holomorphic curves are better
understood for them (see [Sch00, KRT08] and the references therein). Moreover,
the algebraic topology of symplectically aspherical manifolds is well-studied [LO95].
In view of these advantages, it is natural to ask what this topological condition of
symplectic asphericity earns one in complex geometric contexts! Specifically, upon
imposing symplectic asphericity on K¨ahler forms, one can investigate consequences
at the interface of complex and differential geometry, and approach them using
techniques coming also from symplectic and algebraic topology.
The second motivation comes from the placement of symplectically aspherical
K¨ahler manifolds in complex algebraic geometry. In the special setting of K¨ahler
hyperbolic manifolds, all but possibly the middle L2-Betti numbers of the universal
cover vanish (so the Hopf–Singer conjecture is verified) [Gro91] and the canonical
line bundle is ample [CY18]. In the generalized setting of Koll´ar large manifolds,
the K¨ahler universal cover is predicted by the Shafarevich conjecture to be a Stein
manifold [Kol93, Kol95]. So, one can view the class of symplectically aspherical
K¨ahler manifolds as an intermediate class to extend such results as well as to test
such conjectures. As shown recently in [DCDJ25], several members of this class,
remarkably many symmetric products of curves, show promise; see also interesting
results obtained and directions pursued in [JZ00, CX01, CY21, BDET24, DCL24,
DCL26]. Therefore, it becomes quite natural to investigate various complex and
differential geometric properties of general manifolds belonging to this class.
Symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds are topologically more flexible and
slightly better understood than some other K¨ahler manifolds, and at the same
time, they enjoy rich structures and interesting properties like some of their K¨ahler
cousins. For instance, their second homotopy group is not constrained (unlike that
of real hyperbolic manifolds); they can have amenable fundamental group (unlike
K¨ahler hyperbolic manifolds) but not finite fundamental group (unlike Kobayashi
hyperbolic manifolds), see Section 4; they are of general type in complex dimension
2 when they are not aspherical (i.e., do not have contractible universal cover, see
Section 3), and whenever they are of general type, they have ample canonical line
bundle and admit K¨ahler–Einstein metrics of negative Ricci curvature (like K¨ahler
hyperbolic manifolds) [P Li19]; and they are non-positively curved in both K¨ahler
and Riemannian senses (like most aspherical K¨ahler manifolds), see Section 5. Such
features make symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds useful and interesting
from various points of view.

The goal of this paper is to answer several nuanced questions arising in the
study of the geometry and topology of symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds,
and to pose some new ones.
Of our particular interest in this paper are non-
aspherical symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds, primarily those with non-
trivial second homotopy group (see [Gom98] and Section 2 for motivations), which
are especially nice in complex dimension 2 and whose construction we describe in
each dimension. We study which finitely presented discrete groups can be realized
as the fundamental group of these manifolds, and give a complete answer in the case
of free abelian groups. Our findings are analogous to those obtained in [IKRT04,
KRT07] for symplectically aspherical manifolds. We also investigate the positivity
of scalar curvature associated with both Riemannian and K¨ahler metrics on these
manifolds, and (with and without additional assumptions on the topology of the
underlying manifold) we obtain non-existence results that support some natural
conjectures and extend related results from [DCDJ25].
Of course, these results
apply to the class of K¨ahler hyperbolic manifolds, and some of them also extend
to that of Koll´ar large manifolds. Then, we look at the question of what happens
to the K¨ahler cone associated with aspherical K¨ahler forms. In this direction, we
show that the asphericity of the entire K¨ahler cone is a very constraining condition,
and we describe situations where the K¨ahler cone has no other aspherical K¨ahler
forms. These results show, in particular, that closely related K¨ahler forms may
behave wildly differently when pulled back to the universal cover.
Organization of this paper. To make this paper somewhat self-contained, we
begin with a review of symplectically aspherical manifolds in Section 2, where we
also define symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds and discuss examples. Then
in Section 3, we give a general recipe for constructing non-aspherical symplectically
aspherical K¨ahler manifolds in each complex dimension ≥2, and we compare the
class of these manifolds with that of other well-studied K¨ahler manifolds, such as
real hyperbolic, those having Stein universal cover, Kobayashi hyperbolic, K¨ahler
hyperbolic, and those having large fundamental group (called Koll´ar large here).
Let us now describe our main results and the organization of the rest of this
paper.
On the fundamental group. Our first main result is the description of explicit
examples to justify that each free abelian group of even rank ≥4 can be real-
ized as the fundamental group of a symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifold of
complex dimension 2 whose second homotopy group is non-trivial. Actually, our
examples, which come from the symmetric squares of generic curves and certain
branched covers of abelian surfaces, are smooth projective surfaces of general type
having Stein universal cover and non-trivial real spherical homology classes, see
Theorem 4.4.
This, and other results in Section 4, aim to give insights on the
problem of realizing possible finitely presented discrete groups as the fundamental
groups of symplectically aspherical smooth projective varieties having vanishing or
non-vanishing second homotopy, which we formulate in this paper.
On positive scalar curvature. Next, we have results on the scalar curvature
associated with K¨ahler and Riemannian metrics on symplectically aspherical mani-
folds. Specifically, we show in Section 5 that no such smooth projective variety can
support a K¨ahler metric of positive scalar curvature, and consequently, we get the
same conclusion for K¨ahler hyperbolic projective varieties and smooth projective

On K¨ahler cones. Our last set of results concern the study of the K¨ahler cones of
symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds. In particular, we look at the following
two extremes:
(1) when all K¨ahler forms in the K¨ahler cone are aspherical;
(2) when there is an essentially unique aspherical K¨ahler form.
The first of these situations arises, for instance, when the second homotopy
group of the manifold vanishes. Using results on the cones of tame and compat-
ible symplectic forms, we study in Section 6 the implications of having a “totally
aspherical K¨ahler cone” on a manifold. Our results suggest that the topology of
such manifolds is constrained, especially for K¨ahler surfaces, and the constraints
on the vector space of real spherical homology classes of the manifold are similar
to those imposed when all symplectic forms on the manifold are aspherical, see
Proposition 6.1 and Corollary 6.3.
For the second situation above, we give an explicit family of examples using
the symmetric products of curves. More precisely, we show in Proposition 7.1 that
on such a symmetric product, a K¨ahler form that is not cohomologous to a scalar
multiple of the aspherical K¨ahler form defined on the symmetric product using the
canonical aspherical K¨ahler form of its Jacobian is not aspherical. Showing this
involves the use of the description of the N´eron–Severi group of these manifolds,
along with cohomological computations ´a la Macdonald, see Section 7.
Throughout the paper, we mention several questions of different flavors (along
with their motivations) that, we hope, will stimulate further interest and research.
Conventions. In this paper, all manifolds are smooth, connected, and without
boundary, and all projective varieties are complex, unless specified otherwise.

2. Definitions, examples, and overview
Let M be a closed smooth 2n-manifold. We say that a closed 2-form ω on M
is c-symplectic (c for “cohomologically”) if [ω]n ̸= 0 in H2n(M; R). Of course, a
symplectic form on M is c-symplectic since its n-fold exterior product is the volume
form on M, but the converse is not true in general (say, on CP 2#CP 2).
A c-symplectic form ω is called aspherical if for every smooth map f : S2 →M,
we have

Equivalently, ω is aspherical if and only if ⟨[ω], Π(M)⟩= 0, where Π(M) is the
vector space of all real spherical homology classes of M, i.e.,
Π(M) = Im(h ⊗idR : π2(M) ⊗R →H2(M; R)),
where h: π2(M) →H2(M; Z) is the Hurewicz map. If M admits such a form, it is
called c-symplectically aspherical. A closed manifold equipped with an aspherical
symplectic form is called symplectically aspherical.
We now consider manifolds equipped with aspherical K¨ahler forms.
2.1. Definition. Let (M, J) be a closed K¨ahler manifold. If there exists a K¨ahler
form ω on M compatible with J that is aspherical in the aforementioned sense,
then the triad (M, J, ω) is called a symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifold.
If Π(M) = 0 (for example, if π2(M) = 0), then each c-symplectic (and hence,
K¨ahler) form on M is aspherical. Conversely, it is easy to see that Π(M) = 0
if every symplectic form on M is aspherical (see, for instance, [IKRT04, Proposi-
tion 8.1]). We call such manifolds totally symplectically aspherical. In Section 6,
we will study totally symplectically aspherical K¨ahler or totally K¨ahler aspherical
manifolds, i.e., those on which each K¨ahler form, compatible with a given integrable
almost complex structure, is aspherical.
2.2. Example. (1) For each n, the projective space CP n is not symplectically
aspherical since Π(CP n) ∼= H2(CP n; R) ∼= R by the Hurewicz theorem.
(2) The Kodaira–Thurston manifold KT = H3 × S1 (where H3 is the Heisen-
berg 3-manifold) and some of its branched covers are symplectically aspherical
(see [Gom98]), but not K¨ahler (see [Thu76]). Recall that KT is the total space
of a T 2-bundle over T 2.
(3) For each n ≥2, the n-th symmetric product SP n(Mg) of a projective curve Mg
for g ≥2n −1, where Mg has a generic complex structure in its moduli space
Mg, is a symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifold, see [DCDJ25, Theorem 4.1]
(and also Section 7). Recall that SP n(Mg) is the orbit space of the permutation
action of the symmetric group Σn on the Cartesian product (Mg)n. We will
revisit this example multiple times in the paper.
The smooth projective varieties SP n(Mg), and some of Gompf’s branched covers
of KT , have infinite Π, and so they are “non-trivial examples”. Several other such
manifolds are known — for different examples, see [Gom98, IKRT04].
2.3. Remark. We note that symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds are not
just symplectically aspherical manifolds that support a K¨ahler structure. It follows

3. Symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds in complex geometry
In this section, we look at some complex geometric aspects of symplectically
aspherical K¨ahler manifolds and study how these manifolds interact with other
classically studied closed K¨ahler manifolds, such as Kobayashi(–Brody) hyperbolic,
Gromov’s K¨ahler hyperbolic, and Koll´ar large manifolds.
First, we describe a general method for constructing symplectically aspherical
K¨ahler manifolds. Note that closed K¨ahler manifolds that are aspherical are sym-
plectically aspherical K¨ahler for obvious reasons (cf. Section 2), and their examples
are easy to come by (see, for instance, [ABC+96, IKRT04]). Also, several methods
for constructing non-aspherical symplectically aspherical manifolds are known in
the literature (see, for example, [Gom98, IKRT04, FMS04, KRT07] and the refer-
ences therein). In that spirit, our method also yields non-aspherical examples of
symplectically aspherical K¨ahler manifolds in each complex dimension ≥2.
We use the standard cyclic covering construction outlined in [BHPV04, Sec-
tion I. 17].

4. The fundamental group and the realizability problem
The existence of an aspherical K¨ahler form on a closed smooth manifold severely

5. Symplectically apherical manifolds and scalar curvature
In this section, we propose (and provide support towards) an extension of the
following long-standing conjecture.
5.1. Conjecture (Gromov–Lawson). A closed aspherical manifold cannot support
a Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature.
We argue that it is very interesting to consider the following strengthening of
Conjecture 5.1 in the realm of symplectic manifolds.
5.2. Conjecture. A closed symplectically aspherical manifold cannot support a Rie-
mannian metric of positive scalar curvature.
The main idea behind such conjectures is that extra enriched structures on
closed smooth manifolds should be able to prevent the positivity of their scalar
curvature. Specifically, Conjecture 5.2 predicts that symplectic forms on a smooth
manifold that have exact pullbacks on its universal cover should, perhaps because
of topological reasons, suffice to obstruct the existence of Riemannian metrics of
positive scalar curvature on the manifold.

Novikov conjecture. Indeed, the following may be known to experts.
