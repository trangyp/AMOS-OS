Spin-orbit magnetism in altermagnets
Ruojia Wang,1 Yuntian Liu,1 Renzheng Xiong,1 Xiaobing Chen,2, ∗and Qihang Liu1, 2, †
1State Key Laboratory of Quantum Functional Materials, Department of Physics,
and Guangdong Basic Research Center of Excellence for Quantum Science,

2Quantum Science Center of Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area (Guangdong), Shenzhen 518045, China
(Dated: July 9, 2026)
The mechanism enabling antiferromagnets, including altermagnets, to exhibit a prominent anoma-
lous Hall effect despite a vanishingly small net magnetization has long remained elusive. Here, by
employing oriented spin group theory and spin-orbit-coupling tensor expansion, we systematically
disentangle the perturbative behaviors of orbital and spin magnetizations with respect to spin-orbit
coupling. Remarkably, we find that only if the opposite-spin sublattices are connected through a
fourfold rotation, the orbital and spin magnetizations exhibit distinct perturbative orders. In these
altermagnets, we further discover a coaxial Hall effect characterized by the induced spin and orbital
magnetizations aligning parallel to the N´eel vector, which we further demonstrate by first-principles
calculations in the altermagnet KV2Se2O. This effect holds great promise for achieving deterministic
switching of the N´eel order under weak external fields. Our work provides a systematic symmetry
approach to identify potential altermagnetic candidates combining a large anomalous Hall effect
with minimal net magnetization, paving the way for high-performance, stray-field-free spintronic
applications.
Unconventional magnets, which combine the com-
pensated magnetization with ferromagnetic-like physi-
cal properties, emerge as a pivotal platform for next-
generation spintronics[1, 2]. These unconventional mag-
nets comprise two representative branches:
anoma-
lous Hall antiferromagnets and spin-splitting antiferro-
magnets.
One prototypical class of the latter is the
altermagnet[3], which is a collinear antiferromagnet that
exhibits momentum-dependent spin splitting even in
the absence of spin-orbit coupling (SOC)[4–8].
Al-
though the anomalous Hall effect (AHE) was initially
perceived as the transport fingerprint of altermagnets,
recent insights have revealed that this effect originates
from the SOC effect rather than the altermagnetic spin
splitting[1, 9, 10]. However, compared with ferromagnets
where the anomalous Hall conductivity is proportional
to the net magnetization[11], the net magnetization in
altermagnet is generally weak yet varies by several or-
ders of magnitude from 10−6 to 10−2 µB across different
systems[8, 12–17], leaving the underlying connection be-
tween the SOC-induced AHE and the net magnetization
largely unestablished[12, 18].
To elucidate the connection between the AHE and the
net magnetization in altermagnets, symmetry-based ap-
proaches have been adopted to classify diverse anomalous
Hall N´eel textures[19]. However, conventional magnetic
group analysis naturally entangles the contributions from
magnetic order and SOC[20–23], obscuring the micro-
scopic mechanism of the SOC-induced AHE in altermag-
nets. This limitation is fundamentally overcome by the
recent development of spin space group theory[24–27].
Within this framework, recent studies have employed rep-
resentation analysis to study the relationships between
the N´eel order and the net magnetization[28–30]. Fur-
thermore, the spin-orbit tensor expansion method has en-
abled the resolution of the net magnetization into its con-
stituent spin and orbital parts[31, 32], where the latter
directly couples to the Berry curvature that contributes
to the AHE[33], successfully explaining their distinct per-
turbative orders with respect to SOC in ferromagnets[31]
and the coplanar antiferromagnet Mn3Sn[32].
Never-
theless, a comprehensive framework that systematically
quantifies the SOC effects on both spin and orbital mag-
netizations, alongside their dependence on the N´eel vec-
tor orientation, remains absent for altermagnets. This
hinders the symmetry-guided exploration of potential
altermagnets that simultaneously exhibit both a sup-
pressed net magnetization and a substantial AHE.
To address this issue, in this Letter we employ the
newly developed oriented spin space group theory[32] to
systematically investigate the dependence of spin mag-
netization and orbital magnetization on the SOC effect
in altermagnets. We find that when opposite-spin sub-
lattices are connected by a sixfold rotation or multiple
high-fold rotations, both spin and orbital magnetizations
appear as third-order perturbations with respect to SOC.
Conversely, they both follow first-order perturbations
when connected solely by twofold rotations.
Remark-
ably, only if the opposite-spin sublattices are connected
via a fourfold rotation, the spin and orbital magnetiza-
tion can exhibit distinct perturbative orders with respect
to SOC. Intriguingly, such systems host a coaxial Hall ef-
fect where the spin magnetization, orbital magnetization,
and N´eel vector are mutually parallel to each other. We
further screen 7 candidate materials and predict the ex-
istence of the coaxial Hall effect in KV2Se2O via first-
principles calculations.
This work establishes a semi-
quantitative relationship among the net magnetization,
the AHE, and the N´eel vector in altermagnets, provid-
ing explicit guidelines for the design of high-performance

of N. First, whenever N aligns with the rotation axis of

Decoupled scaling hierarchy in LiFe2F6 –
As an ex-
ample, we apply such a theoretical approach to eval-
uate the spin and orbital magnetizations of altermag-
netic LiFe2F6 [Fig. 2(a)], aligning an in-plane N´eel order
N ∥[100] to test the distinct scaling behaviors of MO
and MS. This magnetic structure possess the oriented
spin space group P −142/1m−1n1m∞100m1. In the pres-
ence of SOC, this oriented spin space group is broken into
the magnetic point group m′mm′, which allows a nonzero
net magnetization along the [010] direction. Under sym-
metry constraints for tensors, both MS and MO vectors
lie along the [010] direction.

enables the separation of the possible orbital and spin

Discussion–
Our approach fundamentally uncovers
the dependence of spin and orbital magnetizations on the
SOC strength, thereby resolving a long-standing para-
dox in altermagnetic anomalous Hall transport. Tradi-
tional magnetic group analysis inherently coupled these
two magnetizations, obscuring a core physical picture:
the AHE governed by MO can, in fact, be completely
decoupled from the stray-field-related MS. In type III
altermagnets, this decoupling manifests as a symmetry-
protected disparity in perturbation orders, enabling the
system to maximize the AHE signal while keeping the net
magnetization minimal. Crucially, the emergent coaxial
Hall effect not only yields a unique transverse voltage

actions such as magnetic anisotropy and Dzyaloshinskii-
Moriya interaction, as well as macroscopic physical prop-
erties such as electric polarization and other quantum
transports, establishing a comprehensive framework for
the symmetry-guided design of next-generation quantum
