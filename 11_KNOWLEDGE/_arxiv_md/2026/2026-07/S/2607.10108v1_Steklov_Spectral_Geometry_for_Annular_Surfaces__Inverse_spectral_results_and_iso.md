STEKLOV SPECTRAL GEOMETRY FOR ANNULAR SURFACES:
INVERSE SPECTRAL RESULTS AND ISOSPECTRAL
COMPACTNESS
YUJUN JIN, ZUOQIN WANG
Abstract. We study the inverse spectral problems for the Steklov spectrum
on compact surfaces with boundary. We prove that among flat annular sur-
faces, the lateral surface of a conical frustum is uniquely determined by its
Steklov spectrum.
As a consequence, each circular annulus is uniquely de-
termined among all planar domains, providing the first example of a non-
simply connected Euclidean domain with this property. Furthermore, we show
that any family of Steklov isospectral flat annular surfaces is compact in the
C∞topology, extending previous results for simply connected planar domains.
These results are established through a detailed analysis of the spectral zeta
function and the zeta-regularized determinant associated with the Dirichlet-
to-Neumann operator for annular surfaces.

Conjecture 1.1. Two Riemannian metrics g1 and g2 on a given smooth surface Ω

The first goal of this paper is to investigate this conjecture for flat surfaces, and
in particular, for Euclidean planar domains. Note that if two conformally equivalent
flat metrics g and ρg satisfy ρ|M = 1, it necessarily follows that ρ = 1 throughout
Ω. Therefore, in this setting the conjecture reduces to:
Conjecture 1.1′ (Conjecture 1.1, flat version). Two flat surfaces are Steklov
isospectral if and only if they are isometric.
This problem is analogous to Kac’s famous inverse spectral question [Kac66],
“Can one hear the shape of a drum?”, which asks whether planar domains are
uniquely determined by their Laplacian spectra up to isometry.
Although an-
swered negatively by Gordon, Webb, and Wolpert [GWW92], Kac’s problem re-
mains open for planar domains with smooth boundary. As for the Steklov problem,
the only smooth Euclidean domains currently known to be uniquely determined
by their Steklov spectra are planar disks, due to Edward [Edw93a], and three-
dimensional balls, due to Polterovich and Sher [PS15]. See also Wang and Yun
[WY] for analogous results in 3-dimensional simply connected space forms. On the
other hand, positive results have been obtained when the inverse Steklov problem
is restricted to several special classes. Examples include warped product manifolds
[DKN21, Gen20] and certain special planar polygons [DGM+26].
In this work, we focus on a specific class of surfaces.
Definition 1.2. We call a compact, orientable, connected Riemannian surface
(Ω, g) an annular surface if it is of genus zero with exactly two smooth boundary
components. We denote by A the set of all smooth annular surfaces.
According to the Osgood–Phillips–Sarnak uniformization theorem (see Section
3.1), any annular surface is conformal to some flat cylinder Cl with geodesic bound-
ary.
A typical annular surface is the lateral surface of a conical frustum Ar1,r2,h (see
Section 4.2 for details), endowed with the standard Euclidean metric. Our first main
result in this paper is to prove that these special annular surfaces are spectrally
determined among flat annular surfaces, thereby providing evidence for Conjecture
1.1′.
Theorem 1.3. Among all flat surfaces in A, the lateral surface of each conical
frustum Ar1,r2,h is uniquely determined by its Steklov spectrum.
By [GPPS14], for any compact smooth surface Ω, the Steklov spectrum deter-
mines the number of connected components of its boundary M. Thus Theorem 1.3
directly yields the following result.

step in the proof is to show that among all flat surfaces in A, the surface Ar1,r2,h
uniquely minimizes ζΣ,g(−2).
In the remainder of this paper, we address a related weaker question: the com-
pactness of the family of Steklov isospectral planar domains in the C∞topology.
We shall use the Cheeger–Gromov notion of precompactness, specialized to com-
pact surfaces and with specified regularity of convergence. More precisely, let F
be a family of compact surfaces with boundary. We say that F is compact in the
C∞topology if, for every sequence Sk in F, there exists a subsequence Skj, a com-
pact reference surface Ωtogether with smooth metrics gkj on Ω, and a family of
isometries

such that gkj converges to a smooth metric g in C∞. For s ≥0, compactness in the
Hs topology is defined in the same way, with the convergence gkj →g understood
in Hs(Ω) with respect to any fixed smooth background metric.
While the compactness problem for Laplace-isospectral metrics has been an-
swered positively on surfaces [OPS89, OPS88a, Kim08], only limited results are
known in higher dimensions (see, e.g., [CY90, CX96, LW19]). By contrast, the
analogous question in the Steklov setting remains largely unexplored. One of the
difficulties lies in the lack of sufficiently strong spectral invariants. The classical
approach to establishing the compactness of a family of isospectral metrics relies
on controlling Sobolev norms of geometric quantities via the coefficients of the heat
trace expansion, which are spectral invariants. While this method is effective for
elliptic differential operators such as the Laplacian, it encounters serious obstacles
in the case of the DtN operator. Indeed, as a pseudodifferential operator, only some
of the heat coefficients of the DtN operator are local [Gil04]. Here, “local” means
that these coefficients are given by sums of integrals of quantities that depend only
on the metric near the boundary. Consequently, these coefficients alone do not
currently provide the necessary control of the relevant Sobolev norms.

3. Steklov spectral analysis on flat cylinders
3.1. The Osgood–Phillips–Sarnak uniformization of annular surfaces. In
their study of the extremal properties of the log-determinant of the Laplacian, Os-
good, Phillips, and Sarnak established a new uniformization theorem [OPS88b],
which classifies conformal classes of compact surfaces with non-positive Euler char-
acteristic, both with and without boundary. Specifically, they proved that within
any conformal class of Riemannian metrics on a compact surface with a non-positive
Euler characteristic, the following uniform metrics exist uniquely up to multiplica-
tion by a constant:
(1) For a closed surface, there is a unique constant curvature metric in that class.
(2) For a surface with boundary, there are two distinct types of uniform metrics:
(i) (Type I) a constant curvature metric with geodesic boundary;
(ii) (Type II) a flat metric whose boundary has constant geodesic curvature.
Recall that A denotes the set of smooth annular surfaces. A standard model for
A is given by the cylinders
(3.1)

dt2 + dθ2.
Note that g0 itself can be regarded as both a type I and a type II
uniform metric.
Each surface S ∈A has vanishing Euler characteristic, i.e. χ(S) = 0. In view of
the Gauss–Bonnet theorem and the Osgood–Phillips–Sarnak uniformization theo-
rem mentioned above, S is conformally equivalent to a standard cylinder (Cl, g0)
for some parameter l. In other words, one may identify S with (Cl, g) for some
