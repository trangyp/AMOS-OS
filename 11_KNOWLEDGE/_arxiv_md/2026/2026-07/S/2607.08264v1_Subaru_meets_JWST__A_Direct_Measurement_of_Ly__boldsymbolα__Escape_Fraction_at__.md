ABSTRACT
We present a direct measurement of the Ly𝛼escape fraction, 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc , for H𝛼emitters (HAEs) at 𝑧≃6.2 in the JWST CEERS
field by combining JWST/NIRCam F470N imaging with Subaru/HSC NB872 imaging. This unique pair of narrow-band filters
enables the simultaneous measurement of Ly𝛼and H𝛼fluxes from galaxies during the epoch of reionization (EoR). We select 84
HAEs from F470N excesses, among which 56 have reliable NB872 photometry and 19 are detected in Ly𝛼at > 2𝜎significance.
The completeness-weighted stack of the HAE sample yields a median 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
at 𝑧≃6.2 of 0.106+0.066
−0.044, which is in good agreement
with recent measurements at similar redshifts. We further find no significant dependence of the stacked 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
on the lower
limit of H𝛼luminosity over the luminosity range probed by our sample. If Ly𝛼escape traces Lyman continuum leakage, this
may suggest that relatively luminous HAEs, rather than only the faintest galaxies, can provide an important contribution to
the ionizing photon budget during the EoR. For individual galaxies, 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
positively correlates with Ly𝛼equivalent width and
negatively correlates with the UV continuum slope 𝛽and the rest-frame UV size, while no significant correlation is found with
SED-derived 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), or rest-frame optical size, although these trends are based on a limited sample. These results suggest
that the galaxy-to-galaxy variation in 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
is more closely linked to compact, low-attenuation star-forming components traced
by the UV continuum than to global dust attenuation or the overall stellar structure.

wavelength of 1215.67 Å, corresponding to the 2𝑝→1𝑠transition,
and is one of the most prominent emission lines in the Universe.
Following the early prediction of strong Ly𝛼emission from young
galaxies (Partridge & Peebles 1967), Ly𝛼has long played a central
role in the discovery and confirmation of high-𝑧galaxies, as well
as in studies of their physical properties and the progress of cosmic
reionization (e.g. Hu et al. 1998; Gawiser et al. 2006; Kashikawa et al.
2006; Ouchi et al. 2008, 2010). The particular importance of Ly𝛼
stems from its resonant nature. After being produced in H ii regions,
Ly𝛼photons undergo repeated resonant scattering by H i within and
around galaxies, and at sufficiently high redshift, they can be further
attenuated by the partially neutral intergalactic medium (IGM). This
process greatly increases their effective path lengths, making them
highly susceptible to even small amounts of dust. In addition, the
H i covering fraction, gas kinematics, geometry, and viewing angle
can strongly affect the Ly𝛼line flux, spectral profile, and spatial
distribution (e.g. Verhamme et al. 2006; Dijkstra 2014; Herenz et al.
2025; Almada Monter et al. 2026). Consequently, the observed Ly𝛼
flux cannot in general be translated directly into the star-formation
rate (SFR) or ionizing photon production rate (e.g. Atek et al. 2008;
Hayes et al. 2010). Moreover, Ly𝛼-selected galaxies do not represent
an unbiased subset of the parent star-forming population, but are
often biased toward blue, low-mass, and relatively dust-poor systems
(e.g. Gawiser et al. 2007; Ono et al. 2010b; Arrabal Haro et al. 2020;
Iani et al. 2024).
A commonly used anchor for interpreting these complex radiative-
transfer effects is the non-resonant hydrogen recombination line H𝛼.
Because H𝛼directly traces nebular recombination in ionized gas and
is not subject to strong resonant scattering like Ly𝛼, it provides a
useful estimate of the intrinsic Ly𝛼photon production rate associ-
ated with star formation. Under Case B recombination, the intrinsic
Ly𝛼/H𝛼ratio is commonly taken to be 8.7 for typical nebular condi-
tions (Hummer & Storey 1987); deviations from this value reflect the
effects of H i scattering, dust absorption, gas kinematics, geometry,
and viewing angle (e.g. Laursen et al. 2009; Hayes 2015). Direct
comparisons between H𝛼and Ly𝛼in the same galaxies therefore
provide a powerful way to quantify the Ly𝛼escape fraction, 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc ,
and to investigate its physical origin (e.g. Oteo et al. 2015; Matthee
et al. 2016; Shimakawa et al. 2017; Gazagnes et al. 2020; Lin et al.
2024; Shimizu et al. 2025). Oteo et al. (2015) combined H𝛼-selected
galaxies with existing Ly𝛼data and found that only ≃4.5% of H𝛼
emitters (HAEs) show detectable Ly𝛼emission at 𝑧∼2. They also
showed that 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
decreases with increasing dust attenuation, redder
UV continuum slope, stellar mass (𝑀∗), and SFR. This indicates
that Ly𝛼escape is not governed by star-formation activity alone,
but is strongly regulated by the dust content and the structure of the
interstellar medium (ISM) and circumgalactic medium (CGM).
Such direct comparisons become increasingly difficult from the
ground once H𝛼is redshifted beyond the 𝐾band at 𝑧≳2.5. The
James Webb Space Telescope (JWST, Rigby et al. 2023) has dramat-
ically transformed this situation. In particular, NIRCam wide-field
slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) covers 2.4–5.0 𝜇m and NIRSpec cov-
ers 0.6–5.3 𝜇m, enabling direct access to H𝛼at much higher red-
shift, even during the epoch of reionization (EoR). Using the NIR-
Cam/WFSS data together with deep Ly𝛼spectroscopy, Tang et al.
(2024) characterized the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
distributions of galaxies at 𝑧≃5–6.
They found that galaxies with large 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
are relatively common at
these redshifts, and that Ly𝛼escape tends to be more efficient in
UV-fainter, bluer galaxies with stronger rest-frame optical emission.
These results suggest that Ly𝛼escape at the end of reionization is
closely connected to young, low-dust, intense star-forming systems.
Recent studies based on JWST observations have also reported that
𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
tends to be higher in UV-fainter, bluer galaxies (e.g. Ning et al.
2023, 2026; Lin et al. 2024; Saxena et al. 2024).
In addition to object-by-object measurements, the cosmic-
averaged Ly𝛼escape fraction is a key quantity for interpreting Ly𝛼
observations during the EoR. A conventional approach to estimating
the cosmic-averaged 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
involves comparing the Ly𝛼luminosity
function (LF) with the UV LF (e.g. Hayes et al. 2011; Konno et al.
2016; Goovaerts et al. 2024). With the advent of JWST, it is now
feasible to measure the cosmic-averaged 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
even at 𝑧> 2 by com-
paring the Ly𝛼LF with the H𝛼LF, which shares a similar physical
timescale with Ly𝛼emission (Sun et al. 2023). However, LF-based
methods suffer from systematic uncertainties, including dependen-
cies on the assumed functional form of the LF and the choice of
integration limits. Furthermore, it remains uncertain whether com-
parisons based on fixed multiples of the characteristic luminosity
(𝐿∗) across two different LFs truly trace the same underlying galaxy
population. These uncertainties motivate a direct measurement of
the cosmic-averaged 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
from the same H𝛼-selected galaxies. Lin
et al. (2024) derived the median 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
( 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc,med) at 𝑧= 4.9–6.3 by
stacking the H𝛼and Ly𝛼emission directly measured from NIR-
Cam/WFSS data and VLT/MUSE observations, respectively. They
obtained 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc,med = 0.090 ± 0.006, providing a key spectroscopic
benchmark for the cosmic-averaged 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
at 𝑧≃5–6.
Narrow-band (NB) imaging for H𝛼provides a complementary
route to such measurements. Because it is imaging-based, NB se-
lection can efficiently identify line emitters within a well-defined
wavelength interval while preserving two-dimensional spatial infor-
mation. This makes its selection function distinct from that of slitless
spectroscopy, free from slit losses, spectral-extraction uncertainties,
and spectral overlap or contamination, making it well suited to re-
covering total line fluxes. Recent JWST/NIRCam NB programmes
demonstrate that, in specific wavelength windows, the detection lim-
its for line fluxes can be comparable to, or fainter than, those reached
by existing slitless spectroscopic surveys (Duncan et al. 2025). JWST
NIRCam is equipped with several NB filters, the longest of which is
F470N (central wavelength 𝜆𝑐= 4.708 𝜇m, bandwidth = 0.051 𝜇m),
which can capture H𝛼emission from galaxies at 𝑧≃6.2.
In this study, we show the first measurement of 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
at 𝑧≃6.2
based on dual-NB imaging, using the NB872 filter (central wave-
length 𝜆𝑐= 0.872 𝜇m, bandwidth = 0.0067 𝜇m) of Subaru/Hyper
Suprime-Cam (HSC; Miyazaki et al. 2018; Komiyama et al. 2018;
Kawanomoto et al. 2018; Furusawa et al. 2018) to capture the Ly𝛼
emission from the HAEs detected in F470N. As shown in Figure 1,
the combination of JWST/NIRCam F470N and Subaru/HSC NB872
provides the only available dual-NB filter pair that can simultaneously
capture H𝛼and Ly𝛼fluxes from the same galaxies at 𝑧> 6, offering
a rare observational window to directly measure both lines through
NB imaging. At lower redshift, Matthee et al. (2016) implemented
a closely analogous dual-NB approach at 𝑧= 2.23, measuring Ly𝛼
emission for H𝛼-selected galaxies. They derived both individual and
median 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
and investigated their dependence on galaxy properties.
In this study, we apply the same technique to the EoR for the first
time.

2.4 Construction of the HAE sample
2.4.1 Multi-wavelength photometric catalogue
We construct a photometric catalogue using HST and JWST im-
ages homogenized to a common PSF, which facilitates the robust
selection of emission line galaxies with NB filters. For source detec-
tion, we adopt the F470N image as the detection band and perform
forced photometry in the other bands after convolving each image
to match the F470N PSF. This approach is optimal since all the
HST/JWST images used in this study have smaller PSF sizes than
F470N (FWHM∼0′′.175). Note that the HSC/NB872 data require
a different photometric approach due to their much coarser spatial
resolution compared to JWST, which is described in detail in Sec-
tion 3.2. First, we construct PSF models for all HST and JWST
images. We then generate convolution kernels to match the PSF of
each band to that of F470N using pypher (Boucaud et al. 2016).
The convolution is performed using the photutils v2.0.2 (Bradley
et al. 2024). To validate the PSF homogenization, we compare the
curves of growth of the PSF-matched images with that of F470N.
The enclosed-flux fractions agree to within 2% over the full range of
aperture radii tested for all bands, indicating a sufficiently good PSF
match for typical photometric applications (e.g. Pirie et al. 2025).
Next, we perform source detection on the F470N image using SEx-
tractor (Bertin & Arnouts 1996). We adopt detection parameters
of DETECT_MINAREA= 9 and DETECT_THRESH= 1.5 and employ a
Gaussian filter with an FWHM of 4 pixels (0′′.12). These parameters
are determined after verification with small cutouts so that spectro-
scopically confirmed galaxies at 𝑧≃6.2 (see Section 2.4.3) can be
detected in F470N, and spurious detections can be minimized.
Finally, we perform forced photometry across all bands using SEx-
tractor in dual-image mode, utilizing the F470N image as a de-
tection band. Following Pirie et al. (2025), we adopt two aperture
diameters for photometry, 0′′.3 and 0′′.6. We utilize the 0′′.3-diameter
apertures for the selection of HAEs to maximize the signal-to-noise
ratio (S/N), while we use the 0′′.6-diameter apertures for SED fit-
ting to capture a larger fraction of the flux (approximating the total
flux). Photometric uncertainties estimated by propagating the JWST
pipeline error maps within an aperture may be systematically un-
derestimated because pixel-to-pixel correlations in the resampling
process are not fully accounted for in the error propagation (e.g.
Bagley et al. 2023). To address this issue, we perform an empirical
noise analysis by placing 100,000 apertures at random positions in
each band. We measure the standard deviation of the fluxes in these
apertures and compare it with the uncertainties derived from the error
maps. We find that the pipeline error maps underestimate the noise
by a factor of ∼3.5 for F470N and ∼1.5 for the other CEERS bands.
The final uncertainties are obtained by rescaling the initial aperture
uncertainties derived from the error maps by these correction factors,
allowing them to retain the spatial variation of the local noise across
the mosaics while accounting for the correlated-noise correction.
2.4.2 Selection of NB excess sources
We identify HAEs from the multi-wavelength catalogue by selecting
sources showing a significant flux excess in the F470N relative to
the broad-band continuum. Prior to the selection, we apply several
quality cuts to clean the catalogue. First, we exclude sources affected
by saturation and those lying outside the valid imaging footprint in
F444W and F470N. To ensure a homogeneous selection, we also
exclude sources in low-S/N regions near the footprint edges (25 pix-
els) in the F470N and F444W images. After applying these footprint
and edge masks, the effective F470N-covered area used for the HAE
selection is 85.2 arcmin2. We further exclude sources with S/N < 2
in all bands other than F470N as likely spurious detections. Applying
these criteria yields a sample of 19,836 sources.
When selecting NB excess sources, a non-flat continuum can bias
the inferred NB excess because the NB and broad-band filters have
different central wavelengths. We therefore estimate the continuum
slope using two nearby filters and correct the observed F444W −
F470N colour (e.g. Sobral et al. 2013; Pirie et al. 2025). Although
F444W is the broad-band closest to F470N, it may be contaminated

2.4.4 Removal of AGN/LRD
Our sample of HAEs may be contaminated by active galactic nuclei
(AGNs) and so-called Little Red Dots (LRDs; e.g. Matthee et al.
2024), which are very compact and red objects. While the physical
nature of LRDs remains a subject of ongoing debate, recent studies
suggest they likely host significant AGN activity (e.g. Inayoshi &
Ho 2025). Since our study focuses on the properties of star-forming
galaxies and the escape of Ly𝛼photons, it is crucial to remove these
potential AGNs and LRDs from our sample. First, we cross-match
our catalogue with the Chandra X-ray data from the AEGIS-X Deep
Survey (Laird et al. 2009; Nandra et al. 2015) and, for sources for
which spectroscopic data are available, check for broad H𝛼or H𝛽
components characteristic of broad-line AGNs, such as components
with FWHM ≳1000 km s−1 (e.g. Harikane et al. 2023). As a result,
we find no X-ray counterparts for any of our HAEs, and none of the
spectroscopically confirmed sources show evidence for such broad
components. Next, we identify potential LRDs by applying the colour
and compactness criteria defined by Labbe et al. (2025). We find three
HAEs satisfying these criteria and remove them from the sample.
Notably, one of these sources had already been reported as an LRD
candidate by Kocevski et al. (2025). The complete selection flow for
the HAE sample is summarized in Table 3.

3.1 SED fitting
For the SED fitting of the identified HAEs, we use a 0′′.6-diameter
aperture, which is larger than the aperture used for the source de-
tection, to ensure the recovery of the total flux, as also adopted in
Pirie et al. (2025). However, the larger aperture increases the risk
of contamination from neighbouring sources. To mitigate this is-
sue, we perform the following masked re-measurement. The masks
are generated using the PSF-matched F356W image, which is the
deepest broad-band. For each HAE, we extract a 10′′ × 10′′ cutout,
detect sources with SExtractor, and mask all surrounding sources
except the target before remeasuring the fluxes. We confirm that all
HAEs satisfy the HAE selection criteria even after re-photometry
with masking.
We then derive physical properties by SED fitting to the multi-
band photometry with cigale (Boquien et al. 2019). We use the
latest release, cigale v2025.1. The input photometry comprises 12
bands (HST + JWST), except for NB872.
To perform analyses based on physical parameters (e.g. 𝑀∗, 𝐸(𝐵−
𝑉)) derived from SEDs, the subsample used for fitting is limited
to sources with sufficient photometric constraints. Specifically, we
exclude from the SED-based analysis (but retain in the parent HAE
sample) 17 HAEs that have detections with S/N > 2 in fewer than
six bands in total, or in fewer than two of the UV bands (F090W,
F115W, F150W, and F200W), as performed in Shimizu et al. (2025).
To construct the model SEDs, we employ a delayed exponen-
tial star formation history (SFH), characterized by an e-folding time
(𝜏main) and the age of the onset of star formation. The stellar emis-
sion is modelled using the Bruzual & Charlot (2003) simple stellar
population (SSP) library, assuming a Chabrier (2003) initial mass
function (IMF). We allow the stellar metallicity to vary across four
sub-solar to solar values. To model the dust attenuation, we apply a
modified Calzetti et al. (2000) starburst attenuation law. The colour
excess, 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), is treated as a free parameter, varying from 0.0
to 1.0, and is applied equally to the stellar continuum and nebular
emission. This assumption is commonly adopted for high-𝑧galax-
ies without direct Balmer-decrement constraints and is supported
by recent JWST results suggesting smaller differential reddening at
𝑧≳5 (e.g. Karthikeyan et al. 2026), although it remains subject
to uncertainties related to dust geometry and galaxy properties. For
the redshift input, we adopt spectroscopic redshifts where available;
otherwise, we fix the redshift to 𝑧= 6.174. The complete set of input
parameters and their explored grids are summarized in Table 4. We
note that the stellar and gas metallicities are only weakly constrained
by the available photometric SEDs because of degeneracies with
other parameters. We therefore perform a robustness check in which
both metallicities are fixed to 0.2 𝑍⊙, following Korber et al. (2026).
This value is close to the metallicity for most HAEs in the fiducial
fits. The resulting physical properties, including 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), are nearly
unchanged, and none of our conclusions are affected. We therefore
adopt the fiducial fits with free metallicities in the following analysis.
The SED fitting yields one HAE with a distinctly poor goodness-
of-fit, with 𝜒2 ≃7, while all other HAEs have 𝜒2 < 3. We therefore
remove this outlier from the sample. After excluding this source, the
final HAE sample consists of 84 galaxies, which is used as the parent
HAE sample in the following analyses, as shown in Table 3. Among
them, 63 HAEs have sufficient multi-band detections and acceptable
SED fits, and are used for the analyses of physical parameters based
on the SED fitting (SED fitting sample). Table 5 summarizes the
number of sources in this parent HAE sample as well as in the
subsamples used for the SED-based analyses.

3.2 NB872 photometry
We measure the Ly𝛼flux for each of the 84 identified HAEs in a fixed
1′′.5-diameter aperture on the NB872 image centred at the F470N de-
tection coordinates. This aperture size corresponds to ∼2.5 times the
NB872 PSF FWHM. Although this aperture-to-PSF ratio is smaller
than that adopted for our F470N-based total H𝛼flux measurements,
we choose it to minimize contamination from neighbours while max-
imizing the S/N. The photometric uncertainties are estimated from
the error map produced by hscPipe and rescaled following the same
empirical-noise procedure as in Section 2.4.1. By comparing the
error-map uncertainties with the scatter of randomly placed 1′′.5-
diameter apertures in source-masked background regions, we find
that the error map underestimates the aperture noise by a factor of
1.26. We therefore rescale the NB872 flux uncertainties by this fac-
tor, while retaining the spatial variation of the local noise. Given the
significantly coarser resolution of NB872 compared to HST/JWST
images, contamination from nearby sources can significantly affect
the aperture photometry, and we therefore apply the following mask-
ing procedure. First, we construct a reference image by convolving the
high-resolution JWST F115W image to match the NB872 PSF and
resampling it to the HSC pixel scale (0′′.168) using the reproject
package (Robitaille et al. 2020). Using this PSF-matched reference,
we identify pixels within the photometric aperture whose flux is
dominated by neighbouring sources and mask them on the NB872
image. We classify an HAE as unmeasurable when the PSF-matched
F115W emission from a neighbouring source reaches the central re-
gion of the HAE (𝑟< 2 NB872 pixels from the F470N centroid,
corresponding to about half of the NB872 PSF FWHM). In these
cases, the expected central Ly𝛼-emitting region is contaminated, and
reliable NB872 photometry cannot be obtained even after masking.
These HAEs are therefore excluded from the NB872-based analyses.
We confirm that this exclusion does not introduce a systematic bias
in the HAE sample, as HAEs with and without reliable NB872 pho-
tometry have similar distributions of galaxy properties such as 𝑀∗,
SFR, and 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉).
Consequently, we obtain reliable NB872 photometry for 56 out
of the 84 HAEs. Among these, Ly𝛼is detected for 19 sources at
S/NNB872 > 2. For the remaining sources, we adopt 2𝜎upper limits
in the subsequent analysis. When we restrict to the subsample used
for SED-based analyses (Section 3.1), we have reliable NB872 pho-
tometry for 40 sources, of which 15 are detected at S/NNB872 > 2.

4.2.4 Implications for LyC escape and the ionizing photon budget
The weak variation of 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc,med with the adopted lower H𝛼luminosity
limit may have implications for Lyman-continuum (LyC) leakage.
This is because both Ly𝛼and LyC escape are sensitive to the distri-
bution and covering fraction of H i, and low-column-density channels
in the ISM and CGM are expected to facilitate the escape of ionizing
photons as well as Ly𝛼photons (e.g. Gazagnes et al. 2020; Kimm
et al. 2022). In addition, both observations and simulations suggest
a good correlation between 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
and LyC escape fraction ( 𝑓LyC
esc )
(e.g. Begley et al. 2024; Choustikov et al. 2024). However, this com-
parison should be interpreted with caution. Ly𝛼photons can escape
after being shifted out of resonance by gas kinematics, whereas LyC
photons require genuinely optically thin H i sightlines, and as a re-
sult, Ly𝛼escape may not always fully trace LyC escape. Another
important caveat is that direct LyC measurements determine only
the line-of-sight escape fraction, while the quantity relevant for the
ionizing photon budget is the angle- and time-averaged escape of
LyC photons. Radiation-hydrodynamic simulations show that LyC
escape can be highly stochastic and anisotropic, regulated by stellar
feedback, the opening and closing of low-column-density channels,
and viewing-angle effects (e.g. Wise & Cen 2009; Trebitsch et al.
2017; Rosdahl et al. 2022). Therefore, even if no clear luminosity
dependence is observed in line-of-sight LyC escape measurements,
the intrinsic 𝑓LyC
esc relevant for the comoving ionizing emissivity (¤𝑛ion)
that reaches the IGM could depend on luminosity.
Observationally, the luminosity dependence of 𝑓LyC
esc
is still un-
der debate, and most constraints have been discussed in terms of
UV luminosity. For example, Papovich et al. (2026) inferred the
𝑓LyC
esc
from fitting nebular emission lines and stellar continua mea-
sured from JWST spectroscopy, and found no clear dependence on
UV luminosity. While some direct LyC observations also report no
clear luminosity dependence (e.g. Marchi et al. 2017; Bian & Fan
2020), others have found a significant dependence with higher es-
cape fractions in fainter galaxies (e.g. Steidel et al. 2018; Begley
et al. 2022). Simmonds et al. (2024) showed that models in which
𝑓LyC
esc
increases toward fainter UV magnitudes enhance the relative
contribution of faint UV galaxies to the ¤𝑛ion, compared to models
with a fixed 𝑓LyC
esc across UV magnitudes. Although this comparison
is not directly equivalent to our H𝛼-based result, it illustrates that the
assumed luminosity dependence of 𝑓LyC
esc
can strongly affect which
galaxy population dominates the ionizing photon budget during the
EoR.

We investigate the origin of the galaxy-to-galaxy variation in
𝑓Ly𝛼
esc using individual measurements for the subsample of 40 HAEs
with both reliable NB872 photometry and robust SED constraints, as
summarized in Table 5. Figure 12 presents the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
as a function of
key physical properties: EW0(Ly𝛼), UV slope 𝛽, 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), 𝑅𝑒,UV,
𝑅𝑒,opt, and 𝑀∗. To quantify correlations, we compute the Kendall rank
correlation coefficient, 𝜏, and assess significance using two-sided 𝑝-
values. The 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
and EW0(Ly𝛼) of 40 HAEs include upper limits
for Ly𝛼-undetected HAEs. We therefore treat Ly𝛼-related quantities
as left-censored data and apply the generalized (censored) Kendall 𝜏

involve non-detections and upper limits, making statistical methods
that handle censored data essential (e.g. Lin et al. 2024; Mascia
et al. 2025). Accordingly, we adopt the censored Kendall approach
to incorporate upper limits in our correlation analysis.
Each panel of Figure 12 shows Kendall’s rank correlation coeffi-
cient 𝜏and the 𝑝-value measured using detections only (uncensored),
as well as 𝜏and the 𝑝-value measured using censored data, respec-

galaxies with stronger Ly𝛼emission show more efficient Ly𝛼es-
cape. Multiple studies also report this trend (e.g. Sobral & Matthee
2019; Begley et al. 2024; Napolitano et al. 2024). The UV slope
𝛽also shows a statistically significant negative correlation with the
𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
for both the detections-only case and the censored-data case.
This correlation, where bluer 𝛽corresponds to a larger 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
has
also been reported in the previous studies (Matthee et al. 2016; Lin
et al. 2024; Napolitano et al. 2024; Ning et al. 2026), suggesting
that lower dust attenuation makes Ly𝛼escape easier. However, for
𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), no statistically significant correlation with the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
ap-
pears. The key point here is that 𝛽and 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉) do not necessarily
represent the same quantities. The UV slope 𝛽is weighted toward
the least obscured UV-bright star-forming components that are vis-
ible to the observer, whereas the SED-derived 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉) inferred
from integrated photometry represents a luminosity-weighted aver-
age attenuation across the galaxy. In vigorously star-forming galax-
ies at high-𝑧, complex star-dust geometries can lead to scenarios
where, despite a high overall dust content, optically thin sightlines
remain, resulting in a blue 𝛽as demonstrated by radiative transfer
simulations (Popping et al. 2017; Narayanan et al. 2018). Ly𝛼is a
resonant scattering line, making it more susceptible to dust atten-
uation than the continuum due to the increased path length from
scattering (e.g. Verhamme et al. 2006). Therefore, our results sug-
gest that Ly𝛼escape is more strongly linked to the properties of the
low-attenuation component reaching the observer (as traced by 𝛽)
rather than the galaxy-averaged attenuation (𝐸(𝐵−𝑉)). The physical
picture in which local low-attenuation regions regulate Ly𝛼escape
is also supported by recent spatially resolved JWST observations of
a gravitationally lensed LAE at 𝑧= 6.57, where Ly𝛼emission is
found to emerge predominantly from a young, dust-cleared clump
embedded in a multiphase ISM (Markov et al. 2026). Such results
suggest that local feedback and small-scale dust/gas geometry can
regulate Ly𝛼escape even when the galaxy as a whole is moderately
dusty. We note that the lack of a significant correlation with 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉)
could also be influenced by various factors, such as uncertainties in
the SED-derived 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), the limited sample size, and the metal-
licity dependence of the dust content. Although the metallicities of
individual HAEs in our sample are not tightly constrained by our
SED fitting, high-𝑧low-mass galaxies are typically metal-poor, with
recent JWST/NIRSpec studies suggesting 12 + log(O/H) ∼7.7–
8.0 for galaxies with 𝑀∗∼108–109 𝑀⊙(e.g. Nakajima et al. 2023;
Curti et al. 2024). At such low metallicities, the dust-to-gas ratio is
expected to decrease rapidly, as indicated by the steepening of the
dust-to-gas–metallicity relation below 12 + log(O/H) ∼8.0 (Rémy-
Ruyer et al. 2014; De Vis et al. 2019). In this regime, the dynamic
range of the SED-derived 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉) may narrow, potentially reduc-
ing the reliability of the constraints. These effects, together with the
limited sample size, may dilute an intrinsic dependence of 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
on
𝐸(𝐵−𝑉).

CGM/IGM transmission (e.g. Gurung-López et al. 2025) will be
needed to separate these effects from internal mechanisms regulating
Ly𝛼escape.

should be noted that since we do not have photometry on the longer
wavelength side than H𝛼, the uncertainties in the age estimate may

5 SUMMARY
We directly measure the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc of HAEs at 𝑧≃6.2 in the CEERS field,
using a unique pair of NB filters: JWST/NIRCam F470N (for H𝛼)
and Subaru/HSC NB872 (for Ly𝛼). Through stacking analysis, we
first measure the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc,med and examine its dependence on the adopted
lower H𝛼luminosity limit. We then use individual 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
measure-
ments to investigate the physical conditions that regulate galaxy-to-
galaxy variations in Ly𝛼escape. Our main results are summarized
as follows:
(i) A final sample of 84 HAEs at 𝑧≃6.2 is identified from
F470N excess and photo-𝑧selections, after excluding three LRD
candidates and one source with a poor SED fit. Among these HAEs,
56 have reliable NB872 photometry without severe contamination
from nearby sources, and 19 of them show Ly𝛼detections at > 2𝜎.
(ii) The HAEs are widely distributed on the star-forming MS and
the SB region. In particular, HAEs with large EW0(Ly𝛼) tend to show
higher SFR than the MS, which suggests vigorous star formation
activity. Although a positive correlation is observed between size
and mass, no clear difference in size depending on EW0(Ly𝛼) is
evident.
(iii) Using stacking analysis, the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc,med at 𝑧≃6.2 is measured as
0.090+0.063
−0.032, and 0.106+0.066
−0.044 when weighted to correct for detection
incompleteness. These measurements are consistent with previous
results at 𝑧∼6 based on LFs and spectroscopic stacking, and are
higher than analogous NB measurements at lower redshift, support-
ing an increase in the cosmic-averaged 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
toward the EoR.
(iv) We find no significant dependence of the stacked 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
on
the adopted lower H𝛼luminosity limit within the luminosity range
probed by our sample. If Ly𝛼and LyC photons escape through similar
low-density channels, this weak luminosity dependence may suggest
that 𝑓LyC
esc is not strongly dependent on H𝛼luminosity over the range
probed here. In that case, the ionizing photon budget could receive a
substantial contribution from relatively luminous galaxies closer to
the current observational limits.
(v) Using individual measurements of 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc , including upper lim-
its for Ly𝛼non-detections, the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
is found to show a positive corre-
lation with EW0(Ly𝛼) and a negative correlation with the UV slope
𝛽. A negative correlation with the 𝑅e,UV is also observed, whereas
no significant correlation appears with the 𝑅e,opt or the 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉)
derived from SED fitting. These results suggest that the escape of
Ly𝛼photons is more strongly linked to the properties of the ob-
servable low-attenuation UV-emitting regions, which correspond to
compact star-forming regions, than to the global properties of the
entire galaxy.
(vi) HAEs with higher ΣSFR or ΣsSFR tend to exhibit higher 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc ,
although these are not statistically significant. This result is consis-
tent with the physical interpretation that outflows associated with
concentrated star formation in compact regions reduce the covering
fraction of the surrounding H i and create low-density channels acting
as escape paths for Ly𝛼photons.
In this study, we apply the dual-NB stacking method to the EoR
for the first time, demonstrating that it can still provide a direct mea-
surement of the 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc,med for the H𝛼-selected galaxies with low model
dependence. Future deeper or wider NB observations will be impor-
tant for testing more robustly the H𝛼-luminosity dependence of the
cosmic-averaged 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
over a wider luminosity range. They would
also enable higher-S/N stacked Ly𝛼and H𝛼surface-brightness pro-
files, allowing us to compare the spatial distribution of resonantly
scattered Ly𝛼emission with that of recombination emission associ-
ated with recent star formation. Such measurements would provide
a direct way to separate the effects of galaxy-internal star formation
and circumgalactic scattering, thereby offering deeper insights into
the origin of Ly𝛼haloes and the mechanisms of Ly𝛼escape (e.g.
Leclercq et al. 2017; Kusakabe et al. 2022).
The observed galaxy-to-galaxy variation in 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
suggests that
Ly𝛼escape may be closely linked to the properties of compact, low-
attenuation UV-emitting regions. However, larger samples will be
needed to test these trends more robustly and to separate the effects
of galaxy-internal processes from stochastic line-of-sight variations
in IGM transmission. Such sample will be essential for statistically
averaging over IGM attenuation and for identifying the galaxy-scale
mechanisms that regulate Ly𝛼escape. Moreover, the escape of Ly𝛼
photons is unlikely to be controlled by a single physical quantity, but
rather by a combination of UV slope 𝛽, size, 𝑀∗, 𝐸(𝐵−𝑉), ΣSFR,
ΣsSFR, gas geometry, and gas kinematics such as outflows. Applying
multivariate or interpretable machine-learning approaches, similar
to those used by Yoshioka et al. (2025) to predict Ly𝛼emission from
multiple galaxy properties, to larger samples with direct 𝑓Ly𝛼
esc
mea-
surements would provide a promising route to quantify the relative
