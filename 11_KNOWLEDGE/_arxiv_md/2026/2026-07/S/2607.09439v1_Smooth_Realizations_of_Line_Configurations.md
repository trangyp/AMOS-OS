SMOOTH REALIZATIONS OF LINE CONFIGURATIONS
PAOLO ACETO, DUNCAN MCCOY, AND JUNGHWAN PARK
Abstract. We study the problem of realizing line configurations as collections of 2-spheres smoothly
embedded in the complex projective plane. Building upon prior work by Ruberman and Starkston
on topological realizations, we establish a stronger obstruction in the smooth category. Our proof
relies on lattice-theoretic arguments based on Donaldson’s diagonalization theorem.
1. Introduction
The study of line arrangements and configurations is a classical topic in geometry and combi-
natorics (see, e.g., [BS05, BS13, ABFGB17, RS19, Gol24, KSTG24, Urz22, AG26] for some recent
developments and open problems). A combinatorial line arrangement is a pair of finite sets containing
lines and points and equipped with an incidence relation such that any two distinct lines are incident
to a unique common point. A combinatorial (nk)-configuration, or simply an (nk)-configuration, is
a combinatorial line arrangement consisting of n lines and a distinguished subset K of n points such
that each point of K lies on exactly k lines, and each line contains exactly k points of K. To avoid
trivialities, we always assume k ≥3. By counting incidences on one fixed line, it is easy to see that
any (nk)-configuration must satisfy
(1.1)
n ≥k2 −k + 1.
Although an (nk)-configuration need not exist for every value of n satisfying this inequality, there are
infinitely many examples with n = k2 −k + 1. Such a configuration is called a finite projective plane.
For instance, if q is a prime power, the projective plane P2(Fq) gives an example with k = q + 1 and
n = q2 + q + 1. It is known that not all finite projective planes arise as projective spaces in this
way [Bat97].
A fundamental question in algebraic and incidence geometry is which (nk)-configurations can be
realized by lines in RP2 or CP2; see [Gr¨u09] for the history and state of the art of this question,
as well as related problems. In this article, we focus on CP2. We say that an (nk)-configuration is
geometrically C-realizable if it can be realized by an arrangement of complex projective lines in CP2
with the prescribed incidence relation.
Furthermore, one can change the category and consider realizations in CP2 by locally flat em-
bedded 2-spheres. A topological C-realization is such a realization with the lines replaced by locally
flat embedded 2-spheres in CP2 representing the line class. This problem was recently pursued by
Ruberman and Starkston [RS19], who used the Atiyah–Singer G-signature theorem [AS68, Gor86]
to prove that finite projective planes of the form P2(Fq) are not topologically C-realizable.
Moreover, they showed that there is no topological C-realization of the unique (144)-configuration.
This is the first case of interest beyond finite projective planes not accounted for by the geometrically
realizable (83)-configuration. More recently, the first author and Golla [AG26] improved these results
by proving that if an (nk)-configuration is topologically C-realizable, then n ≥k2 −5. In this article,
we improve this bound in the smooth category. Following [RS19], we call such realizations smooth
C-realizations; see Section 2 for precise definitions.

2. Line arrangements and configurations
First, we recall the relevant definitions (see also [RS19]).
Definition 2.1. A combinatorial line arrangement A is a pair of finite sets L and P, consisting of
lines and points, respectively, equipped with a function i: L × P →{0, 1}, the incidence relation,
such that for every pair of distinct lines L, L′ ∈L there exists a unique point P ∈P such that
i(L, P) = i(L′, P) = 1.
We say that a line L contains a point P or that P lies on L if i(L, P) = 1.
Definition 2.2. A smooth C-realization of a combinatorial line arrangement A = (L, P) is a collec-
tion of |L| smoothly embedded 2-spheres in CP2 such that:
• each sphere represents the homology class of a complex projective line;
• all intersections are positive, transverse, and prescribed by the incidence relation of A.
Similarly, a topological C-realization is defined by simply replacing smooth embeddings with locally
flat topological embeddings.
Definition 2.3. An (nk)-configuration is a combinatorial line arrangement A = (L, P) where |L| = n
and P contains a subset K of n points such that each point in K lies on exactly k lines and each line
contains exactly k points from K.
As mentioned in the introduction, every (nk)-configuration satisfies
n ≥k2 −k + 1.
Given a fixed choice of line L in the configuration, each of the k points from K on L provides an
intersection with k −1 other lines in the configuration. Since L intersects every other line in a unique
point, this gives at least k2 −k lines intersecting L, and hence the inequality. For future reference,
we state the topological bound already mentioned in the introduction.
Theorem 2.4 ([AG26]). If there exists a topological C-realization of an (nk)-configuration with
k ≥4, then
n ≥k2 −5.
In particular, the same inequality holds for any (nk)-configuration admitting a smooth C-realization.
