SHAPE ANALYSIS IN SCHAUDER SPACES OF THE ENERGY OF HEAT
PROBLEMS IN PERTURBED ANNULAR DOMAINS
LUCA DI PERSIO AND RICCARDO MOLINAROLO
Abstract: This paper is devoted to the shape analysis of the energy of boundary value problems
for the heat equation in a bounded perforated domain Ωo \Ωi[ϕ] of Rn, where the outer boundary
is fixed, and the inner boundary is given by a C1,α-perturbation ϕ of the boundary of a reference
cavity. Under standard Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, we prove that, in a suitable
neighborhood of the identity ϕ0, the domain-to-energy map is of class C∞. The proof is based on
the construction of a global diffeomorphism, smoothly depending on ϕ, from the reference annulus
onto the perturbed one, on a decomposition of the fixed domain into regions near, intermediate
to, and far from the cavity, and on the smooth dependence of the layer heat potentials upon
support perturbations.
Keywords: Heat equation, Shape perturbation, Layer heat potentials, Shape sensitivity of the
solution, Shape analysis of the energy.

1. Introduction
The present work lies at the intersection of parabolic potential theory, shape sensitivity analysis,
and boundary integral methods for time-dependent problems. For the general theory of parabolic
equations and classical Schauder regularity, we refer the reader to the classical monographs by
Ladyženskaja, Solonnikov, and Ural’tseva [31], Friedman [21], Lieberman [38], and Lunardi [39].
The paper aims to study the dependence of the energy of solutions of linear boundary value
problems for the heat equation in bounded perforated domains upon perturbation of the internal
boundary.
Generally speaking, understanding how certain quantities depend on the shape of the domain
is a fundamental quest with significant relevance in many real-world scenarios, especially when

established field of Shape Optimization, for which we refer the reader to the classical monographs
by Henrot and Pierre [25] and by Sokołowski and Zolesio [47], along with the references therein.
From a mathematical perspective, the analysis of the shape optimization problem is often
closely related to studying how the solutions - or other related quantities - to some boundary value
problems are affected by the perturbation of the domain of definition. In particular, this requires
the understanding of the regularity of the map that associates the shape of the domain to the
solution, or to other related quantities, of the problems. In fact, regarding the solution, it is well
known that the continuity of this map implies that small variations in the perturbation parameter
correspond to small changes in the solution; the differentiability allows for the characterization
of an optimal configuration, while smoothness allows approximation with a Taylor expansion to
any desired degree of accuracy.
Many authors have used potential theoretic methods to study the perturbation sensitivity
of solutions of boundary value problems, exploiting integral operators to transform the original
problem into a system of boundary integral equations. We mention the classical monograph on
integral equations by Kress [30], as well as the systematic analysis of boundary integral operators
for the heat equation by Brown [3, 4] and by Costabel [8, 9]. In particular, analyzing how layer
potentials and, more generally, integral operators depend on perturbation parameters (such as
densities or the support of integration) becomes a crucial first step.
This strategy has been performed for the sensitivity analysis of the solutions of boundary
value problems, especially in the context of elliptic equations.
We mention Potthast [45, 46]
for the study of layer potential for the Helmholtz equation, Haddar and Kress [23], Kirsch [29],
Charalabopoulos [7], Costabel and Le Louer [10, 11] for applications to scattering theory, fluid
dynamics, and elasticity theory.
Few results prove regularity beyond differentiability. Notably, exceptions are the works of Lanza
de Cristoforis and collaborators. The method developed was called Functional Analytic Approach,
and it heavily relies on potential-theoretic techniques: for a detailed description of the method,
see the monograph [13] and reference therein. This method has been used for both regular and
singular perturbations and, generally speaking, it aims to prove smooth or even analytical results
for the layer potential and for the domain-to-solution map. It has been extensively adopted: far
from being exhaustive, we refer to the seminal papers [32, 36, 37] for the smoothness of classical
layer potentials, and the more general case of elliptic differential operators studied in [12].
For the sake of completeness, we mention that, apart from the results stemming from Lanza
de Cristoforis’s work, it is possible to obtain analyticity results through Shape Holomorphy. We
refer to Henríquez and Schwab [24] for the study of the Calderón projector, to Pinto, Henríquez,
and Jerez-Hanckes [44] and Dölz and Henríquez [20] for boundary integral operators, and to
Jerez-Hanckes, Schwab, and Zech [28] for the electromagnetic wave scattering problem.
We point out that, generally speaking, the results available in the literature on the shape
sensitivity of solutions concern mainly elliptic problems; fewer results are known for the parabolic
ones. There are, of course, some exceptions: we mention Bacchelli, Di Cristo, Sincich and Vessella
[1], Chapko, Kress, and Yoon [5, 6], Hettlich and Rungell [26], and Nakamura and Wang [42, 43],
in the context of inverse problem (see also the monograph by Isakov [27] on the topic). We also
refer to Biegert and Warma [2] for generalized nonlinear Robin boundary conditions.
The closest antecedents of the present paper are the recent works [16, 17], where the shape
analysis of mixed nonlinear Robin boundary value problem for the heat equation and the Dirichlet-
to-Neumann map were analyzed in perturbed perforated domains, and [40, 15], where analogous
ideas were developed for periodic transmission problems. All the mentioned results heavily rely
on the smoothness of pullback layer heat potentials developed by Luzzini and Dalla Riva [14] (see
also [33, 34]).
The novelty of the present manuscript is not merely to revisit the existence and smoothness
results in [17], providing an alternative representation for caloric functions in an annular domain
by means of the sum of double layer potential: these techniques are well-known. Furthermore,

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we fix some notations, we recall the definition of
parabolic Schauder spaces and the properties of single and double layer heat potentials that will
be used later, including the smooth dependence of pullback layers heat potential on perturbations
of the support. Section 3 is devoted to the study of the Dirichlet problem. In Subsection 3.1 we
provide a double layer representation formula for caloric functions in annular domains (the proof
of this result is postponed to Appendix A). In Subsections 3.2 & 3.3, we implement the Functional

the domain-to-solution map. Subsection 3.4 contains the shape analysis of the Dirichlet energy,
including the construction of the global diffeomorphism, and the proof of smooth dependence
of the energy map via the decomposition of the pullback energy. Section 4 is devoted to the
Neumann problem. There, we recall the representation based on single layer heat potentials, and
the corresponding existence and smoothness results. We finally prove the analogous result of
smooth dependence of the corresponding energy map.
