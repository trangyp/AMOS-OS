Staggered Potential and Elliptical Light Driven Topological Phase Transitions
in α-T3 Lattice
Muhammad Faisal, Muhammad Irfan Sarwar∗
Department of Physics, Quaid-I-Azam University, Islamabad 45320, Pakistan
Abstract
We theoretically investigate the influence of hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) on the electronic properties of
an α-T3 lattice driven by an off-resonant elliptically polarized light field. The staggered potential M breaks
the sublattice inversion symmetry, transforming the initial semimetal into a trivial insulator with Chern
number C = 0. We identify a fundamental geometric singularity at α = 1/
√
2, independent of M, below
which no finite drive can close the lower band gap, creating a stable topological window where conduction–
flat band inversion yields a Chern insulator with C = 1. Above this critical value the lower gap closes
with finite intensity, allowing a transition from C = 1 to C = 2 as the dice limit (α = 1) is approached.
The topological phases are characterized by quantized anomalous Hall plateaus at σxy = e2/h (C = 1) and
σxy = 2e2/h (C = 2), with each valley contributing exactly 1
2e2/h. The C = 1 plateau remains robust from
0 K to 300 K, while C = 2 requires T < 100 K due to its narrower gap. A highly asymmetric thermoelectric
Seebeck response further serves as an experimental fingerprint of each phase, providing a realistic framework
for realizing stable high-Chern-number phases in substrate-supported α-T3 materials.
Keywords:
Floquet topological insulators, α-T3 lattice, Staggered potential, Elliptical polarization, Berry
curvature, Anomalous Hall conductivity, Thermoelectric transport

Topological phases can be induced in a material by

Floquet Topological Insulators (FTIs) [3, 16] offer
a significant advantage over static TIs: their topo-

the α-T3 lattice, which interpolates continuously

The α-T3

to topological phases [22, 26, 27].

A fundamental experimental challenge in the imple-
mentation of Floquet theory [28] is the unavoidable
influence of the substrate on the 2D material [29].
The standard approach to improving carrier mobil-
ity in 2D materials is to place them on a hexag-
onal boron nitride (h-BN) substrate [29–31]. The
substrate interaction and twistronics-induced Moiré
patterns [32, 33] create a static crystal potential
that breaks sublattice symmetry, opening a trivial
(C = 0) band gap [34]. This static mass M induced
by the substrate then interacts with the dynamic
Floquet mass ∆F generated by the applied polar-
ized laser field [35].
While the effects of circular
polarization on the massless α-T3 lattice have been
studied [36], the rich interplay between a substrate-
induced static mass and a Floquet drive, including
its thermoelectric signatures, remains largely unex-
plored.
In this work we address this gap by mapping the
complete topological phase diagram of the massive
α-T3 lattice under elliptical Floquet driving.
We
study the realistic case in which the α-T3 lattice
is subject to both a staggered static potential [37]
and an off-resonant elliptical laser field. We show
that the topological properties of the lattice are
not destroyed by the substrate; rather, there ex-
ists a critical phase boundary where the dynamic
Floquet interaction overcomes the static mass, in-
verting the band gap and restoring a Chern insu-
lator.

the lattice undergoes a transition from C = 1 to
C = 2 at α = 1/

10. Conclusion
We investigated the topological phase transitions
of the α-T3 lattice under the combined influence of
an h-BN substrate and off-resonant elliptical Flo-
quet driving.
With a staggered potential M =
0.05τ, we identified a unique topological protec-
tion mechanism that fundamentally distinguishes
the substrate-supported system from the pristine
lattice. Our main findings are as follows.
First, we identified a fundamental geometric singu-
larity at α = 1/
√
2, independent of M. This point
marks the critical boundary below which the lower
band gap (between the flat and valence bands) can-
not be closed by any finite drive intensity.
Our
analytical derivation of ∆upper
F,crit and ∆lower
F,crit reveals
that the denominator of the lower-gap threshold be-
comes non-positive for α ≤1/
√
2, effectively push-
13


## Page 14


ing the valence band further from the flat band as
the light intensity increases.
Second, as α →1 the system symmetry is restored
and the two critical intensities converge, enabling
simultaneous double-gap closure, resulting in the
C = 2 phase.
Third, the ellipticity provides a convenient exper-
imental knob [41]. Since the Floquet mass scales
as ∆F ∝2η/(1 + η2), tuning the polarization η at
fixed laser power moves the system vertically across
the phase diagram, allowing the C = 0, C = 1, and
C = 2 phases to be accessed without changing the
beam intensity.
The predicted topological phases can be detected
experimentally through transport measurements.
The Hall conductivity shows quantized steps at
σxy = e2/h for C = 1 and σxy = 2e2/h for
C = 2, where each valley contributes equally with
1
2e2/h [49, 53]. The two phases differ noticeably in
thermal robustness. The C = 1 plateau is protected
by the large gap gap1 ≈0.213τ, remains sharp up
to room temperature, whereas the C = 2 plateau,
protected only by the smaller gap gap2 ≈0.087τ,
requires lower temperatures (T ≲100 K) for clear
observation. The Seebeck coefficient shows a sin-
gle sharp peak in the protected regime (α = 0.5)
and two symmetric peaks in the high-Chern regime
(α = 0.9), serving as a direct experimental signa-
ture of the underlying topological phase.
Finally, our results provide a necessary correction
to prior massless models [36]. The introduction of
a staggered potential M completely rewrites the
phase diagram. The initial state is a trivial insu-
lator (C = 0); the transition to C = 1 proceeds
through a controlled band-crossing involving only
the upper gap; and the transition to C = 2 requires
both finite α > 1/
√
2 and sufficient drive intensity.
This provides a more realistic framework for ex-
perimentalists seeking to realize stable high-Chern-
number phases in h-BN-supported α-T3 materials.
