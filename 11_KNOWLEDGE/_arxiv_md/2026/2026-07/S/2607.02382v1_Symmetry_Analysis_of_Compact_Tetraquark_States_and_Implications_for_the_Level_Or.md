Symmetry analysis of compact tetraquark states and
implications for the level ordering of the fully charmed
candidates X(6600), X(6900), and X(7100)
Shuai Yin1, Ti Gong1*, and Jingya Zhu1†
1 School of Physics and Electronics, Henan University, Kaifeng 475004, China
July 3, 2026
Abstract
Motivated by recent experimental observations, we investigate the JP distribution of low-
energy compact tetraquark states. Assuming that two quarks and two antiquarks are arranged in
either a tetrahedral or a square conﬁguration, we employ the restricted representations of the per-
mutation group S4 onto S2 × S2 to derive the inherent nodal structure of the qq¯q¯q system from
that of the qqqq system for orbital angular momentum L ≤3. Based on this framework, we
determine the distribution of accessible JP states and ﬁnd that low-energy compact tetraquark
states are likely to favor JP = 2+. Our analysis yields two observations that further support the
dynamical robustness of symmetry-based classiﬁcations in exotic hadron spectroscopy. First, the
symmetry-induced JP distribution of compact tetraquark states closely resembles that obtained
for the three-ﬂavor four-quark system. Second, the location of the distribution peak remains un-
changed when chromomagnetic interaction (CMI) effects are incorporated. Together, these results
suggest that the dominant features of the low-lying spectrum are governed primarily by symmetry
constraints rather than by the details of the underlying dynamics. These ﬁndings further imply that
the fully charmed tetraquark candidates X(6600), X(6900), and X(7100) may occupy relatively
low-lying levels of the fully charmed tetraquark spectrum. They also indicate that mechanisms
beyond CMI dynamics are likely involved, potentially mitigating or competing with the effects of
CMI in compact fully charmed tetraquark states.

Introduction
Conventional quark models have achieved remarkable success in describing the spectra of mesons
(q¯q) and baryons (qqq or ¯q¯q¯q) [1–6]. Since the discovery of the X(3872) in 2003 [7], however, a large
number of exotic resonances that cannot be readily accommodated within the conventional meson–
baryon framework have been observed. Representative examples include the states ﬁrst reported in
references [8–18]. The emergence of these exotic hadrons has posed a major challenge to our under-
standing of strong-interaction dynamics and has stimulated extensive theoretical efforts to elucidate
their internal structures and underlying formation mechanisms. Consequently, establishing a theoret-
ical framework capable of characterizing the structural properties of these states has become a central
objective in contemporary hadron spectroscopy (for reviews, see references [19–38]).
Despite more than half a century of theoretical development since the prediction of multiquark
states [39, 40], no consensus has yet emerged regarding the fundamental properties of most exotic
hadrons observed over the past two decades, including their bound conﬁgurations, ﬂavor composi-
tions, spin assignments, and parities. Recently, the CMS Collaboration reported three highly signiﬁ-
cant resonance structures near 6.6, 6.9, and 7.1 GeV in the J/ψJ/ψ invariant-mass spectrum [41], cor-
responding to the states X(6600), X(6900), and X(7100), respectively. These resonances have been
widely regarded as prominent candidates for fully charmed tetraquark states [42, 43]. The existence
of X(6900) has also been independently conﬁrmed by the LHCb and ATLAS Collaborations [17, 44].
More recently, the CMS Collaboration determined the quantum numbers of X(6600), X(6900), and
X(7100) to be JP C = 2++ [45]. This result excludes spin assignments of J = 0 and J = 1 and
places stringent constraints on their possible internal structures.
Theoretical studies of fully charmed tetraquark states span a broad range of approaches and in-
terpretations [46–125]. Nevertheless, some key assumptions adopted in a number of these studies are
now incompatible with the experimental determination of JP C = 2++. As the ﬁrst conﬁrmed exotic
hadrons with spin J = 2 and the leading candidates for fully charmed tetraquark states, X(6600),

X(6900), and X(7100) occupy a unique position in hadron spectroscopy. They also represent some
of the most compelling candidates for compact multiquark states identiﬁed to date.
Because perturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD) becomes inapplicable in the low-energy
regime, the investigation of hadronic structure necessarily relies on non-perturbative methods. These
include ﬁrst-principles lattice QCD (LQCD) calculations [126–154], as well as a range of dynam-
ical phenomenological approaches, including diquark models [155–158], chiral effective ﬁeld the-
ory [159–162], QCD sum rules [163–165], hadronic molecular models [166–174], etc., and symmetry-
based analyses [175]. These frameworks constitute the main theoretical tools for the study of exotic
hadrons.
LQCD provides a systematically improvable ab initio formulation of QCD, but its practical im-
plementation is often computationally demanding [176, 177]. By contrast, phenomenological ap-
proaches typically rely on speciﬁc dynamical assumptions and model-dependent parameterizations,
which may limit their predictive power and universality [37].
Symmetry-based analyses offer a
complementary perspective: by circumventing detailed dynamical modeling and instead employing
group-theoretical methods applied directly to hadronic wave functions, they allow for the determi-
nation of allowed quantum numbers and, in certain cases, qualitative constraints on the ordering of
energy levels. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the relative positioning of LQCD, phenomenolog-
ical approaches, and symmetry-based methods within the “impossible trinity” of quantitativeness,
generality, and computational efﬁciency.
In 1977, three seminal papers extended the MIT bag model and the chromomagnetic interaction
(CMI) framework [178–180], thereby establishing a foundational setting for symmetry-based anal-
yses of compact multiquark systems in which spin, ﬂavor, and color degrees of freedom are treated
on an equal footing. Subsequently, from the 1990s to the 2000s, a systematic symmetry-analysis
program was developed in the study of low-energy spectra of atomic, nucleon, and dibaryon sys-
tems [181–189]. In these works, the symmetry properties of speciﬁc spatial conﬁgurations under
the combined actions of the rotation group O(3) and the permutation group SN were analyzed to
determine the accessible quantum numbers Lπλ, where L denotes the orbital angular momentum, π
the orbital parity, and λ the irreducible representation (IRREP) of SN. This classiﬁcation provides a
natural ordering scheme for low-lying states.

Solution to spin-statistics and color-singlet constraint
Compact QCD bound states include mesons, baryons, compact multiquark states, glueballs, hy-
brid hadrons, and related conﬁgurations. Such states may contain multiple identical constituents. The
dynamical degrees of freedom of quarks and antiquarks inside hadrons can be characterized by four
components: orbital, spin, ﬂavor, and color degrees of freedom. When electromagnetic interactions
are neglected and ﬂavor-dependent effects are ignored, quarks and antiquarks may be effectively
treated as two distinct species of identical particles. In contrast, since gluons do not carry ﬂavor
quantum numbers, their internal degrees of freedom are independent of ﬂavor structure, and they can
therefore be treated straightforwardly as identical particles within hadronic systems.
The wave function of a compact QCD bound state is subject to two fundamental constraints.
First, from the perspective of the spin–statistics theorem, quarks and antiquarks are fermions, whereas
gluons are bosons. Consequently, the total wave function, including all degrees of freedom, must be
antisymmetric under the exchange of any two quarks or any two antiquarks, and symmetric under the
exchange of any two gluons. This requirement is referred to as the spin–statistics constraint. Second,
due to QCD color conﬁnement, physical hadronic states must be color neutral. This implies that the
color wave function must transform as a trivial (singlet) representation of SU(3)C, i.e., the hadron
must be a color singlet. This condition is referred to as the color-singlet constraint. Both constraints
are model-independent and follow directly from fundamental principles of quantum ﬁeld theory and
QCD.

INS structure of orbital wave functions
A nodal surface is deﬁned as a hypersurface in conﬁguration space on which the orbital wave
function vanishes. When such a nodal surface is determined solely by the geometric symmetries of
the underlying conﬁguration and is independent of the dynamical details of the system, it is referred
to as an INS. Within the INS framework, accessible quantum states can be determined without re-
course to speciﬁc dynamical models, by solving a system of linear equations associated with invariant
subgroups. The general procedure for constructing the INS structure of identical-particle few-body
systems has been developed in references [181–189], and will not be repeated here. By combining the
resulting INS constraints with the two fundamental conditions introduced above, the spatial structure
of low-energy compact multiquark states can be systematically analyzed.

Summary and remarks
We ﬁnd that, at low energies, compact tetraquark states are predominantly composed of higher-
angular-momentum components with JP = 2+, 2−, and 3−, while the contributions from low-
angular-momentum components such as 0+ and 0−are comparatively suppressed. Upon inclusion
of CMI effects, the contribution of the 0+ component increases noticeably; however, the 2+ and 3−
components remain dominant in the overall state distribution. These results indicate that, within the
symmetry-determined structure governed in particular by the INS geometry, CMI effects play a sub-

Combining these observations with related theoretical analyses, we examine the triplet X(6600),
X(6900), and X(7100) and draw two main conclusions. First, the observed JP = 2+ states may
correspond to the lowest-lying levels in the fully charmed tetraquark spectrum, which could natu-
rally account for their earlier experimental observation relative to states with other quantum numbers.
Second, additional dynamical mechanisms in fully charmed tetraquark systems may exist that coun-
terbalance or partially offset the effects of CMI in determining the energy-level ordering.
Furthermore, when comparing compact tetraquark systems with three-ﬂavor four-quark systems,
one ﬁnds formal differences in the implementation of the color-singlet constraint; in particular, the
latter can be traced to constructions originating from compact pentaquark conﬁgurations. In addition,
the two systems correspond to distinct Lie-algebra representations in both ﬂavor and color degrees
of freedom. Nevertheless, when restricting attention to the INS structure and neglecting dynamical
effects, the resulting distributions of accessible states exhibit a similar overall shape. These formal
differences in the color-singlet constraint, as well as the differing representation structures associ-
ated with quarks and antiquarks in ﬂavor and color space, do not signiﬁcantly alter the qualitative
distribution pattern.

This observation naturally suggests the following conjecture: for compact multiquark systems
containing equal numbers of quarks and antiquarks, and within a ﬁxed class of spatial conﬁgurations,

color-singlet conditions, and spin–statistics symmetry is largely insensitive to the speciﬁc assignment
of quark and antiquark degrees of freedom.
However, when CMI effects are incorporated in compact tetraquark systems, the resulting modi-

breaking this apparent universality of the distribution shape.
