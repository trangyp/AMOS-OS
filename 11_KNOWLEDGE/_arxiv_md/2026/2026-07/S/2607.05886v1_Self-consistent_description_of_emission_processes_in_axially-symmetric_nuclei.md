Abstract.
We present a theory of cluster emission processes in terms of proton
(π) and neutron (ν) single–particle (sp) degrees of freedom within a self–consistent
mean–ﬁeld (SCMF) constructed from a two–particle interaction having relative (rel)
and center of mass (com) terms centered on the nucler surface, the latter describing
the interaction between the com of a pair of particles and the surface of an axially–
symmetric nucleus. In this way the α-clustering phenomenon becomes enhanced on
the nuclear surface. We present applications for unstable nuclei that decay through
the emission of α–particles above
Sn
100
,
Pb
208
and within the actinidies series.
Keywords: axially–symmetric Hartree–Fock method, surface Gaussian interaction, self–
consistent ﬁeld theory, emission process

1. Introduction
The theory of α–decay was ﬁrst established by Gamow [1] and independently by
Gurney & Condon [2], representing the ﬁrst theoretical framework for the probabilistic
interpretation of quantum mechanics.
Historical notes on radioactivity and a broad
overview of the theoretical research and some experimental aspects investigated over
the decades can be found in Refs. [3, 4, 5, 6, 7]. Of particular interest for this work
is the microscopic theory of α–decay, particularly the clustering mechanism described
in terms of antisymmetrized nucleonic degrees of freedom.
Foundational work was
carried out in the 1960s [8, 9, 10] based on earlier results of Lane [11]. The techniques
for rigorous antisymmetrization were investigated by Fliessbach throughout the 1970s
[12, 13, 14, 15]. In the 1990s the problem of nucleonic clustering was studied extensively

2. CHFBCS theory
In this section we generalize the approach of Ref.
[7] to axially–symmetric nuclei,
presenting the corresponding CHFBCS theory where the cluster is once more illustrated
by an α–particle.
Our goal is to obtain simultaneously a good description of the
ground–state (gs) properties of an unstable nucleus together with a good estimation
of its decay width in the α–channel. Section 2.1 details the properties of the Surface
Gaussian Interaction (SGI) from which the SCMFs are constructed. Section 2.2 covers
the formal derivation of the CHFBCS equations and section 2.3 details the calculation
of the antisymmetrized α–particle formation amplitude in an axially–symmetric ﬁeld.
Let us mentions in this context that the standard Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov (HFB)
approach describing deformed nuclei uses a cranking procedure on the quadrupole
moment in order to induce deformation.
In this case one uses the generalized
quasiparticle representation involving all sp orbitals due to the non–diagonality of the
quadrupole operator.
We will induce deformation by a surface clustering term and
therefore the quasiparticle representation needs only the Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieﬀer
(BCS) treatment coupling sp states with opposite spin projections.
