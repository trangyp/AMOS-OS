The nature of φ(2170) remains an open question in hadron spectroscopy. In this work, we simulate
its production in e+e−collisions at √s = 4.95 GeV using the parton and hadron cascade model
PACIAE 4.0, which sequentially generates the ﬁnal partonic state (FPS) and the ﬁnal hadronic state
(FHS). While previous studies have interpreted φ(2170) as an ss¯s¯s or a u¯us¯s tetraquark state, the
U(1) anomaly coupling allows non-strange quarks to couple to a vector s¯s component via soft-gluon
interactions.
This motivates us to also explore the d ¯ds¯s tetraquark conﬁguration.
In addition,
we consider φ(2170) as an excited strangeonium state, an s¯sg hybrid state, a ¯ΛΛ bound state,
and a φK+K−resonance state. The strangeonium, hybrid, and tetraquark candidates are formed
by coalescing their constituent partons in the FPS using the dynamically constrained phase-space
coalescence model. The ¯ΛΛ and φK+K−states are produced via recombination of their constituent
hadrons in the FHS. We calculate the orbital angular momentum quantum number of each candidate
in its rest frame and perform spectral classiﬁcation. Given JP C = 1−−, φ(2170) can be interpreted
as a D-wave s¯s, a P-wave s¯sg, a P-wave u¯us¯s/d ¯ds¯s/ss¯s¯s, an S-wave ¯ΛΛ, or an S-wave φK+K−
state. We estimate the production yields for all these conﬁgurations: those of the D-wave s¯s, P-
wave s¯sg, u¯us¯s, and d ¯ds¯s states are of order 10−4; those for the S-wave ¯ΛΛ and φK+K−states
are of order 10−5; while the P-wave ss¯s¯s yield is of order 10−6. Moreover, signiﬁcant discrepancies
are observed in the rapidity distributions and the transverse momentum spectra among the various
candidates. These discrepancies could serve as valuable criteria for unraveling the nature of φ(2170).
I.
INTRODUCTION
A central challenge in modern physics is under-
standing how quarks and gluons bind into hadrons.
The vast majority of observed hadrons are either
quark-antiquark pairs (mesons) or three-quark systems
(baryons). The non-Abelian property of quantum chro-
modynamics (QCD) permits the existence of new types
of hadrons, such as glueballs, hybrids, and multiquark
states [1–3]. These exotic hadrons provide a distinctive
environment to investigate the strong interactions and
the conﬁnement mechanism [4]. The ﬁrst exotic hadron,
X(3872), was discovered by the Belle Collaboration in
e+e−collisions in 2003. Since then, several exotic can-
didates consistent with tetraquark interpretations have
been observed, including X(2900) [15], T +
cc(3875) [16],
Zc(3900) [17], X(6900) [18–20]. Among these, X(6900)
is interpreted as a fully charm tetraquark state cc¯c¯c,
with a mass around 6.9 GeV/c2 and an enhanced sig-
nal in the J/ψJ/ψ decay channel [21–25].

by the BaBar Collaboration in 2006 via the initial-state
radiation (ISR) process e+e−→γISRφf0(980) in the
φf0(980) channel [28]. Its observation was subsequently
conﬁrmed by the Belle Collaboration [29], the BES and
BESIII Collaborations [27, 30–34], and further supported
by later BaBar analyses [35, 36].

In this work, we use the parton and hadron cascade
model PACIAE 4.0 [56] to simulate the production of
φ(2170) in e+e−collisions at √s = 4.95 GeV. This en-
ergy is selected because BESIII has accumulated high-
luminosity data at this point, where clear φ(2170) sig-
nals appear in the φη and φη′ invariant mass spectra
[26]. In the PACIAE 4.0 model, the ﬁnal partonic state
(FPS) and the ﬁnal hadronic state (FHS) are simulated
and recorded sequentially. The U(1) anomaly coupling
allows non-strange quarks to couple to a vector s¯s com-
ponent via soft-gluon interactions. This motivates us to
explore the d ¯ds¯s tetraquark conﬁguration in addition to
the previously proposed ss¯s¯s and u¯us¯s scenarios.
We
also investigate four other possibilities: an excited s¯s
state, an s¯sg hybrid state, a ¯ΛΛ bound state, and a
φK+K−resonance state. The excited strangeonium, hy-
brid, and tetraquark candidates are formed by coalescing
the constituent partons in the FPS using the dynami-
cally constrained phase-space coalescence (DCPC) model
[57], which is inspired by quantum statistical mechan-
ics. Speciﬁcally, an s¯s pair coalesces into the strangeo-
nium candidate, an s¯sg system into the hybrid candi-
date, and a four-quark system (u¯us¯s, d ¯ds¯s or ss¯s¯s) into
the tetraquark candidate. By contrast, the ¯ΛΛ bound
state and the φK+K−resonance state are produced via
recombination of their constituent baryons (Λ and ¯Λ)
and mesons (φ, K+, K−) in the FHS, respectively. For
each φ(2170) candidate, we compute its orbital angular
momentum L in the rest frame and classify its spectrum
using the standard n2S+1LJ notation [58], where n, S, L,
and J denote the radial excitation number, total spin, or-
bital angular momentum, and total angular momentum,
respectively. The S, P, D, . . . wave shapes correspond
to L = 0, 1, 2, . . ., respectively.
Given that φ(2170) has JP C = 1−−[27], it can be iden-
tiﬁed as either a D-wave s¯s state, a P-wave s¯sg hybrid
state, a P-wave u¯us¯s/d ¯ds¯s/ss¯s¯s tetraquark state, an S-
wave ¯ΛΛ bound state, or an S-wave φK+K−resonance
state. Although X(2300) is regarded as a candidate for
a φη′/φη hadro-strangeonium state [59], φ(2170) cannot
be regarded as such a state. The reason lies in the par-
ity (P) and charge-conjugation parity (C) of a φη′/φη
system, which consists of a vector meson (φ) and a pseu-
doscalar meson (η or η′). For such a system, P = (−1)L
and C = (−1)L+1, where L is the relative orbital angu-
lar momentum. Hence P and C always have opposite
signs regardless of L.
With X(2300) having 1+−and
φ(2170) having 1−−, only X(2300) satisﬁes the P-C rela-
tion of the φη′/φη system. Consequently, φ(2170) cannot
be a φη′ or φη hadro-strangeonium state. In this work,
we will compare the production yields, rapidity distribu-
tions, and transverse momentum spectra of the candidate
conﬁgurations described above. The observed diﬀerences
are expected to serve as key criteria for identifying the
true nature of φ(2170).
II.
THE MODEL AND METHODOLOGY
In the PACIAE 4.0 model, the simulation of e+e−an-
nihilation proceeds as follows. First, PYTHIA6 [60] is
executed with hadronization disabled and with the subse-
quent breaking of strings and diquarks/antidiquarks into
quarks and antiquarks. To retain gluons for hybrid state
formation, the splitting of gluons into quark-antiquark
pairs is disabled. This yields an initial partonic state.
Next, an energy deexcitation process is applied to en-
ergetic quarks (or antiquarks). The ﬁnal partonic state
(FPS) is then obtained after partonic 2 →2 rescatter-
ing, using leading-order perturbative QCD cross sections
[61, 62].
The FPS contains many quarks, antiquarks,
and gluons, each with four-dimensional coordinates and
momenta. An intermediate hadronic state is produced
by hadronizing the FPS via the Lund string fragmen-
tation scheme [60]. Finally, this intermediate state un-
dergoes 2 →2 hadronic rescattering [63, 64], leading to
kinetic freeze-out and generating the ﬁnal hadronic state
(FHS), which consists of abundant hadrons with their
four-dimensional coordinates and momenta.
The DCPC model was originally proposed by us to
study light nuclei production in proton-proton collisions
at LHC energies [57].
Based on the ﬁnal partonic
or hadronic states generated by PACIAE, the DCPC
model has been successfully applied to calculate the
production yields of various exotic hadronic states, in-
cluding X(3872) [65–67], Z±
c (3900) [68], G(3900) [69],

a list of component mesons, including φ, K+, and K−,
is constructed using the FHS simulated by the PACIAE
model.

all mesons in this list. Each combination of one φ, one
K+, and one K−that satisﬁes the constraints given in
Eq. (3) is accepted as a φ(2170) candidate.
The con-

distinct P and C formulas. If φ(2170) is interpreted as
an excited s¯s state, the parity is P = Ps · P¯s · (−1)L =
(−1)L+1, where L is the total orbital angular momen-

follows the common form: C = (−1)L+S, where S de-

lower production threshold of the u quark in the initial
hard scattering process. This initial advantage is sup-
pressed during parton rescattering and the dynamically
constrained phase-space coalescence, ultimately yielding
a ratio of about 1.23:1. Furthermore, the ss¯s¯s yield is
about two orders of magnitude lower than that of u¯us¯s,
which is a combined result of the suppression of strange
quark initial production and the diﬃculty of recombining
multiple rare quark pairs.

1, there is a peak at mid-rapidity for the rapid-
ity distributions of the φ(2170) candidates with diﬀerent
conﬁgurations. This can be understood as follows: the
central rapidity region collects particles from all event
types regardless of jet direction, while the large-rapidity
region receives contributions only from events with jets
aligned close to the beam axis. Consequently, the yield
at central rapidity is signiﬁcantly enhanced, producing
a well-deﬁned peak. Furthermore, the peak height fol-
lows the hierarchy u¯us¯s > d ¯ds¯s > s¯s > s¯sg > ¯ΛΛ >
φK+K−> ss¯s¯s. Although the s¯sg conﬁguration has the
largest yield, its peak height is not the highest among
all conﬁgurations. This is because the rapidity distribu-
tion of the s¯sg state is broader than those of the other
conﬁgurations, which lowers its peak height. In the up-
per panel of Fig. 2, there is a peak at low pT for the
pT single-diﬀerential distributions of the φ(2170) candi-
dates with diﬀerent conﬁgurations. While the D-wave
s¯s, the P-wave u¯us¯s, d ¯ds¯s, and ss¯s¯s, as well as the S-
wave ¯ΛΛ and φK+K−states all exhibit a peak around
pT ≃0.3 GeV/c, the P-wave s¯sg hybrid state shows
a harder spectrum with its peak shifted to pT ≃0.5
GeV/c. This shift can be attributed to the diﬀerent ori-
gins of transverse momentum. For the pure quark sys-
tems (s¯s and tetraquarks), the pT is inherited from the
relatively soft transverse momentum distribution of the
constituent quarks produced in the parton shower, which
typically peaks around 0.3 GeV/c. In contrast, the s¯sg
hybrid state contains a gluon, and gluons in parton cas-
cades have intrinsically harder pT spectra due to their
stronger radiation and self-coupling [80]. This leads to
a broader pT distribution for the hybrid state and shifts
its peak to a higher value of 0.5 GeV/c. For the S-wave
¯ΛΛ and φK+K−states, which are formed via recom-
bination in the FHS, the transverse momentum spectra
reﬂect the intrinsic pT distributions of the constituent
hadrons produced by the Lund string fragmentation. In
particular, the ¯ΛΛ state involves heavy baryons.
Due
to their larger masses, these baryons are produced with
intrinsically softer transverse momentum spectra in the
fragmentation process, leading to a lower eﬀective tem-
perature and thus a lower peak position. The φK+K−
state, on the other hand, is a three-body system. Its for-
mation via recombination in the FHS requires the three
constituent momenta to sum to a total invariant mass
within the narrow resonance window of φ(2170). High-
pT components tend to push the invariant mass outside
this window and are therefore kinematically suppressed.
As a result, both of these states naturally exhibit soft pT
spectra with peaks around 0.3 GeV/c.
The observed diﬀerences in the yields, rapidity dis-
tributions, and pT spectra among the various conﬁgu-
rations of the φ(2170) candidates may provide valuable
criteria for unraveling the nature of φ(2170). We sug-
gest that these observables be measured experimentally
in e+e−collisions at BESIII energies and compared with
our present predictions.
