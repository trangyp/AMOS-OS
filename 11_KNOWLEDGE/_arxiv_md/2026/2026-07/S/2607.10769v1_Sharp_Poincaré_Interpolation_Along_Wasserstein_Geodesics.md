We prove a sharp interpolation inequality for the Poincar´e constant along quadratic
Wasserstein geodesics. Let µi, i = 0, 1, be κi-strongly log-concave probability measures on Rn,
and let (µt)t∈[0,1] be their optimal displacement interpolation. Then

This estimate is optimal for every t, κ0, κ1, holds for all test functions without symmetry
assumptions, and remains valid for extended-valued potentials.
We also characterize equality at an interior time: it holds if and only if the two endpoints
split off curvature-saturating Gaussian factors in a common direction. The equality directions
form the maximal subspace on which both endpoints have the corresponding Gaussian factors.
The proof uses a weighted Poisson equation adapted to the Brenier map. Its solution
produces compatible divergence fields at the source and target. Two Hessian symmetries
allow the vector Bochner identity to be applied separately at the endpoints, and the resulting
estimates are then interpolated along the optimal transport map. The resulting two-endpoint
Bochner method is the main structural ingredient: it converts curvature information available
only at the endpoints directly into a sharp spectral estimate along the connecting geodesic,
bypassing the generally inaccessible curvature of the intermediate measures.
As a special case, we resolve a question of Aishwarya and Rotem concerning odd functions
along optimal interpolations between even strongly log-concave measures. For 1-strongly
log-concave endpoints, the full Poincar´e estimate further yields a finite-time (2, n)-distortion
inequality for translation-reduced Gaussian entropy, as well as Gaussian Borell–Brascamp–Lieb
and dimensional Brunn–Minkowski inequalities under a Gaussian centroid condition.

Introduction
A basic problem in Wasserstein geometry is to determine which analytic consequences of endpoint
curvature survive displacement interpolation. This is subtler than preservation of the underlying
geometric structure. Even when both endpoints are log-concave or have convex supports, their
Wasserstein midpoint need not retain the corresponding convexity properties; see the constructions
of Santambrogio and Wang [41]. Thus analytic information at intermediate times need not follow
from preservation of convexity.
Optimal transport has long linked interpolation inequalities, entropy, and curvature. Otto
and Villani developed fundamental connections between transport and entropy inequalities [39],
while Cordero-Erausquin, McCann, and Schmuckenschl¨ager established a Riemannian interpolation
inequality through optimal transport [12]. Von Renesse and Sturm then characterized lower Ricci
curvature in terms of transport inequalities and displacement convexity of entropy [43]; the first
author later proved rigidity of the reference measures underlying this convexity theory [24]. These
works motivate the study of spectral inequalities along a prescribed Wasserstein geodesic when
curvature assumptions are imposed only at its endpoints.

Poisson equations as dual certificates: Stein and isoperimetry
There is a useful Stein interpretation of the Poisson lift. For a probability measure dµ = Z−1e−V dx,
the weighted divergence −divµ is a canonical vector-valued Stein operator: given a centered test
function h, one seeks a field q satisfying
−divµ q = h
and estimates the size or regularity of q. Such estimates are usually referred to as Stein-factor
bounds. The relation between spectral gaps, weak Stein equations, and the construction of Stein
kernels has been developed, among others, by Courtade, Fathi, and Pananjady [14], Mijoule, Raiˇc,
Reinert, and Swan [37]; see also Fathi’s moment-map construction [22]. In this language,

compatible Stein fields at two prescribed endpoints, and the endpoint Bochner estimates interpolate
the corresponding L2 Stein factor along the Wasserstein geodesic.
