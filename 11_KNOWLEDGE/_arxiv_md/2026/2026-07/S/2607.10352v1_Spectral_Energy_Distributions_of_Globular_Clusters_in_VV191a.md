ABSTRACT
We investigate globular cluster (GC) candidates associated with the elliptical galaxy
VV191a using JWST NIRCam and archival HST imaging. Beginning with an initial
catalog of over 500 candidate sources, we apply morphological filtering, photometric
redshift selection, and spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting using BAGPIPES to
isolate a refined sample of 61 probable GCs, including a high-confidence subset of
20 objects. The derived age distribution is concentrated between ∼6–10 Gyr, with
a peak near ∼8 Gyr, while metallicities are predominantly near and super-solar.
Unlike many GC systems in elliptical galaxies, the VV191a sample does not exhibit a
clear bimodal metallicity distribution. Derived stellar masses span a broad range and
suggest significant degeneracies related to dust attenuation and foreground contami-
nation from the overlapping spiral galaxy VV191b. These results support the presence
of an evolved GC population in VV191a while highlighting limitations imposed by
broadband SED fitting and foreground contamination.
1. INTRODUCTION
Globular clusters (GCs) exist as some of the oldest stellar systems in galaxies and
serve as powerful tracers of their evolution across cosmic time. Derived ages, masses,
and metallicities encode information about conditions of their formation, making
them useful probes of a galaxy’s assembly history (Kruijssen 2012; Beasley 2020). In
particular, GCs in elliptical galaxies often exhibit particular characteristic features—
such as old ages and bimodal metallicity—that reflect multiple epochs of star forma-
tion and growth via mergers.

Evidence has been found in previous work of probable GCs in VV191a (Berkheimer
et al. 2024), but uncertainties remain regarding their physical properties and the
reliability of their classification. In this work, we refine a catalog of candidate objects
and model their spectral energy distributions (SEDs) to constrain key properties such
as age, metallicity, and stellar mass. By analyzing these properties, we aim to assess
the nature of the GC population in VV191a.
2. DATA AND METHODS
The VV191 system presents a unique opportunity to study such a population in a
more complex observational environment, enabling investigation of GCs in the ellip-
tical while simultaneously introducing observational challenges due to contamination
from the foreground spiral galaxy VV191b. We utilize imaging of the VV191 sys-
tem (at DOI: 10.17909/9a9t-3q60) obtained with the James Webb Space Telescope
(JWST) NIRCam instrument in the F090W, F150W, F356W, and F444W filters as
part of the PEARLS survey found in Windhorst et al. (2023) (PID: 1176). This data
is complemented by archival Hubble Space Telescope (HST) imaging in the F606W
filter taken from MAST (PID: 13695). The JWST data was processed using the stan-
dard calibration pipeline (Bushouse et al. 2023) and combined into mosaics with a
pixel scale of 0.03′′.
We began with a catalog of ∼500 sources identified in Berkheimer et al. (2024),
which includes candidate GCs, foreground, and background objects. Due to observed
substantial overlap of the spiral arms associated with VV191b, it is expected that
there will be significant contamination from foreground and background objects (Keel
et al. 2023). As such, a multi-stage procedure was implemented to isolate and select
a robust candidate GC sample.
2.1. SED Selection
We performed aperture photometry for each candidate across all available filters
using the PhotUtils aperture photometry routines.
Background subtraction was
performed using annuli with inner and outer radii of 0.5′′ and 0.75′′, respectively. Un-
certainties were similarly calculated directly. We compared resulting SEDs to model
predictions for compact star clusters from Faisst et al. (2022). Sources displaying ir-
regular photometric behavior inconsistent with GC expectations were excluded from
the final sample.
2.2. Photometric Redshift Selection
Following morphological filtering, we computed photometric redshifts for the re-
maining candidates using the EAZY-Py package (Brammer 2023). Each object is com-
pared against the known redshift of VV191a (z ≈0.0513), with a relatively broad
tolerance threshold of ∆z = ±0.15 to account for broadband degeneracies in the
photometry.

As an additional consistency check, we examine the spatial distribution of selected
sources via their placement on a color–magnitude diagram, where genuine GCs are
expected to occupy a relatively narrow locus reflecting evolved stellar populations.
2.3. SED Modeling
We construct and model the SED of each remaining candidate using the BAGPIPES
fitting framework (Carnall et al. 2018). This method fits stellar population synthesis
models to the observed photometry, allowing us to infer key physical parameters such
as stellar age, mass, and metallicity.
We adopt a delta-function-style burst star formation history, consistent with expec-
tations that GCs go through short and intense episodes of formation. The models
assume a Bruzual & Charlot (2003) stellar population synthesis framework, a Kroupa
(2001) IMF, and the MILES stellar library (Falcon-Barroso et al. 2011). To account
for dust attenuation by VV191b’s spiral arms, we model it via a Cardelli extinction
law (Cardelli et al. 1989).
The fitting procedure explores a wide parameter space in age (1-14 Gyr), metallicity
(log Z/Z⊙= 0 - 2), mass (103-1015 M⊙), and dust attenuation (0 < AV < 1), with
uniform priors, to avoid biasing toward specific solutions.
2.4. Fit Quality and Parameter Degeneracies
The quality of each SED fit is evaluated using a χ2
phot statistic, and objects with
χ2
phot > 50 are excluded as poorly constrained or likely contaminants. Even for well-
fit objects, significant degeneracies exist between parameters such as age, metallicity,
and dust attenuation (Conroy 2013; Worthey 1994; Papovich et al. 2001).
These
degeneracies are exacerbated by the limited number of photometric bands and the
complex foreground environment. After performing this check of the χ2, we obtain a
high-confidence sample of 20 candidate objects.
3. RESULTS
SED Fitting of the high-confidence candidates from BAGPIPES in general reproduced
observed photometry well across available wavelengths.
An example of the fitted
SEDs is shown in Figure 1, with the best-fit model capturing the declining spectral
shape anticipated for an evolved stellar population dominated primarily by older
stars.
Figure 1 additionally shows derived ages of the 20 high-confidence objects. The age
distribution centers on a concentration at ∼6 −10 Gyr, peaking at 8 Gyr. While
this is broadly consistent with expectations of an older stellar population, the peak
at 8 Gyr suggests slightly younger ages than the canonical ≳10 Gyr.
The metallicity distribution is strongly skewed toward peaks at near-solar and super-
solar metallicity. Only a very small number of candidates extend toward lower metal-
licities.
Unlike a majority of GC systems in elliptical galaxies, this sample from
VV191a does not show an expected bimodal distribution (Forbes et al. 1997; Harris

1999). Combined with younger ages, a super-solar metallicity at 6-11 Gyr is notewor-
thy. Local GC systems typically have ages ranging from 12-13 Gyr (Mar´ın-Franch
et al. 2009), and the age–metallicity relation generally predicts metal-rich GCs form
early and are rapidly enriched (Leaman et al. 2013). However, intermediate-aged (∼8
Gyr), metal-rich GCs have been identified in merger-remnant and lenticular galax-
ies (Puzia 2003); this suggests VV191a may have experienced a more extended GC
formation history. Alternatively, age–metallicity degeneracies might bias estimates
toward younger, metal-rich solutions.
Masses derived from the fits suggest these objects span a range between log(M/M⊙)
∼5 to 9.5. There is a slight peak at log(M/M⊙) ∼7 – 7.5, but the distribution is
very wide with no obvious Gaussian or bimodal shape.
Interpretation of the derived physical properties is limited by several systematic
uncertainties. In particular, the age–metallicity degeneracy allows older, metal-poor
stellar populations to mimic the photometric behavior of younger, metal-rich systems
(Worthey 1994; Conroy 2013). Additional uncertainty comes from the excess of dust
via the foreground galaxy VV191b, which can alter observed fluxes and affect param-
eters derived for the candidate objects. While the overall distributions are broadly
consistent with expected GC populations in the VV191a system, the parameters of
individual objects are still weakly constrained.

4. CONCLUSION
We refined a sample of GC candidates associated with the galaxy VV191a us-
ing JWST and HST photometry, using morphological filtering, photometric redshift
checks, and SED modeling. We investigated the properties of the refined sample of
61 candidates, with a high-confidence sample of 20 probable GCs.
The resulting population is broadly consistent with an evolved stellar system, with
derived ages primarily concentrated around ∼6–10 Gyr and metallicities dominated
by near-solar and super-solar values. However, inferred properties differ from expec-
tations of classical GC systems in several ways. The lack of a bimodal metallicity
distribution and comparatively younger GC ages likely reflects observational limita-
tions and degeneracies common in broadband SED fitting. Further analysis of these
parameters compared to the spatial distribution of these candidates is ongoing.
Foreground contamination from the overlapping spiral galaxy VV191b remains a
significant challenge, introducing uncertainties in source identification and parameter
estimation. The limited number of photometric bands further restricts the ability to
uniquely constrain stellar population properties.
