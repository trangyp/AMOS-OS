We study the geometry of reset distributions for absorbed Markov processes with geometric re-
setting, working at an abstract level that isolates the structural mechanism underlying reset-neutral
invariance. We show that the spectral duality endows the simplex of reset distributions with a non-
trivial spectral response geometry: the simplex ∆m−1 carries a foliation by level sets of the coupling
functional C, organized around a critical manifold Σ that acts as a global orientation boundary
for the reset response. Under four structural conditions (S1)–(S4) on the coupling functional, we
establish the existence and explicit characterization of the separatrix Σ, derive the invariant value

sign principle in the two-site case. The linear functional ψ(γ) emerges as a global orientation field:

I.
INTRODUCTION
First-passage problems in confined domains lie at
the intersection of probability theory and statistical
physics [1–3]. The classical gambler’s ruin problem—the
probability that a biased random walk on a finite interval
reaches one absorbing boundary before the other [4]—
provides the canonical setting in which questions of ab-
sorption time, splitting probabilities, and survival statis-
tics admit explicit closed-form solutions.
Stochastic resetting has emerged in recent years as
a fundamental mechanism in non-equilibrium stochas-
tic processes, with applications to first-passage problems,
search processes, and transport in confined domains [5–
7]. For absorbed Markov processes, resetting can pro-
foundly alter absorption probabilities and induce non-
trivial stationary behavior [8, 9].
Recent studies have revealed the existence of reset-
neutral distributions, for which certain observables re-
main invariant under changes in the resetting rate. While
this phenomenon has been explicitly identified in specific
models, such as biased random walks with geometric re-
setting [10, 11], its structural origin has remained to be
clarified.
The purpose of this work is to identify and formalize
the structural mechanism underlying these invariances.
Rather than focusing on a specific model, we adopt an
abstract perspective and isolate the minimal structural
conditions under which such behavior arises. Our start-
ing point is the observation that, in concrete realizations,
the relevant observables can be expressed in terms of
spectral decompositions involving families of coefficients
{Aν(z)}, {Bν(z)} and γ-dependent weights. Motivated
by this structure, we consider observables of the form
C : ∆m−1 ×(0, 1) →R satisfying a spectral duality of the
form
Bν(z) = κ(z) Aν(σ(z)),
where σ is an involution on the reset sites and the weights
κ(z) are independent of the spectral index ν.

framework applies whenever (S1)–(S4) hold, regardless of
which process produces the observable.

C.
Critical distributions and the separatrix
A distribution π∗∈∆m−1 is reset-neutral (or critical)
if the coupling functional is independent of the resetting
rate, i.e., if there exists a constant C∗such that
C(π∗, γ) = C∗
for all γ ∈(0, 1).
The set of reset-neutral distributions,
Σ := {π ∈∆m−1 : C(π, γ) is constant in γ} ,
will be called the separatrix. This set acts as a geometric
boundary separating regions of the simplex where the γ-
dependence of C exhibits qualitatively distinct behavior:
on one side C increases with γ, on the other it decreases,
and on Σ it remains constant. The global structure of
these regions will be analyzed in Section VIII.
Remark 2.4 (Spatial vs. dynamical criticality).
At
π∗, the derivative ∂γC vanishes, not the gradient ∇πC.
The distribution π∗is critical with respect to γ, not
necessarily an extremum of C on the simplex.
Under
(S1), the condition C(π∗, γ) = const is equivalent to
∂γC(π∗, γ) = 0 for all γ.
The existence and characterization of the separatrix,
together with the geometric structure it induces on the
simplex, are the central objects of study in this work.
