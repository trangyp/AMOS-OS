SPLENDID EXTENSIONS
SHIMON GARTI
Abstract. We show that one can force the non-existence of κ+-splendid
models, where κ is an infinite cardinal. We prove a similar result in ZFC
under the assumption of an ineffable cardinal.
2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 03H15.
Key words and phrases. Models of Peano, Aronszajn trees, Kaufmann models, recursive
saturation, splendid models, large cardinals.

0. Introduction
Let N be a model, and let I be a cut of N. The collection of X ⊆I for
which there is Y ∈Def(N) so that X = Y ∩I is denoted by Cod(N/I).
Usually, we deal with Cod(N/M) where both M and N are models and N
is an extension of M.1 The following comes from [KS06, Corollary 8.2.5],
and it is fundamental for the results in the current paper.
Proposition 0.1. Every countable recursively saturated model M has two
countable recursively saturated elementary end-extensions M0 and M1 such
that Cod(M0/M) ∩Cod(M1/M) = Def(M).
It should be noted that if M ≺e N and N is recursively saturated then N
is not conservative over M, see [KS06, Proposition 10.1.2]. Thus, recursive
saturation contradicts conservativity if the extension is elementary.
The
above statement shows that one can get something that resembles conser-
vativity, by forking over M with two recursively saturated models M0, M1,
each of which may add some new definable sets but together they form a
parallel to a conservative extension.
An essential component of the proof is the Baire category theorem, in
which one intersects countably many dense sets in some relevant topology.
In particular, it is not clear whether the countability of the pertinent models
is indispensable in the above proposition. The main result of the paper reads
as follows.
Theorem 0.2. Let κ be a successor cardinal. It is consistent that every
recursively saturated model of cardinality κ extends to a model M, where
|M| = κ, and if M0, M1 are recursively saturated elementary end-extensions
of M of size κ then Cod(M0/M) ∩Cod(M1/M) ̸= Def(M).
We assume that there are large cardinals in the ground model, in order to
force this result, though we do not know whether it has consistency strength.
Moreover, under the assumption of an ineffable cardinal κ we prove, without
forcing, that there are such models of cardinality less than κ.
The paper contains three additional sections. The first section collects
basic facts and definitions. In the second section we prove the main theorem
with respect to ℵ1 and larger successor cardinals. In the third section we
obtain a similar result below large cardinals, and pose some natural open
problems. Our notation is (hopefully) standard, and we refer to [Kay91] and
[KS06] for background in models of Peano arithmetic.

1. Preliminaries
Let us commence with the central definition of the paper.
Definition 1.1. Let κ be an infinite cardinal and let M be a model of
cardinality κ.
A pair of models (M0, M1) is a splendid pair over M iff
|M0| = |M1| = κ, both are recursively saturated elementary end extensions
of M, and Cod(M0/M) ∩Cod(M1/M) = Def(M).
As mentioned in the introduction, if κ = ℵ0 then every model M of size
κ admits a splendid pair.
The situation might be different for κ = ℵ1,
or for higher cardinalities. In order to prove this, we need the concept of
Kaufmann models.
Let M be a model of size κ. One says that M is κ-like if |M| = κ but
|aM| < κ for every a ∈M, where aM = {b ∈M | M |= b ≤a}. If κ = ℵ0
then the only κ-like model of PA is the standard model, but if κ > ℵ0 then
one can construct many κ-like models of PA. The common way to build
such models is by constructing an increasing continuous chain of elementary
end-extensions of a given model M, using the MacDowell-Specker theorem
from [MDS61].
A model M is recursively saturated if every recursive type over M is
realized in M. We assume that there is a fixed G¨odel numbering of first-
order formulae in our language, and p is a recursive type if the set of natural
numbers corresponding to the formulas in p is a recursive set. Note that
if M is non-standard then every Σn-recursive type is realized in M. Being
recursively saturated is slightly stronger, as a recursive type p might contain
formulas with unbounded degree in the arithmetic hierarchy. By and large,
recursive saturation indicates that the model is rich in some sense.
A subset X of M is called a class if X ∩a is definable in M for every
a ∈M. It is fairly possible that X itself is not definable in M. In fact, the
following statement holds.
Observation 1.2. Let M be a model of PA for the language L ⊇LPA,
where |M| = λ, |L| ≤λ and cf(λ) = ω. Then there is a class X ⊆M so
that X /∈Def(M).
Proof.
If X ⊆M, otp(X) = ω and X is unbounded in M then X is a class of M
since every finite subset of M is definable. There are λω ≥λ+ such classes,
but only λ-many definable classes since |M|, |L| ≤λ.
□1.2
A model M is rather classless if every class of M is definable.2 Being
rather classless means that M is poor in some sense. The seminal paper of
Kaufmann, [Kau77], investigates the possibility that a model M is both rich
and poor.3
2The term rather classless was coined by Kaufmann in [Kau77].
3See the story of the poor man’s ewe lamb, [EfrCE, Chapter 12].

Definition 1.3. Let M be a model of PA and let κ be an infinite cardinal.
One says that M is κ-Kaufmann iff M enjoys the following properties:
(ℵ) M is κ-like.
(ℶ) M is recursively saturated.
()גM is rather classless.
By Observation 1.2, if κ = ℵ0 or κ > cf(κ) = ω then there are no κ-
Kaufmann models. On the other hand, if κ > cf(κ) > ω then there exist
κ-Kaufmann models as proved by Schmerl in [Sch81]. We focus, therefore,
on regular uncountable cardinals.
Kaufmann proved in [Kau77] that if ♢ℵ1 holds then there exists an ℵ1-
Kaufmann model.
Recall that (Aα | α ∈ω1) is a diamond sequence if
{α ∈ω1 | A ∩α = Aα} is a stationary subset of ω1 whenever A ⊆ω1.
One says that ♢ℵ1 holds if there exists a diamond sequence on ω1. Clearly,
♢ℵ1 implies CH and hence this principle is not provable in ZFC. However,
ℵ1-Kaufmann models do exist in ZFC. By an absoluteness result of Shelah
from [She78], Kaufmann’s theorem actually holds in ZFC.
The situation is different if κ > ℵ1. Schmerl showed in [Sch81, Theorem
3] that if κ = cf(κ) > ℵ0 and there exists a κ-Kaufmann model then there
is a κ-Aronszajn tree. The existence of ℵ1-Aronszajn trees is a consequence
of ZFC, see [Kur35], but already at the second uncountable cardinal one
can force the non-existence of ℵ2-Aronszajn trees.4 This result is due to
Mitchell, [Mit73], and in this model there are no ℵ2-Kaufmann models.
The main purpose of the current paper is to show that consistently there
are models of cardinality κ+ with no splendid extension. This statement
is forceable at every successor cardinal. The argument proceeds as follows.
Let M be a model of size κ+. Build a tree in which M is the root, and
at every successor stage there are two models of cardinality κ+ that form a
splendid extension over the previous model. At limit stages take unions. If
this construction is possible for κ++-many stages, and if the combinatorial
principle Φκ++ holds,5 then there is an κ++-Kaufmann model. Therefore,
if one forces the non-existence of κ++-Kaufmann models and Φκ++ holds,
then the above construction is impossible.
Namely, at some stage there
will be a model M of size κ+ with no splendid extension. Remark that in
this universe there are many models with no splendid extension. In fact,
every model of size κ+ extends to a model of the same size with no splendid
extension.
To round out the picture, let us recall the definition of the Devlin-Shelah
weak diamond. This principle was defined in [DS78] with respect to ℵ1. We
phrase here the straightforward generalization to κ.

Definition 1.4. Let κ be regular and uncountable.
The weak diamond
at κ is the following statement: for every coloring c : <κ2 →{0, 1} there
exists a weak diamond function g ∈κ2 such that for every f ∈κ2 the set
{α ∈κ | c(f ↾α) = g(α)} is a stationary subset of κ.
The weak diamond on κ is denoted by Φκ. It has been proved in [DS78]
that if 2ℵ0 < 2ℵ1 then Φℵ1 holds. The converse is true as well, as indicated
by U. Abraham. More generally, if 2κ < 2κ+ then Φκ+ holds, where the
proof is identical with the case of ℵ1. Likewise, Φκ+ implies 2κ < 2κ+, see
[Gar17, Proposition 1.2].
As mentioned above, Kaufmann used diamond at ℵ1 in his construction.
An alternative proof with weak diamond at ℵ1 is suggested in [KS06]. In
the next section we show that this construction works equally well at ev-
ery uncountable regular cardinal, provided there are appropriate splendid
extensions. Combining this fact with the ability to force models with the
tree property at many regular uncountable cardinals, we derive our main
theorem.

2. Weak diamond and splendid extensions
Let κ be an infinite cardinal and let M be a model of size κ. MacDowell
and Specker proved in [MDS61] that there exists a model N of size κ so
that M ≺e N.6 A slightly stronger statement has been proved by Gaifman
in [Gai76]. Call N a conservative extension of M if Cod(N/M) = Def(M).
Gaifman showed that every model M extends to an elementary conservative
extension N of the same cardinality. It is easy to see that a conservative
elementary extension is an end extension.
The model of Gaifman must be minimal over M, in some sense.
In
particular, it cannot be recursively saturated. However, if |M| = ℵ0 then one
can obtain a splendid pair over M, which is quite close to being conservative,
see Theorem 0.1. The situation is different at uncountable cardinals. The
following will be useful for the proof of the main theorem.
Definition 2.1. Let κ be a regular cardinal. We shall say that κ is re-
cursively amenable if every recursively saturated model M of size κ has a
splendid extension of the same cardinality.

3. Large cardinals
The results in the previous section were obtained by forcing, and thus the
existence of models with no splendid extension is only a consistency result.
In this section we show that something similar holds without forcing if there
are sufficiently strong concepts of large cardinals at our disposal.
Our strategy would be similar: we wish to combine weak diamonds with
the tree property. Observe that weak diamond holds, in ZFC, at a proper
class of cardinals, as shown in [BGP23]. This is not the case, however, with
respect to the tree property. But if κ is a sufficiently large cardinal then
(unlike the case of successor cardinals) one regains the desired properties.
Definition 3.1. A cardinal κ is ineffable if every coloring c : [κ]2 →2
admits a monochromatic stationary subset S of κ.
An ineffable cardinal is weakly compact, and hence satisfies the tree prop-
erty. Likewise, if κ is ineffable then ♢κ holds, so a fortiori Φκ holds.9 For the
main theorem of this section we redefine the concept of recursive amenabil-
ity by dropping the requirement that the models in a splendid extension of
M have the same cardinality as M.
Definition 3.2. Let κ be a regular cardinal and let θ < κ.
One says
that κ is θ-recursively amenable if every recursively saturated model M
whose cardinality belongs to [θ, κ) has a splendid extension (M0, M1) so
that |M| ≤|M0|, |M1| < κ.
Our goal is to show that ineffable cardinals do not satisfy the above defini-
tion. It will follow that models of size less than κ with no splendid extension
exist, if κ is ineffable.

θ-recursively amenable. Hence there exists a model M of size λ ∈[θ, κ) with
no splendid extension.

tension, as proved in the above theorem, is carried-out in ZFC (and the
assumption that there is an ineffable cardinal). However, the proof does not
specify the cardinal λ on which there is such a model. The theorem of the
previous section is proved by forcing, but it gives such models at prescribed
specific cardinals. One interesting aspect of ineffability is that this large
cardinal does not contradict the axiom of constructibility.
Corollary 3.5. If V = L and there exists an ineffable cardinal κ, then for
some λ ∈κ there are models of cardinality λ with no splendid extension.
□3.5
From this corollary we deduce that an attempt to prove that models of
uncountable size always admit a splendid extension would be somewhat com-
plicated. In particular, it does not follow immediately from the assumption
V = L. In this light, we would like to phrase a few open problems that
emerge out of the above results.
Question 3.6. What is the consistency strength of a non-Kaufmann model
M of size ℵ1 with no splendid extension?
The consistency strength of the tree property at ℵ2 is well-established,
but maybe there is a different strategy with respect to splendid extensions.
Question 3.7. Is it consistent that for every κ = cf(κ) > ℵ0 there is a
model of size κ with no splendid extension simultaneously?
If one follows the strategy of the above proof then the problem resembles
Magidor’s question about the possible consistency of the tree property at
every κ = cf(κ) > ℵ1.
But maybe there is a different way to eliminate
splendid extensions.
Question 3.8. Let κ be strongly inaccessible.
(a) Does every model of size κ admit a splendid extension of cardinality
κ?

(b) Does there necessarily exist, in ZFC, a κ+-Kaufmann model?
The connection between the two parts of the problem is clear in the light
of the above theorems.
Since ℵ0, the first strongly inaccessible cardinal,
demonstrates a positive answer to both parts of the problem, it is reasonable

Finally, one may wonder whether consistently there are splendid exten-

Question 3.9. Is it consistent that every model of cardinality ℵ1 has a
splendid extension?
A possible direction is to force a stronger version of the Baire category
theorem in which one can intersect ℵ1-many dense sets. We note, however,
that the problem seems to be more complicated and, in particular, one needs
more than that. To see this, consider PFA, under which such a version of
the Baire category theorem holds. By [Bau84], the tree property holds at ℵ2
under PFA.10 Moreover, 2ℵ1 = ℵ2 is a consequence of PFA, thus models of
size ℵ1 with no splendid extension exist under PFA as follows from Theorem
