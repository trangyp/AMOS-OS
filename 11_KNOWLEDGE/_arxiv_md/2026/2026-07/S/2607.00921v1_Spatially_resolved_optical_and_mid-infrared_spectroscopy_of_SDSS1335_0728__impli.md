ABSTRACT
Aims. The galaxy SDSS1335+0728 brightened abruptly in December 2019 (the Ansky event) and has since been confirmed as the host
of extreme X-ray quasi-periodic eruptions (QPEs) of debated origin. We constrain the origin of its transient activity by characterising
the galaxy properties and nuclear accretion history with spatially resolved VLT/MUSE and JWST MIRI/MRS spectroscopy.
Methods. We extract stellar and gas kinematics and emission-line fluxes, construct emission-line ionisation diagnostic maps, recon-
struct the nuclear ionisation history via a Balmer-line light-echo analysis, and measure the mid-infrared silicate feature strength.
Results. The stellar kinematics reveal two counter-rotating stellar regions and kinematically cold gas (σgas ≲60 km s−1), consistent
with a past minor merger. Stellar populations show an old host with ongoing star formation confined to a ring at intermediate radii.
Ionisation diagnostics reveal a three-zone structure: a central region powered by SMBH accretion, where high-ionisation coronal lines
([Ne vi]λ7.65µm, [Ne v]λ14.32µm, [O iv]λ25.89µm) are confined, a star-forming ring, and a LINER-like outer region. A Balmer-line
light-echo analysis yields a minimum ionising luminosity log Lion,min ≈40.5 erg s−1 sustained over at least ∼1 500 yr. Broad silicate
emission at 9.7 and 18 µm indicates optically thin dust, inconsistent with a classical active galactic nucleus (AGN) dusty torus.
Conclusions. The data are consistent with two scenarios for the pre-2019 accretion: a persisting or gradually fading low-luminosity
AGN, or a long-lived tidal disruption event (TDE) remnant disc. In both, Ansky corresponds to a slow, faint transient in a ∼106 M⊙
SMBH with already ongoing accretion, challenging the “faded AGN” interpretation proposed for some QPE hosts.

IFS observations, primarily utilizing the Multi Unit Spec-
80
troscopic Explorer (MUSE; Bacon et al. 2010) at the Very
Large Telescope (VLT), have provided crucial insights into
the environments that host QPEs. Observations presented by
Wevers et al. (2024) reveal that three out of five known QPE
host galaxies observed with MUSE (GSN 069, RX J1301, and
eRO-QPE2) present Extended Emission-Line Regions (EELRs)
of ionised gas that extend up to 10 kpc from the galactic nu-
cleus, while Xiong et al. (2025) presented similar observations
for the host of AT2019qiz. These EELRs exhibit peculiar kine-
matics: they are physically and kinematically decoupled from
90
the stellar continuum, displaying low velocities (< 100 km/s)
and low velocity dispersions (≲75 km/s). Spectral line diagnos-
tics indicate that the gas is being photoionised by a hard, non-
stellar continuum. However, the current bolometric luminosities
of these galactic nuclei are insufficient to power the observed
large-scale emission. From this energy deficit, recent works have
proposed that these galaxies hosted an AGN that recently faded
(within the last ∼15, 000 to 30, 000 years; e.g., Jiang & Pan
2025; Xiong et al. 2025). Conversely, recent theoretical work
by Mummery et al. (2025) proposes a compelling alternative
100
(in agreement with observations presented in Wevers & French
2024; Wevers et al. 2024): the long-lived accretion discs formed
by TDEs themselves produce sufficient ionising radiation to
power EELRs on galaxy-wide scales (extending up to ∼104
light-years). This mechanism naturally explains the observations
without requiring a faded AGN: galaxies with intrinsically high
TDE rates are simply more likely to exhibit these macroscopic
EELRs, which act as light echoes from prior stellar disrup-
tions. Furthermore, QPE host galaxies show a striking statisti-
cal resemblance to TDE host galaxies (Wevers & French 2024;
110
Wevers et al. 2024). Both are significantly overrepresented in
post-starburst (PSB) and quiescent Balmer-strong (QBS) galax-
ies, indicating a shared preference for gas-rich, post-merger en-
vironments where a massive BH has recently ceased its active ac-
cretion phase. This overlapping host demographic suggests that
TDEs and QPEs share a common formation channel, potentially
linked to the disruption of nuclear stellar dynamics following a
minor merger.

We reduced the data using the JWST Science Calibration

240

250

Emission-line fluxes, velocity centroids, and line widths

Gaussian profile plus a local linear continuum independently to a
set of targeted transitions spanning high-IP coronal lines, low-IP
ISM tracers, and H2 rotational lines (see Appendix C for details).
Lines were considered detected at SNR ≥3, |∆v| ≤1000 km s−1,
and FWHM ≥100 km s−1. Spatially resolved maps of line flux

geometric effect in the covering-fraction correction. Specifically,

occurs, coinciding spatially with the transition between the two

σ or 2σ feature, with σ referring to the stellar velocity disper-
530

host galaxies (e.g. Wevers & French 2024; Wevers et al. 2024).

the low gas velocity dispersion (σgas ≲60 km s−1) indicates
that the gas is dynamically cold and settled, consistent with
the aftermath of a minor merger as proposed for other QPE
hosts (Wevers et al. 2024). A low σgas alone does not discrim-

the undisturbed morphology in the continuum (white image in

merger in which accreted gas settled into a counter-rotating re-

2018; Martinsson et al. 2018).

4. Discussion
In the following sections, we discuss the implications of the
results presented in Section 3 for the accretion history of
SDSS1335+0728 and the origin of the Ansky event, as well as
for the QPE population in general.
4.1. Implications for the origin of the Ansky event
The light-echo analysis (Section 3.1.3) constrains the recent
ionising history of the nucleus. Restricting attention to the
740
accretion-driven-classified bins, where the assumption of a cen-
tral ionising source is physically justified, we see that a mini-
mum ionising luminosity of log Lion,min ≈40.5 erg s−1 out to
light-travel delays of several ×103 yr is required to explain the
data. Two caveats temper further interpretation. First, Lion,min is
a strict lower limit on the nuclear ionising luminosity. Second,
the projected distance yields only a minimum light-travel de-
lay, and the isodelay-surface geometry smears the inferred time
axis (Mummery et al. 2025). The derived log Lion,min therefore
implies that a dramatically brighter past is not required to ex-
750
plain the extended emission. The data are fully accounted for by
a source sustained at ∼1040.5 erg s−1 over ≳several ×103 yr.
This luminosity lies ∼2 dex below the pre-2019 BBB luminos-
ity from the SED modelling of Sánchez-Sáez et al. (2024), and
above the BBB lower limit (2.5% limit) reported in the same
work (log Lbol = 37.84 erg s−1). The natural reading is that the
nucleus was not dormant prior to December 2019, and that the
Ansky event temporarily elevated the luminosity above a pre-
existing baseline. The ZTF light curve (Figure 1) independently
shows the source declining back towards, but not yet reaching,
760
its pre-2019 flux level.

The emission-line maps (Sections 3.1.1 and 3.2.2) reveal a
clear spatial structure. The low-IP tracers ([Ne ii], [S iii], [Ar ii])
and [O iii] are spatially extended across the host, confirming
widespread ionisation well beyond the nucleus, whereas the
high-IP coronal lines ([Ne vi], [Ne v], [O iv]) are confined to
the central resolution element. This differential compactness en-
codes the ionising history: the coronal lines, with their short re-
combination times (trec ∼102–103 yr at narrow line region –
NLR– densities), track the continuum on ∼102 yr timescales,
770
while the extended [O iii] integrates the ∼103–104 yr history
(Osterbrock & Ferland 2006). The compactness of the high-IP
emission is consistent with production by the Ansky event, yet
the JWST MIRI/MRS PSF (≳1′′ at the relevant wavelengths)
limits the effective spatial resolution to a few hundred parsecs,
comparable to the maximum extent over which [Ne v] can be
sustained even by SMBH accretion-driven photoionisation (see
Figure 21 in Mummery et al. 2025); we therefore cannot rule
out that the observed coronal-line flux is partly a relic of prior
nuclear activity. In contrast, the low- and intermediate-IP lines
780
are detected on scales of several kiloparsecs, without showing
any preferential direction, well beyond the region that the 2019-
onset event alone can have illuminated, requiring a source of soft
ionising photons that has been active for at least several 103 yr.
The broad 9.7 and 18 µm silicate features observed in emis-
sion (Section 3.2.1) indicate optically thin dust directly illumi-
nated by the central source, incompatible with a classical com-
pact torus. The inferred silicate strengths lie closer to the MIR-
selected TDE population of Masterson et al. (2025) than to typ-
ical AGN, but the suppression or absence of a thick torus is it-
790
self predicted at these low luminosities (Lbol ≲1042 erg s−1;
Elitzur & Shlosman 2006), so the dust properties alone do not
discriminate between a TDE and an LLAGN origin for the pre-
cursor activity.
We therefore consider two scenarios for the pre-2019 accre-
tion history. In both pictures, the Ansky event corresponds to a
slow and faint transient event associated with a ∼106M⊙SMBH
with previously ongoing accretion, which has implications for
the evolution observed (e.g., interaction between the accreted
and precursor material; Zhang et al. 2025). We note however that
800
observationally, the available data do not decisively favour any
single scenario:
– Scenario A: an LLAGN. An LLAGN sustained at log λEdd ≈
−3.6 (RIAF) for at least several thousand years naturally
reproduces the Lion,min profile, the pre-2019 BBB emission
required by the GALEX photometry (Sánchez-Sáez et al.
2024), and the optically thin dust (Elitzur & Shlosman
2006). Crucially, the weak, compact [Ne v] is expected: for
Lbol ≈1040.5 erg s−1 and MBH ≈106 M⊙, the Eddington ratio
is λEdd ≈2×10−4 (log λEdd ≈−3.6), at or below the threshold
810
of log(Lbol/LEdd) = −3.5+0.6
−1.0 below which the ReveaLLAGN
sample of Goold et al. (2026) falls systematically below the
L[Ne v]–LX relation. The data are also consistent with a still-
accreting LLAGN that may have been gradually declining in
luminosity over the last 1500 years.
– Scenario B: a long-lived TDE remnant disc. The frame-
work of Mummery et al. (2025) demonstrates that TDE discs
can power EELRs, coronal lines, and MIR line ratios that
mimic a classical AGN on standard diagnostic diagrams,
without requiring one. A freely evolving remnant declines
820
as Lbol ∝t−1.2 (Cannizzo et al. 1990; Mummery et al. 2025),
while the disc temperature cools more slowly (Tp ∝t−0.3),
sustaining ionising radiation for tION ∼200 yr. Light-travel-
time effects (isodelay contours) and the long recombination
timescales of low-density (nH ∼101–103 cm−3) gas then al-
low a single disruption to produce observable EELR features
for 103–104 yr after the TDE disc itself has faded. The en-
ergetics are consistent: L ≈1040.5 erg s−1 over 5 × 103 yr
requires only ∼5 × 1051 erg, or ∼0.03 M⊙accreted at
η = 0.1, well within a single solar-mass disruption. Our
830
reconstructed Lion,min implies ongoing accretion over the
last ∼1500 years. Sustaining an ionising output over this
timescale would require either a favourable cloud geometry
or a disc fed over an extended period, for instance through re-
peated and/or partial tidal disruptions, whose rates can reach
∼10−1 galaxy−1 yr−1 under certain conditions (see Sec-
tion 4.5 of Wevers & French 2024, and references therein).
Recent works have claimed either a connection between
faded AGNs and TDE and QPE events, or a connection between
turn-on AGNs and TDEs. Our results can be associated with
840
both. The gas-rich minor-merger host, the MBH ∼106 M⊙, and
the kpc-scale EELR match the properties that other works have
associated with recently faded AGNs in QPE and TDE hosts
(e.g., Jiang & Pan 2025; Xiong et al. 2025). Scenario A, how-
ever, does not require a faded nucleus: the Lion,min profile is con-
sistent with ongoing accretion before the Ansky event, at either a
roughly constant luminosity or with a slow decline. This places
Ansky at an earlier stage than the fully faded hosts, but possi-
bly within the same evolutionary picture. Independently, both
scenarios A and B can be linked to the “starfall” mechanism of
850
McKernan et al. (2022), in which the loss of orbital angular mo-
mentum during the first ∼0.1 Myr of an AGN phase drives a
heavy rain of stars onto the SMBH. This connection is most di-
rect for Scenario A, where the rain of stars feeds an ongoing
low-level nucleus; in Scenario B, the same process could supply
the star whose disruption produced the long-lived remnant disc.
In either case, the kinematically misaligned gas observed in An-
sky supports this picture: external accretion can provide both the
fuel and the angular-momentum perturbations required to feed
the SMBH (see Storchi-Bergmann & Schnorr-Müller 2019, for
860
a review), and thereby enhance the rate of TDEs.
4.2. Implications for the QPE population
IFS studies of QPE hosts have shown EELRs and ele-
vated [O iii] emission (Wevers et al. 2024; Xiong et al. 2025).
Jiang & Pan (2025) attributed these to recently faded AGNs.
SDSS1335+0728 complicates this picture. As shown above, the
fossil ionisation tracers in Ansky’s host are degenerate between
an ongoing LLAGN (Scenario A) and a long-lived TDE remnant
disc (Scenario B), and neither viable scenario requires a pre-
viously luminous, now-totally-faded nucleus. At the population
870
level, this implies that the EELR and elevated [O iii] used to infer
faded AGN are not, on their own, a unique signature of fading:
an ongoing low-level or transient accretion source reproduces
the same fossil signatures. We refrain from concluding that the
population-level faded-AGN interpretation must be revised on
the basis of a single object. Spatially resolved analyses of fur-
ther QPE hosts with VLT/MUSE NFM or JWST MIRI/MRS are
needed before the population-level scenario can be reassessed.
The lack of QPEs detected before February 2024 also de-
serves comment. It is worth recalling how poorly the earlier
880
epochs were sampled. The QPEs in Ansky are days-long soft
X-ray flares that recur roughly every 4 −15 days, with vary-
ing period and flaring time scales, yet the pre-2019 soft X-ray
record of SDSS1335+0728 amounts to only a handful of archival
snapshots, with no sensitive dedicated monitoring, so a non-

detection over this period tells us very little about whether QPEs
were actually occurring. If the absence is nonetheless real, the
disc–orbiter framework (e.g. Linial & Sari 2023; Franchini et al.

it. The simplest possibility ties directly to Scenario A of Sec-

at λEdd ≈10−4, collisions between the orbiter and the disc
would have produced much weaker and softer flares than the
present-day eruptions, most likely below any detectable level.
The 2019 event would then have assembled the thin, dense disc
that the QPE mechanism requires, which would explain why the
eruptions only appeared after the transient. Other configurations
could produce the same outcome: the earlier disc may simply
have been too compact to be struck at the orbiter’s pericentre, or
the orbiter may have shared the plane of the pre-existing disc,

so that no detectable eruptions arose until the 2019 event built a
new, misaligned disc.
In all of these channels the minor-merger history provides
the dynamical origin: merger-driven loss-cone scattering can
produce both the TDEs (Ansky event) and the QPE orbiter,
while the ongoing ring of star formation confirms that the
merger was recent enough to leave nuclear dynamical imprints.
SDSS1335+0728 is thus the first QPE host in which resolved
host-galaxy dynamics can be connected directly to the nuclear
transient phenomena.

might have contributed significantly to the observed circum-

possible that the radiation from those prior active periods con-

Ansky, given the large mass budget inferred from the outflows,
which place an upper limit of ≲1000 eruptions before total en-
ergy budget of the orbiter-SMBH system is depleted. We thus
consider it unlikely that the QPEs themselves contribute signifi-

cantly to the observed ionisation profile.
5. Summary
We have presented spatially resolved optical (VLT/MUSE WFM
and NFM) and mid-infrared (JWST MIRI/MRS) spectroscopy
of SDSS1335+0728, the host galaxy of the Ansky event and the
most extreme known QPE source. Our main findings are as fol-
lows.
The stellar kinematics reveal a counter-rotating outer region
and kinematically decoupled gas with extremely low velocity
dispersion (σgas ≲60 km s−1), consistent with a past minor
940
merger involving retrograde gas accretion (with respect to the
original rotation sense of the galaxy). The accreted gas fuelled
the ring-like star formation confirmed independently by the BPT
diagnostics (Section 3.1.2), by the younger light-weighted stel-
lar ages at comparable radius (Section 3.1.5), and by the spa-
tially extended low-IP MIR emission (Section 3.2.2). The near-
solar central metallicity superimposed on an old, metal-poorer
outer region is likewise consistent with merger-triggered, cen-
trally concentrated star formation. This minor-merger history
places SDSS1335+0728 among the gas-rich, post-merger en-
950
vironments that are preferentially found among TDE and QPE
hosts (Wevers & French 2024; Wevers et al. 2024), although its
ongoing star formation distinguishes it from the post-starburst
and quiescent Balmer-strong galaxies that are over-represented
among these hosts relative to the general galaxy population. The
M–σ relation yields MBH ∼106 M⊙, in agreement with previous
estimates and with the mass range of known QPE hosts.
BPT and [Ne iii]/[Ne ii] diagnostic maps consistently iden-
tify a three-zone radial structure: a nucleus ionised by SMBH
accretion, an intermediate star-forming ring at ∼0.5–1.4 kpc con-
960
firmed by the youngest light-weighted stellar ages and extended
low-IP MIR emission, and a LINER-like outer region. High-IP
coronal lines ([Ne vi], [Ne v], [O iv]) are confined to the nuclear
region, and are associated to the ionising flux created by the An-
sky event.
A Balmer-line light-echo analysis yields a minimum ionis-
ing luminosity of log Lion,min ∼40.5 erg s−1 across the accretion-
driven-classified bins over at least ∼1 500 yr of look-back time.
This demonstrates that the nucleus was not dormant before De-
cember 2019; rather, the Ansky event temporarily elevated the
970
luminosity above a pre-existing baseline. This is consistent with
the SED analysis presented in Sánchez-Sáez et al. (2024), in
which BBB emission was required to account for the UV emis-
sion prior to 2019.
Broad silicate emission at 9.7 and 18 µm indicates optically
thin dust inconsistent with a classical AGN torus, compatible
with both MIR-selected TDEs and LLAGNs with dissipating
tori.
We propose two viable scenarios for the pre-2019 accre-
tion history: (A) an LLAGN at log λEdd ≈−3.6, either per-
980
sistent over the last several thousand years or gradually fad-
ing but not yet dormant, or (B) a long-lived TDE remnant
disc powering the extended emission. In both scenarios, the
Ansky event corresponded to a slow and faint transient event
in a ∼106 M⊙SMBH with already ongoing accretion. Both
challenge the “faded AGN” interpretation recently invoked for
QPE host galaxies: if Scenario B applies more broadly, the
faded-AGN signatures proposed for other QPE hosts may in-
stead trace prior stellar disruptions. The minor-merger history
of SDSS1335+0728 provides the dynamical channel connecting
990
host-galaxy evolution to nuclear transient phenomena, making
it the first QPE host in which resolved kinematics tie a merger-
driven environment to the gas-fuelling conditions conducive to
nuclear transient activity.

Appendix A: MUSE Stellar Continuum and Emission-Line Fitting with pPXF
Each MUSE WFM and NFM Voronoi bin was fitted using pPXF (Cappellari 2023). Stellar kinematics and emission-line fluxes
were extracted simultaneously fitting stellar continuum templates drawn from the MILES SSP library (Vazdekis et al. 2010) with a
Kroupa (2001) IMF and BaSTI isochrones (Pietrinferni et al. 2004). The fits were done over the rest-frame range 4600–7200 Å. Prior
to fitting, the MILES templates (native resolution FWHMMILES = 2.51 Å) were convolved to the wavelength-dependent MUSE LSF
following Bacon et al. (2017), with the observed-frame LSF FWHM de-redshifted to the rest-frame before computing the quadrature
difference; spectra and templates were then log-rebinned onto a common velocity scale with a template-to-galaxy oversampling 1180
ratio of 2. The fit included a degree-4 additive Legendre polynomial to account for residual continuum shape mismatches, and
stellar kinematics were recovered with two kinematic moments (V and σ; bounds [−500, +500] and [10, 500] km s−1). We note that
no differences in the fits are found when using four Gauss–Hermite moments (V, σ, h3, h4).
ZTF photometry contemporaneous with the MUSE observations confirms ongoing accretion activity; thus, an additional
accretion-driven continuum component was included in the fit of the central region (r < 0′′.5 for the NFM, and r < 1′′ for the
WFM), modelled as a linear combination of power laws Fλ ∝λ α with slopes α ∈{−3.5, −3.0, −2.5, −2.0, −1.5, −1.0, −0.5, −0.1}
normalised at 5500 Å; outside this aperture the power-law component was omitted.
Narrow emission-line templates for Hβ, [O iii]λλ4959,5007, [O i]λλ6300,6364, [N ii]λλ6548,6583, Hα, and [S ii]λλ6716,6731
were generated simultaneously via emission_lines and fitted with two kinematic moments (V, σ; bounds [−1500, +1500] and
[1, 600] km s−1), with no constraints imposed on flux ratios; bad pixels were linearly interpolated prior to log rebinning and excluded 1190
from the fit via the goodpixels mask. As for the stellar component, we note that no differences in the fits are found when using
four Gauss–Hermite moments (V, σ, h3, h4).
The reduced-χ2 of the WFM pPXF fits per bin has a median, 2.5% percentile, and 97.5% percentile of 1.01, 0.75, and 2.54,
respectively. For the NFM, the reduced-χ2 has a median, 2.5% percentile, and 97.5% percentile of 4.73, 1.32, and 23.23, respectively,
however when restricting the analysis to the inner ring within a radius of 1′′.5, these correspond to 3.11, 1.24, 7.57, respectively.
Figures A.1 and A.2 show representative pPXF spectral fits for a selection of spatial bins spanning the full radial range of the
galaxy. The stellar continuum, accretion-driven power-law continuum (nuclear bins only), and narrow emission-line components
are shown separately, demonstrating the quality of the simultaneous decomposition across regions of varying ionisation and stellar
population properties.

Appendix B: MUSE PSF characterisation using the accretion-driven power-law continuum
1200
The PSF of the WFM observations was estimated empirically from unsaturated field stars detected within the MUSE FoV using
DAOStarFinder (Stetson 1987) available in photutils3, which identifies point sources via a Gaussian-derivative convolution
kernel; each detected source was fitted with a two-dimensional Gaussian profile to derive the PSF FWHM. From this, we estimated
a PSF of 0′′.62 at 6720 Å (at the Hα region).
As an independent consistency check, we also characterised the PSF from the spatial profile of the AGN power-law continuum
component recovered by pPXF in the nuclear bins. Since the AGN is unresolved at the WFM resolution, its power-law continuum
flux density at 6200 Å, evaluated per spatial bin and averaged in radial windows of ∆r = 0′′.2 (one WFM pixel), traces the PSF
directly; the half-flux radius r50 of this profile therefore provides an estimate of the PSF half-width at half-maximum (HWHM), and
thus FWHMPSF ≈2 r50. From this, we obtained a PSF of 0′′.64 at 6200 Å (rest-frame). Therefore, both methods yield consistent
PSF FWHM estimates, validating the power-law profile approach.

Appendix C: JWST MIRI/MRS spectral fitting
Emission-line fluxes and kinematic parameters were measured from the stitched 1D spectrum for a set of 17 targeted tran-
sitions, comprising high-IP coronal lines ([Mg vii], [Mg v], [Ne vi], [Fe vii], [S iv], [Ne v]λ14.32, [Ne iii]λ15.56, [Ne v]λ24.32,
[O iv]λ25.89), low-IP ISM and star-formation tracers ([Fe ii]λ5.34, [Ar ii]λ6.99, [Ne ii]λ12.81, [Fe ii]λ17.94, [S iii]λ18.71), and H2
rotational lines (S(5)λ6.91, S(3)λ9.67, S(1)λ17.04). Each line was fitted independently with a single Gaussian profile plus a local
linear continuum, determined from side-band regions lying between ±700 and ±3500 km s−1 from the line centre via a weighted
least-squares fit using the inverse variance as weights, within a total fitting window of ±4000 km s−1. The continuum-subtracted 1220
spectrum was fitted using scipy.optimize.curve_fit (Virtanen et al. 2020), with the amplitude constrained to be positive and
the maximum Gaussian dispersion limited to 8000 km s−1. From the best-fit parameters we derived the integrated line flux, the
FWHM, and the velocity offset from systemic. The SNR was defined as the fitted amplitude divided by the rms of the continuum
residuals in the side-band windows. A line was considered detected if it simultaneously satisfied SNR ≥3, |∆v| ≤1000 km s−1, and
FWHM ≥100 km s−1.
Spatially resolved emission-line maps were produced by applying the same single-Gaussian fitting procedure independently at
each spaxel of the MIRI/MRS spectral cubes. The local noise was estimated per spaxel from the rms of the continuum residuals,
and spaxels with non-zero data-quality flags in more than half the channels within the line window were excluded. Fits yielding
FWHM < 100 km s−1 were rejected as unresolved noise features, and only spaxels with peak SNR ≥3 were retained. Cube surface
brightnesses (MJy sr−1) were converted to line fluxes per spaxel (erg s−1 cm−2 spaxel−1) using the spaxel solid angle derived from 1230
the CDELT1 keyword of each cube header, which varies from 0′′.13 in channel 1 to 0′′.35 in channel 4. All maps were reprojected
onto a common astrometric grid of 0′′.20 pixel−1 using bilinear interpolation (reproject; Robitaille et al. 2020).

Figure D.1 shows the cumulative [O iii]λ5007 luminosity as a function of aperture radius in the WFM field, integrating the per-bin

Fig. G.1: Measurement of the 9.7 µm and 18 µm silicate feature strengths in SDSS J1335+0728. The grey curve shows the observed
MIRI/MRS spectrum (rest-frame). The red dashed line shows the power-law continuum fit of Goold et al. (2026) (α = 1.09),
anchored in the rest-frame windows 7.3–7.7 and 13.5–14.0 µm (shaded regions). The blue dot-dashed line shows the natural cubic
spline continuum of Masterson et al. (2025), with anchor points indicated by blue diamonds at 5.0, 6.25, 7.5, 14.0, 23.0, and 25.5 µm.
We measure the silicate feature strength from the 1D JWST MIRI/MRS spectrum using two independent continuum estimation
methods shown in Figure G.1. Following Goold et al. (2026), we fit a power-law continuum anchored at 7.3–7.7 and 13.5–14.0 µm
(rest-frame) and define the silicate strength as S sil = ln(fobs/fcont) at 9.7 µm (S sil[9.7µm]), adopting the original Spoon et al. 1250
(2007) sign convention (note that Goold et al. 2026 followed the sign convention of Spoon et al. 2022). Moreover, following
Masterson et al. (2025), we alternatively fit a cubic spline through six rest-frame anchor points at 5.0, 6.25, 7.5, 14.0, 23.0, and
25.5 µm and measured S sil at 9.7 µm and 18 µm (S sil[18µm]). Following power-law continuum continuum approach, we obtained
S sil[9.7µm] = 0.99, while following the cubic spline continuum approach, we obtained S sil[9.7µm] = 1.12 and S sil[18µm] = 0.36.
