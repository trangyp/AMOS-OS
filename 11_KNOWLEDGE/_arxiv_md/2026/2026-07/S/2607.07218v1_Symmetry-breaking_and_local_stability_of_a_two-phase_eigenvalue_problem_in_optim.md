Symmetry-breaking and local stability of a two-phase eigenvalue
problem in optimal insulation
Emanuele Cristoforoni, Federico Villone
Abstract
We consider the first eigenvalue, λβ(Ω, A), of a two-phase eigenvalue problem for the Laplacian
with Robin boundary conditions, where the two phases are characterised by different ellipticity
constants. We characterise the conditions under which a ball BR is a local minimum under a
volume constraint for the minimisation problem A 7→λβ(Br, A), in terms of the principal Neumann
eigenvalue of the fixed inner ball Br.
Keywords: symmetry-breaking, Faber-Krahn, shape optimisation, Robin Boundary
conditions, thermal insulation

The optimisation of two-phase problems, and in particular two-phase eigenvalue problems, has been
extensively studied in the context of optimal design. Namely, fixing the outer set A and allowing the
inner set Ω, or rather the ellipticity constant, to vary. We refer, for instance, to [19, 18, 23, 39]. It
is well known that this type of control problem usually does not admit a solution (see, for instance,
[16, 15] and the references within). However, in the particular case in which A is a ball, the problem
admits a radially symmetric solution [5, Theorem 2.1], but not necessarily given by a ball [17, 37].
The case of (1.2) where the inner set Ωis fixed and A is allowed to vary, has been partially addressed
in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions in [23, Section 3] from the point of view of shape variation.
On the other hand, optimisation problems of this kind have been extensively studied in the thin-layer
regime, that is, in the case where the insulating layer Σah = A \ ¯Ωcan be described as a normal
sub-graph on the boundary of Ωof a smooth, positive function h times the parameter a,
Σah = {x ∈Rn : x = y + tνΩ(y), y ∈∂Ω, 0 < t < ah(y)},
and the problem is studied in the limit as a →0+. We refer to [30, 10, 4] for the characterisation of
the limit equations (see also [42, 38, 41, 2, 21]).
In this asymptotic regime, optimisation problems of the form (1.2) reduce to an optimisation
problem in the control variable h, and the measure constraint on A is approximated as a constraint on

This behaviour is in stark contrast with the classical Faber–Krahn inequality [29, 35], and its Robin
counterpart due to Bossel and Daners [9, 25], which assert that, in the one-phase case (i.e. a = 1), the
ball uniquely minimises (1.2) for every β > 0 and every m > 0. Thus, when a and the thickness of the
layer A \ Ωapproach 0, symmetry-breaking phenomena may occur, while in the case a = 1 this is not
the case. This raises a natural question:
"In which regimes can symmetry-breaking be detected and characterised directly for problem (1.2),
without passing to a limiting problem?"
In the present paper we answer this question for smooth, local perturbations of the outer ball.
Our main result shows that, locally, symmetry-breaking can be established directly at the level of the
original functional, under suitable conditions on the parameters of the problem.
In this spirit, we fix Ω= Br the ball centred at the origin of radius r. We are interested in
understanding whether balls centred at the origin are local minima of (1.2). We consider R > 0 such
that |BR| = m and we optimise λβ(Br, ·) in the class of nearly spherical domains with fixed measure,
defined in Definition 2.2. We divide the cases a < 1 and a > 1, and we obtain the following results.
