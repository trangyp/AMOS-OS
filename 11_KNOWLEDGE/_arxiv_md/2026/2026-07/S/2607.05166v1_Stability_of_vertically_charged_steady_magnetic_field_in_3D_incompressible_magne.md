STABILITY OF VERTICALLY CHARGED STEADY MAGNETIC FIELD IN 3D
INCOMPRESSIBLE MAGNETO-MICROPOLAR FLUIDS WITHOUT
MAGNETIC AND ANGULAR VISCOSITY IN A STRIP DOMAIN
ZEFU FENG, GUANGYI HONG∗, JIAHONG WU, AND KUN ZHAO
Abstract. This paper intends to understand the regularity and stability problem on the 3D incom-
pressible magneto-micropolar equations with zero magnetic and angular viscosities in a strip domain.
The magneto-micropolar system models the electrically conducting micropolar fluid in the presence of
a magnetic field. The lack of magnetic diffusion and angular dissipation makes it impossible to prove
even small data global well-posedness result, let alone general large data global regularity. This paper
presents a steady-state setup around which any perturbations can be shown to be globally regular
and stable. More precisely, any small perturbation near a steady magnetic field perpendicular to the
horizontal boundary leads to a unique global classical solution. In addition, the solution is shown to
converge to the steady state at an almost exponential rate as time goes to infinity. These appear to
be the very first rigorous global results on the magneto-micropolar equations concerned here.

We recall some background information. Micropolar fluids are characterized by nonsymmetric
stress tensor and exhibit micro-rotational effects and micro-rotational inertia (cf. [9]). Physical ex-
amples of micropolar fluids include liquid crystals made up of dumbbell particles, animal blood,
bubbly liquids, and ferrofluids, etc (cf. [8, 9]). Comparing with general microfluids, micropolar fluids
are more manageable from the modeling perspective. The motion of an incompressible and electri-
cally conducting micropolar fluid in the presence of a magnetic field in R3 can be modeled by the
following system (cf. [2, 9]):

Mathematical study of the magneto-micropolar equations has been carried out since the 1970s.
We recall some results that are relevant to this work. For the fully dissipative model (1.2) (i.e.,
µ, ν, ζ, κ1, κ2, κ3 > 0), the existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions were studied by Rojas-
Medar-Boldrini [27] and Xiang-Yang [35].
A blow-up criterion for the 2D Cauchy problem was
established by Wang-Wang [34]. For the 2D model with mixed partial viscosities, the global well-
posedness of classical solutions was obtained by Cheng-Liu [5] and Regmi-Wu [24]. Later, Yamazaki
[37] investigated the global regularity of the 2D system with zero angular viscosities (κ1 = κ2 =
κ3 = 0). Recently, Lin-Xiang [21] proved the global well-posedness and long-time behavior of strong
solutions around the equilibrium (0, e1, 0) for the fully dissipative 2D model. More results on the
magneto-micropolar equations can be found in [23, 29, 30, 38].
To put things into broader perspective, we note that system (1.2) reduces to the incompressible
magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations when ζ = 0 and w ≡0. There is a rich literature on
the mathematical studies of the incompressible MHD model. For the viscous and resistive system
(i.e., µ > 0 and ν > 0), the local well-posedness of large-data solutions and global well-posedness
of small-data solutions were established in the classical Sobolev space Hs(Rd) (s ≥d) by Duvaut-
Lions [7].
The global well-posedness result in the two-dimensional space was later improved by
Sermange-Temam [28] by removing the smallness restriction on the initial data. Cao et al. [3, 4]
further upgraded the result of [28] to the 2D model with anisotropic dissipation and resistivity. For
the viscous and non-resistive system (i.e., µ > 0 and ν = 0), Lin-Xu-Zhang [20] and Xu-Zhang [36]
constructed global classical solutions near non-trivial steady magnetic fields to the Cauchy problem
in two- and three-dimensional spaces, respectively (see [26, 32] for similar results in horizontal strip
domains). The long-time behaviors of such solutions were investigated in [25] for the 2D Cauchy
problem and in [32] for the 3D IBVP in a strip domain. In addition to the mentioned works on the
incompressible MHD system, we would also like to refer the readers to [10, 14–17, 19, 28] for related
results on the compressible MHD model.
In this paper, we are interested in the mathematical analysis of (1.2) when the magnetic and
angular viscosities are negligible, i.e., ν = κ1 = κ2 = κ3 = 0. Our work is motivated by recent studies
on the 2D magneto-micropolar equations without angular viscosities [37], the 3D non-resistive MHD
equations [32], and the 3D compressible version of (1.1) [11]. However, the problem studied here is
considerably more complicated than its predecessors. On one side, comparing with the 2D micro-
rotationally inviscid (i.e., κ1 = κ2 = κ3 = 0) version of (1.2), the higher dimensionality and absence
of the magnetic diffusion bring the difficulty of the mathematical analysis of (1.1) to a different level.
On the other side, in addition to the lack of magnetic diffusion (the main difficulty of the non-resistive
MHD), the coupling of the fluid and angular velocity fields through curls presents an anti-symmetric
form in the system, which formally leads to the loss of derivatives of ˜w. Such a situation has been
encountered in the recent study of the compressible version of (1.1) [11], where the global dynamics
of classical solutions to an IBVP of the model is studied via Lagrangian formulation and the two-tier
energy method developed in [12, 13, 32]. One of the main ideas in [11] is to study an auxiliary system
formed by the equations of the fluid momentum and the vorticity of the angular velocity. Through
the auxiliary system an intrinsic coupling structure is captured and the desired estimates of ˜u and
˜w are established via elliptic theory and div-curl analysis. For the incompressible model, however,
since the pressure function is determined by an elliptic equation involving both the fluid and angular
velocity fields, rather than the density function alone in the compressible case, the crucial analysis
in [11] cannot be accessed for the problem considered in this paper. Collectively, the synergy of
high dimensionality, absence of magnetic and angular viscosities, and strong coupling between the
unknown functions presents a significant challenge to the mathematical analysis of (1.1).
