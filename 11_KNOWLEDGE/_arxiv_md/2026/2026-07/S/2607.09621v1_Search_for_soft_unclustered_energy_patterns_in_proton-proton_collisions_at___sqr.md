Search for soft unclustered energy patterns in
proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeV using data scouting
The CMS Collaboration*
Abstract
A search for soft unclustered energy patterns (SUEPs) is conducted using proton-
proton collision data corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 127 fb−1 at a
center-of-mass energy of 13 TeV, collected via the data scouting stream of the CMS
experiment at the LHC. Only the results of the high-level trigger reconstruction are
recorded to enable a lower threshold on the hadronic activity. This increases the ac-
ceptance for SUEP signatures, which are predicted by hidden-valley models with a
large ’t Hooft coupling. The observed results are consistent with the standard model
background prediction. The most stringent limits to date are set on the gluon fusion
production of heavy scalar mediators resulting in SUEP-like signals.

Hidden-valley models with a strongly coupled dark sector (DS) [1] are a credible possibility for
physics beyond the standard model (BSM) at the CERN LHC. These models can provide dark-
matter candidates and realizations of neutral naturalness [2]—a solution to the hierarchy prob-
lem [3] involving new particles not charged under standard-model (SM) gauge groups. They
can also lead to a wide range of unconventional signatures [4–7]. The observable phenomena
from collider production are determined by the coupling between the hidden and SM sectors
and by the dynamics of the DS [1]. Specifically, soft unclustered energy patterns (SUEPs),
which consist of high multiplicities of diffusely distributed, low-momentum charged particles,
are difficult to distinguish from SM backgrounds at the trigger level in current LHC experi-
ments. This Letter presents a search for SUEPs from the decay of a scalar mediator produced
by gluon-gluon fusion (ggF), using the data-scouting strategy [8] of the CMS experiment. With
limited bandwidth, data scouting lowers trigger thresholds relative to standard data taking
by recording only partial information for each event, namely the trigger-level reconstruction
output, thereby increasing acceptance for SUEP signals.
At the LHC, DS particles can be produced via a mediator that couples to both the SM par-
ticles and the DS. The DS extends the SM with a new non-Abelian gauge group including
dark quarks and dark gluons, analogous to the SM quarks and gluons, with behavior similar
to the SM quantum chromodynamics (QCD). If the dark-quark masses are below the dark-
confinement scale, ΛD, and ΛD ≪√s, the dark quarks will shower and hadronize to dark
hadrons, which may subsequently decay to SM particles. Typical models assume collinear
emissions leading to collimated jets similar to those in the SM QCD. However, if the DS pos-
sesses a large ’t Hooft coupling (λ ≡αDNcD ≫1, where αD is the dark QCD coupling constant
and NcD is the number of dark colors), then large-angle emissions are unsuppressed for en-
ergies above the confinement scale [6]. In this case, DS particles will be emitted isotropically
in the center-of-mass frame of the shower without large hierarchies in energy. This process is
analogous to hadron production in QCD at high temperatures [9–12]. If there is a large mass
difference between the mediator and the dark hadrons, radiation is efficient over a large energy
range during the showering, resulting in a large multiplicity of low-momentum particles, the
lightest states in the DS spectrum [13]. The distribution of the dark-hadron momenta can be
described by a relativistic Boltzmann distribution [14], characterized only by the dark-hadron
mass and a temperature parameter related to the confinement scale. Similar signatures of large
multiplicity and isotropic events are predicted in scenarios with microscopic black holes in
models with large extra spatial dimensions [15].
This search targets a broad range of experimentally accessible variations of the characteris-
tic SUEP signature. These variations are encompassed by simplified models with a few pa-
rameters that control the kinematic behavior of the SUEP cluster. In the simplified models,
the DS is accessed by a heavy scalar mediator S produced via ggF through an effective cou-
pling, SGµνGµν or SGµν eG
µν, and assumed to decay to a SUEP with 100% branching fraction.
The nominal signal cross sections correspond to BSM Higgs boson production via a point-like
effective interaction [16] compatible with experimental limits. The calculation includes next-
to-next-to-next-to-leading order QCD corrections, but no additional electroweak or off-shell
production effects, and uses the PDF4LHC15 nnlo 100 set of parton distribution functions [17].
The showering and hadronization in the DS are modeled as the emission of low-mass dark
ϕ mesons, with momenta sampled from a relativistic Boltzmann distribution that depends on
the dark-meson mass mϕ and temperature TD at the confinement scale ΛD. High-multiplicity
low-momentum emissions are obtained by requiring mϕ and TD to be at the same energy scale
of a few GeV. The dark mesons decay into a pair of dark photons A′ via a ϕA′µνA′
µν operator.

Simulated SUEP events are generated with a custom package [6] interfaced with the PYTHIA
8.240 [18] generator for mediator masses mS ranging from 125 GeV to 2 TeV, dark-meson masses
mϕ from 1 to 8 GeV, and temperatures TD from mϕ/4 to 4mϕ. Three dark-photon scenarios, with
different masses mA′ and corresponding e+e−, µ+µ−, and π+π−decay modes, are considered:
mA′ = 0.5 GeV and lepton-dominated decays with branching fractions B = 40, 40, and 20%,
respectively; mA′ = 0.7 GeV and hadron-dominated decays with B = 15, 15, and 70%, respec-
tively; and mA′ = 1 GeV with only fully hadronic decays A′ →π+π−. The mediator mass,
dark-meson mass, temperature, and dark-photon decay mode collectively determine the mul-
tiplicity, energy spectrum, and flavor composition of the constituents of the SUEP cluster. The
PYTHIA generator with the CP5 underlying-event tune [19] is also used to simulate multiple
proton-proton (pp) interactions (pileup). The GEANT4 software [20] is used to simulate parti-
cle interactions with the CMS detector.
The CMS apparatus [21, 22] is a multipurpose, nearly hermetic detector, designed to recon-
struct and identify electrons, muons, photons, and hadrons [23–25]. A global “particle-flow”
(PF) algorithm [26] aims to reconstruct all individual particles in an event, combining infor-
mation provided by the all-silicon inner tracker and by the crystal electromagnetic and brass-
scintillator hadron calorimeters, operating inside a 3.8 T superconducting solenoid, with data
from the gas-ionization muon detectors embedded in the flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
The reconstructed particles are used to build jets and missing transverse momentum [27, 28].
Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system. The first level (L1), composed
of custom hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to
select events at a rate of around 100 kHz within a fixed latency of about 4 µs [29]. The second
level, known as the high-level trigger (HLT), consists of a farm of processors running a version
of the full event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing, and reduces the event
rate to around 1 kHz before data processing and storage for the standard physics program [30],
as well as around 5 kHz each for the scouting and parking streams [31].
The data analyzed in this Letter correspond to an integrated luminosity of 127 fb−1 from pp
collisions at √s = 13 TeV collected with the CMS detector during 2016–2018. The data were
recorded by requiring high hadronic activity at L1 and HLT, measured by HHLT

Primary vertices (PVs) are reconstructed by clustering tracks based on their distance of closest
approach to the pp interaction point along the z axis. The vertex with the largest Σp2
T of asso-
ciated tracks is identified as the PV. Charged PF candidates are required to have pT > 0.75 GeV
and |η| < 2.4, and only those associated with the PV are retained in order to distinguish high-
multiplicity, isotropic signal events from pileup collisions. The per-particle reconstruction ef-
ficiency for the signal ranges from 45 to 95%, depending on the charged-particle pT. This is
lower than the ≈90% offline efficiency because of the simplified tracking algorithm used to
satisfy the HLT computational constraints [8]. Jets are clustered from PF candidates using the
anti-kT (AK) algorithm with a distance parameter of R = 0.4 (AK4) [35, 36] and are required to
have pT > 30 GeV and |η| < 2.6.
Events that pass the trigger, basic data quality criteria, and the HT > 560 GeV requirement,
based on the HT calculated as a pT sum of the AK4 PF jets, which ensures the trigger is fully
efficient, are preselected. The SUEP final state particles are boosted to form a jet, with a radius
that scales as mS/pS

into wide AK jets with a distance parameter of R = 1.5 (AK15) and pT > 50 GeV. The R value
is chosen to contain as much of the SUEP activity as possible, while preserving separation from
the ISR system. The SUEP-ISR system is defined by the two AK15 jets with the largest pT.

The background estimation method is validated by applying the approach to simulated QCD
multijet events and comparing the predictions to the values directly taken from the simulation
in the SR. The method is further validated in data using the first SR bin [50, 60) as the validation
region (VR), as it has negligible signal contributions and is adjacent to the other SR bins, to test
the agreement between the predicted and observed yields and distributions. Details of the
validation are given in Appendix A.2.
The signal simulation modeling is subject to systematic uncertainties in the trigger efficiency,
the AK4 jet energy scale propagated to HT, the pileup distribution, the trigger prefiring proba-
bility in 2016–2017 [29], the integrated luminosity measurement [39–41], and the parton shower
modeling. For the mS = 125 GeV signal sample with the same mS and production mechanism
as the Higgs boson, higher-order corrections for the S kinematic behavior [42] are applied as
a pT-dependent reweighting with a corresponding uncertainty. The dominant uncertainty is
in the tracking efficiency, up to 50% for the highest nSUEP
constituent SR bins, while other uncertain-
ties are small. The tracking efficiency uncertainty is estimated by randomly removing tracks
based on the difference in the track reconstruction efficiency for D∗mesons in data and simula-
tion [43, 44]. These probabilities, measured for the offline tracks, are scaled up by a factor of ≈3
to account for the differences between the scouting and offline tracking efficiency uncertainties,
derived from the variations of the efficiency across data-taking periods.
A binned maximum likelihood fit [45] is performed to the nSUEP
constituent distribution, including the
CRs and SR, but excluding the VR, treating the three data-taking years separately. The expected
central value, the sum of background and signal, in each bin or region follows a Poisson dis-
tribution. The expected background yields in the SR bins are constrained by Eq. (1), while the
CR yields are unconstrained. The expected signal distributions are taken from simulation, and
the normalization is determined by the fit. The observations are consistent with the SM back-
ground predictions. The background-only fit results are shown in Fig. 2. The 95% confidence
level (CL) upper limits on the signal production cross section are computed using the modified
frequentist CLs [46, 47] approach with pseudo-experiments (Fig. 3) and an asymptotic approx-
imation [48] (Fig. 4), with similar performance. Limits as functions of the mediator mass for
several signal models are shown in Fig. 3 and compared to the limits set by an earlier search
based on offline reconstruction [32]. The present search improves the earlier constraints by as
much as one order of magnitude in most of the parameter space, except for a small region with
low nSUEP
constituent, where the lower scouting track reconstruction efficiency reduces the sensitivity.
Figure 4 presents the exclusion limits as functions of TD and mϕ, for models with different mS
values for fully hadronic A′ decays. The limits for signal models with leptonic A′ decays are
comparable, since they exhibit similar kinematic properties and the analysis does not explicitly
exploit particle flavor information. Tabulated results are available in the HEPData record for
this analysis [49].
In summary, a search has been reported for soft unclustered energy patterns (SUEPs) at the
LHC using scouting proton-proton collision data at √s = 13 TeV, corresponding to an inte-
grated luminosity of 127 fb−1. This data set is recorded by a high-level trigger with a lower
threshold on the scalar sum (HT) of jet transverse momenta (pT) compared to the standard
HT triggers, offsetting the increase in rate by saving limited event content. The characteristic
isotropic event shape of SUEP signals is recovered by analyzing the jet in the SUEP-candidate
rest frame and selecting particles from only the SUEP candidate, which is chosen as the jet with
the highest constituent multiplicity out of the two highest pT large-radius jets. The standard

Additional details
A.1
Corrections for the background estimation in the signal region
In the extended ABCD method, the nSUEP
constituent distribution in CR F is used as a proxy for the
background shape in the SR. However, because of higher-order correlations between nSUEP
constituent
and SSUEP
boosted, the SR background shape deviates from that observed in region F. This deviation
is estimated by comparing the normalized nSUEP
constituent distributions in regions F and C, moti-
vated by the fact that regions C, F, and the SR share the same nSUEP
constituent subdivisions and have
adjacent SSUEP
boosted ranges. Assuming only weak correlations between nSUEP
constituent and SSUEP
boosted, the
trend of the SR-to-F shape ratio (Nbin
SR /NSR)/(Nbin
F /NF) can be approximated by the F-to-C
shape ratio, (Nbin
F /NF)/(Nbin
C /NC).
The estimated F-to-C ratios are then used as correction factors cbin applied to the predicted
SR background distribution in Eq. (1). To mitigate statistical fluctuations in high-nSUEP
constituent
bins, where data are sparse, a linear fit is used to smooth the ratio as a function of constituent
multiplicity. To ensure a stable fit, the nSUEP
constituent histograms in regions F and C are constructed
with finer binning, especially at low multiplicities where event yields are higher. The linear
fit is performed on the bin-by-bin F-to-C ratios, weighted by the inverse squared statistical
uncertainties in the ratios. The fit is carried out using the following nSUEP
constituent bin boundaries:
50, 52, 54, 56, 58, 60, 62, 64, 66, 68, 70, 75, 80, and 120. The F-to-C ratios are fitted as a linear
function of nSUEP
constituent at the corresponding bin centers. The resulting linear function is then
evaluated at the centers of bins with lower edges at 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, and 120 GeV, used
in the SR estimation, to obtain the cbin values. For the last bin [120, ∞), the correction is taken
from the fit value at nSUEP
constituent = 125.
This procedure is repeated separately for each data-taking year (2016, 2017, and 2018), and
the corresponding year-specific corrections are applied to the prediction of the SR background
distribution.
A.2
Validations of the background estimation method
In Fig. 5, the extended ABCD background estimation is applied to the QCD multijet simulation
for data-taking years 2017 and 2018, and the predicted background in the SR is compared to the
event yields taken directly from the simulation in the SR. The results of the background-only
fit are compatible with the simulation. The prediction from two signal models with mS = 300
and 1000 GeV, TD = mϕ = 3 GeV, and mA′ = 1 GeV (fully hadronic decays) are also shown. The
signal distributions are distinct from the background.
