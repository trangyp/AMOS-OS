INSTABILITY OF GRAY SOLITONS IN A GROSS-PITAEVSKII MODEL WITH
A MOVING IMPURITY
PAOLO ANTONELLI AND MARTINO CALIARO
Abstract. The effect of a moving impurity in a dilute Bose-Einstein condensate is investigated by
means of the one-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii model (GP) with non-zero boundary conditions at
infinity. The impurity is modeled as a localized external potential, that travels at constant speed
v P R. In a co-moving reference frame, we study the existence and stability of time-independent
solutions. The latter are of physical relevance, being associated with the superfluid behavior of the
condensate.
For every non-zero velocity v in the subsonic regime, we show the existence of a family of time-
independent solutions which bifurcates from a (displaced) gray soliton ϕ0,vpx ´ s0q, with s0 P R,
of the GP equation. The position s0 is determined as an extremal point of an effective potential
explicitly defined. Moreover, we study the spectral stability of these states. For small values of the
potential strength, we show that the families originating from the maxima of the effective potential
are spectrally unstable. For this last result, we employ an Evans function approach. Finally, we
formally apply the instability result to the case of a repulsive delta potential.

On the other hand, for |v| ě 1 the only time-independent solution is the constant u “ 1. Dark solitons
play a central role in our analysis. They have been extensively studied in the past [8, 11] and they
are usually split into two kinds: the black soliton or kink, corresponding to the case v “ 0; and the
gray solitons, corresponding to v P p´1, 1qzt0u. As shown by Lin in [26] (see also [6, 8, 11]) and by Di
Menza and Gallo in [13] (see also [9, 32]), dark solitons are orbitally stable solutions to (GP-V).
The existence of time-independent solutions to (GP-V) for ε ‰ 0 has been studied by Pelinovsky and
Kevrekidis in [32] for the case v “ 0. By means of a Lyapunov-Schmidt procedure, they show the
existence of a family of time-independent solutions to (GP-V), which arise as regular perturbations of
the black soliton ϕ0,0. Moreover, combining an Evans function approach with a matched asymptotic
analysis, they show the linear instability of these states.
The case of our interest is the one in which v P p´1, 1qzt0u. In this regime, the existence of time-
independent solutions to (GP-V) with ε ‰ 0 has been studied by Hakim in [18] and by Mari¸s in
[27]. For weak potentials, i.e. for |ε| ăă 1, Hakim used a formal perturbation argument to show,
for each v P p´1, 1qzt0u and under a non-degeneracy condition, the existence of two families of time-
independent solutions. As ε varies from zero, one family bifurcates from the gray soliton ϕ0,v, and its
elements we denote by ϕε,v; the other family bifurcates from the constant solution u “ 1.
The stability of these two families of solutions is investigated by Hakim by means of numerical simu-
lations (see also [33, 34]). While the solutions belonging to the second family, i.e. the one bifurcating
from u “ 1, appear to be stable under (GP-V), the states ϕε,v appear to be unstable. The instability
manifests in a decrease in the fluid density at the impurity location, followed by the emission of gray
solitons in the downstream direction and sound waves in the upstream direction. This behavior is sim-
ilar to the one observed in the instability of steady flows for the two dimensional version of equation
(GP-V), with gray solitons replaced by quantum vortices [15].
The main goal of the present work is to prove the linear instability of the states ϕε,v, for each
v P p´1, 1qzt0u. The approach we follow consists in linearizing (GP-V) around ϕε,v and in study-
ing the corresponding eigenvalue problem Lεu “ λu. The linear instability of ϕε,v corresponds to the
existence of an eigenvalue λ P C with positive real part. Differently from the case v “ 0 treated in
[32], the matrix operator Lε is not off-diagonal. As we will see, this is due to the presence of the drift
term ivBxu in (GP-V) and to the complex-valued nature of gray solitons. Because of this structure,
the analysis of Lε will present additional difficulties with respect to the case v “ 0.
The main tool we use in order to locate the eigenvalues of Lε and to show the instability of ϕε,v is
the Evans function Epλ, εq, as we will see in Section 4. However, in order to fully utilize this tool, it
is useful to have more detailed information on the family of solutions ϕε,v. For this reason, we begin
by studying the existence and properties of the family of time-independent solution ϕε,v, providing in
this way a rigorous justification of the formal results in [18]. Then, we proceed to study the instability
of these states.

1.2. Linear instability of the family ϕε,v. After we studied the existence and properties of the
gray modes ϕε,vpx ´ sεq, we turn to study their stability. Our goal is to show that they are spectrally
unstable states. We do this by linearizing (GP-V) around one of these states and by studying the
corresponding eigenvalue problem Lεu “ λu. The eigenvalues of Lε determine the stability of the
wave. In particular, the instability of ϕε,vpx ´ sεq corresponds to the existence of an eigenvalue λ P C
of Lε with positive real part. As we describe below, the Evans function is the tool we use to determine
the existence of unstable eigenvalues.
When ε “ 0, due to the invariance of (GP-V) under translations and phase shifts, λ “ 0 is a multiple
eigenvalue of L0. When ε acquires a non-zero value the translational symmetry is broken, and the
zero eigenvalue associated with this symmetry splits into different eigenvalues. Our goal is to track
the motion of these eigenvalues as ε varies and to show that at least one of them enters the half-plane
tλ P C, ℜλ ą 0u, leading to the instability of the gray mode ϕε,vpx ´ sεq. As we will see, a main
mathematical difficulty lies in the fact that the imaginary axis belongs to the essential spectrum of Lε,
for any ε. Thus, the splitting of the zero eigenvalue is associated with an edge bifurcation, i.e. to the
bifurcation of eigenvalues out of the essential spectrum [22].
The major tool we use in order to track the location of the eigenvalues of Lε is the Evans function
Epλ, εq.
This is a complex-valued function with the property that Epλ, εq “ 0 if λ is an isolated
eigenvalue of Lε. A priori, the Evans function is defined only away from the essential spectrum of Lε,
and for this reason it cannot be immediately used in order to describe an edge bifurcation. However, as
shown by Kapitula and Sandstede in [23] and by Gardner and Zumbrun in [16] with the Gap Lemma,
under some assumptions the Evans function can be extended analytically across the essential spectrum.
Using this extension, we are able to study the bifurcation of the zero eigenvalue.

[22], where the persistence and stability of the black soliton ϕ0,0 under non-hamiltonian perturbations

the point λ “ 0 (and the whole imaginary axis). Then, we extend analytically the Evans function in
the vicinity of λ “ 0. Here, differently from [22] where Epλ, εq is extended on a 2-sheeted Riemann
surface, the extension is done directly in a neighborhood of zero in C (see Lemma 4.7). Finally, we
