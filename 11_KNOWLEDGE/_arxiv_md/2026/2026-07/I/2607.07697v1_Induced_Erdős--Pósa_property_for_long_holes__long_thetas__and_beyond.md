Induced Erdős–Pósa property
for long holes, long thetas, and beyond

Abstract
The induced Erdős–Pósa property in graphs relates the maximum packing of pairwise anti-adjacent copies
of an object with the minimal number of neighborhoods required to hit all copies. In this paper, the objects we
consider are long cycles and long thetas, both as induced minors. Let Ct denote the cycle with t vertices and
let Θt be the graph consisting of three internally disjoint and anti-adjacent paths, each with t internal vertices,
connecting the same pair of distinct vertices.
We show that for every fixed t, both Ct and Θt have the induced Erdős–Pósa property with respect to the
induced minor relation. More precisely, for every integer k and a graph G, one of the two outcomes occurs:
• G contains k pairwise vertex-disjoint and anti-adjacent copies of Ct (resp., Θt) as induced minors, or
• there is some X ⊆V (G) of size O(tk log k) such that the set N[X], consisting of X and its neighbors,
hits all Ct (resp., all Θt) induced minors in G.
This resolves in a strong form a special case of a conjecture of Ahn, Gollin, Huynh, and Kwon [SODA 2025].
From these results we derive that graphs that exclude kΘt as an induced minor admit balanced separators
consisting of the neighborhood of O(tk log k) vertices. This in turn resolves a special case of a conjecture
of Gartland and Lokshtanov and, combined with known techniques, yields a QPTAS for Maximum Weight
Independent Set and a number of its generalizations.

Introduction
Many classic combinatorial problems can be expressed as packing versus hitting certain objects in a graph. The
interplay between these two notions is a well established area of research in graph theory [Men27,Gal64,RS95].
The best known example is the case of cycles: we want to relate the size of a largest family, or packing, of vertex-
disjoint cycles in a graph, with the size of a smallest set of vertices intersecting all cycles (a feedback vertex set).
Clearly, the existence of a large cycle packing is evidence that we cannot hit all cycles with few vertices. However,
it is far from obvious whether this is the only structure that forces feedback vertex set to be large. By a celebrated
result of Erdős and Pósa [EP65], this is indeed true: Every graph G = (V, E) contains either k pairwise disjoint
cycles or a set X ⊆V of size O(k log k) such that G −X has no cycles. Furthermore, the function O(k log k)
governing the gap between packing and hitting outcomes turns out to be asymptotically optimal.
This sparked a flurry of results of similar flavour, now dubbed Erdős–Pósa properties, for variations of the initial
problem. Two main variations emerging from the literature consist in altering the objects being packed (cycles),
and altering the relation imposed on the packing (disjointness). The interplay between possible weakenings and
strengthenings of the two uncovers an intricate boundary of where the Erdős–Pósa property holds.
If we still ask for a disjoint packing, and wish to generalize the graphs being packed, we can not only consider
graphs other than cycles, but also alter their containment relation. One of the most general results in this vein,
due to Robertson and Seymour [RS86], is that planar graphs have the Erdős–Pósa property, with respect to the
minor relation. That is, for every planar graph H, there exists a function fH : N →N such that every graph
G either has k vertex-disjoint subgraphs, each containing H as a minor, or a set X of fH(k) vertices such that
G −X is H-minor-free. Furthermore, such a statement is false if H is not planar.
Following the original motivation of Erdős and Pósa, a long-studied line of research studies Erdős–Pósa
properties for variants of cycles, in particular, long cycles and holes. Regarding the former, it is easily derived
from [RS86] that for any t, cycles of length at least t satisfy the Erdős–Pósa property. Then, obtaining the best gov-
erning function (asymptotically) was an active effort [Tho88,BBR07], culminating in an optimal O(kt + k log k)
due to Mousset, Noever, Škorić, and Weissenberger [MNŠW17]. The case of holes, i.e., induced cycles of length
at least 4, was investigated by Kim and Kwon [KK20], who showed that they do indeed satisfy the Erdős–Pósa
property. On the negative side, they ruled out this possibility for induced cycles of length at least 5.
If we now wish to alter the packing relation, there are two directions in which one can go. On the one
hand, there are countless examples of objects that do not satisfy the Erdős–Pósa property, begging for weakened
packing notions. This is notably the case for non-planar minors or parity-constrained cycles, where the Erdős–
Pósa property has been recovered by asking for “half-integral” packings, see Reed [Ree99] and Liu [Liu22]. Of
particular relevance to our problem is the fact that no variation of the packing relation is known to recover the
Erdős–Pósa property for long holes, and it is known that the fractional packing relaxation fails [KK20]. On the
other hand, for objects that do satisfy the Erdős–Pósa property, one should look to strengthen the packing relation.
It is natural then to ask not only for disjointness, but also non-adjacency. Formally, an induced packing of some
object (for any containment relation), is a packing of pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent copies of it. Then, an
induced Erdős–Pósa property gives a counterpart to the non-existence of an induced packing.
With such a strong packing notion, what should the corresponding notion of a hitting set be? It is easy to see
that hitting with individual vertices is usually not enough,1 and the first natural weakening is to hit with closed
neighborhoods, i.e., radius-1 balls (which is canonical in the induced realm [HJ25]). That is, we should ask for
a few vertices X, such that their closed neighborhood N[X] hits all copies of cycles. This question sits among
many problems aiming at induced analogues of results known for subgraphs or minors, see e.g. [Kor23, Gar23].
This is in particular exemplified by the conjectured induced analogue of Menger’s theorem [HJ25], asking for
many pairwise non-adjacent paths, or a small set of vertices whose neighborhoods hit all paths.
Regarding the induced Erdős–Pósa property, the following was shown for long cycles.

Theorem 1.1 (Ahn, Gollin, Huynh, Kwon [AGHK25]). Let k ⩾1, t ⩾3 be integers. Every graph G = (V, E)
contains either:
• k pairwise disjoint and anti-adjacent cycles, each of length at least t, or
• a set X ⊆V of size O(t · k log k) such that G −N[X] has no induced cycle of length at least t.
We emphasize that the cycles found in Theorem 1.1 are non-necessarily induced. This leaves the following
question open: Do long induced cycles have the induced Erdős–Pósa property?
This question can be conveniently stated in terms of induced minors. A graph H is an induced minor of a graph
G, if it can be obtained from G by deleting vertices and contracting edges. We say that G is H-induced-minor-free
if it does not contain H as an induced minor.
With this terminology, we can formulate the following conjecture, of an induced minor analogue to the afore-
mentioned result of Robertson and Seymour for minors [RS86]. (For a connected graph H, we denote by kH the
graph consisting of k disjoint copies of H.)
Conjecture 1.2. For every planar graph H, there exists a function f : N →N such that every graph G = (V, E)
contains either:
• kH as an induced minor, or
• a set X ⊆V of size at most f(k) such that G −N[X] is H-induced-minor-free.
Conjecture 1.2 is a strengthening of a conjecture of Ahn, Gollin, Huynh, Kwon [AGHK25, Conjecture 8.5] whose
statement only requires that G becomes H-induced-minor-free after deleting f(k) balls of some constant radius.
Dujmović, Joret, Micek, and Morin [DJMM24] recently answered a question of Georgakopoulos and Papa-
soglu [GP25] and settled a coarse analog of Conjecture 1.2 for cycles, establishing the Erdős–Pósa property for
packing cycles at pairwise distance greater than d versus hitting cycles with radius-19d balls. Note that the d = 1
case is exactly the induced packing case.
With this background in mind, we proceed to present our results.
Induced Erdős–Pósa for long holes.
As the first result, we confirm Conjecture 1.2 for the case where H is any
cycle, answering the aforementioned question about the induced Erdős–Pósa property for long induced cycles.
Furthermore, our proof yields a polynomial-time algorithm that returns one of the two outcomes in the statement
of Conjecture 1.2.
Theorem 1.3 (Algorithmic induced Erdős–Pósa for long holes). There exists an algorithm that, given a graph G
and an integer t, in time |V (G)|O(t), outputs an integer k and the following objects:
• a family of k vertex-disjoint anti-adjacent induced cycles, each of length at least t (i.e., an induced minor model
of kCt in G); and
• a set X ⊆V (G) of size O(tk log k) such that G −N[X] does not contain any induced cycle of length at least
t (i.e., an induced minor model of Ct).
We remark that a slight modification of the arguments of [AGHK25] (take a 2-subdivision of the lower bound
construction for non-induced C⩾t of [MNŠW17]) yields a Ω(tk + k log k) lower bound for Theorem 1.3. This
leaves a small room for possible improvement.
Induced Erdős–Pósa for Long thetas and three-path-configurations.
Actually, Theorem 1.3 can be derived
from an analogous statement for another type of objects, namely, long three-path-configurations. Let us introduce
some notation first; see Figure 1 for an illustration.
By C⩾t we denote a cycle of length at least t. A theta, denoted by Θ, is any (multi)graph obtained from the
two-vertex multigraph Θ0 with three parallel edges by a series of edge subdivisions. A Θt is a Θ where each path
is of length exactly t + 1 (so that they have exactly t internal vertices each). A Θ⩾t is defined analogously with
“exactly” replaced by “at least.” Note that for every t ⩾1, every Θ⩾t contains Θt as an induced minor.

A t-long three path configuration (t-long 3PC), denoted Ξ⩾t, is a set of three induced paths, each of length
more than t (i.e., having at least t internal vertices), that are pairwise disjoint and anti-adjacent, except for the
following: one can specify which endpoint is a start and which is an end in such a manner, that all three starts
are either equal or pairwise distinct and adjacent, and the same holds for all three ends.
We have the following lemma saying that Ξ⩾t is almost an induced minor model of a Θt. A detailed proof for
it can be found in Section 4.1.
Lemma 1.4. Let t be a positive integer.
1. Every induced minor model of a Θ⩾t+2 contains a Ξ⩾t.
2. Every Ξ⩾t contains Θ⩾t as an induced minor.
Furthermore, given the assumed structure, the claimed structure can be found in polynomial time.
A t-long 3PC is almost like an induced minor model of a Θ⩾t, but there are some subtle differences in corner
cases (see Figure 3). In particular, it is relatively easy to check if a given graph contains a Ξ⩾t in time nO(t) (this
is formalized later as Lemma 6.4), while the complexity status of checking whether a graph contains Θt as an
induced minor remains open, with the t = 1 case solved recently [DDH+24]. This is the main reason we will be
able to obtain an algorithmic Erdős–Pósa result for Ξ⩾t, but only an existential one for Θt induced minors.
Theorem 1.5 (Induced Erdős–Pósa for long thetas). For every graph G and an integer t ⩾1, if k is the maximum
integer such that G contains kΘt as an induced minor, then G contains a set X ⊆V (G) of size O(tk log k) such
that G −N[X] is Θt-induced-minor-free.
Theorem 1.6 (Algorithmic induced Erdős–Pósa for long 3PCs). There exists an algorithm that, given a graph G
and an integer t, in time |V (G)|O(t), outputs an integer k and the following objects:
• a family of k vertex-disjoint anti-adjacent Ξ⩾ts in G; and
• a set X ⊆V (G) of size O(tk log k) such that G −N[X] does not contain any Ξ⩾t.
In Lemma 3.1, we show that in the class of graph without a Ξ⩾t, the cycles C⩾t have the induced Erdős–Pósa
property with the size of X bounded by O(tk). Consequently, the lower bound of Ω(tk + k log k) extends also
to Theorems 1.5 and 1.6.
Note that even though Θt and Ξ⩾t are closely related by Lemma 1.4 and both contain C⩾t, the results of
Theorems 1.3, 1.5 and 1.6 are not immediate consequences of each other. However, our approach actually allows
us to derive Theorems 1.3 and 1.5 from Theorem 1.6.
Dominated balanced separators.
It turns out that our approach can be used to derive some results outside
the realm of the (induced) Erdős–Pósa property. Let G be a graph with vertex weights w : V (G) →R⩾0. For
a set X ⊆V (G), we write w(X) = P
x∈X w(x). A balanced separator in (G, w) is a set S ⊆V (G) such that
every component of G −S has weight at most w(V (G))/2. We say that a class G of graphs admits d-dominated
balanced separators, for some integer d, if for every G ∈G and every weight function w, there exists X ⊆V (G)
of size at most d such that N[X] is a balanced separator in (G, w).
We show the following result.

Theorem 1.7. For every k, t ⩾1, the following classes of graphs admit O(tk log k)-dominated balanced separators:
• kCt-induced-minor-free graphs,
• kΘt-induced-minor-free graphs,
• graphs with no k vertex-disjoint and anti-adjacent Ξ⩾ts.
Furthermore, given a vertex-weighted graph G on n vertices, in time nO(tk log k) one can either find a O(tk log k)-
dominated balanced separator, or a family of k vertex-disjoint and anti-adjacent Ξ⩾ts. From the latter outcome one
can, in polynomial time, obtain an induced minor model of kΘt and kCt.
Recall that the complexity of finding Θt as an induced minor is unclear. Still, Theorem 1.7 circumvents this
issue by either returning an induced minor model, or balanced separator dominated by few vertices.
Again, Theorem 1.7 confirms a special case of a well-known conjecture, this time by Gartland and Loksh-
tanov [Gar23].
Conjecture 1.8 (Gartland and Lokshtanov [Gar23]). For every planar graph H, there exists a constant d such that
H-induced-minor-free graphs admit d-dominated balanced separators.
Conjecture 1.8 is wide open and so far was confirmed only for very restricted classes of planar graphs H, in-
cluding paths [BLM+19], cycles [CPPT24], wheels [CGKM25], K2,q for any fixed q, or K5 minus an edge [DMŠ24].
Theorem 1.7 has some interesting consequences. To discuss the first one, we need to introduce the concept
of tree-independence number. The independence number of a tree decomposition is the maximum size of an inde-
pendent set contained in a single bag. The tree-independence number of a graph G is the minimum independence
number of a tree decomposition of G. Graphs with small tree-independence number attract a significant atten-
tion, both from the structural [DMŠ24, CGH+25, CHLS26] and the algorithmic [dMM+26, LPR26] point of view.
One of well-known open problems in the area is the following conjecture. (A graph is H-free if it does not contain
H as an induced subgraph.)
Conjecture 1.9 (Dallard, Krnc, Kwon, Milanič, Munaro, Štorgel, and Wiederrecht [DKK+24]). For every integer
ℓand every planar graph H, there exists an integer cℓ,H such that every graph which is H-induced minor-free and
K1,ℓ-free has tree independence number at most cℓ,H.
Note that if a graph G is kΘt-induced-minor-free and K1,ℓ-free, then the largest independent set in the sub-
graph induced by the balanced separator given by Theorem 1.7 is of size at most O(tℓ· k log k). Combining
this observation with known results concerning building tree decompositions of small tree-independence num-
ber from separators with no large independent sets [CHLS26, Lemma 7.1], we immediately obtain the following
corollary, confirming Conjecture 1.9 in the case that every component of H is a theta.
Corollary 1.10. For every k, t, and ℓ, every graph that is kΘt-induced-minor-free and K1,ℓ-free has tree-independence
number O(tℓ· k log k).
Algorithmic consequences.
Theorem 1.7 yields also some algorithmic corollaries. First, combining it with
known techniques [GOS+19,BLM+19,CPPT24,CGKM25], we immediately obtain the following result.
Theorem 1.11. Let k, t be fixed constants. The Max Weight Independent Set (MWIS) problem admits a QPTAS
in kΘt-induced-minor-free graphs. Furthermore, MWIS and List-3-Coloring can be solved in time 2O(√n log n) in
n-vertex kΘt-induced-minor-free graphs.
In all cases, the algorithm can accept any graph on input and, within the claimed running time bound, produce
the desired output or an induced minor model of kΘt.

2.1
Definitions
We define t-models, which capture induced minor models of a t-subdivided graph, where subdivision paths are
actual induced paths of G (see also Figure 2). Two vertex sets do not touch if they are disjoint and anti-adjacent.
Definition 2.1 (t-model). Given a graph G and a multigraph H, a t-model of H in G is a tuple H = (H, η), where
the bag function η : V (H) ∪E(H) 7→2V (G) satisfies the following.
(i) For any v ∈V (H), η(v) induces a connected subgraph of G, and for any u ∈V (H), u ̸= v, the sets η(u)
and η(v) do not touch.
(ii) For any e = uv ∈E(H), η(e) induces a path of length more than t in G (i.e., with at least t internal
vertices) with one endpoint η(e, u) ∈η(e) ∩η(u) and the other endpoint η(e, v) ∈η(e) ∩η(v).

(iii) For any v ∈e ∈E(H), η(e) \ {η(e, v)} and η(v) \ {η(e, v)} do not touch.
(iv) For any e ∈E(H) and w ∈V (H), if w /∈e, then the sets η(e) and η(w) do not touch.
(v) For any e, f ∈E(H), e ̸= f, the sets η(e) and η(f) do not touch, except for the following possibility: if e
and f have a common endpoint v then we allow η(e, v) = η(f, v) or η(e, v)η(f, v) ∈E(G).
Note that G can be a multigraph, and hence e and f in the last point can be two parallel edges.
Let H = (H, η) be a t-model of H in G. Let e = uv ∈E(H). The length-ℓprefixes of η(e) starting at η(e, u)
and η(e, v) are denoted ηℓ(e, u) and ηℓ(e, v) respectively; ηℓ(e, v) = ηℓ(e, u) = η(e) if the length of η(e) is at
most ℓ. We denote by η(e)[a, b] the subpath of η(e) between a, b ∈η(e).
We let V (H) be the union of all vertex and edge bags, and, when we consider H as a subgraph of G, we mean
the subgraph induced by V (H). We shorten N(V (H)) to N(H). We have the following immediate observation
from the definition.
Observation 2.2. If H is a t-model of H in G, then an induced minor model of a t-subdivision of H can be constructed
from G[V (H)] by contracting every vertex bag into a single vertex and contracting every edge bag to a path of length
exactly t + 1.
An immediate corollary of Observation 2.2 is that a packing of disjoint cycles in H projects to a packing of
anti-adjacent long holes in G. (Lemma 5.1 is an analog of this statement that extracts anti-adjacent Ξ⩾ts from a
packing of disjoint Θ models in H.)
Corollary 2.3. If H is a t-model of H in G and H admits a packing of k vertex-disjoint cycles, then G[V (H)] admits
a packing of k anti-adjacent C⩾ts.
A guarded t-model consists of a t-model H = (H, η) and a set R ⊆V (H) of guards or reserved vertices.
Somewhat abusing the notation, we will denote a guarded t-model as a triple H = (H, η, R) and still use the
notation V (H) for the union of the values of η.
We define ears of the model as the paths only touching H at their endpoints, while avoiding N[R].
Definition 2.4 (ear). Given a guarded t-model H = (H, η, R) in G, an ear of H is an induced path P = p1, ..., pk
of G such that p1, pk ∈N(H) −N[R], and for any i ∈[2, k −1], pi /∈N[H].

f. The distance of an ear P = p1, p2, . . . , pk in H is the minimum distance in G[V (H)] between N(p1) ∩H and
N(pk) ∩H. The length of an ear P is defined naturally as the length of the path P.
We now define short t-models.
Definition 2.5 (short t-model). For any t ⩾1, a short t-model of H in G is a guarded t-model H = (H, η, R)
where:
• For any v ∈V (H), |η(v)| ⩽4 and η(v) ⊆R. For any e = uv ∈E(H), ηt+3(e, u) ∪ηt+3(e, v) ⊆R.
• For any v ∈N(H) \ N[R], set N(v) ∩H is contained in a 3-vertex subpath of some η(e). In particular, any
ear is between (possibly equal) edges of H.

Observe that, due to the first point, any ear connecting two different edges of H is of distance at least t + 3, and
hence, due to the last point, of length more than t.

Definition 2.10 (Ear-decomposition). Given a short t-model H0, a t-ear-decomposition starting from H0 is a
sequence of models H0, H1, . . . , Hℓsuch that for any i ⩽ℓ−1, Hi+1 is obtained from Hi = (Hi, ηi, Ri) by adding

We refer to indices 0 ⩽i < ℓas steps. A step is proper if the ear added to obtain Hi+1 from Hi is between two
distinct edges of H.
2.3
Properties
In our applications, the starting short t-model H0 will be of constant size. A step increases the number of degree-
3 vertices of H by 2, and does not change the number of vertices of degree at most 2. A step can decrease the
number of parallel edges by subdividing some of them. Furthermore, a proper step does not add parallel edges,
while an improper step adds two parallel edges. Finally, each ear addition adds at most 2(3 + (t + 3)) = 2t + 12
vertices to R. Thus, we have the following observation.
Observation 2.11. Let H0, . . . , Hℓbe a t-ear-decomposition that contains ℓ′ improper steps and H0 = (H0, η0, R0).
Then, Hℓcontains at most |V (H0)| vertices of degree at most 2, at most |E(H0)| + 2ℓ′ parallel edges, and at least 2ℓ
vertices of degree 3. Furthermore, |R| ⩽|R0| + (2t + 12)ℓ.
Thus, if we start with a constant-size graph and generally avoid improper steps, we end with a graph that
is “mostly” simple and cubic. The lower bound on the number of degree-3 vertices suffices to establish that Hℓ
contains many vertex-disjoint cycles thanks to Theorem 1.14, but vertices of lower degree and parallel edges
count negatively if we want to extract thetas and we need to control the number of them.

Preliminaries
Basic graph notation.
Given a (multi)graph G, we denote the set of vertices of G as V (G) and the set of edges
as E(G). We write G[A] for the subgraph induced by A ⊆V (G); G −A is a shorthand for G[V (G) \ A]. If it
does not lead to confusion, we do not distinguish between sets of vertices and subgraphs induced by them. In
particular, we identify components and their vertex sets.
An edge is incident to both its endpoints, and a pair of edges is incident if they share an endpoint. We say
that subsets A, B ⊆V (G) touch if they either intersect or are adjacent. Two sets are anti-adjacent if they do not
touch.
The degree of a vertex v is the number of edges incident to a vertex v, where a loop contributes two to the
degree of its only incident vertex. A graph H is called subcubic if all its vertices have degree at most 3. A graph
H is called cubic if all its vertices have degree 3. For a subcubic G we denote by n⩽2(G) and n3(G) the number
of vertices of degree at most 2 and exactly 3, respectively.
The set of neighbors of a vertex u is denoted as NG(u) (so called open neighborhood) and the set NG(u)∪{u}
is denoted as NG[v] (so called closed neighborhood). For a set of vertices X of G, its open neighborhood is
NG(X) = S
v∈X NG(v) \ X and its closed neighborhood is NG[X] = S
v∈X NG(v) ∪X.
Packings.
Let H be a family of graphs. An induced H-packing in a graph G is a family of pairwise disjoint and
anti-adjacent subsets of V (G), each of which induces a graph from H. The size of a largest induced H-packing
in G, i.e., the number of sets in the packing, is denoted as packH(G).
Tripods.
In a few places, we will need a simple well-known fact that a minimal induced subgraph connecting
three given vertices is a tree or a line graph of a tree. We formalize it with the following notion.
Definition 4.1 (tripod). A tripod in a graph G is a set of three induced paths that are disjoint and anti-adjacent
except for the following: each path has a designated one endpoint as a start and the second endpoint as an end
and we require that the starts are either all equal or all pairwise adjacent (i.e., form a triangle). The ends of the
three paths are called the ends of the tripod. We explicitly allow the paths in a tripod to be of length 0.
It is straightforward to verify (see e.g. [GLP+21, Claim 2]) the following:
Lemma 4.2. Let G be a graph and x, y, z ∈V (G) be three distinct vertices in the same connected component of G.
Then, any inclusion-wise minimal induced subgraph of G that is connected and contains x, y, and z, is a tripod with
ends x, y, z. Furthermore, such an induced subgraph can be found in polynomial time.

Algorithmic induced Erdős–Pósa property for long 3PCs
In this section, we prove the main technical result of the paper, namely Theorem 1.6.
We start by showing that a t-long 3PC always gives raise to a short t-model H0 of Θ0 (Lemma 6.4). We would

From induced Erdős–Pósa property to dominated balanced separators
In this section, we show that Theorem 1.7 is a consequence of the results shown so far and the following simple
observation: an induced Erdős–Pósa property allows us to reduce the problem of finding balanced separators in
kH-(induced-minor-)free graphs to the same problem for H(induced-minor-)free graphs.
Lemma 8.1. Let H be a family of graphs. Suppose that:
1. H-free graphs admit d-dominated balanced separators,
2. there exists a function f : N →N such that for every k ⩾1, every graph G either satisfies packH(G) ⩾k or
it has a set X ⊆V (G) of size at most f(k) such that G −N[X] is H-free.
Then, for every k ⩾1, graphs with packH(G) < k admit (d + f(k))-dominated balanced separators.
Furthermore, if the second property is algorithmic, i.e., there is an algorithm that runs in time T(n, k, t) in n-
vertex graphs and outputs either the desired set X or k pairwise disjoint and anti-adjacent members of H as an
induced subgraph of G, then in time T(n, k, t) + nO(d) one can either find a required balanced separator, or k
pairwise disjoint and anti-adjacent members of H as an induced subgraph of G.
Proof. Let G be a graph with packH(G) < k. By the second assumption, there exists X ⊆V (G) of size at most
f(k) such that G −N[X] is H-free. By the first assumption, there exists Y ⊆V (G) \ N[X] of size at most d
such that N[Y ] is a balanced separator in G−N[X]. It is straightforward to observe that N[X ∪Y ] is a balanced
separator in G. As |X ∪Y | ⩽d + f(k), the proof is complete.
To obtain the algorithmic version, we first run the algorithm for the second property; this takes time T(n, k, t).
If it returns the second outcome, we are done. Otherwise, it returns a set X of size at most f(k) such that G−N[X]
is H-free. Then we exhaustively look for a set Y of size at most d such that N[Y ] is a balanced separator in
G −N[X]; this takes time nO(d).
Theorem 1.7. For every k, t ⩾1, the following classes of graphs admit O(tk log k)-dominated balanced separators:
• kCt-induced-minor-free graphs,
• kΘt-induced-minor-free graphs,
• graphs with no k vertex-disjoint and anti-adjacent Ξ⩾ts.
Furthermore, given a vertex-weighted graph G on n vertices, in time nO(tk log k) one can either find a O(tk log k)-
dominated balanced separator, or a family of k vertex-disjoint and anti-adjacent Ξ⩾ts. From the latter outcome one
can, in polynomial time, obtain an induced minor model of kΘt and kCt.

Conclusion and further work
Let us conclude the paper by pointing out some open questions and directions for future research.
First of all, let us recall three conjectures concerning graphs that exclude a planar graph as an induced minor,
which were the motivation for our work.
Conjecture 1.2. For every planar graph H, there exists a function f : N →N such that every graph G = (V, E)
contains either:
• kH as an induced minor, or
• a set X ⊆V of size at most f(k) such that G −N[X] is H-induced-minor-free.
Conjecture 1.8 (Gartland and Lokshtanov [Gar23]). For every planar graph H, there exists a constant d such that
H-induced-minor-free graphs admit d-dominated balanced separators.
29


## Page 32


Conjecture 1.13 (Gartland, Lokshtanov [Gar23]). For every planar graph H, every problem expressible as (tw ⩽
r, ψ)-MWIS is polynomial-time-solvable in H-induced-minor-free graphs.
We are very far from confirming these conjectures. Perhaps an interesting next case would be an analogue of
a long Θ⩾t, but with more than three long paths?
Second, recall that in all our induced Erdős–Pósa results, the bound on the size of the hitting set is O(tk log k),
where k is the number of objects to be packed and t corresponds to the size of a single object. On the other hand,
the lower bound obtained by a modification of a construction in [AGHK25] is Ω(tk + k log k). We believe it
would be interesting to close this gap. Let us remark that Ahn, Gollin, Huynh, and Kwon [AGHK25] conjectured
that in their setting, i.e., for induced packing of long, but not necessarily induced cycles, the lower bound is tight,
i.e., that the upper bound can be improved to O(tk + k log k).
Another possible direction is to consider induced Erdős–Pósa property just for some cycles. Given a graph
G and a set S ⊆V (G), an S-cycle is a cycle that intersects S. As announced by Ahn and Kwon [AK26], the
techniques used to prove Theorem 1.1 can be extended to show the induced Erdős–Pósa property for long (but
not necessarily induced) S-cycles. Our approach does not seem to generalize to such a setting. Of course, in an
analogous way one can define S-thetas etc.
Finally, let us recall that we proved algorithmic induced Erdős–Pósa property for long cycles and for Ξ⩾t, but
only an existential statement for Θ⩾t. A fundamental reason for this is that we do not know how to efficiently find
Θ⩾t as an induced minor. Recently, Dallard, Dumas, Hilaire, Milanič, Perez, and Trotignon [DDH+24] showed
that this problem is polynomial-time solvable for t = 1, but the general case remains open.
It appears that the difficulty lies in detecting minor models that resemble the one in Figure 3, i.e., the image
of each of degree-3 vertices is a path. A naive approach would be to guess the images of degree-2 vertices, and
then try to connect the endpoints by finding two disjoint and anti-adjacent paths between specified terminals.
However, the latter problem is NP-hard even in restricted classes of graphs [ABPT25]. Thus, a polynomial-time
algorithm for detecting Θ⩾t as an induced minor would require a more involved approach. We also empha-
size that there are some constant-size graphs H (even trees) such that it is NP-hard to detect H as an induced
minor [ABPT25,KL24].
