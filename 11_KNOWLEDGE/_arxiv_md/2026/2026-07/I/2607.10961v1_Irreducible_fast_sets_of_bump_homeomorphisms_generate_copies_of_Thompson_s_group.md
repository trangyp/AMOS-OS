IRREDUCIBLE FAST SETS OF BUMP HOMEOMORPHISMS
GENERATE COPIES OF THOMPSON’S GROUPS Fn
GILI GOLAN
Abstract. A homeomorphism of an interval is a positive bump if its support is a single
open interval on which it moves every point to the right. Choosing a fundamental domain
[m, b(m)) for the action of a positive bump b on its support splits the remainder of the
support into two intervals, called the feet of b. A finite set of positive bumps is geometrically
fast if fundamental domains can be chosen so that all the resulting feet are pairwise disjoint.
The crossing graph of such a set has the bumps as its vertices, two bumps being adjacent
whenever their supports overlap but are not nested, and the set is irreducible if its crossing
graph is connected. We prove that for every n ≥2, every group generated by an irreducible
geometrically fast set of n positive bumps is isomorphic to the n-ary Thompson group
Fn.
This answers the strong version of a problem posed by Brin and Zaremsky in the
Oberwolfach report “Cohomological and metric properties of groups of homeomorphisms of
R” (Oberwolfach Rep. 15 (2018)).
1. Introduction
Let I ⊆R be an interval. A positive bump is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism b
of I whose support is a single open interval
supt(b) = (ab, cb)
and which moves every point of its support to the right. Choose a point mb ∈supt(b), called
a marker. The interval [mb, b(mb)) is a fundamental domain for the action of the cyclic group
⟨b⟩on supt(b), and its complement in the support consists of two intervals,
Lb = (ab, mb)
and
Rb = [b(mb), cb),
called the left and right feet of b. Following Bleak, Brin, Kassabov, Moore and Zaremsky [1],
a finite set B of positive bumps is geometrically fast, or simply fast, if markers can be chosen
so that all the feet of all the bumps in B are pairwise disjoint.
Since the feet of a fast set are pairwise disjoint intervals, they inherit a left-to-right order.
The dynamical diagram of a fast set records this order, together with the pairing of the two
feet belonging to each bump. One may draw the diagram by placing the feet on a line and
joining the two feet of each bump by an arc, as in Fig. 1. A fundamental theorem of [1]
says that this finite combinatorial object determines the marked isomorphism type: if two
fast sets have the same dynamical diagram, then the natural bijection between their bumps
extends to an isomorphism between the groups they generate (see Theorem 2.6).
Two bumps x and y of a fast set cross when their feet interlace:
Lx < Ly < Rx < Ry
or
Ly < Lx < Ry < Rx;
equivalently, when their supports overlap and neither contains the other. The crossing graph
of a fast set has one vertex for each bump and one edge for each crossing pair, and, following
Belk and Stott [4], we say that a fast set is irreducible when its crossing graph is connected.

As noted in [5], a fast set of positive bumps that is not irreducible generates a group which
decomposes non-trivially as a direct product or as a permutational wreath product of groups
generated by smaller fast sets. For n ≥2, let Cn denote the class of groups generated by
irreducible geometrically fast sets of n positive bumps (Definition 2.11).
The basic example of an irreducible fast set is a chain of n bumps: a fast set {b1, . . . , bn}
whose feet are ordered
L1 < L2 < R1 < L3 < R2 < · · · < Ln < Rn−1 < Rn,
so that consecutive bumps cross and the supports of non-consecutive bumps are disjoint. Its
crossing graph is a path, and the group it generates is the n-ary Thompson group Fn [1]: the
group of piecewise-linear orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of [0, 1] with finitely many
breakpoints, all in Z[1/n], and slopes integral powers of n, so that F2 is Thompson’s group
F. In particular, Fn belongs to Cn.
The problem of identifying the groups in Cn was raised in the Oberwolfach report [5].
Questions 71 and 72 of the report ask, for all n ≥2, whether every group in Cn is of type
F∞and whether all the groups in Cn are isomorphic; equivalently, whether they are all
isomorphic to Thompson’s group Fn.
A similar question appears there as Question 109,
attributed to Brin and Zaremsky, and possibly others. The report records that the answer
to the isomorphism question was suspected to be negative for n > 3.
The answer was previously known to be positive in small cases. For n = 2 it is immediate:
the only irreducible dynamical diagram of two bumps is the crossing pair, that is, the chain
of two bumps (Fig. 1), so every group in C2 is isomorphic to F [1]. For n = 3, Brin, Bleak
and Moore showed that every group in C3 is isomorphic to the 3-ary Thompson group F3
[5, p. 1610]: using conjugations that preserve both fastness and the generated group, every
irreducible dynamical diagram of three bumps can be transformed into the chain of three
bumps. For n = 4, they showed that the groups in C4 form at most two isomorphism classes,
one of which is the 4-ary Thompson group F4 and the other of which they named pseudo-F4;
however, they were not able to determine whether pseudo-F4 is isomorphic to F4 [5]. Belk
and Stott subsequently proved that pseudo-F4 is isomorphic to F4 [4]; hence every group in
C4 is isomorphic to F4.
Our main theorem shows that, contrary to what was suspected, this pattern persists for
every n, and it determines the classes Cn completely.
Main Theorem. For every n ≥2, every group generated by an irreducible geometrically fast
set of n positive bumps is isomorphic to Thompson’s group Fn. Equivalently, Cn consists of
a single isomorphism class, represented by Fn.

We conclude the introduction with a brief sketch of the proof, which combines the dynamics
of groups of homeomorphisms of the interval with the theory of diagram groups; it has two
halves.
The first is dynamical: we introduce the swap move, a local rearrangement of a
dynamical diagram which is implemented on any fast realization by conjugating some of
the generators by one of the bumps, so that it preserves fastness and does not change the
generated group. Using swap moves, we show that every irreducible fast set can be replaced,
without changing the group it generates, by one whose dynamical diagram is peelable: it
remains irreducible under repeated deletion of the rightmost bump. This is what makes an
induction on the number of bumps possible.
The second half takes place in the world of diagram groups. By a theorem of Belk and
Stott [4], the group generated by a fast set is a diagram group over an explicit semigroup pre-
sentation read off from its dynamical diagram. We realize (an extension of) this presentation
as a directed 2-complex and show, by induction along the peeling and using the Tietze-like
moves of Guba and Sapir [15], that for a peelable fast set of n bumps the complex can be
transformed, without changing its diagram group, into one of a family of model complexes
built around the Stallings core of Fn [12, 9], which we call dilated cores and whose diagram
groups (over certain base paths) we prove to be isomorphic to Fn.
Organization of the paper. Section 2 collects the necessary background on fast sets
and dynamical diagrams, on diagram groups over directed 2-complexes and the Guba–Sapir
moves, and on the groups Fn. Section 3 introduces the swap move and proves the peelability
theorem. Section 4 presents the core of Fn and introduces dilated cores. Section 5 constructs
the directed 2-complex of a dynamical diagram. Section 6 proves the Reduction Theorem,
and Section 7 completes the proof of the Main Theorem.

Definition 2.3 (The dynamical diagram of a fast set). Let B = {b1, . . . , bn} be a fast set of
positive bumps with the indicated enumeration and with a marking witnessing fastness. The
dynamical diagram of B is the linear order on the formal letters L1, R1, . . . , Ln, Rn in which
Li and Ri occupy the positions of the left and right feet of bi, respectively.
Remark 2.4 (The diagram does not depend on the marking). Any two markings witnessing
fastness give the same order of the feet, so the dynamical diagram depends only on the
enumerated set of bumps. The order is determined by the support endpoints; for distinct
b, b′ ∈B,
Lb < Lb′ ⇐⇒ab < ab′,
Rb < Rb′ ⇐⇒cb < cb′,
Lb < Rb′ ⇐⇒ab < cb′.

We now regard these linear orders as combinatorial objects in their own right.
Definition 2.5 (Dynamical diagrams). A dynamical diagram on n bumps is a linear order

i-th bump of D is the formal pair {Li, Ri}, whose members are its left foot and right foot; its
span is the open position interval span(i) = (Li, Ri).
Two distinct bumps cross if their feet interlace, that is, if
Li < Lj < Ri < Rj
or
Lj < Li < Rj < Ri.
The j-th bump is nested in the i-th if
Li < Lj < Rj < Ri.
If two bumps do not cross and neither is nested in the other, then their spans are disjoint.
For a subset S of the bumps, the sub-diagram D|S is the induced order on the feet of the
bumps in S.
A fast set B of n positive bumps realizes D if it admits an enumeration B = {b1, . . . , bn}

is a fast realization of D.
In a realization B = {b1, . . . , bn}, the bumps in the diagram corresponding to bi and

corresponding to bj is nested in the bump corresponding to bi precisely when supt(bj) ⊆
supt(bi). Finally, the spans of the two diagram bumps are disjoint precisely when the supports
of bi and bj are disjoint.

Definition 2.7 (Isolated bumps; [1, §3]). Let B be a fast set. A bump b ∈B is isolated
