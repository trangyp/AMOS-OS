Identifying Σ(1380) and Σ(1430) in the J/ψ →Λπ¯Σ reaction
Wen-Tao Lyu,1, 2, ∗Eulogio Oset,2, † De-Min Li,1, ‡ and En Wang1, §
1School of Physics, Zhengzhou University, Zhengzhou 450001, China
2Departamento de F´ısica Te´orica and IFIC, Centro Mixto Universidad de

(Dated: July 3, 2026)
We study the J/ψ →Λπ ¯Σ reaction by looking at the π+Λ mass distribution at low energies,
in search of signals for the low lying Σ+ states. Apart from a clear signal of the Σ(1385)(3/2+)
state, we find a smaller peak for the predicted Σ(1430)(1/2−), which has already been confirmed by
the Belle Collaboration. A first analysis, considering only the πΛ interaction, shows that the low
energy part of the spectrum is better reproduced including contributions from the Σ(1430) and the
predicted Σ(1380)(1/2−) state that has been claimed before from analyses of different experiments.
However, when we consider the π ¯Σ interaction the need for the Σ(1380) disappears.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The Σ∗states are the subject of continuous discussions
concerning their existence and properties [1]. In this work
we shall address two of them which are still object of
much discussion at present, the Σ(1430)(JP = 1/2−) and
the Σ(1380)(JP = 1/2−). The Σ(1430) was predicted
theoretically in the study of the meson baryon interac-
tion in the context of the chiral unitary approach [2], and
it appeared as a cusp in the ¯KN threshold, or as a slightly
bound state depending of some small differences in the
input of the interaction. Those results are corroborated
by further studies imposing constraints from πN and ¯KN
data [3]. Other works along the lines of the chiral unitary
approach in coupled channels come to support these find-
ings [2, 4–9], and the I = 1 state around the ¯KN thresh-
old remains when the mixing of pseudoscalar-baryon and
vector-baryon components is implemented [10, 11]. The
data from πΣ photoproduction in γp →KΣπ [12] were
considered in Ref. [13] to constrain the parameters of the
theory and, again, a cusp-like structure in |T|2 appeared
for the different amplitudes at the ¯KN threshold.
To search for the Σ(1430), many reactions have been
proposed. In Ref. [14] the γn →K+Σ(1430)−reaction
was proposed to observe this state. In Refs. [15, 16] the
¯νlp →l+ΦB reaction, with Φ and B being mesons and
baryons of the SU(3) octet, was also shown to be appro-
priate to observe this state. It was proposed in Ref. [17]
to search for the Σ(1430) in the Σπ mass distribution of
the process χc0(1P) →Σ¯Σπ. A similar work suggested
using the χc0 →Λ¯Σπ decay, considering the contribu-
tions from the πΣ and πΛ final state interactions [18].
In Ref. [19], the Λ+
c
→π+π0π−Σ+ reaction was ana-
lyzed in terms of a triangle singularity that reinforced
the production of the Σ(1430) state. Also, in Ref. [19], it
was suggested to look at the Λ+

The other state, Σ(1380), is still more elusive.
Its
mass is around that of the Σ(1385)(JP = 3/2+) which
shows up overwhelmingly in most reactions in the πΛ
mass distribution, making the identification of Σ(1380)
difficult. But the Σ(1380) was suggested in Ref. [22] as
a means to improve the agreement with the data of the
K−p →Λπ−π+ reaction of Ref. [23]. One thing that one
should make clear from the beginning is that the Σ(1380)
cannot have a molecular structure as the Σ(1430). The
reason is that all the Σ(1/2−) states stemmimg from the
interaction of meson baryon coupled channels were al-
ready investigated in Ref. [24], and there were only two
isospin I = 1 states, one the mentioned Σ(1430) and
another one at higher energies that was identified with
the Σ(1620)(1/2−). There was no room for more I = 1
states with 1/2−. However, there could be states with
other structures that we cannot rule out. In that sense
in Ref. [25] it is shown that ordinary three quarks states
in the quark model would have masses even higher than
1430 MeV, but some particular pentaquark structures
could have a mass compatible with 1380 MeV. Similar
conclusions are reached in Ref. [26].
Further support for the Σ(1380) state has come from
different works. In Ref. [27] the contribution of Σ(1380)
was shown to improve the agreement with the LEPS data
of the KΣ∗photoproduction [28]. Following that line of
research, it was shown in Ref. [29] that, using linearly
polarized beam in the γN →K+Σ(1385) →K+πΛ re-

two 1/2−states could also be visible in the πΛ spec-

the πΛ interaction alone and then also including the π¯Σ
interaction. We find that, while considering only the πΛ
interaction, a contribution from the Σ(1380) state is wel-
come, the need for this resonance disappears when the
π¯Σ interaction is considered in addition.

easily accounted for by the tree level determined from the

the results for the π+Λ mass distribution are basically
independent on the ˜A/ ˜B ratio. Indeed, we have made
calculations of the MπΛ distribution using values of ˜A/ ˜B

to the data changing ˜C and ˜b. Thus, our conclusion that
the Σ(1380) state is not needed to explain the πΛ mass
distribution is firm.
To finish with the subject we include now a contri-
bution of the Σ(1430) and show the results in Fig. 7.
This is obtained using ˜a′ = 9.0 × 10−4. We see that the
data welcome a small contribution, but it is based on a
single data point. One obviously can not draw any con-
clusion from there, but only suggest that measurements
with high statistics be performed to eventually show a
clear signal of the Σ(1430), which we expect to be small,
as already shown in the Belle experiment [20].
VI.
CONCLUSIONS
We studied the J/ψ →Λπ¯Σ reaction measured by the
BESIII Collaboration, looking at the π+Λ mass distri-
bution at low energies, where Σ states should show up.
Indeed, there is a big peak in the mass distribution which
corresponds to the Σ(1385) excitation, but we took ad-


## Page 7


7
vantage to see if there would also be some signal for the
Σ(1430), predicted by works using the chiral unitary ap-
proach in coupled channels, and already observed exper-
imentally in a Belle experiment, and eventually a signal
for the resonance Σ(1380), claimed by many works from
analysis of different reactions, and predicted as a pen-
taquark state in some quark model works.
A first analysis of the data using only structures in the
invariant mass of π+Λ shows that the low energy part
of the π+Λ spectrum demands the contribution of the
Σ(1380), but also there is room for a smaller contribution
of the Σ(1430).
In a second step we look at the double differential mass
distribution by considering the interaction of π¯Σ in order
to see if this consideration can fill the missing estrength
in the π+Λ mass distribution at low energies.
What we see after taking into account the π¯Σ interac-
tion, together with that of other possible coupled chan-
nels, there is no longer need for the Σ(1380) contribution.
The terms coming from the π¯Σ interaction, together with
that of other possible coupled channels, provide a non-
negligible contribution to the πΛ mass distribution and
also interfere with the dominant Σ(1385) contribution,
such that now a good reproduction of the data is ob-
tained. It is also rewarding to see that the results ob-
tained are independent on the ratio of two parameters of
the theory and, up to a global normalization, only de-
pend on the strength of the Σ(1385) term, which makes
the conclusions rather solid.
In summary we show that the present reaction can-
not be used to claim the need for a Σ(1380) resonance.
Nothing can be said about other reactions from where it
has been claimed. However, the exercise done here shows
that a careful analysis of data is needed, and subtle is-
sues or small contributions can change the conclusions
concerning the need of that resonance to explain certain
experimental data.
