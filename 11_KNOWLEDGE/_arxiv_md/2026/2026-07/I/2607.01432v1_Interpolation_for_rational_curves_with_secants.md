INTERPOLATION FOR RATIONAL CURVES WITH SECANTS
ALESSIO CELA AND CARL LIAN
Abstract. In arbitrary characteristic, we determine the maximum number of general
points through which a rational curve of degree d in Pr passes, subject to an additional
secancy condition along a linear space. We consider the cases both where the points on the
curve are unprescribed and prescribed, which amount to the determination of the normal
and restricted tangent bundles of a general rational curve in BlPs(Pr), respectively. In the
appendix, we enumerate the interpolating curves in the case of prescribed points on the
curve.

1. Introduction
1.1. Interpolation with secants. Let K be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary char-
acteristic. In this paper, we address the following questions.
Question 1.1.1. Let d, k, r, s, n be integers with d > k ≥0 and r −2 ≥s ≥0. Let Ps ⊂Pr
be a linear space, and let x1, . . . , xn ∈Pr be general points. Write C = P1.
(a) Does there exist a rational curve f : C →Pr of degree d, meeting Ps ⊂Pr with
multiplicity k, and passing through x1, . . . , xn?
(b) Let p1, . . . , pn ∈C be general points. Does there exist a rational curve f : C →Pr of
degree d, meeting Ps ⊂Pr with multiplicity k, and such that f(pi) = xi for all i?
When the set of maps f is finite, it is also natural to ask for the number of f. One can
more generally replace the points xi ∈Pr with higher-dimensional subvarieties. These are
instances of the question of interpolation, which has been studied in different guises by many
authors, see [Sac80, EdV81, EdV82, Ram90, Ran07, Ata15, Lar16, ALY19, CR19, Ran21,
CLV22, LV23, CLV24, Ran24, Mio25] for an incomplete list.

A rational curve f : C →Pr of degree d meeting Ps ⊂Pr with multiplicity k is equivalently
a map
f : C →Xr,s := BlPs(Pr)
of degree (d, k). By degree (d, k), we mean that the degree of f against the hyperplane
class, pulled back from Pr, equals d, and the degree of f against the exceptional divisor
of Xr,s equals k. We assume that d > k so that the composition of f with the projection
π: Xr,s →Pr−s−1 is not constant.
In this language, Questions 1.1.1(a) and (b) ask whether the evaluation maps
ρ: M0,n(Xr,s, (d, k)) →(Xr,s)n,
and
τ : M0,n(Xr,s, (d, k)) →M0,n × (Xr,s)n
respectively, are dominant. M0,n(Xr,s, (d, k)) denotes the moduli space of n-pointed maps
from a rational curve to Xr,s of degree (d, k), which is smooth and irreducible of dimension
(n −3) + (r + 1)d −(r −s −1)k + r.
A necessary condition for ρ (resp. τ) to be dominant is for dim(M0,n(Xr,s, (d, k))) to be at
least dim((Xr,s)n) (resp. dim(M0,n × (Xr,s)n)), but we will see that neither is sufficient in
general (§3, Proposition 7.0.1).
1.2. Normal and tangent bundles. In fact, our main results answer the following more
refined questions.
Question 1.2.1. Let d, k, r, s be as in Question 1.1.1. Let f : C = P1 →Xr,s be a general
curve of degree (d, k).
(a) What is the normal bundle NC/Xr,s?
(b) What is the restricted tangent bundle f ∗TXr,s?
We will see that f is unramified (§2.1), so the normal bundle NC/Xr,s is sensible. The
bundles in question split as direct sums of line bundles:
NC/Xr,s ∼= O(a1) ⊕· · · ⊕O(ar−1),
f ∗TXr,s ∼= O(b1) ⊕· · · ⊕O(br).
Question 1.2.1 asks for the values of the integers aj, bj. In characteristic 0, the splitting
of the normal and restricted tangent bundles determine the answers to Question 1.1.1(a)
and 1.1.1(b), respectively.
Indeed, if char(K) = 0, then the map
ρ: M0,n(Xr,s, (d, k)) →(Xr,s)n
is dominant if and only if it is smooth at a general point f ∈M0,n(Xr,s, (d, k)).
The
relative obstruction space at f is H1(C, NC/Xr,s(−p1 −. . . −pn)), which vanishes if and only
if n ≤min(aj) + 1. Therefore, the answer to Question 1.1.1(a) is affirmative if and only
if n ≤min(aj) + 1. Similarly, the answer to Question 1.1.1(b) is affirmative if and only if
H1(C, f ∗TXr,s(−p1 −. . . −pn)) = 0, if and only if n ≤min(bj) + 1.
We will determine the answers to Question 1.2.1 in arbitrary characteristic, thereby an-
swering Question 1.1.1 in characteristic 0.
We will see furthermore that the answers to
Question 1.1.1 are uniform in the characteristic (Proposition 5.4.1, Corollary 7.0.3), so are
therefore determined for any K. We will find that the splitting type of f ∗TXr,s is also uniform
in the characteristic, but the splitting type of NC/Xr,s is not. In particular, if char(K) = 2,

Definition 1.2.2. We say that a vector bundle E on P1 is balanced if E ∼= O(a)x ⊕O(a+1)y
for some integers a, x, y, possibly 0.
Up to isomorphism, there is a unique balanced vector bundle on P1 of any given degree and
rank. If H1(E) = 0, then E is balanced if and only if it satisfies interpolation, in the sense
of [ALY19, §4.1], see Definition 2.2.1. By the discussion above, in characteristic 0, NC/Xr,s
(resp. f ∗TXr,s) is balanced if and only if ρ (resp. τ) is dominant for the largest possible value
of n allowed by the constraint that the dimension of the target be at most that of the source.
See [Ata15, §3, §8] for a detailed discussion of different notions of interpolation of vector
bundles on curves, including restrictions that amount to imposing incidence conditions at
arbitrary linear spaces.
While our results are more general, we will focus in the remainder of the introduction on
the question of when NC/Xr,s and f ∗TXr,s are balanced.

1.4. Obstructions to balancedness of normal bundles. In this section, we describe our
new results.
Definition 1.4.1. We say that f : C →Xr,s is non-degenerate if it is so after post-composing
with both maps b : Xr,s →Pr and π : Xr,s →Pr−s−1. We say it is degenerate otherwise.

also say that the tuple (d, k; r, s) (or (d, k), when r, s are implicit) is non-degenerate (resp.

factors through the proper transform of a linear space Y ⊂Xr,s. As in the case of projective
spaces, degeneracy can obstruct balancedness of NC/Xr,s, see §3.1.

1.6. Methods. We prove our positive results for balancedness of vector bundles, Theorems
1.4.2, 1.4.3, and 1.5.1, by degeneration. The property of interpolation is open in families
of nodal curves, so it suffices to consider normal and tangent bundles of singular curves
f : C ∪L1 · · · ∪Lm →Xr,s.
For normal bundles, we take the Lj to be a collection of r −s −2 general lines of degree
(1, 0) and one of degree (1, 1).
This allows us to deduce balancedness of Nd+(r−s−1),k+1
from that of Nd,k (Proposition 4.1.2). The existence of many exceptions to balancedness
poses difficulties, but in fact the degeneration is sufficiently robust to deduce balancing of
Nd+(r−s−1),k+1 even if Nd,k is “close” to being balanced. Combining this degeneration with an
analysis of the projection sequence (1) is almost enough to determine all normal bundles Nd,k,
taking Theorem 1.3.1 as input. To cover the remaining cases, we borrow some additional
degeneration arguments from [LV23], see Proposition 4.2.1.
The case of tangent bundles is easier, requiring one line L1 of degree (1, 0) for the degen-
eration argument, as in [Lar16]. The projection sequence (3) provides sufficiently many base
cases.
1.7. Plan of paper. After preliminaries in §2, the main geometric input into the determi-
nation of normal bundles Nd,k is given in §3 and §4. The results of §3 “bound unbalancedness
from below,” and those of §4 “bound unbalancedness from above.” The determination of
Nd,k is completed in §5 (in characteristic not equal to 2) and §6 (in characteristic 2). In
particular, the answer to Question 1.1.1(a) is given in §5.4. We deal with restricted tan-
gent bundles Td,k and Question 1.1.1(b) in §7. Our enumerative calculations, which involve
somewhat different methods, appear in the appendix.

linear subspaces of Xr,s. That is, we address the following question:
Question A.0.1. Let p1, . . . , pn ∈P1 be general points, and let Λ1, . . . , Λn ⊂Xr,s be linear
subspaces (§A.1) of any dimension. How many f : P1 →Xr,s of degree (d, k) are there, such
that f(pi) ∈Λi for all i?
One should take the sum of the codimensions of the Λi ⊂Xr,s to equal the dimension of
the moduli space of maps f of degree (d, k) in order to expect a finite answer, see Situation
A.4.1 and Proposition A.5.1. In positive characteristic, the enumeration should in general
be taken with possibly non-reduced multiplicities.
We will obtain these counts as intersection numbers on moduli space of quasimaps. The
techniques are parallel to those developed in [CL23, LS25], where the primary interest was in
the case in which the Λi are points (“Tevelev degrees”). We refer to the reader to those works
for discussion of the history of the fixed-domain curve-counting problem. While variants
of the moduli space of quasimaps adapted to their respective settings were employed in
[CL23, LS25], the ordinary space of quasimaps suffices here.
