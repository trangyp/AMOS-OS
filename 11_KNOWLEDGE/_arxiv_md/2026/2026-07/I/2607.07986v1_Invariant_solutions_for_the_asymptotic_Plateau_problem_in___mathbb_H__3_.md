Abstract
In this paper, we present solutions to the asymptotic Plateau problem
in the hyperbolic space H3. In this context, we exhibit solutions for curves
that are invariant under the action of a one-parameter subgroup of isome-
tries of H3. To achieve this, we prove the existence of foliations of H3
by minimal surfaces that are properly embedded, complete, and invariant
under these subgroups, which are then used to solve the problem.
1
Introduction
In this paper we study the asymptotic Plateau problem (APP) in the hy-
perbolic space H3, the complete simply connected Riemannian manifold with
constant sectional curvature -1. The main question of this problem is about the
existence of a minimal surface with asymptotic boundary Γ, where Γ is a given
curve in the asymptotic boundary of H3 (∂∞H3). In fact, this problem has been
widely studied in H3 and it has been solved for a large class of curves. We are
also interested in describing the solutions to this problem in some particular
cases.
In [1], Anderson proved for each immersed closed curve Γ in the asymptotic
boundary of H3 there exists a complete, absolutely area-minimazing locally in-
tegral 1-current Σ in H3 such that the asymptotic boundary of Σ is Γ. Guan and
Spruck in [6] considered the asymptotic Dirichlet problem (ADP) for hyperbolic
Killing graphs and exhibited a family of curves Γ ⊂∂∞H3 (precisely the curves
that correspond to a graph of a continuous function defined on ∂∞H2) that can
be minimally filled, that is, there is Σ a complete, embedded simply connected
minimal surface with asymptotc boundary Γ. They also proved this solution is
unique and it is a radial graph. Furthermore, the forth author and Telichevesky
in [9] generalized Guan and Spruck’s result to parabolic Killing graphs.
In this paper we are interested in the asymptotic Plateau problem for in-
variant curves. A Killing field in H3 is of one of the following types: elliptic,

and every orbit is a horocicle containg p. A hyperbolic Killing field has the flux

asymptotic boundary of H3. A curve C ⊂∂∞H3 is called invariant by the field

Given C ⊂∂∞H3 an invariant curve, we are interested in the question of
whether C bounds an invariant symply connected minimal surface. The simplest
situation occurs when one considers an elliptic Killing field X : Let γ be the
geodesic fixed by X. Through every point γ(t) of γ, there is a totally geodesic
surface H2(t) orthogonal to γ. The asymptotic boundaries of these H2(t) form a
family of circles in ∂∞H3 which are invariant by the flux of X and are minimaly
filiable. These surfaces are well known in the literature and also correspond to
the solution of the ADP considered in [6] and [9] for constant boundary data.
An invariant curve C at ∂∞H3, associated to the other three types of Killing
fields, may not be a Killing graph associated to a parabolic or hyperbolic Killing
field. For these cases, the knowledge about the APP is restricted to Andersons
result. Nevertheless invariant minimal surfaces were studied in [2] and they are
the surfaces we use to prove the following result
Theorem 1.1. Let G be 1-parameter subgroup of Iso(H3) and let Γ be a
G−invariant connected closed curve at ∂∞H3, that bounds a connected re-
gion of ∂∞H3. Then there exists a complete properly embedded simply connected
G−invariant minimal surface Σ with ∂∞Σ = Γ.

The Foliation Theorem
In this section we prove the Foliation Theorem. First, let us set some basic
notation and present a minimal version of Proposition 2.3 from [3].
Let (M, g) be a complete three-dimensional Riemannian manifold and X a
Killing field. Denote by G the one-parameter subgroup of isometries associated
with X in M and suppose G acts freely and properly on M; hence one can
define the quotient manifold. Let M/G be the quotient manifold and equip
M/G with the metric g such that the natural projection π : (M, g) →(M/G, g)
is a Riemannian submersion. We denote π−1(p) by G(p) := {h(p) ∈M | h ∈G},
that is, G(p) is the orbit of p in M.
Let v : M →R be the function defined by v(p) = g(X, X)(p). Since X is a
Killing field on M, v is constant along G(p) and consequently v is well defined
on M/G.
Definition 2.1. Let Σ be a surface in M and G ⊂Iso(M) a group. We say
that Σ is G-invariant if G(Σ) ⊂Σ.
Theorem 2.2. Under the above conditions, let G ⊂Iso(M) be the one-parameter
subgroup associated with the Killing field X and let Σ ⊂M be a surface invari-
ant under G. Let α be a parametrization of the curve π(Σ) ⊂(M/G, g∗) such
that g∗(α′, α′) ≡1, where g∗= vg. Then Σ is a minimal surface if and only if
α is a geodesic in (M/G, g∗).
Theorem 2.2 is a minimal version of Proposition 2.3 from [3]. Now we can
prove the Foliation Theorem.

Helicoidal Killing fields
In this subsection we prove that there exists a foliation of H3 by helicoidal
minimal surfaces. First we define a rotational Killing field.
Definition 3.16. We say that a Killing field X in H3 is rotational if the orbits
of X are geodesic circles whose centers lie on a fixed geodesic.
Now we define a helicoidal Killing field.
Definition 3.17. We say that a Killing field X in H3 is helicoidal if X = Y +Z,
where Y is hyperbolic, Z is rotational and [Y, Z] = 0.
The condition on the Lie brackets ensures that the rotation occurs around
the geodesic invariant under the hyperbolic Killing field. Thus the orbits of a
helicoidal Killing field are a kind of helix, as in R3, whose axis is the geodesic
fixed by the rotation.
