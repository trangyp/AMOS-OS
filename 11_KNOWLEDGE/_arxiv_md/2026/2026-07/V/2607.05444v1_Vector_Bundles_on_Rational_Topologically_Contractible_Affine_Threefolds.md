HAOYANG LIU AND BIMAN ROY
Abstract. The generalized Serre question asks whether any algebraic vector bundle over a
topologically contractible, smooth, affine, complex variety X is trivial. In this article, we prove
an affirmative answer to this question, if the dimension of X is 3 and X is rationally connected.
As an example, this proves that every algebraic vector bundle over any Koras-Russell threefold
(first or second kind and certain third kind) is trivial.

A smooth complex variety naturally carries the structure of a smooth complex manifold.
Consequently, any topological vector bundle over a topologically contractible smooth complex
variety is trivial. Inspired by this, the generalized Serre question asks the following:
Question 1.0.1. ([Za˘ı99, §8][AØ21, Question 6, Chapter 5]) Suppose, X is a topologically
contractible, smooth, affine variety over C. Then is any algebraic vector bundle over X trivial?
Here the affineness assumption is crucial; there is a topologically contractible, smooth, strictly
quasi-affine complex fourfold which admits a non-trivial rank 2 algebraic vector bundle [AØ21,
Example 5.3.5.4]. Fujita showed that the topologically contractible smooth complex surfaces
are affine [Fuj82]. However, it is not known whether any topologically contractible complex
threefold is affine [AØ21, Question 5.3.5.5].
The Chow groups of algebraic cycles play an important role in the theory of algebraic vector
bundles. The Chern classes associate an algebraic vector bundle on X ∈Sm/k to an element
of the Chow group CHi(X) of codimension i-cycles.

To determine the triviality of algebraic vector bundles over a complex affine variety of dimension
d, Serre’s splitting principle allows us to take care only of the algebraic vector bundles of rank
up to d. Any topologically contractible smooth complex variety has trivial Picard group [AØ21,
Theorem 5.5.2.7] and this coincides with CH1(X). If X is a smooth, affine complex threefold,
Asok-Fasel and Mohan Kumar-Murthy showed the Chern classes
V2(X) →CH1(X) × CH2(X)
is a bijection [AØ21, Theorem 5.5.2.8]. Gurjar and Shastri [GS89a, GS89b] subsequently proved
that the topologically contractible smooth complex surfaces are indeed rational. This in partic-
ular implies that the generalized Serre question has an affirmative answer in dimensions ≤2.
Theorem 1.0.2. [AØ21, Theorem 5.5.2.8] Suppose X is a smooth complex variety of dimension
at most 2, which is also topologically contractible. Then any algebraic vector bundle over X is
trivial.
More generally, Asok showed that a topologically contractible smooth complex surface has the
Voevodsky motive same with the motive of a point [Aso12, Theorem 1]. Mohan Kumar-Murthy
showed the Chern Classes
V3(X) →CH1(X) × CH2(X) × CH3(X)
is a bijection [KM82, Theorem 2.1(iii)]. Thus in dimension 3, the following criterion is known:
Theorem 1.0.3. [AØ21, Theorem 5.5.2.10] Suppose, X is a smooth, affine threefold over C,
which is also topologically contractible. Then any algebraic vector bundle over X is trivial if
and only if CH2(X) and CH3(X) are trivial.
However, it is not known whether the Chow groups or more generally, the Voevodsky motive
of a topologically contractible smooth complex variety of dimension greater than 2 are trivial.
Thus the generalized Serre question is open in higher dimensions. Unless otherwise stated, we
will assume that the base field is C.

In this context, the generalized van de Ven question asks the following:
Question 1.0.4. [AØ21, Question 8] Suppose, X is a smooth, topologically contractible, com-
plex variety; is X rational?
There are also several important classes of “nearly rational” varieties; namely stably rational,
retract rational and unirational varieties. All these varieties belong to a general class, namely
rationally connected varieties. A smooth proper variety X is called rationally connected if any
two general points of X can be joined by a P1 in X. If the dimension of X is at most 2, then
the rational connectedness of X is equivalent to the rationality of X. A rationally connected
variety X is uniruled, that is X admits a dominant rational map [Kol96, Section 1.1, Chapter
IV]

Several cohomological invariants have been studied to detect the rationality properties. Artin-
Mumford constructed a unirational threefold X which is not rational [AM72], by showing that
the torsion subgroup of H3(X, Z) is non-trivial.

threefolds.

plex, projective variety X is a K¨ahler manifold. The singular cohomology Hn(X, C) admits a
canonical Hodge decomposition

only if P1,2 = P2,2 = 0. A projective variety X of dimension d, for which the integers Pm,d

negative Kodaira dimension. Miyaoka-Mori theorem asserts that the converse is true, if X is a
threefold. We refer [Kol96] to the reader for the details.
A smooth affine variety X over C admits mixed Hodge structure [Del71]. Therefore, if ¯X
is a smooth projective compactification of X such that ¯X \ X is a divisor with simple normal
crossings, then the Hodge numbers hp,0( ¯X) = 0, for p ≥1 (Theorem 2.0.2). On the other hand,
if ¯X is rationally connected, then CH0(X) ∼= Z. More generally, generalized Mumford’s theorem
[Voi14b, Theorem 3.13] implies that if CH0( ¯X) is isomorphic to Z, then hp,0( ¯X) = 0 [Voi14b,
Theorem 3.13]. The Bloch’s conjecture asks the converse:
Conjecture 1.0.5. [Voi14b, Conjecture 1.11] Suppose, X is a smooth, projective variety over
C such that hp,0(X) = 0, for every p ≥1. Then CH0(X) ∼= Z.
Suppose also, the affine variety X is topologically contractible. Then ¯X is simply connected
(Theorem 2.0.1). Therefore, CH0( ¯X) ∼= Z is equivalent to the fact that CH0( ¯X) is supported
on a curve (Corollary 4.0.4). The main theorem in this article is the following:
Theorem 1.0.6. (Theorem 4.0.2) Suppose, X is a topologically contractible, smooth, affine,
complex threefold that admits a smooth projective compactification ¯X such that D = ¯X \ X is
a divisor with simple normal crossings. Also assume that CH0( ¯X) is isomorphic to Z. Then
CH1(X) is trivial.
Therefore, using the criteria of triviality of vector bundles by Chow groups we have the
following partial answer to the generalized Serre question in dimension 3:

Morel and Voevodsky [MV99] established A1-homotopy theory, synthesizing algebraic geom-
etry with homotopy-theoretic methods. They considered the Nisnevich topology on Sm/k and
the category of simplicial presheaves ∆opPSh(Sm/k) on Sm/k, which has a model structure
(namely, projective model structure). By inverting the Nisnevich local weak equivalences and
the projection maps X ×k A1
k →X, for X ∈Sm/k, using model category techniques, they
constructed the unstable A1-homotopy category H(k). By construction, X and X ×k A1
k are
isomorphic in H(k). A variety X ∈Sm/k is called A1-contractible if the structure morphism
X →Spec k is an isomorphism in H(k). An A1-contractible complex variety is also topologically
contractible. However, topological contractibility is weaker than the A1-contractibility [CR24,
Corollary 1.3]. By construction, the affine n-spaces An
k’s are A1-contractible. The isomorphisms
in H(k) are termed as A1-weak equivalences. For example, if E →X is an algebraic vector
bundle, the it is an A1-weak equivalence.
Analogous to the topological setting, the representability theorem for vector bundles hold in
H(k), if we restrict to the affine varieties. The functor on smooth affine k-varieties associating
X to Vr(X) is representable in H(k) by the infinite Grassmannian Grr [AØ21, Theorem 5.2.4.8]:
Vr(X) ∼
−→HomH(k)(X, Grr).
In particular, if X is a smooth affine variety over k that is A1-contractible, then any algebraic
vector bundle over X is trivial. However, outside the affine case, this fails; there exists an A1-
contractible, smooth, strictly quasi-affine complex fourfold admitting a non-trivial rank 2 vector
bundle [AØ21, Example 5.3.5.4]. This is an example of a topologically contractible complex
fourfold, which admits non-trivial algebraic vector bundle; however the fourfold is not affine.

A variety X is called A1-connected if πA1

this abstract definition, understanding the geometry of πA1

rationally connected and also, CH0(X) is universally trivial. An A1-connected smooth variety
X (not necessarily proper) is A1-uniruled, that is X admits a dominant morphism
A1 × W →X, with dim(W) = dim(X) −1;
therefore X has negative logarithmic Kodaira dimension [CR26, Theorem 2.11, Corollary 2.12].

4. Algebraic Vector bundles over contractible threefolds
The algebraic vector bundles over smooth affine threefolds over C are classified by the Chern
classes evaluated in the Chow groups. By results of Mohan Kumar-Murthy, Asok-Fasel ([KM82,
Theorem 2.1(iii)] and [AF14, Theorem 1]), for a threefold X, the vector bundles over X are
trivial if and only if CHi(X) vanishes, for every i. Gurjar-Shastri proved that any topologically
contractible surface is rational ([GS89a], [GS89b]). Thus, if the dimension of X is atmost 2,
then any algebraic vector bundle over X is trivial.
In this section we study the properties of Chow groups of a topologically contractible, smooth,
affine threefold X over C to address the generalized Serre question on the triviality of the vector
bundles (Question 1.0.1). In Theorem 4.0.2, we will prove that if X is rationally connected,
then CH1(X) is trivial and hence all the vector bundles over X are trivial (Corollary 4.0.4).
This is main theorem in this article. Thus, it turns out that an affirmative answer to the Bloch’s
conjecture (Conjecture 1.0.5, Theorem 2.0.2) for threefolds implies an affirmative answer to the
generalized Serre Question in dimension 3. Moreover, if X admits a projective compactification
¯X, which is also uniruled, then an affirmative answer to the Bloch’s Conjecture in case of
surfaces implies that any vector bundle over X is trivial (Section 4.1). Before proving the main
