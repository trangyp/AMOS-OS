Abstract
We generalize the geometric construction of vertex operator algebra (VOA) bundles
and their associated automorphic forms from the elliptic modular curve to arbitrary
Fuchsian groups Γ ⊂PSL2(R). A sharp topological dichotomy emerges regarding the
existence of a holomorphic weight-2 quasi-automorphic generator EΓ
2 . When Γ has a
cusp, we construct EΓ
2 via the analytic continuation of parabolic Eisenstein series and
prove that the space of quasi-automorphic forms is a free polynomial extension, allowing
the algebraic setup of the genus one theory, including the quasi-VOA structure and
the characterization of strict automorphic forms via a lowering operator. Conversely,
when Γ is cocompact of genus g ≥2, Atiyah’s theorem on holomorphic connections
rigorously obstructs the existence of EΓ
2 . For this obstructed case, we provide exact
dimension formulas that link the shortage in lifting quasi-automorphic forms directly
to the failure of quasi-primarity within the VOA, fully resolving the torsion-free case
and conjecturing the extension to groups with elliptic points.
Keywords: Vertex operator algebra; quasi-automorphic forms; Fuchsian groups; Riemann surfaces;
vector bundles; holomorphic connections; Teichmuller space.
Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 17B69, 11F12, 30F35, 14H60, 30F60, 11F11.

Introduction
Barake, Chuchman, Franc, Mason and Nasserden [3] recently constructed, for a vertex op-
erator algebra (VOA) or quasi-VOA V , a vector bundle VX(1) on the elliptic modular curve
X(1), equipped this bundle with a connection, and used it to give a complete description of
the space M(V ) of V -valued modular forms as the kernel of an explicit lowering operator Λ
inside the larger space Q(V ) of V -valued quasi-modular forms. In this paper we extend their
construction to an arbitrary Fuchsian group Γ ⊂PSL2(R) of signature (g; m1, . . . , mr; n),
i.e., to Riemann surfaces X = Γ\H of arbitrary genus g, possibly with elliptic points and
cusps, including the compact case n = 0. The overall construction, common to [3] and to
the present paper, is summarized in the following diagram

A VOA/QVOA module V determines an automorphic vector bundle VX on X, equipped
with a connection ∇(Theorem 3.1), whose holomorphic and quasi-holomorphic sections
are exactly the vector-valued automorphic and quasi-automorphic forms M(V, Γ) ⊂Q(V, Γ)

We define the VOA bundle VX on X and show that the connection ∇= d + L(−1) ⊗dτ
of [3] is again a connection on VX for every Fuchsian Γ. We give a precise structural account
of the condition imposed at elliptic points, showing (Lemma 3.4, Proposition 3.6) that the
relevant operator is indeed of finite order despite involving a nilpotent term, but that this does
not force V -valued forms into the quasi-primary subspace in general, only in the constant-
section case that anchors the rest of the theory. The genus-zero, level-one theory of V -valued
quasi-modular forms used crucially the existence of the classical quasi-modular Eisenstein
series E2. We show that this is related with the topology of Γ. Namely, when Γ has at
least one cusp, a literal analogue EΓ
2 exists. Its existence and exact transformation law are
based on the classical (Hecke-Selberg-Roelcke) analytic continuation of the parabolic real-
analytic Eisenstein series of weight 2, which we use. From this single analytic input we give
a complete, self-contained proof, not only an analogy with the level-one case, that Q(Γ) =
M(Γ)[EΓ
2 ] is a free polynomial extension of M(Γ) (Lemma 2.8). Then the entire algebraic
machinery of [3], i.e., the doubled quasi-vertex operator algebra Q(V, Γ), the kernel theorem
M(V, Γ) = ker Λ, and the weight-preserving isomorphism M(Γ)⊗V (2) ∼= M(V, Γ), is applied
similarly to our case. We also give, for the first time, a complete derivation of the higher-
genus Ramanujan-type identity satisfied by EΓ
2 , from which the quasi-vertex-operator-algebra
bracket relations follow directly rather than by analogy. When Γ is cocompact of genus g ≥2,
by contrast, we prove that no such generator exists, for every signature (with or without
elliptic points).
A literal EΓ
2 would produce a holomorphic connection on the positive-
degree canonical bundle, contradicting Atiyah’s theorem on holomorphic connections. In
the presence of elliptic points we establish this rigorously not by an orbifold form of Atiyah’s
theorem, but by an elementary reduction, via Selberg’s lemma, to a torsion-free finite-index
subgroup, on which the ordinary (non-orbifold) obstruction applies directly. We show this
obstruction is completely understood at the level of dimensions. For torsion-free Γ we prove
an exact dimension formula in which the filtration bound of [3]-type arguments fails only at
the top conformal degree, by precisely the failure of quasi-primarity in V . We conjecture
the natural extension to elliptic points. We compute the relevant graded dimensions via
the Riemann-Roch theorem for Fuchsian groups and describe the generalization of Hecke
operators to correspondences between commensurable Fuchsian groups. In Part II of this
same paper (§10-§14 below) we lift the whole construction to a family over Teichm¨uller space
Tg,n, define Teichm¨uller-automorphic forms, and discuss descent to the moduli space Mg,n.
These are established results, direct fiberwise consequences of Part I. We also explore, as an
explicitly speculative direction rather than a theorem (§12), a possible relation between the
variation of the uniformizing projective connection and the Weil-Petersson geometry of Tg,n.

2.1
Signature and uniformization
Let Γ ⊂PSL2(R) be a finitely generated discrete group of finite hyperbolic covolume (a
Fuchsian group of the first kind).
Such a group has a signature (g; m1, . . . , mr; n): the
compactified quotient X := Γ\H (where H = H ∪{cusps of Γ}) is a compact Riemann
surface of genus g. Γ has r Γ-conjugacy classes of maximal finite cyclic subgroups, of orders
m1, . . . , mr ≥2, fixing points τ1, . . . , τr ∈H (the elliptic points); and Γ has n Γ-inequivalent
cusps. We write Y := Γ\H = X ∖{n cusps}. The group Γ(1) = PSL2(Z) treated in [3] is the
special case of signature (0; 2, 3; 1). The case of present interest for higher-genus geometry
is g ≥2, n = 0 (no cusps), for which Γ is cocompact. We allow r = 0 (torsion-free Γ)
throughout as a leading special case. As in [3, §1.1.3], for γ = ( a b
c d ) ∈GL2(R)+ we write
j(γ, τ) = cτ + d and X(γ, τ) = c/j(γ, τ), and for a meromorphic U-valued function f (U a
finite-dimensional vector space) we write f|kγ(τ) = j(γ, τ)−k(det γ)k/2f(γτ).
Conventions on orbifold versus ordinary bundles. Every line bundle on X considered
below (e.g., KX, and the purely informal ω of §2.3) is understood, by default, as an orbifold
line bundle: equivalently, as the datum of a Γ-automorphy factor on the simply connected
H. This is unavoidable once r ≥1, since X then carries orbifold structure at the elliptic
points τ1, . . . , τr. We reserve the word ordinary for the torsion-free case r = 0, where X
(or Y ) is a Riemann surface, every orbifold correction below vanishes identically, and an
orbifold line bundle is simply an ordinary line bundle in the usual sense. We work with
automorphy factors on H as the primary object throughout, so that no orbifold or stack-
theoretic formalism is ever actually needed, and invoke results proper to a smooth compact
Riemann surface (Riemann-Roch, Atiyah’s theorem) only once r = 0, either because Γ is
assumed torsion-free from the outset, or after the reduction of Lemma 2.5 below.

Concluding remarks and open problems
We have shown that the bundle-theoretic approach of [3] to vertex operator algebra valued
modular forms on the elliptic modular curve extends to an arbitrary Fuchsian group Γ in two
different regimes. The bundle VX and its connection ∇(Theorems A, B) are available, for
every Fuchsian group, with no change to the arguments of [3]. The richer algebraic theory
built on the quasi-modular generator E2, the doubled QVOA Q(V, Γ), the kernel theorem,
the explicit free-module description M(Γ) ⊗V (2) ∼= M(V, Γ), and the resulting Hilbert series
appear, but only when Γ has a cusp (Theorems C, D, E) constructed via Hecke’s classical
analytic continuation (§2.3). When Γ is cocompact of genus g ≥2, the case of primary
interest for higher-genus geometry, this second layer of structure is obstructed. No holo-
morphic weight-2 quasi-automorphic generator exists at all (Theorem 2.6) by an application
of Atiyah’s theorem on holomorphic connections. We have shown, however, that this ob-
struction is completely understood at the level of dimensions. For torsion-free cocompact
Γ, Theorem 8.4 replaces the inequality of Theorem 8.1 with an exact formula, in which the
entire deficiency is concentrated at the top conformal degree and equals precisely the failure
of quasi-primarity in V . Conjecture 8.5 proposes the natural extension to elliptic points, of
which we have proved the top-degree half (Proposition 8.3). We have also discussed, with a
full structural account (Lemma 3.4, Proposition 3.6), the behavior of V -valued automorphic
forms at elliptic points. The cusp/cocompact dichotomy for EΓ
2 itself (entirely invisible at
level one where the unique cusp of Γ(1) supplies exactly the escape from Atiyah’s obstruc-
tion) remains, in our view, the most interesting structural feature uncovered by passing to
higher genus.
Freeness of Q(Γ) = M(Γ)[EΓ
2 ], on which most of Part I depends, is given by Lemma 2.8, a
complete proof derived from existence of one holomorphic depth-1 generator. The existence
(Lemma 2.7) is cited precisely, to the classical work of Selberg and Roelcke. The extension
of the Atiyah obstruction to groups with elliptic points (Theorem 2.6) does not invoke
orbifold degree statements, but reduces elementarily, via Selberg’s lemma (Lemma 2.5), to
the torsion-free case. The generic-resonance hypothesis of Proposition 3.6 is stated, and its
scope precisely delimited, in Remark 3.7. It is not needed anywhere in this paper except
for the general-signature case of Conjecture 8.5 beyond its top-degree term, which remains
open. The quasi-vertex-operator-algebra structure of Theorem 5.5 now rests on an explicitly
derived Ramanujan-type identity (Lemma 5.4) and an explicit verification that [θ, δ] acts
as a scalar (in the proof of Theorem 5.5), rather than a dimension count. Throughout,

Γ of general signature. As the discussion following Proposition 8.6 makes precise, this re-
duces to a single further ingredient: an orbifold (parabolic) Serre duality and Riemann-Roch
theorem showing H1(X, Qj) = 0 for the relevant orbifold line bundles Qj at all conformal
degrees j below the top. A parallel problem is the multiple-resonance case of Proposition
3.6, more than one resonant conformal degree at a single elliptic point within a single FNV ,
which the proof of Conjecture 8.5 along these lines would need to confront directly once k
is large relative to the orders m1, . . . , mr.
Arithmetic Hecke theory. Consider the V -valued Hecke eigensystems of §9 explicitly
for Shimura curve groups Γ coming from quaternion orders (which have no cusps, thus this
sits inside the cocompact theory of §8), generalizing [3, §8.4].
This requires identifying
the relevant analogue of the classical Eichler-Selberg trace formula for Γ, and relating the
resulting L-functions to those of automorphic forms on the associated quaternion algebra.
The real-analytic structure theory.
Independently of Conjecture 8.5, make the real-
analytic substitute built from the Maass-Shimura operator δk of Remark 2.12 precise: develop
the structure theory of the nearly holomorphic space eQ(V, Γ) (its QVOA-type structure, a
lowering operator built from 1/4πℑτ, and a real-analytic analogue of the P-isomorphism).
Such a theory would give an independent, Maass-Shimura-theoretic route to Conjecture 8.5,
alongside the algebro-geometric route of Item 1.
The Teichm¨uller-space direction. The discussion of §12 is explicitly exploratory rather
than a conjecture of this paper. Making precise any relationship between the variation of the
uniformizing projective connection over Teichm¨uller space, the Weil-Petersson geometry of
Tg,n, and the projectively flat KZB-type connection on the bundle of V -conformal blocks over
Mg,n studied in [22, 8] would be a substantial undertaking in its own right, likely requiring
tools (such as the Quillen metric on determinant lines, the precise comparison of sections
versus coinvariants) well beyond the methods used in this paper.
Degeneration to the boundary of moduli space. Extend the construction of §10-11 across
the Deligne-Mumford boundary ∂Mg,n, where Γt degenerates to a noded Fuchsian group.
This should be controlled by the factorization properties of V familiar from the theory
of conformal blocks [22, 8], and would give a compactified version of the bundle Mk(V )
constructed here.

[9, 17, 14] and condenced matter physics [24, 30, 2, 23, 27, 29, 28, 5].
