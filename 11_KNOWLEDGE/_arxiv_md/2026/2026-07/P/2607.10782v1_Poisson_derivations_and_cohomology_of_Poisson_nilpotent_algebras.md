Poisson derivations and cohomology of Poisson nilpotent
algebras
S Launois, S A Lopes, and I Oppong
Abstract. We compute the Poisson derivations and the first Poisson coho-
mology group of uniparameter Poisson Nilpotent Algebras (PNA for short)
under the hypothesis that Poisson-normal elements are Poisson-central. This
applies in particular to certain Poisson algebras associated to Bott–Samelson
varieties.
1. Introduction
Our aim in this article is to initiate the study of Poisson (co)homology of Pois-
son Nilpotent Algebras (PNA for short), also known as Poisson CGL extensions.
These Poisson polynomial algebras have remarkable properties; for instance, under
some technical hypotheses, Goodearl and Yakimov have shown that they possess a
Poisson cluster algebra structure [2]. Recent work of Lu and Matviichuk [7] further
emphasizes the relevance of Poisson cohomology in this setting: they show that
symmetric Poisson CGL extensions can be understood as Poisson deformations of
log-canonical Poisson structures, with the corresponding deformation and classifi-
cation theory governed by suitable second Poisson cohomology groups (see also [9]
for the case of Poisson superalgebras). Examples of such PNAs include coordinate
rings of Bott–Samelson varieties due to a result of Elek and Lu [1].
In [3], we computed the derivations and first Hochschild cohomology group of
Quantum Nilpotent Algebras (QNA for short) under a technical assumption which
covers the positive part of any quantized universal enveloping algebra of a complex
simple Lie algebra, but which excludes some important classes of QNAs, such as
quantum matrices.
Our aim in this article is to compute the Poisson derivations and first Poisson
cohomology group for PNAs under the technical condition that Poisson-normal
elements are Poisson-central. Examples of such PNAs include certain (coordinate
rings of) Bott–Samelson varieties. We could relax our hypotheses so as to mimic
the technical assumptions in [3], but here we favoured a smoother exposition at the
cost of the more restrictive assumption that Poisson-normal elements are Poisson-
central.

To overcome these difficulties, we introduce an intermediate localization RE−1
of R which has the same Poisson center as R and which can also be described as
a localization of K[y1, . . . , yN] at some of the yi. This second description is key
for us as it allows to describe its Poisson derivations from those of the associated
Poisson torus.
In particular, we show that each Poisson derivation of RE−1 is
a sum of a Hamiltonian derivation and a Poisson-central derivation (that is, a
Poisson derivation that acts by multiplication by a Poisson central element on the
non-Poisson-central variables yi). The fact that the Poisson center of RE−1 is equal
to the Poisson center of R is key as this later allows us to prove that the defining
element of the Hamiltonian derivation actually belongs to R (and not just RE−1).
Our main results show that any Poisson derivation of R is the sum of a Hamil-
tonian derivation of R and a Poisson central derivation of R, acting on each xi
by multiplication by a Poisson-central element of R. It turns out that this central
multiple depends only on the homogeneous degree of xi (in the sense discussed in
Subsection 3.1), whence the first Poisson cohomology group of R is free over the
Poisson center of R, with rank equal to the rank of R as a PNA. Our main results
apply in particular to various Poisson algebras related to Bott–Samelson varieties
for which the Poisson Rigidity Theorem of Levitt-Yakimov [5, Theorem B] shows
that the Poisson automorphism group is (isomorphic to) the semi-direct product of
a torus of rank the rank of the PNA by a finite group.
Due to results of Goodearl and Yakimov [2], many Poisson CGL have a Poisson
cluster algebra structure (see also [6]) and our article suggests computing Poisson
derivations of Poisson cluster algebras. We will go back to these in future work.

2. Poisson polynomial algebras
2.1. Poisson algebras. Fix a base field K of characteristic zero throughout.
All algebras are assumed to be over K, and all relevant maps (automorphisms,
derivations, etc.) are assumed to be K-linear.
A Poisson algebra is a commutative, associative K-algebra R endowed with
a skew-symmetric K-bilinear map {−, −} : R × R →R which satisfies both the
Leibniz rule and the Jacobi identity. In particular, {−, −} is a Lie bracket and is
often referred to as the Poisson bracket of R.
There are many examples of Poisson algebras in the literature. For now, we
just mention the specific examples of Poisson affine spaces and Poisson tori which
will play a crucial role in this article.
Given any skew-symmetric matrix Λ = (λij) ∈Mn(K), one defines a (unique)
Poisson bracket on the polynomial algebra R = K[x1, . . . , xn] via {xi, xj} = λijxixj.
We call this Poisson algebra the Poisson affine space associated to Λ. This Poisson
structure on R extends uniquely to a Poisson structure on T := K[x±1
1 , . . . , x±1
n ]
(and in fact to any localization of R), which we call the Poisson torus associated
to Λ.
We finish this paragraph with a bit of terminology: A Poisson ideal of R is an
ideal J such that {R, J} ⊆J. A Poisson algebra R is Poisson-simple if its only
Poisson ideals are 0 and R. The Poisson center of a Poisson algebra R is defined
as
ZP (R) := {z ∈R | {z, R} = 0}.
An element c ∈R os called Poisson-normal if cR is a Poisson ideal of R; equiva-
lently, if {c, R} ⊆cR. The set of Poisson-normal elements of R is not in general
closed under addition, so we define the Poisson-normal subalgebra of R as the
subalgebra of R generated by its Poisson-normal elements.
This algebra is de-
noted NP (R) and consists of finite sums of Poisson-normal elements of R. Clearly,
ZP (R) ⊆NP (R).
A Poisson automorphism of a Poisson algebra is an algebra automorphism that
preserves the Poisson bracket. Similarly, a Poisson derivation of a Poisson algebra
is a (K-linear) derivation for both the associative and the Lie structures. As usual,
we denote by PAut(R) the group of Poisson automorphisms of R, and by PDer(R)
its Lie algebra of Poisson derivations. A very natural and important class of Poisson
derivations consists of the Hamiltonian derivations (also known as Poisson inner
derivations) Hamx := {x, −}, where Hamx(y) := {x, y} for all x, y ∈R.
Then
Ham(R) := {Hamx | x ∈R} is a Lie ideal of PDer(R) and the quotient Lie algebra
PH1(R) := PDer(R)/ Ham(R) is the first Poisson cohomology group of R.

2.2. Poisson polynomial algebras. Let A be a Poisson K-algebra and σ, δ :
A →A be K-linear maps. Then, by [8, Theorem 1.1], we can extend the Poisson
structure on A to a Poisson bracket on the polynomial algebra R = A[x] in such
way that {x, a} = σ(a)x+δ(a) for all a ∈A if and only if the following are satisfied:
1. σ({a, b}) = {σ(a), b} + {a, σ(b)} for all a, b ∈A; that is, σ ∈PDer(A).
2. δ({a, b}) = {δ(a), b} + {a, δ(b)} + σ(a)δ(b) −δ(a)σ(b) for all a, b ∈A. In
this case, we say that δ is a Poisson σ-derivation of A.
If these conditions are satisfied, we say that R is a Poisson-Ore extension of A, and
we write R = A[x; σ, δ]P . This construction can easily be iterated to give rise to
so-called iterated Poisson-Ore extensions over K, which take the following form:
R = K[x1]P [x2; σ2, δ2]P · · · [xN; σN, δN]P ,
where σk and δk are Poisson derivations and Poisson σk-derivations of
Rk−1 := K[x1]P [x2; σ2, δ2]P · · · [xk−1; σk−1, δk−1]P
for each k ∈[1, N]. Note that R0 = K and σ1 = δ1 = 0.
We note that a Poisson affine space can be presented as an iterated Poisson-Ore
extension with all Poisson σ-derivations equal to zero.
2.3. Poisson nilpotent algebras. In this article, we will mainly be con-
cerned with Poisson nilpotent algebras (PNAs for short) (also known as Poisson
CGL-extensions). These Poisson algebras were introduced by Goodearl and Yaki-
mov in [2] as Poisson analogues of the so-called quantum nilpotent algebras.
Definition 2.1. [2, Def. 5.1] A Poisson nilpotent algebra (PNA) is an iterated
Poisson-Ore extension of the form
(2.1)
R = K[x1]P [x2; σ2, δ2]P · · · [xN; σN, δN]P ,
equipped with a rational Poisson action of a torus H = (K∗)m such that the fol-
lowing properties are satisfied:
(a) the generators x1, . . . , xN are H-eigenvectors;
(b) for every k ∈[2, N], δk is a locally nilpotent Poisson σk-derivation of the
subalgebra Rk−1 of R;
(c) For every k ∈[1, N], there exists hk ∈Lie(H) (the Lie algebra of the
algebraic group H) such that σk is the restriction of hk to Rk−1 and the
hk-eigenvalue of xk, to be denoted by λk, is nonzero.
Note for later use that xj is an eigenvector for σk (j < k). We denote by λkj
the corresponding eigenvalue, that is σk(xj) = λkjxj. We then say that the PNA
is uniparameter if λk, λkj ∈Q, for all k, j ∈[1, N], with j < k.
Observe that the group of invertible elements of a PNA is reduced to K∗since
any PNA is a commutative polynomial algebra over K.
Examples of PNAs include Poisson affine spaces, Poisson matrix varieties and
Poisson structures attached to Bott–Samelson varieties (see [1, Theorem 5.12]).
Let n be the rank of the PNA R; that is, rk(R) := |{i ∈[1, N] | δi = 0}| = n.
(The rank of R is also equal to the number of height one Poisson-prime ideals of R
which are invariant under H, see [2, Sec. 5.2] and Section 3.1 below). It follows from
[2, Theorem 6.8] that we can (and will) assume that m = n, so that H = (K∗)n.
In other words, we assume that H is the largest torus making R into a PNA.

4. Structure theorem for Poisson derivations of partially localized
Poisson affine spaces
An important invariant of a Poisson algebra A is its Poisson cohomology. In
low degrees, these Poisson cohomology groups have concrete interpretations. For
instance, the Poisson center of A is its Poisson cohomology group of degree zero, and
the Poisson cohomology group of degree one can be identified with the Lie algebra
of Poisson derivations modulo its ideal Ham(A) = {Hamx | x ∈A} of Hamiltonian
derivations.
