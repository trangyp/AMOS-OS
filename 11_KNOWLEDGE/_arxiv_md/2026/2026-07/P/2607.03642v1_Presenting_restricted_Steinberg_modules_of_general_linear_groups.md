Presenting restricted Steinberg modules of general linear groups
PETER XU
ABSTRACT. We give generators and presentations for various local restrictions of Steinberg modules over fields
and relate them to partial Borel–Serre compactifications of locally symmetric spaces in the case of number fields,
extending the existing theory of partial modular symbols for GL2pQq. Along the way, we clarify the relationship
between “circuit” and “Bykovskii”-type presentations for such modules. In an appendix, we relate the existence
of such presentations to Koszulity properties of the Steinberg VB-algebra.

1. INTRODUCTION
The Steinberg representation StG attached to an algebraic group G over a field F is a ubiquitous construction
in number theory and representation theory, naturally appearing in a wide array of cohomological and geomet-
ric phenomena. Correspondingly, it has various definitions, the most common being as the top-dimensional
reduced homology of the spherical Tits building rHr´1pTGq, where r is the F-rank of G modulo the center, and
TG is a simplicial complex built from the poset of parabolic subgroups of G. The celebrated Solomon–Tits
theorem says that this homology group totally captures the homotopy type of this building, in the sense that
TG is homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of pr ´ 1q-spheres.

free F-module1 M, we may also write StpMq. In this case, parabolics are the same as flags of subspaces,

the boundary of locally symmetric spaces is more moral than practical: the locally symmetric spaces for
general linear groups are rarely Shimura varieties, making it difficult to endow the extension classes with
any Hodge or Galois structure. Our more immediate motivation is that we make extensive use of restricted
Steinberg modules (and Orlik–Solomon-type resolutions thereof) in several papers on explicit “Eisenstein”
cocycles: to date, namely the articles [X2] and (jointly) [RX2] over Q, and (jointly) [SX] over function
fields. A major purpose of this article is therefore to give a unified and precise treatment to the corresponding

2.2. Connectivity conditions. Let S be a simplicial complex of dimension d. We say that S is d-spherical if
it has the homotopy type of a bouquet of d-spheres. This is equivalent to asking for it to be pd´1q-connected
(i.e. have vanishing homotopy groups in degrees ď d ´ 1), and also to asking for it to be simply connected
with homology concentrated in top degree (by the Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems). If one drops the
simply-connected requirement, we say instead that it is homologically d-spherical.
Let σ Ă S be any simplex of dimension s ď d. Let the link of σ, Lkpσq Ă S, be the subcomplex consisting of
the union of all simplices τ Ă S disjoint from σ, such that the union of the vertices of τ and of σ is a simplex
of S. Then Lkpσq Ă S is a subcomplex of dimension at most d´s´1. This motivates the following stronger
version of sphericality, which amounts to adding that the dimension locally looks as expected everywhere:
Definition 2.2. The d-dimensional simplicial complex S is (homologically) Cohen–Macaulay if every simplex
of dimension s has link which is (homologically) pd ´ s ´ 1q-spherical.
Note that by the Hurewicz theorem, the only difference between the homological and homotopical notion is
simple connectivity. In this article, we will always work with the weaker homologically Cohen–Macaulay
condition, and so we drop the “homological” going forward. Most, or perhaps all, of the complexes we will
consider this for are surely actually homotopy Cohen–Macaulay, but the extra simple connectivity statement
does not seem relevant to any of our applications, so we take the simpler route.
