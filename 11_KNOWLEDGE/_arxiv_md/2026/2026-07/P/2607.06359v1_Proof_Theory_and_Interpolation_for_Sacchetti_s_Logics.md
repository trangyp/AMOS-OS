We study the proof theory of Sacchetti’s modal logics wGL𝑛, a family of logics generalizing Gödel–Löb
provability logic GL by replacing transitivity with 𝑛-transitivity. We make three main contributions. First,
we solve an open problem of Iwata by providing an effective cut elimination procedure for wGL𝑛. Second,
building on this result, we introduce a new non-wellfounded sequent calculus for wGL𝑛with an improved
subformula property. Third, using this calculus together with interpolation templates, we prove that wGL𝑛
has the uniform Lyndon interpolation property, substantially strengthening previous interpolation results
for these logics.

Introduction
Provability logic is the branch of modal logic that studies the notion of mathematical proof through the
lens of modal operators, by interpreting the modality □as provable in some mathematical theory (most
commonly an arithmetical theory such as Peano Arithmetic PA). A landmark result in the area is Solovay’s
characterization Solovay [1976] of the provability logic of PA as the modal logic GL, a celebrated system
with a rich theory and numerous remarkable properties.
One of the most important such properties is the existence of fixed points with respect to modalized
variables, known as the Fixed Point Theorem. This result, due independently to de Jongh (unpublished)
and Sambin Sambin [1976], Valentini [1983], has attracted considerable attention and has been studied in
a variety of settings (see Lindström [2006], Visser [1981], de Jongh and Visser [1991], Iwata et al. [2024],
Mardaev [1993]). In Sacchetti [2001], Sacchetti initiated a systematic study of modal logics satisfying the
Fixed Point Theorem and introduced a family of logics, denoted wGL𝑛for 𝑛≥1, that generalize GL by
replacing transitivity with 𝑛-transitivity (so that wGL1 = GL). Sacchetti proved that each wGL𝑛satisfies
the Fixed Point Theorem Sacchetti [2001]. An explicit method for computing these fixed points, which will
play a central role for our results, was subsequently given in Kurahashi and Okawa [2022]. Although these
logics did not originate in the study of provability, they have since been shown to be complete with respect
to an appropriate arithmetical semantics Kurahashi [2018].
The proof theory of wGL𝑛was first investigated in Iwata [2021], where a wellfounded sequent calculus is
defined and cut admissibility is established via a non-constructive argument through the Kripke semantics
of wGL𝑛. That work also introduces a non-wellfounded sequent calculus for wGL𝑛and uses it to prove Lyndon
interpolation.
The present paper makes the following contributions:
1. We solve an open problem from Iwata [2021] by giving an effective cut elimination procedure for the
well-founded sequent calculus of wGL𝑛. The key tool is a non-wellfounded (cyclic) proof system together
with the general cut elimination procedure developed in Sierra Miranda et al. [2024].
2. This is the first description of an effective cut elimination procedure based on the method introduced
in Sierra Miranda et al. [2024]. On a technical note, we remark that since the calculi for wGL𝑛do

In this subsection we will discuss the effectiveness of the cut elimination for GwGL𝑛that we provided at
Subsections 4.2 and 4.3. We will show that, instead of non-wellfounded proofs, the use of cyclic proofs suffices
and thus all the steps can be effectively computed, thus answering a question from Iwata [2021].
The cut elimination procedure for GwGL𝑛presented in the previous section consists of three steps:
1. Translate the proof from GwGL𝑛+ Cut to G∞wGL𝑛+ Cut, corecursively.
2. Eliminate the cuts in G∞wGL𝑛+ Cut using corecursion on the local fragments.
3. Translate the proof from G∞wGL𝑛to GwGL𝑛by corecursion on the local fragments.
In this section, we will restructure this procedure as follows.
1. Translate the proof from GwGL𝑛+ Cut to a cyclic proof in G∞wGL𝑛+ Cut, recursively.
2. Translate from cyclic proofs in G∞wGL𝑛+ Cut to GwGL𝑛by induction. For this we will need an
inductively defined function that from a cyclic proof in G∞wGL𝑛+Cut produces a cyclic locally cut free
proof in G∞wGL𝑛+ Cut.
In some sense, the second step of this procedure is obtained from merging the last two steps of the previously
defined procedure. This can be understood as follows: to obtain a proof in GwGL𝑛from a proof in G∞wGL𝑛it
is not necessary to have the full proof in G∞wGL𝑛but just a big enough fragment of it (as the non-wellfounded
behaviour of the proofs in G∞wGL𝑛is relatively simple). For that reason, we never have to calculate the full
cut free proof of G∞wGL𝑛.
In order to simplify the proofs a bit, we will use the concept of locally backedge-free cyclic proof and
unfolding. For the rest of the subsection we make the following convention.

Conclusion and future work
In this paper we gave the first effective cut elimination proof for the sequent calculus of GwGL𝑛. Additionally,
we provided an alternative non-wellfounded sequent calculus that has an improved subformula property,
which we used to provide a proof of uniform Lyndon interpolation for all Sacchetti’s logics. For this work we
checked that, in wGL𝑛, there is a class of modal equational systems where the depth of the variables at the
solution can be adequately handled.
As future work, we leave open the option of exploring the notion of depth in interpolation. It seems
possible to consider a strengthening of interpolation, in which the depth of variables is part of the common
vocabulary (instead of focusing on the polarity). Our proofs suggest, although it should be checked in de-
tail, that wGL𝑛has this alternative interpolation property (refining the equational system of interpolation
templates to restrict properly the depth of variables, instead of allowing any depth). Understanding the
consequences of a logic having this “depth” interpolation property is also left as future work.
