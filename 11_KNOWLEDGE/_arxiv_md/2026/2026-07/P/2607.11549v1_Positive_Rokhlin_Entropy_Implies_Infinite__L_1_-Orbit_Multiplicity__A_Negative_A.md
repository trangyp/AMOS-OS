POSITIVE ROKHLIN ENTROPY IMPLIES INFINITE L1-ORBIT
MULTIPLICITY: A NEGATIVE ANSWER TO THOUVENOT’S QUESTION
ZONGRUI HU, LEIYE XU, AND SHUHAO ZHANG
Abstract. We prove that every free ergodic measure-preserving action of a countably infinite
amenable group with positive Rokhlin entropy has infinite complex L1-orbit multiplicity, both
on L1 and on its mean-zero subspace L1
0.
This gives a negative answer to a question of J.-P.
Thouvenot recorded by Iwanik and establishes the corresponding endpoint statement at p = 1 of
Iwanik’s theorem that positive entropy implies infinite Lp-multiplicity for every p > 1. The proof
combines Malykhin’s rigidity theorem for independent random variables, Seward’s Bernoulli factor
theorem, and a Følner set argument.

For free ergodic actions of countably infinite amenable groups, Rokhlin entropy agrees with
Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy [10, 11]. Hence, for Γ = Z, Theorem 1.1 answers Thouvenot’s question
in the negative for every free ergodic transformation of positive entropy. It also shows that no finite
family of functions has dense Koopman orbit span. In particular, every nontrivial Bernoulli shift
over a countably infinite amenable group has infinite L1- and L1
0-orbit multiplicity; see Corollary 4.2.
Burguet and Shi [2] introduced the related notion of topological multiplicity, defined via the com-
position operator on C(Y ) for a homeomorphism T of a compact metrizable space Y . If µ is a
T-invariant Borel probability measure, then every topological generating family, after complexifi-
cation, also generates L1(Y, µ; C). Hence
multL1(Z ↷(Y, µ)) ≤Multtop(T).
Thus finite topological multiplicity is an a priori stronger condition than finite L1-orbit multiplicity,
and the entropy theorem of Burguet and Shi does not by itself settle the L1 endpoint considered
here. Our proof uses instead a Bernoulli factor together with an L1 rigidity estimate for independent
random variables. Moreover, when Γ = Z, Theorem 1.1, combined with the variational principle,
gives another proof of
htop(T) > 0
=⇒
Multtop(T) = ∞.
To prove the theorem, suppose that m Koopman orbits generate L1. Seward’s theorem gives a
Bernoulli factor with r = 2m + 1 independent binary coordinates and base entropy smaller than
hRok(Γ ↷X). Approximate these coordinates by finite orbit sums and translate the approximants
over a Følner set F. The resulting r|F| independent normalized random variables are arbitrarily
close to a real subspace of dimension at most 2m|FK|. Choosing F so that |FK| < (1 + δ)|F|
contradicts Theorem 2.1.

2.2. Amenability and Følner sets. Recall that a countable group Γ is amenable if and only if
it admits a (right) Følner sequence; consequently, for every finite set K ⊆Γ and every δ > 0 there
is a nonempty finite set F ⊆Γ with |FK \ F| ≤δ|F|. We use this in the following packaged form;
see, e.g., Kerr and Li [7, Ch. 4].
Lemma 2.3 (Følner packing). Let Γ be a countable amenable group, let K ⊆Γ be finite with
e ∈K, and let δ > 0. Then there is a nonempty finite set F ⊆Γ with |FK| ≤(1 + δ)|F|.
Proof. By amenability choose finite nonempty F with |FK \ F| ≤δ|F| [7, Ch. 4]. Since e ∈K we
have F ⊆FK, whence |FK| = |F| + |FK \ F| ≤(1 + δ)|F|.

Question 5.1. Does a nontrivial Bernoulli shift over the free group F2 have an L1-cyclic vector?
Is its L1-orbit multiplicity finite or infinite?
5.2. Zero entropy. Zero entropy is compatible with an L1-cyclic vector: an irrational rotation
has a cyclic vector in L2, whose orbit span is also dense in L1. On the other hand, zero-entropy
systems may have purely absolutely continuous spectrum on L2. Thus L2 spectral multiplicity
alone does not determine L1-orbit multiplicity.
