Abstract: The CMS Collaboration reported a local excess at 146 GeV in the search for
the lepton-flavor-violating decay of the Higgs boson and additional Higgs bosons in the eµ
final state at √s = 13 TeV. If confirmed, this would constitute a major piece of evidence
of charged lepton flavor violation (cLFV). We investigate the compatibility of the claimed
signal with the full suite of existing low-energy cLFV constraints in a bottom-up effective
description: a single real scalar of mass 146 GeV coupled to gluons and to all charged-lepton
bilinears, with seven free parameters that simultaneously control the LHC production cross
section, every di-lepton decay channel, and every low-energy cLFV observable. A Bayesian
MCMC analysis against µ −e conversion, muonium-antimuonium oscillation, three-lepton
and radiative LFV decays, semileptonic τ LFV decays, and LHC di-lepton searches yields
a preferred mode with peaked value Yeµ ∼10−4.09, already cut into by the current µ −e
conversion limits. The projected sensitivities of Mu2e, COMET, Mu3e, MACE, MEG II,
Belle II, STCF, and the HL-LHC directly probe the region of coupling space selected by
the CMS excess, so the complementarity between high-energy and low-energy cLFV probes
will either corroborate or decisively exclude the scalar interpretation of the anomaly within
the next decade.

Introduction
The CMS Collaboration has reported a local excess in the search for the lepton-flavor-
violating decay of the Higgs boson and additional Higgs bosons in the eµ final state, using
138 fb−1 of proton-proton collision data at √s = 13 TeV [1]. The excess is consistent with a
new scalar resonance of mass 146 GeV decaying into eµ, with a global (local) significance of
2.8σ (3.8σ) and an inferred cross section σ(pp →ϕ →eµ) ≃3.89 fb(6 fb). No corresponding
excess is observed in the Standard Model (SM) Higgs boson decay h →eµ, and the ATLAS
Collaboration has not reported an analogous feature. If confirmed by future data, this
would constitute a major piece of evidence of charged lepton flavor violation (cLFV) in
any experiment.
The SM contains no source of cLFV: contributions induced by neutrino masses are
suppressed by the GIM mechanism to branching ratios of O(10−54) [2–6], rendering any
signal unambiguously beyond the SM. Decades of dedicated low-energy searches have re-
turned null results and placed stringent limits on the same operators that would produce
a 146 GeV resonance decaying to eµ. These include searches for µ →eγ at MEG II [7],
µ →eee at SINDRUM [8] with the substantial improvement expected from Mu3e [9],
µ −e conversion in nuclei at SINDRUM II [10] with projected gains at Mu2e [11] and
COMET [12], muonium-antimuonium oscillation at MACS [13] with a next-generation
program at MACE [14], and τ LFV decays at Belle [15], Belle II [16, 17], LHCb [18], AT-
LAS [19], and the proposed STCF [20]. With a mediator near the electroweak scale, the
mass scale of new physics alone does not suppress any of these processes, so reconciling
the CMS hint with the null results requires a specific arrangement of couplings. Latest
global constraints for those operators have been performed in [21–23].
The sensitivity
of future colliders to those operators has also been studied [24–28]. Theoretically, such
cLFV operators can arise in a variety of BSM scenarios. Recent works have investigated
those including 2HDM [29], leptoquark [30], charged Higgs [31], vector boson [32], and
Flavon [33–35] models. For more discussions on general cLFV, see Refs. [36–38].
Several explanations of the 146 GeV hint have been proposed within specific ultraviolet
frameworks. Koivunen and Raidal [33] have studied the Froggatt–Nielsen mechanism and
shown that the purely leptophilic flavon cannot reach the reported cross section, while a
version with additional flavon-quark couplings requires a tuned cancellation between top
and charm contributions to µ −e conversion. Primulando et al. [39] have addressed the
same signal within type-III two-Higgs-doublet models, and Afik, Dev, and Thapa [40] have
proposed a leptophilic two-Higgs-doublet scenario in which the new scalars are produced
resonantly through the lepton content of the proton rather than through gluon-gluon fusion
(ggF). Liu and Ivanov [41] have embedded the signal in a CP4-symmetric three-Higgs-
doublet model, and identified a viable scenario that accommodates the 146 GeV excess
while satisfying current low-energy cLFV constraints. Each of these studies is valuable,
but they commit to a particular UV framework, or to a particular production mechanism,
or to the assumption of SM-Higgs-like couplings, making the question of whether the CMS
signal is compatible with the full suite of cLFV bounds conditional on those choices. A
complementary and logically prior question is whether any scalar mediator, independent

plays the role of ϕ, with the off-diagonal Yukawas arising from the second-doublet Yukawa
matrix and κgg generated by a top loop proportional to the doublet’s quark couplings.
A global fit of flavor-violating charged-lepton Yukawa couplings has been performed in
Ref. [23] under the assumption of SM-Higgs-like production. In what follows, we treat
Eq. (2.1) directly and report bounds on its seven parameters.
3
Observables
The seven parameters of Eq. (2.1) enter two classes of observables. The first is the LHC
production and decay of ϕ itself, which for a 146 GeV scalar produced in ggF is controlled
by the combination κ2
gg × Br(ϕ →X) and is therefore sensitive to every Yukawa coupling
through the total width. The second is a set of low-energy processes in which ϕ appears
only as a virtual mediator, contributing at tree level to µ −e conversion, three-lepton LFV
decays, muonium-antimuonium oscillation, and semileptonic τ LFV decays, and at one
loop to radiative LFV decays. Each of these observables depends on a specific combination
of κgg and Yij, so the 146 GeV signal and the low-energy bounds carve the parameter space
along different directions.
The calculations for the cross section or width of the relevant observables are well
understood. Here we review each of them in turn.

3.2
ϕ-mediated Low Energy cLFV Processes
The low-energy observables fall into two sub-classes. Those mediated by a tree-level ϕ ex-
change, including µ−e conversion, three-lepton decays, muonium-antimuonium oscillation,
and semileptonic τ decays, probe specific Yukawa products directly, so their experimental
bounds translate into clean exclusions in the corresponding coupling plane. The one-loop
radiative decays ℓα →ℓβγ instead probe Yukawa products through a sum over intermediate
leptons and therefore mix several couplings at once; the resulting constraint turns out to
be the tightest in the (Yeτ, Yµτ) plane, as we will see in Sec. 4.

Set up of MCMC Analysis
The major result of this work is delivered through a Bayesian approach, focusing on the
viable parameter space given the various experimental and theoretical constraints pre-
sented in Sec. 3. Therefore, a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analysis is performed
to explore the parameter space of this toy model, with the free parameters κgg,Yeµ,Yeτ,
Yµτ,Yee,Yµµ, and Yττ. We use package Zeus [61, 62] to perform the MCMC process and the
visualization of the posterior distribution is managed through the corner package [63].
Almost all physical observables discussed in Sec. 3 involve only the squares of the
Yukawa couplings, so the sign of each Yij is largely irrelevant.2 For simplicity we denote
Yij as the corresponding absolute values. We take their prior distributions to be log-uniform
distributions, i.e. the MCMC sampling variables are defined as
x = (log10(κggGeV), log10 Yeµ, log10 Yeτ, log10 Yµτ, log10 Yee, log10 Yµµ, log10 Yττ) ,
(4.1)
reflecting maximal ignorance about the order of magnitude of the couplings and ensuring
sensitivity across multiple decades. In the absence of theoretical guidance on the expected
scale of these couplings, this choice is standard for positive parameters that can span several
orders of magnitude. The prior distributions are assumed to be uncorrelated for simplicity.
The upper and lower limits of each dimension of x are taken to be −10 and 0, respectively.
Aside from displaying the marginalized posterior distributions, the credible intervals of
each x component are also reported. For the BSM couplings other than κgg and Yeµ, which
are not directly constrained by the CMS excess, the marginalized posterior distributions

95% credible intervals are therefore one-sided, and we quote only upper limits. Because such
one-sided limits depend on the choice of the prior’s lower bound, a mild prior dependence
of these values is introduced. A discussion of this prior sensitivity is presented in App. B, in
which we also comment on the analogous dependence of two-dimensional credible regions.

5
Conclusion
The recent CMS hint of a ∼146 GeV resonance decaying into eµ, if confirmed, would
constitute a major piece of evidence of charged lepton flavor violation. In this work, we
examined which regions of scalar-mediator coupling space are simultaneously compatible
with the claimed LHC signal and existing cLFV constraints in a model-agnostic manner.
Answering this question maps out the present status and future sensitivity of the global
experimental program for cLFV.
To this end, we introduced a minimal effective description: a single real scalar ϕ of mass
146 GeV coupled to gluons through a dimension-five operator and to all charged-lepton
– 17 –


## Page 19


bilinears through symmetric Yukawa couplings, giving seven free parameters in total. A
Bayesian MCMC analysis with log-uniform priors spanning ten orders of magnitude was
used to explore the posterior distribution of these couplings against the current experi-
mental constraints. The resulting posterior reveals a bimodal structure: the CMS excess
favors a non-zero Yeµ and therefore a genuine cLFV signal, yet a second mode with tiny
cLFV couplings remains viable because the global significance of the excess is only ∼2.8σ.
Within the LHC-signal-favoring branch, Yeµ is driven to a preferred non-zero value, and can
be partially probed by the current µ −e conversion experiment. The τ-sector off-diagonal
Yukawas Yeτ and Yµτ are constrained primarily through their product by the one-loop con-
tribution to µ →eγ, which currently sets a tighter bound than any direct τ LFV search.
The LHC same-flavor di-lepton resonance searches provide supplemental constraints for
the diagonal couplings Yµµ and Yττ.
The complementarity between high-energy and low-energy cLFV probes is quantita-
tive: the LHC measurement fixes one coupling combination, the low-energy experiments
carve out the remaining directions in the seven-parameter space, and the projected sensi-
tivities align with the preferred region rather than merely improving existing exclusions.
This structure is a generic feature of scalar-mediator interpretations of the excess and is
robust against the choice of UV completion. We have worked throughout in the ggF pro-
duction regime; a parallel analysis in the complementary lepton-PDF production scenario
of Ref. [40] is left for future work. Ultimately, the resolution of the 146 GeV question
will come from experiment, and the framework presented here identifies the coupling di-
rections that each experimental program will constrain and provides a unified language for
interpreting their combined reach.
Future experiments will directly probe the parameter regions indicated by the poste-
rior. The projected sensitivities of Mu2e, COMET, Mu3e, and MACE will cover the full
signal-preferred range of Yeµ within the next several years, thereby also resolving the two
posterior modes. MEG II will tighten the µ →eγ constraint on the product YeτYµτ by an
order of magnitude, while Belle II and the proposed STCF will improve the direct τ-sector
probes. The HL-LHC with 3000 fb−1 of integrated luminosity will extend the reach on the
diagonal Yµµ and Yττ couplings correspondingly. More broadly, the CMS 146 GeV excess,
if it reflects new physics, is a scenario that many independent experiments will address
in parallel within the next decade. A heterogeneous dataset gathered from multiple in-
dependent experiments and frontiers in the future shall be able to confirm or exclude the
scalar-mediator interpretation.
