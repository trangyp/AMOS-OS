POLYNOMIALS OVER IDEMPOTENT SEMIFIELDS
PAUL PONCET
ABSTRACT. We study univariate polynomials with coefficients in an
idempotent semifield and their factorization.
We do not assume the
idempotent semifield under consideration to be totally ordered, in con-
trast with most of the existing work on this topic. We notably determine
when a polynomial splits into linear factors, and when its associated
polynomial function does so. These results lead us to characterize al-
gebraically closed idempotent semifields—those in which every polyno-
mial function splits. We prove in particular that every complete idempo-
tent semifield is algebraically closed. We also relate algebraic closedness
to the properties of preradicability and radicability and to the existence
of solutions to polynomial equations or inequalities.
1. INTRODUCTION
1.1. General context and motivations. Polynomials with coefficients in
a commutative ring A can have a non-unique factorization. For instance,
given A = Z/n2Z, where n is a natural number > 1, we have the identity
(X + n)2 = X(X + 2n).
For polynomials with coefficients in an idempotent semifield k, one encoun-
ters the same situation. A semifield is a semiring (k, +, 0, ×, 1) in which
every non-zero element is invertible; it is an idempotent semifield if more-
over x + x = x for all x ∈k. In an idempotent semifield k we have
(X + a)(X + b) = (X + a ∨b)(X + a ∧b),
where a ∨b := a + b is the sup of {a, b} and a ∧b is the inf of {a, b}. If k
is the max-plus algebra (also called tropical semifield), i.e. the idempotent
semifield
Rmax = (R ∪{−∞}, max, −∞, +, 0),
or more generally if k is totally ordered, then {a, b} = {a ∨b, a ∧b}, and
the factorization is unique. However, this is not the case in general.

In the setting of totally ordered idempotent semifields, such as the max-
plus algebra, a rich factorization theory has been developed, with major
contributions from Cuninghame-Green and Meijer [16], Baccelli et al. [4],
Akian, Bapat, and Gaubert [1], Butkovic [10, Section 5], Castella [11, 12],
Hong and Sendra [24]. A striking feature of this theory is that many struc-
tural properties of polynomials—such as factorization into linear factors
and the existence of roots—can be described in terms of order-theoretic and
convexity properties. However, the assumption of total order is restrictive
and is not preserved under natural constructions such as Cartesian products
or quotients.
The purpose of this paper is to develop a theory of polynomial factoriza-
tion over idempotent semifields without assuming total order. In that regard,
this work can be seen as a continuation of Rump [28], [29]. In this general
setting, several difficulties arise compared to polynomials over a field with
characteristic 0 or a totally ordered idempotent semifield, in addition to the
loss of unique factorization. Most notably, the natural map that associates
to a polynomial its polynomial function is no longer injective, so that for-
mal polynomials cannot be identified with their associated functions. This
leads to the coexistence of several distinct notions of roots and algebraic
closedness.
1.2. Main results. To address these hurdles, we revisit notions imported
from the totally ordered case. A polynomial p is said to be closable if a
certain family of infima associated with its polynomial function exists; in
this case, one can canonically associate to it a maximal representative p
having the same polynomial function, with a construction reminiscent of
the Legendre–Fenchel transform. A polynomial p is closed if it is closable
and such that p = p.
We prove that closed polynomials coincide with polynomials that split
into products of linear factors. More precisely, we obtain the following
result, which originates from Cuninghame-Green and Meijer [16, Theo-
rem 8], who discovered the fundamental result of factorization of polynomi-
als with coefficients in the tropical semifield Rmax. It also extends Baccelli
et al. ([4, Lemma 3.41], [4, Theorem 3.43]) and Rump [28, Proposition 3].
Theorem (First fundamental theorem, simplified version). Let p ∈k[X] be
a polynomial of degree n > 0. Then p is closed if and only if p splits in k.
In this case,

Theorem (Second fundamental theorem). Let p ∈k[X] be a polynomial of
degree n > 0. Then p is closable if and only if bp splits in k. In this case,

As a consequence, we obtain a characterization of algebraically closed
idempotent semifields in terms of closability of polynomials.
Corollary. An idempotent semifield k is algebraically closed if and only if
every polynomial of k[X] is closable.
Here, an idempotent semifield k is algebraically closed if, for every poly-
nomial p ∈k[X] of degree > 0, the associated polynomial function bp splits
in k. Other definitions associated to the term of “algebraic closedness” can
be found in the literature. An idempotent semifield k is radicable (alge-
braically closed in the sense of Shpiz [30]) if, for all y ∈k, n ∈N∗, there
is an element (necessarily unique) x ∈k such that xn = y. Shpiz proved
that, in this situation, every polynomial equation of the form bp(x) = t has
a (greatest) solution if and only if p0 ⩽t, and this solution is unique if
p0 = 0. More generally, we prove the existence of solutions to certain
polynomial equations of the form bp(x) = bq(x) under the assumption of
radicability. We also introduce the weaker notion of preradicability, which
helps in proving the existence of solutions to polynomials inequalities of
the form bp(x) ⩽bq(x).
Also, an idempotent semifield k is equationally closed (algebraically
closed in the sense of Rump [28]) if, for all p, q ∈k[X], the existence
of a solution to bp(x) = bq(x) in an extension of k implies the existence of
a solution in k itself. Rump characterized equationally closed idempotent
semifields as those idempotent semifields being radicable and having the
property that, whenever x ∧y = 1 and x + y ⩽z, there exists x′ ⩾x
and y′ ⩾y such that x′ ∧y′ = 1 and x′ + y′ = z. Interestingly, this latter
property always holds in the totally ordered case.
Gathering results from the literature and results proved in this paper, we
highlight the way these notions are linked with one another as follows.
Theorem. Let k be an idempotent semifield. Consider the following condi-
tions:

(2) k is radicable;

1.3. Structure of the article. The paper is organized as follows. In Sec-
tion 2, we recall usual definitions from poset theory and semiring theory, as
well as some essential properties of idempotent semirings and idempotent
semifields. In Section 3, we recall the definition of a polynomial with coef-
ficients in an idempotent semifield k. Thanks to a result due to Crosby, we
prove that the set k[X] of polynomials over k has the structure of a residu-
ated idempotent semiring without zero divisors. We then consider polyno-
mial functions and show that they follow a series of interesting properties.
Notably, every polynomial function preserves arbitrary sups of nonempty
subsets, and arbitrary non-zero infs of nonempty subsets; remarkably, it is
an order-embedding (hence is injective) whenever it maps 0 to 0. Section 4
is a cornerstone of the paper, as we introduce the notions of closable and
closed polynomial. We prove that a polynomial is closable if and only if
there is a greatest polynomial with the same polynomial function. Closable
and closed polynomials reveal their importance in Section 5 with our first
and second “fundamental theorems”, where we prove that a polynomial is
closed (resp. closable) if and only if it splits (resp. its polynomial function
splits). Calling an idempotent semifield algebraically closed if every poly-
nomial function splits, we obtain the characterization that an idempotent
semifield is algebraically closed if and only if every polynomial is closable.
It is then a trivial consequence that every complete idempotent semifield, in
particular Rmax, is algebraically closed. In passing, we prove that a basket
of roots of size k < n of a closed polynomial of degree n can be com-
pleted to a basket of roots of size n. Section 6 focuses on the concept of
a preradicable idempotent semifield, a property that is shown to be useful
for solving polynomial inequalities. We also prove that every algebraically
closed idempotent semifield is preradicable, and that the converse statement
holds true if the idempotent semifield is totally ordered. In Section 7, we
introduce radicability as a property stronger than preradicability to qualify
an idempotent semifield. This property attests to its strength as it provides
existence of solutions to polynomial equations. We also prove that every
radicable idempotent semifield is algebraically closed and order-dense, and
that the converse statement holds true if the idempotent semifield is totally
ordered. In the last part of the section, we consider rational polynomials,
defined as formal polynomials modulo a non-zero polynomial factor. We

2. SEMIRINGS AND SEMIFIELDS
In this section, we recall usual definitions from poset theory and semiring
theory, as well as some properties of idempotent semirings and idempotent
semifields that will be used repeatedly along this paper.
2.1. Partially ordered sets and lattices. A partially ordered set or poset
(P, ⩽) is a set P together with a reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric
binary relation ⩽. An upper bound of a subset A is an element u such that
x ⩽u for all x ∈A. An upper bound of A is the least upper bound or sup
of A, written W A, if W A ⩽u for every upper bound u of A. The notions
of lower bound and inf are defined dually; the inf of a subset A, if it exists,
is written V A. A lattice is a poset in which every nonempty finite subset
has both an inf and a sup; we write x∨y for W{x, y} and x∧y for V{x, y}.
2.2. Definition of semirings and semifields. A semiring is a commuta-
tive monoid (S, +, 0) endowed with an additional binary relation × (the
multiplication) that is associative, has an identity 1, distributes over +, and
admits 0 as absorbing element, i.e. 0x = x0 = 0 for all x ∈S.
A semiring S is:
• idempotent if x + x = x, for all x ∈S;
• commutative if xy = yx, for all x, y ∈S;
• without zero divisors if 0 = xy ⇒0 ∈{x, y}, for all x, y ∈S;
• ×-cancellative if (xz = yz or zx = zy) ⇒x = y, for all x, y ∈S
and z ∈S \ {0}.
On semiring theory, the reader may refer to Golan’s books [19] and [20],
Glazek’s survey [18], and Gondran and Minoux’s monograph [21].
An idempotent semiring has a natural structure of partially ordered set
with x ⩽y ⇔x + y = y, whose least element is 0. This makes x + y the
sup of the pair {x, y}, i.e.
x + y = x ∨y,
for all x, y. Therefore, given elements x, y, z, the reader should get used to
assertions such as (x ⩽z and y ⩽z) ⇔x + y ⩽z.

A semifield is a semiring with an identity 1 ̸= 0 in which every non-zero
element has a multiplicative inverse. Idempotent semifields coincide with
the class of semifields with characteristic one, where the characteristic of a
semifield is the least k ∈N∗, if it exists, such that (k+1)1 = 1 (if it does not
exists, it is conventionally set to 0). The concept of characteristic one has
been introduced simultaneously by Connes and Consani [14] and Castella
[11]; see also the early work of Zeleznekow [33] on inverse semirings, and
Rump [29, Section 2].
An idempotent semiring or semifield is totally ordered if x + y ∈{x, y},
for all x, y; equivalently, if x ⩽y or y ⩽x, for all x, y. Note that linearly
ordered is a classical synonym for totally ordered; one may also find in the
literature terms like bipotent (see [27]) or selective (see [21], [31]).
Example 2.1 (Boolean semifield). The Boolean semifield B is defined as
the set {0, 1} with 0 ̸= 1, endowed with the usual multiplication and the
addition + defined by 0 + 0 = 0 and 1 + 0 = 0 + 1 = 1 + 1 = 1. Recall
that B is the only finite idempotent semifield [20, Proposition 1.35].
Example 2.2 (Tropical semifield). The tropical semifield (or max-plus al-
gebra) Rmax is defined as
Rmax := (R ∪{−∞}, max, −∞, +, 0).
This idempotent semifield and its dual Rmin := (R∪{+∞}, min, +∞, +, 0)
have been rediscovered many times due to their versatility in various areas
of pure and applied mathematics, see e.g. Akian et al. [2] for background
and references.

and the dual divisibility relation ⪯, so that x ⪯y if y divides x, in the sense

3. POLYNOMIALS AND POLYNOMIAL FUNCTIONS
In this section, we recall the definition of a polynomial with coefficients
in an idempotent semifield k. Thanks to a result due to Crosby, we prove
that the set k[X] of polynomials over k has the structure of a residuated
idempotent semiring without zero divisors. We then consider polynomial
functions and show that they follow a series of interesting properties. No-
tably, every polynomial function
• preserves arbitrary sups of nonempty subsets;
• preserves arbitrary non-zero infs of nonempty subsets;
• is a lattice morphism, so is additive in particular, and
• is an order-embedding (hence is injective) if it maps 0 to 0.

We show that every idempotent semifield k can be embedded into a rad-

this aim, we define on N∗× k the following relation:
(n, x) ∼(m, y) ⇐⇒xm = yn.
This is an equivalence relation; transitivity is a consequence of the implica-
tion (∃n ∈N∗: xn = yn) ⇒(x = y), see Lemma 2.6. The equivalence
class of (n, x) is written (n, x)∼, and we denote by ek the associated quotient
set. It is possible to endow ek with the structure of an idempotent semifield
by
