AND SUBDIVISIONS
ALLEN KNUTSON, MARIO SANCHEZ, AND MELISSA SHERMAN-BENNETT
Abstract. We present an embedded (in G/B) degeneration of Lusztig varieties (which general-
ize type A Hessenberg varieties) to certain unions of Richardson varieties, giving a simultaneous
reproof (and extension) of results of Anderson–Tymoczko, Harada–Horiguchi–Masuda–Park, and
Kim. Although torus-equivariant, the degeneration is not Gr¨obner. In the case that the Lusztig
variety is the permutahedral toric variety, this degeneration provides a dissection of the permu-
tahedron into Bruhat interval polytopes, and we prove a more general result showing equivariant
degenerations of projective toric varieties produce subdivisions (as was shown in the Gr¨obner case
by Sturmfels) not just dissections. A Gr¨obner degeneration would result in a regular subdivision,
and despite our degeneration not being Gr¨obner we show in types A, B, C that our subdivisions of
the permutahedron are indeed regular.

1. Introduction
1.1. Overview. The (n −1)-dimensional permutahedron Π is the convex hull of the n! per-
mutations, thought of as vectors in Rn. The permutahedral variety Perm is the toric variety
associated with Π; one construction of it is as a generic torus orbit closure in the type A flag variety
Fln. It is a central object in the intersection of algebraic geometry and combinatorics, appearing
for instance in the Hodge theory of matroids [Huh14], the study of moduli spaces [LM00], Chow
quotients of the Grassmannian [Kap93, 4.3.10], and Hessenberg varieties [Lin24]. This paper is
inspired by a formula of Anderson–Tymoczko [AT10] for the cohomology class1 [Perm] ∈H∗(Fln),
and a parallel result of Harada–Horiguchi–Masuda–Park [HHMP19] on a decomposition of Π. These
results involve Richardson varieties Xv

varieties, and their moment polytopes Φ(Xv

2. Degenerations and class formulas
In this section, we obtain embedded degenerations of Lusztig varieties in G/B, and use this to
give formulas for the cohomology and K-classes of Lusztig varieties. As a corollary, we obtain many
semitoric degenerations of the permutahedral variety in G/B, and thereby obtain many formulas
for the cohomology and K-class of the permutahedral variety, one for every Coxeter element c ∈W.
For our purposes, a degeneration of Z1 to Z0 will be a flat (or more precisely, free5) family
F →A1 where the fiber F1 is isomorphic to Z1 and the “special fiber” F0 is isomorphic to Z0.
(Sometimes one wants all fibers Ft≠0 to be isomorphic, typically in order to state an inequality
relating some level of singularity of Z1 vs. Z0, but our results are not of that sort and we won’t ask
this.) If a group G acts on F, and the map F →A1 is G-invariant, call the family G-invariant.
If Z1 and Z0 are closed subschemes of X, and the degeneration F is a closed subscheme inside
the trivial family X × A1 →A1, we call F an embedded degeneration. It will be clear from
context what the larger scheme X is. Most commonly, one obtains embedded degenerations F
using6 a Gm-action on X (hence diagonally on X × A1), taking F ∶= Gm ⋅(Z1 × {1}); however our
later embedded degenerations will not be of this sort.
Our interest in embedded degenerations stems from the following result.

We include another standard result about families, that is not relevant for our class formulas but
will show up when we discuss subdivisions.
Theorem 2.2 (Zariski’s Main Theorem, usual formulation). Let F ′ →C be a birational map from
a variety F ′ to a normal variety C, with finite fibers. Then it is an isomorphism to an open subset
of C.
Corollary 2.3. Let F be a variety over an algebraically closed field. Let τ ∶F →A1 (or any other
normal variety) be a proper map whose general fibers are connected (in particular, nonempty). Then
every fiber is connected.
Proof. We Stein factorize τ ∶F →A1 as F →F ′ →A1, i.e. F ′ is the global spectrum Specτ∗OF
of the pushforward of the structure sheaf. This replaces each fiber by its affinization, not changing
the number of connected components.
Since F →A1 is proper, the fibers of τ ′ ∶F ′ →A1 are now proper and affine, hence finite. Since
the general fibers of τ were connected, the general fibers of τ ′ are too. Being finite and connected,
the general fibers are points. So over an open set, our map τ ′ is bijective; since our base field is
algebraically closed, our map τ ′ is birational.
We can finally apply Zariski’s Main Theorem to say that τ ′ is an isomorphism to an open set.
But now since τ is proper, so is τ ′, so that open set is all of A1. Now that every fiber of τ ′ is a
point, every fiber of τ was connected.
□

3.2. Dissections and subdivisions. In the literature, one can find many references to a philoso-
phy that semitoric degenerations of toric varieties should correspond to regular subdivisions of their
moment polytopes, where each cell in the subdivision is the moment polytope of a component in
the special fiber. It is true that regular subdivisions give rise to semitoric degenerations [Stu91].
One has to be more careful with the converse: Gr¨obner degenerations (which, if X is toric, are
automatically semitoric) do give rise to regular subdivisions [Stu91]. If the semitoric degeneration
is not necessarily Gr¨obner, then, as we will show, we still obtain a subdivision but it is not a priori
regular.
Definition 3.8. Let P be a d-dimensional polytope. A collection {Pi} of d-dimensional polytopes
forms a dissection of P if ⋃Pi = P and the polytopes Pi have pairwise disjoint interiors. (Equiv-
alently, Lebesgue measure on P is the sum of the Lebesgue measures on the Pi.) We call the
polytopes Pi the cells of the subdivision. A dissection is a subdivision if any intersection Pi ∩Pj
is a face of each (or empty). It is a regular subdivision if it is induced from a piecewise-linear
continuous convex function on P, where the Pi are the domains of linearity.
