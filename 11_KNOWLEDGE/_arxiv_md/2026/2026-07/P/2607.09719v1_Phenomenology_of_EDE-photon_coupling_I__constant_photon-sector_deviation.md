Abstract.
We investigate a phenomenological extension of the photon sector motivated
by an early-time interaction between a scalar field component and radiation. The model
is described by a constant parameter ϵ which measures the departure of the photon energy
density and CMB temperature-redshift relation from their standard adiabatic evolution. The
standard photon sector is recovered when ϵ = 0. We implement the modified photon scal-
ing in CLASS and verify that the numerical background evolution agrees with the analytic
constant-ϵ prediction. We then study the diagnostic response of the recombination history,
visibility function and CMB temperature spectrum. These diagnostics show that small val-
ues of ϵ can shift the recombination history and modify the acoustic peak structure of the
CMB temperature anisotropy spectrum. These CMB outputs are used only as consistency
and response diagnostics not as full CMB likelihood constraints. As a preliminary statis-
tical application, we combine the modified CLASS implementation with MontePython and
constrain the model using Pantheon+SH0ES supernova data together with BAO distance
measurements. The late-time analysis gives ϵ = 0.0230 ± 0.0065 and improves the best-fit
likelihood relative to the fixed ϵ = 0 baseline with ∆χ2
eff = −10.518 and ∆AIC = −8.518.
These results indicate that the constant-ϵ extension is favoured by this preliminary late-time
data combination according to the AIC criterion.
Keywords: early dark energy, CMB temperature-redshift relation, photon-sector coupling,
recombination history, late-time cosmological constraints

Introduction
The standard ΛCDM model provides a remarkably successful description of the observed Uni-
verse. It accounts for the large-scale expansion history, structure formation and the main fea-
tures of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature and polarization anisotropies
with a small number of parameters [1, 2, 3].
In particular, the acoustic structure of the
CMB provides a precise probe of the physical conditions before and during recombination
while late-time distance measurements from supernovae and baryon acoustic oscillations con-
strain the subsequent expansion history. Despite this success, several observational tensions
and open theoretical questions continue to motivate extensions of the standard cosmological
framework.
One of the most discussed discrepancies is the tension between the value of the Hubble
constant inferred from early-Universe CMB data within ΛCDM and the value measured
using late-time distance indicators. This so-called Hubble tension has motivated a broad
range of proposed modifications to the pre-recombination and late-time expansion histories
[4]. Among these proposals, early dark energy (EDE) models [5, 6] are of particular interest
because they modify the energy budget before recombination and can therefore alter the

sound horizon inferred from the CMB. Scalar field realizations of EDE and related modified-
gravity constructions have been studied extensively in order to change the pre-recombination
dynamics while remaining close to the standard cosmological model at late times [7, 8, 9, 10,

A separate but closely related diagnostic of early-Universe physics is the CMB temperature-
redshift relation. In the standard adiabatic cosmology, photon number is conserved after ther-
malization and the CMB temperature evolves as T(z) ∝(1+z). Any departure from this law
signals either photon non-conservation, nonstandard energy exchange or a modification of the
thermal history of the radiation sector. Deviations from the standard temperature-redshift
relation have been discussed in connection with photon-axion conversion [14, 15], decaying
vacuum models [16, 17, 18], scalar-photon couplings [19, 20, 21], cosmic opacity [22, 23] and
interactions in the dark sector [6, 7, 8, 9]. Such effects are strongly constrained by the near-
perfect blackbody spectrum of the CMB and by measurements of the CMB temperature at
different redshifts [25, 26, 27]. More generally, CMB anisotropies can also constrain time-
dependent changes in fundamental constants or radiation sector physics through their impact
on recombination and acoustic oscillations [25, 26].
In the present work we consider a phenomenological realization of this idea in which an
early-time scalar-field component is allowed to exchange energy with the radiation sector. The
motivation is to examine whether an EDE-photon coupling can be encoded as a controlled
deviation from the standard photon dilution law and from the usual CMB temperature-
redshift relation.
Starting from a scalar field action with a non-minimal coupling to the
matter Lagrangian, we derive an instantaneous deviation parameter ϵ(a) that measures the
logarithmic departure of the photon energy density from the standard scaling ργ ∝a−4. In
the constant-ϵ limit, the photon energy density and temperature evolve as ργ ∝a−4+ϵ and
T(z) ∝(1 + z)1−ϵ/4, respectively. The aim of this paper is to focus on this constant- ϵ limit
as a minimal phenomenological description of the photon sector deviation. This allows us
to implement the modified photon scaling in the Boltzmann code CLASS [31] and to test
its impact on the background evolution, recombination diagnostics and CMB temperature
spectra. Since the modification affects the photon temperature entering the recombination
calculation, it can also shift the free-electron fraction, the visibility function and the acoustic
structure of the CMB anisotropy spectra.
We then use the modified CLASS implementation interfaced with MontePython to per-
form a preliminary late-time likelihood analysis based on the Pantheon+SH0ES supernova
data and BAO measurements. The purpose of this analysis is to test whether the late-time
distance data show a preference for a non-zero constant ϵ and whether the improvement in
the likelihood compensates for the additional degree of freedom. The statistical part of the
present work is therefore restricted to late-time distance data while the CMB spectra are
used as diagnostic outputs of the modified Boltzmann calculation.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we derive the modified CMB temperature-
redshift relation from the scalar field coupling. In Section 3, we introduce the constant-ϵ
background model and summarize its main phenomenological consequences. In Section 4, we
describe the CLASS implementation and validate the modified photon-density scaling at the
background level. In Section 5, we present recombination and CMB diagnostic responses of
the model. In Section 6, we give preliminary Pantheon+SH0ES+BAO constraints and com-
pare the constant-ϵ extension with the standard ϵ = 0 baseline. The final section summarizes
the results and discusses the limitations of the present analysis.

Recombination and CMB diagnostic response
The background-level results of the previous section verify that the modified CLASS imple-
mentation reproduces the expected photon-density scaling given by (24). Since the same
modification also changes the photon temperature entering the recombination calculation, it
is useful to examine its qualitative impact on the ionization history, the visibility function
and the CMB temperature angular power spectrum. The results in this section are diagnostic
outputs of the modified Boltzmann calculation. They show how the recombination history
and CMB spectra respond to the constant-ϵ photon-sector modification but they are not used
to define a CMB likelihood or to derive CMB parameter constraints.
5.1
Recombination history
The first diagnostic quantity is the free-electron fraction,

behaviour is consistent with the modified temperature evolution derived in section 2 and with
the background scaling discussed in section 3.

Preliminary Pantheon+SH0ES+BAO constraints and model
comparison
In this section we use the validated background-level CLASS implementation described in
previous sections to obtain preliminary observational constraints on the constant photon-
sector deviation parameter.
The aim is to test whether late-time distance data show a
preference for a non-zero value of ϵ and whether the resulting improvement in the likelihood
is sufficient to compensate for the additional free parameter. The analysis is carried out by
MontePython [32, 33] interfaced with the modified version of CLASS [31]. We consider two
models. The first is the baseline model in which the standard photon dilution law is recovered
by fixing ϵ = 0. The second is the extended constant-ϵ model in which ϵ is sampled as an
additional cosmological parameter. In both cases the same late-time data combination is
used. The likelihood includes the Pantheon+SH0ES supernova likelihood together with the
BAO likelihoods available in the MontePython setup. The primordial helium mass fraction,
YHe, is kept fixed in the present analysis. This choice is made because the present calculation
is intended as a preliminary late-time constraint based on the background implementation
rather than as a fully BBN-consistent or CMB-level analysis.

A direct comparison between the standard photon sector baseline and the extended constant-

values [34]. The quantity Lbest is the maximum value of this likelihood over the sampled
parameter space.
A smaller value of −ln Lbest therefore corresponds to a better best-fit
agreement with the data.

and perturbations.

Conclusions
In this work we investigated a phenomenological photon-sector extension motivated by an
early-time interaction between a scalar-field component and radiation. Starting from a scalar-
field action with a coupling to the matter Lagrangian, we derived an instantaneous deviation
parameter ϵ(a) that measures the departure of the photon energy density from the standard
adiabatic dilution law. In the present analysis, we focused on the constant-ϵ limit in which
this deviation is treated as a fixed effective parameter controlling both the modified photon
density scaling and the CMB temperature-redshift relation.
We implemented the constant-ϵ photon-density scaling in CLASS and validated the modi-
fied background evolution against the analytic prediction. The CLASS output reproduces the
expected photon-density ratio for the representative values of ϵ considered in the analysis.
This confirms that the numerical implementation correctly captures the intended photon-
sector modification at the background level. The background diagnostics also show the ex-
pected behaviour: negative values of ϵ increase the photon temperature and photon energy
density at high redshift leading to a larger expansion rate while positive values of ϵ produce
the opposite trend.
We then studied the diagnostic response of recombination and the CMB temperature
spectrum. The modified temperature-redshift relation changes the thermal history of the
photon bath and therefore affects the free-electron fraction, the visibility function and the
acoustic structure of the CMB temperature anisotropies. These diagnostics show that even
small values of ϵ can produce visible shifts in the recombination history and scale-dependent
changes in the CMB temperature spectrum. Thus, the photon-sector deviation considered
here is not only a background modification; it also propagates into recombination physics
and into the acoustic observables that characterize the early Universe.
As a preliminary statistical application, we used the Pantheon+SH0ES supernova likeli-
hood together with BAO distance data to compare the fixed ϵ = 0 baseline with the extended
constant-ϵ model. The late-time analysis gives ϵ = 0.0230 ± 0.0065 indicating that this data
combination favours the positive branch of the photon-sector deviation. In the convention
used here, positive ϵ corresponds to a photon temperature and photon energy density that
are lower at high redshift than in the standard adiabatic case while negative ϵ would produce
the opposite behaviour. The preferred positive sign is therefore not only a statistical result
but also fixes the direction of the inferred departure from the standard photon dilution law.
The best-fit likelihood improves relative to the fixed ϵ = 0 baseline with ∆χ2
eff = −10.518.
After accounting for the additional parameter using the Akaike information criterion, we find
∆AIC = −8.518. Therefore, within this late-time data combination and according to the
AIC criterion the constant-ϵ photon-sector extension is favoured over the standard ϵ = 0
baseline.
These results define the constant-ϵ case as the first step in a broader program on EDE-
radiation coupling. In this first step, the photon-sector deviation is compressed into a single
effective parameter allowing its impact on the background evolution, recombination diagnos-
tics and late-time distances to be isolated in a controlled way. The natural continuation is
to promote this parameter to a dynamical quantity ϵ(z) derived from the evolution of the
coupled scalar field. Such a formulation would connect the photon-sector deviation directly
to the scalar field potential, the field trajectory and the time dependence of the energy ex-
change. Future measurements of the CMB temperature-redshift relation, CMB anisotropies,
BAO distances and calibrated supernova samples will provide increasingly sensitive tests of
whether a constant or evolving photon sector deviation is compatible with the thermal and
expansion history of the Universe [39, 40, 41, 42]. This dynamical extension is left for future
