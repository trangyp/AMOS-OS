1. Introduction
The origination of theory of fuzzy sets (FSs) was traced back to Zadeh’s work where the
crisp set was extended to fuzzy sets [15]. The concept of intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs) was
pioneered by Atanassov [1]. Cuong and Kreinovich [3] put forward the notion of picture
fuzzy sets (PFSs) as a generalisation of fuzzy sets and intuitionistic fuzzy sets.
Yagar [14] introduced fuzzy multisets (FMS) allowing each element occurring more that
once. This notion was extended [7] by Shinoj and Sunil to intuitionistic fuzzy multisets. Cao
et al [2] initiated picture fuzzy multisets (PFMSs) as a generalisation of FM and IFMS and
also as an extension of PFS. In [10, 11] some properties of PFMSs were investigated. This
notion has been extended to relations, and as a result some properties were established in
[12, 13].
Rosenfeld [5] generalised fuzzy sets to fuzzy groups (FGs). In [8], Shinoj et al extended
Rosenfeld to fuzzy multigroup, and Shinoj and Sunil [9] studied the work in [8] to introduce
the concept of intuitionistic fuzzy multigroups.
In this paper, the concept of picture fuzzy multigroups was introduced as an extension of
the work in [9]. We also established some of the properties associated with them.

Definition 2.5. [3] Let Q and R be two PFSs. Then, the inclusion, equality, union, inter-
section and complement are defined as follow:
(i) Q ⊆R if and only if for all y ∈Y , σQ(y) ≤σR(y), τQ(y) ≤τR(y) and γQ(y) ≥γR(y).
(ii) Q = R if and only if Q ⊆R and R ⊆Q.
(iii) Q ∪R = {(y, σQ(y) ∨σR(y), τQ(y) ∧τR(y)), γQ(y) ∧γR(y))|y ∈Y }.
(iv) Q ∩R = {(y, σQ(y) ∧σR(y), τQ(y) ∧τR(y)), γQ(y) ∨γR(y))|y ∈Y }.
