PETERSEN GRAPH AND MONODROMY OF THE 27 LINES ON
THE CLEBSCH SURFACE
TATHAGATA BASAK
Abstract. Let G be the orbifold fundamental group of the moduli space of
smooth cubic surfaces Msm in P3
C with base point at the Clebsch surface X1.
The image of the monodromy action G →{Permutations of 27 lines on X1}
is famously the Weyl group of type E6. Here we give a description of this
monodromy action in terms of the Petersen graph P by working out the action
of ten explicit generators of G by elementary calculation. These ten genera-
tors were found in joint work with Allcock and Looijenga while studying the
description of Msm as a discriminant complement in a complex 4-ball quotient.
1. introduction
Cubic surfaces in P3 with the configuration of 27 lines on them are one of the
most studied examples in algebraic geometry going back to Cayley-Salmon (1849).
There is an enormous amount of literature on them.
For two detailed modern
treatments, see [H] or chapter 9 of [D].
Let P denote the Petersen graph; see figure 1. Let Mst and Msm denote the
moduli space of stable and smooth cubic surfaces respectively. Let X1 denote the
Clebsch cubic surface; this is the unique smooth cubic surface with S5 symmetry.
Let G be the orbifold fundamental group of Msm with base-point at X1. By [ACT],
Mst is a complex 4-ball quotient. While studying this ball quotient, we found ten
natural generators {gA : A ∈P} of G (see 4.5) that yield a nice presentation of G
as a quotient of the Artin group of P (this is joint work in progress [ABL]). Let
L(X1) be the set of 27 lines on X1. The elements of L(X1) can be naturally labeled
by the 15 edges and the 12 pentagons in P. We fix such a labeling in 5.5. If S is an
edge of P or a pentagon in P, then let LS temporarily denote the corresponding line
on X1. If we move X1 along a loop in the moduli space Msm, the 27 lines on X1
get permuted by the time we get back to X1. This defines the famous monodromy
representation of G on the set L(X1). Our job here is to explicitly describe this
monodromy action. With the above notation, the action of the ten generators gA
on the set L(X1) can be described in terms of P as follows (also see Figure 2):
1.1. Theorem (See 5.6 for a more detailed statement). Let A be a vertex of the
Petersen graph P. Let {Ei : i ∈Z/6Z} be the edges of the hexagon in P that is not
connected to A, in cyclic order. So Ei+3 is the edge opposite Ei. Let Pen(A, Ei)
be the unique pentagon in P containing A and Ei. Then the monodromy action of
gA on L(X1) exchanges the lines LEi and LPen(A,Ei+3) for i ∈Z/6Z. These twelve
lines form a double-six configuration. The remaining fifteen lines are fixed by gA.

an open subset of Mst can be represented by a four parameter family X of cubic
forms (called the Sylvester family) such that X →Mst is a S5-cover. It turns out
that, locally near τ, the map B(L) →Mst is also an S5-cover. So there is a open
neighborhood of τ in B(L) such that every point in it can be uniquely represented by
a form in the Sylvester family and this identification is S5 equivariant. Using this,
we write down ten explicit loops gij’s in the space X based at X1 that correspond
to the (µij, Rij)’s and then work out how the 27 lines on X1 move as we go around
these ten loops. This yields our main theorem 1.1 or rather its more detailed version
5.6. In theorem 1.1 we write gA = gij if A = {i, j} is a vertex of P.
We describe easy to remember names of the 27 lines on X1 and the action of the
ten generators gij are written down as ten explicit permutations of L(X1). One
verifies easily that these ten permutations generate the full Weyl group W(E6); see
[HR]. Because of the S5 symmetry of the entire set-up, one only needs to compute
the action of a single gij on L(X1). The ball quotient description of the moduli space
from [ACT] was only used to find the specific generators gij of the fundamental
group G. Our proofs of theorems 1.1, 5.6. are completely elementary calculations,
and the ball quotient description play no role in the proof. As expected, all of the
geometry can be described in terms of the combinatorics on the Petersen graph.
For a recent calculation of monodromy action of G on a family of cubic surfaces
branching over smooth cubic curves, see [Me]; in that case the image turns out to

2.1. Lemma. Let w : [0, 1] →C be the parametrized unit circle w(t) = e2πit.
Consider the one parameter family of degree n polynomials in a variable x given by
ft(x) = xn −w(t).
(1) There exists unique continuous function a(t) : [0, 1] →C satisfying the
conditions that a(t) is a solution of ft(x) = 0 and that a(0) = 1.
(2) Assume n = 2. There exists unique continuous functions a(t), b(t) : [0, 1] →
C satisfying the conditions that ft(a(t)) = ft(b(t)) = 0 and (a(0), b(0)) =
(1, −1).
Proof. The n roots of ft(x) are e2πi(k+t)/n where k = 0, 1, · · · , n−1 . The condition
a(0) = 1 together with continuity of a(t) implies that a(t) must be the solution
branch a(t) = e2πit/n. This proves part (a). Part (b) is immediate from part (a)
since a(t) + b(t) = 0 for all t.
□
