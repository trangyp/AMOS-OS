1. Introduction
Let R be a standard graded algebra over an infinite field k. The central object of study in Boij–S¨oderberg
theory is the rational Betti cone BQ(R), defined as the cone generated by all nonnegative rational linear
combinations of Betti tables of finitely generated graded R-modules. Understanding elements of BQ(R) is
therefore equivalent to understanding its fundamental building blocks, namely the extremal rays.
Motivated by the multiplicity conjecture of Herzog–Huneke–Srinivasan (see [19]), Boij and S¨oderberg con-
jectured in [9] that for R = k[X1, . . . , Xn], the extremal rays of BQ(R) are spanned by the Betti tables of
Cohen–Macaulay modules with pure resolutions. They supported this conjecture by proving it in the case
n = 2, and, assuming its validity in general, derived a proof of the multiplicity conjecture. Subsequently,
Eisenbud and Schreyer proved the Boij–S¨oderberg conjectures in [16]. In addition, they provided an algorithm
for decomposing an arbitrary Betti table in BQ(R) into a finite nonnegative rational combination of extremal
Betti tables. Thus, over a polynomial ring, the Betti cone BQ(R) coincides with its subcone Bpure
Q
(R), gen-
erated by all pure Betti tables. While a complete description of the Betti cone BQ(R) is known when R is a
polynomial ring, very little is understood in the general setting of standard graded k-algebras. In particular,
characterizing those algebras for which the equality BQ(R) = Bpure
Q
(R) holds is an extremely difficult prob-
lem. Our article aims to establish several necessary conditions for the equality BQ(R) = Bpure
Q
(R) to hold,
and provides general guiding questions and conjectures for this equality.
The equality of the pure and Betti cones has been established only in a handful of cases among standard graded
k-algebras that are not polynomial rings: for quadric hypersurfaces by Berkesch–Burke–Erman–Gibbons [7],
for short Gorenstein rings of embedding dimension two by Gibbons [17], and for Cohen–Macaulay k-algebras
of dimension at most one with minimal multiplicity by Ananthnarayan–Kumar [3]. More recently, Iyengar–
Ma–Walker [20] extended the results for polynomial rings to modules of finite projective dimension over all
finitely generated graded rings admitting linear Noether normalizations. In particular, they showed that if R
is a Cohen–Macaulay standard graded k-algebra, then the Betti cone generated by modules of finite projective
dimension coincides with the corresponding pure Betti cone. Nevertheless, a complete description of BQ(R)

Due to the work of Ananthnarayan–Kumar [3] it is known that given any standard graded algebra R with
the homogeneous maximal ideal m, the Betti tables of shifts of the modules of the form (a) R/mj, where
j ≥0, and (b) the modules M with pure resolution satisfying codim(M) = pdimR(M) always span an
extremal ray in BQ(R). In fact, as noted earlier, these are the only modules that give rise to extremal Betti
tables when R is a polynomial ring. In a way, (a) and (b) are form the smallest possible set of extremal rays.
One of the main results of this article characterizes all standard graded k-algebras R for which (a) and (b)
give a complete set of extremal rays of BQ(R). We show that such rings are precisely Cohen–Macaulay of
dimension at most one with minimal multiplicity or are polynomial rings (see Theorem 5.8). This result was
established by Ananthnarayan–Javadekar–Kumar [1] for the class of fibre products of dimension ≥1. In
addition, we provide a complete description of the Betti cone of the maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over
Cohen–Macaulay algebras of any dimension with minimal multiplicity (see Proposition 6.4), and further show
that this description characterizes such algebras (see Theorem 6.11).

(R) for specific classes
of rings, as discussed above, general necessary and sufficient conditions for this equality remain largely un-
known. A major portion of this article is devoted to deriving several necessary conditions. We begin by
proving a notable result that a minimal free resolution of the residue field over a standard graded k-algebra R
with edim(R) ≥2 is pure if and only if it is linear, i.e., if and only if the algebra is Koszul (see Theorem 3.2).
We use this to obtain the first necessary condition for the equality BQ(R) = Bpure
Q
(R), and show that R must

rays of BQ(R) are pure, rather than assuming the full equality above (see Theorem 3.3). Using this, we further
show that if the two cones coincide, then all finitely generated R-modules must have rational Poincar´e series
with a common denominator, i.e., R must be good in the sense of Roos (see Theorem 3.7). It is worth noting
that the classes of rings for which the equality BQ(R) = Bpure
Q
(R) is known to hold are all Golod over a

no known examples of good Koszul algebras that are not Golod over complete intersections.
After proving the above necessary conditions, we focus on some special classes of k-algebras and study the
equality of the pure and Betti cones for them. We prove that if the equality holds for an algebra R with
depth(R) = 0, then R must be an Artinian and level algebra (see Theorem 4.3). This result can be seen as the
evidence for the conjecture that if BQ(R) = Bpure

generic Gorenstein Artin algebras satisfying BQ(R) = Bpure
Q
(R) (see Theorem 4.12).
This article is structured as follows. In Section 2, we collect definitions, set-up the notation, and recall known
results relevant for the rest of the article. Section 3 is devoted to the study of purity of the resolution of k and
its applications to the equality BQ(R) = Bpure
Q
(R). Section 4 contains the analysis of this equality for the

minimal multiplicity with dimension at most one. Section 6 gives a complete description of the Betti cone of
maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over Cohen–Macaulay algebras of minimal multiplicity, and provides a
characterization of such algebras in terms of the description obtained. Finally, in Section 7, we make conclud-
ing remarks and state a few open questions and conjectures.

2.3. Koszul Algebras. Recall that a k-algebra R is Koszul if k has a linear resolution over R. In this subsec-
tion, we collect the necessary known facts about Koszul algebras.
The following theorem due to Avramov–Peeva gives a characterization of Koszul algebras.
Theorem 2.9. ([6]) Let R be standard graded k-algebra. Then the following are equivalent:
(i) reg(M) is finite for every R-module M.
(ii) reg(k) is finite.
(iii) R is Koszul.
The linearity of the resolution of k is in fact equivalent to the weaker property of purity of the resolution. We
show this in Theorem 3.2.
The following result of Avramov–Eisenbud provides an upper bound on the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of an R-module M.
Theorem 2.10 ([5], Theorem 1). Let R be a Koszul algebra, and let S be the polynomial ring mapping onto R.
Then the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of any module M over R is finite; in fact,
regR(M) ≤regS(M).
The next theorem is a useful tool to construct Koszul algebras, and also to detect Koszulness.
Theorem 2.11 ([12], Theorem 3.2). Let R be a Koszul algebra, and let R′ be a quotient of R. Then
(a) If regR(R′) ≤1 and R is Koszul, then R′ is Koszul.
(b) If regR(R′) is finite and R′ is Koszul, then R is Koszul.
Remark 2.12. As a consequence of the above theorem, we see that if x ∈R1 is a nonzerodivisor on R, then
R is Koszul if and only if R/⟨x⟩is Koszul.
