PLASTICITY OF THE UNIT BALL OF THE REAL
BANACH SPACE ℓ∞
RAINIS HALLER AND NIKITA LEO
Abstract. We prove that the closed unit ball of the real Banach space
ℓ∞is plastic, that is, every non-expansive bijection from the unit ball
onto itself is an isometry. The main step is to show that every non-
expansive bijection of this ball maps extreme points to extreme points.
This is done by using elementary coverings of the unit ball by balls of
radius one. The conclusion then follows from a theorem by Fakhoury.
The same argument also shows that, for arbitrary Γ, every non-expansive
bijection of Bℓ∞(Γ) preserves extreme points.
1. Introduction
A mapping between metric spaces is called non-expansive if it is 1-Lipschitz.
Following Naimpally, Piotrowski, and Wingler [9], a metric space M is called
plastic if every non-expansive bijection F : M →M is an isometry.
A natural question asks whether the closed unit ball BX of every real
Banach space X is plastic. This problem was explicitly posed and studied
by Cascales, Kadets, Orihuela, and Wingler [2], who proved that the unit
ball is plastic in every strictly convex Banach space. Since then, several
further positive results have been obtained, including the cases of ℓ1 and c;
see, for instance, [1,3–7].
We shall use a result of Fakhoury [3], which says that for Bℓ∞a non-
expansive bijection preserving extreme points is an isometry.
Levchenko and Zavarzina [8] recently proved plasticity results for the unit
spheres of ℓ1, ℓ∞, and c. Their result for Sℓ∞does not imply the result below
for Bℓ∞.
Our main result is the following.
Theorem 1. Let B be the closed unit ball of the real Banach space ℓ∞. Then
every non-expansive bijection F : B →B is an isometry. Equivalently, Bℓ∞
is plastic.
The proof is elementary up to the final application of Fakhoury’s re-
sult. We use the following simple observation: if 0 ̸= a, b ∈B and B ⊂
B(a, 1) ∪B(b, 1), then a and b must be non-zero opposite multiples of
the same coordinate vector. Applying this to the images of the coverings
B ⊂B(en, 1) ∪B(−en, 1) shows that the intervals [−en, en] are sent into
coordinate lines, up to signs and a permutation of the coordinates. The

3. A remark on ℓ∞(Γ)
Let Γ be a non-empty set and let B denote the closed unit ball of the real
Banach space ℓ∞(Γ).
Proposition 4. Let F : B →B be a non-expansive bijection. Then
F(ext B) ⊂ext B.
Proof. The coordinate part of the proof of Theorem 1 goes through verbatim
with N replaced by Γ. More precisely, the analogue of Lemma 3 says that if
0 ̸= a, b ∈B and B ⊂B(a, 1) ∪B(b, 1), then a and b are non-zero opposite
multiples of the same coordinate vector eγ for some γ ∈Γ. Hence, there
is an injective map σ: Γ →Γ and signs θγ ∈{−1, 1} such that, for every
x ∈B,
supp(F(x)) ⊂σ(supp x)
together with the corresponding sign preservation. The same argument as
above shows that σ is surjective.
Composing with the inverse signed permutation, we get a non-expansive
bijection G: B →B which preserves coordinate signs.
Since ext B =
{−1, 1}Γ, the general extreme point preimage result [2, Theorem 2.3] gives
G−1(x) ∈ext B for every x ∈ext B. The sign-preservation argument from
the proof of our main theorem then shows that G fixes every extreme point.
Therefore, F sends every extreme point to an extreme point.
□
