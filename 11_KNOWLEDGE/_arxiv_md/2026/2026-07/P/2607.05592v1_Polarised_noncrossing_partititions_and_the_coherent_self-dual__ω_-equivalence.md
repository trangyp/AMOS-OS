Polarised noncrossing partitions
and the coherent self-dual ω-equivalence
Amar Hadzihasanovic
Tallinn University of Technology
Abstract. We construct an acyclic augmented chain complex
of abelian groups whose entry in degree n > 0 is free on the
set of noncrossing partitions of degree n−1 equipped with a
{0, 1}-labelling of their gaps. The definition of the differen-
tial in this complex is related, via a restricted Leibniz rule,
to the gap-insertion operad of Ebrahimi-Fard, Foissy, Kock,
and Patras. We conjecture that this augmented chain com-
plex is the linearisation of a polygraph presenting a self-dual
model of the coherent walking ω-equivalence constructed by
the author, Loubaton, Ozornova, and Rovelli, and provide
evidence for this conjecture.

Introduction
Part of any sufficiently structured model of higher categories is a specifica-
tion of what it means for two objects in a higher category to be equivalent
[OR24]. In models of homotopy-theoretic provenance, the natural answer is
often extrinsic: two objects are equivalent if they are connected by a path in
some underlying space. In algebraic models, a most natural answer—definable
in the algebra of cells, units, and composition—is provided by the notion of
ω-equivalence, also known as pseudoinvertibility or weak invertibility [Che07].
This postulates that two objects s, t are equivalent if they admit a pair of
morphisms f : s →t, g: t →s, such that the composite gf is itself equival-
ent to ids and the composite fg is equivalent to idt in the higher categories
hom(s, s), hom(t, t), respectively. More in general, there are “truncated” no-
tions of (n −1)-equivalence between n-categories for each n ∈N. This notion
of equivalence has recently received renewed attention both in strict [HLOR25]
and in weak [FHM24, BM24, CH25] models of higher categories.

For computational purposes, it is useful to conceive of weak invertibility as
structure on a morphism; that is, consider that an ω-equivalence f : s →t
comes equipped with a weak inverse g: t →s and higher cells witnessing the
inversion. In particular, it is desirable that such data can be “packaged” in
a higher category—the free higher category containing an ω-equivalence, also
known as the walking ω-equivalence —since this provides an explicit construc-
tion for the localisation of a higher category at a morphism: to localise, attach
a copy of the walking ω-equivalence.
As discussed in [Lac04] in the case of equivalences of 2-categories, in or-
der for such an invertibility structure to be essentially unique, it is essential
that the walking equivalence be contractible in the appropriate homotopy the-
ory.
However, already in the setting of strict ω-categories—which can be
construed as a “toy model” of higher category theory, with the advantage
that more computational tools are currently available compared to fully weak
and homotopical models—the “naive” version of the walking ω-equivalence
fails to be contractible in the appropriate homotopical sense, as provided by
the folk model structure of Lafont, Métayer, and Worytkiewicz [LMW10]. In
fact, already the “naive walking equivalence” of 2-categories is not contractible
[OR24, Section 1.2.4].
In [OR24, Section 1.5.4], Ozornova and Rovelli proposed a candidate model
for the coherent (that is, contractible) walking ω-equivalence of strict ω-cat-
egories, also inducing models of coherent walking (n−1)-equivalences of strict
n-categories. This was eventually proven to be contractible by Ozornova and
Rovelli jointly with the author and Loubaton in [HLOR25].
While this solved the question from a homotopical standpoint, from a com-
putational standpoint this model has a downside. The notion of ω-equivalence
is manifestly symmetric: if s is equivalent to t, then t is equivalent to s. More
graphically, one would imagine an equivalence between s and t to be some-
thing like an “undirected” edge, a path that can be traversed both ways; so
one would expect that the data of an equivalence should be self-dual under the
reversal of s and t. However, the Ozornova–Rovelli model is asymmetric: it
dodges the non-coherence problem by separately assigning both a left inverse
gL and a right inverse gR to f.
The naive walking ω-equivalence is self-dual, but not coherent. A coherent
self-dual model of the walking 1-equivalence exists: it is the walking adjoint
equivalence. There is also a conjecturally coherent self-dual model of walking
2-equivalence, due to Gurski [Gur12]. To our knowledge, no self-dual coherent
models for higher dimensions appear in the literature.
This article aims to be a first step towards the construction of an explicit
self-dual model of the coherent ω-equivalence. The initial difficulty in building
such a model is simply to come up with an enumeration of the necessary
coherence cells in each dimension.
The article presents an ansatz for the
combinatorics underlying such an enumeration, connecting them to objects

the sum of generating (n −1)-cells appearing in the source of x, counted with
their multiplicity.

While we stop short of solving the problem, we find that the construction
of E• is in itself interesting and non-trivial, and that the unexpected point of
contact revealed between operads arising in combinatorics and higher categor-
ies via chain complexes is noteworthy, and might invite further exploration
of their connections. The form of our definition of a chain complex from an
operad—with a differential that reduces the arity of operations, instead of
the size of trees, and satisfies a Leibniz rule with respect to a particular de-
composition, instead of summing over all decompositions—is unusual, and we
could not find analogous prior examples. Similarly, for the methodology of
attempting to lift an augmented chain complex which is not a Steiner com-
plex to a polygraph, seeing it as a “linearised” shadow of a higher-categorical
structure, we could not find antecedents in the literature. Thus, we believe
that the mathematical and methodological questions that this work raises are
at least as valuable as the partial answers that it offers.
