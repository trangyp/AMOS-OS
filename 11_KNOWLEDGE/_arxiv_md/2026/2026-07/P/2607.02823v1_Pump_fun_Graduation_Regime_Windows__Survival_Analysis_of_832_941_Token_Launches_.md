Pump.fun Graduation Regime Windows:
Survival Analysis of 832,941 Token Launches
and the Social-Presence Effect
Arati Uday Kamat
ORCID: 0009-0000-4781-312X
June 10, 2026
Abstract
We present a Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional-hazards survival analysis of 832,941 Solana pump.fun token
launches with 24-hour graduation outcomes, observed continuously between 2026-05-08 and 2026-06-10. The
pooled graduation rate is 0.198% (Wilson 95% CI [0.189%, 0.208%]), a 3.18 ×  decline from the 0.63% rate
reported by Marino et al. (2026) for September-October 2025. (After excluding a four-day graduation-tracker warm-
up at the start of our window and the partial last day, the steady-state rate is 0.207% [0.198%, 0.218%]; the
structural effects we report below replicate within decimal-place noise under this exclusion.) We formalise the
windowed comparison as the Graduation Regime Windows (GRW) framework, name our 34-day window the RED-
PUMP-2026-v1 regime, and release the underlying 860,213-launch record-level dataset under CC-BY-4.0 on
Zenodo with a frozen schema. Social-channel presence exerts a large effect: launches advertising a Telegram
channel graduate at 1.485% versus 0.166% without (8.94 ×  lift, log-rank 𝑝< 10−300); the multivariate Cox
hazard ratio for Telegram is 5.40 (95% CI [4.73, 6.17], 𝑝= 6.6 × 10−138). Launches advertising all three social
channels graduate at 1.919% versus 0.110% for launches with none (17.4 ×  lift). Initial market-capitalisation above
the platform default of 30 SOL-a proxy for creator self-buying-raises the hazard ratio to 4.51, and the top quartile
(mcap > 31 SOL) graduates at 0.634%, almost exactly matching the 2025 pooled rate of Marino et al. (2026). The
cross-regime decline is therefore substantially attributable to a shift in the launch composition toward zero-self-buy
tokens, not to a fall in the success rate among self-buy launches. We make four contributions: (i) the first published
Kaplan-Meier graduation curve for pump.fun, (ii) the RED-PUMP-2026-v1 public benchmark, (iii) a quantified
longitudinal regime comparison, and (iv) the GRW framework and Pump.fun Graduation Index (PGI) standardised
metric. The dataset is deposited on Zenodo at concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20633486; analysis code is reproducible
from the deposit. Concordance of the Cox model is 0.858.
Keywords: pump.fun, Solana, memecoin, Kaplan-Meier, Cox proportional hazards, survival analysis, crypto
microstructure, benchmark dataset, regime comparison.
Introduction
The Solana-based pump.fun platform issued more than 12.8 million tokens by October 2025 (Liu et al.,
2026) and accounted for the majority of Solana token mints during late 2024 (Mzoughi et al., 2025). A
token “graduates” when its bonding-curve reserve reaches a threshold (currently ≈ 85 SOL) and migrates
to the PumpSwap (formerly Raydium) DEX. Graduation is the rare event in this market: Marino et al.
(2026), the first peer-quality empirical study of the platform, report a 0.63% graduation rate over a
655,770-token sample from September-October 2025.
Three things are missing from the current literature, and the present paper addresses each.


## Page 2


First, there is no published record-level public benchmark. The Marino et al. (2026) sample is not released;
industry dashboards (The Block (2026); Dune Analytics (2026)) publish aggregates but not the underlying
labelled rows. This makes regime comparison-and any future methodological replication-infeasible without
re-scraping the chain at substantial cost. We release RED-PUMP-2026-v1, a 860,213-launch labelled
dataset spanning 34 continuous days, under CC-BY-4.0.
Second, no published study has applied survival-analytic methods to pump.fun graduation. Marino et al.
(2026) explicitly defer hazard-rate modelling to future work. We fit Kaplan-Meier curves overall and by
stratum, and a multivariate Cox proportional-hazards model.
Third, off-chain social-presence features-whether the launch metadata advertises a Twitter (X) account,
website, or Telegram channel-have not been studied as graduation predictors at scale. Marino et al. (2026)
are explicit that their feature set is purely on-chain. We find these features highly predictive, with the
Telegram indicator alone producing an 8.94 ×  lift in the graduation rate (𝑝< 10−300).
We also formalise the windowed-comparison problem. Pump.fun graduation behaviour has shifted
substantially since 2024: industry-aggregator and academic reports place graduation rates near 2% in Q4
2024 (Mzoughi et al., 2025), 0.6% in September 2025 (Marino et al., 2026; ChainCatcher, 2025), and-in
this paper-0.198% in May-June 2026. We propose Graduation Regime Windows (GRW) as the naming
convention for these epochs and the Pump.fun Graduation Index (PGI) as the comparable per-window
metric. We name the September-October 2025 window the Marino et al. (2026) window and the present 34-
day window RED-PUMP-2026-v1.
Contributions.
1. The first publicly released record-level pump.fun benchmark dataset, RED-PUMP-2026-v1, with a
frozen schema and CC-BY-4.0 licence (Section 3).
2. The first published Kaplan-Meier survival curve and Cox PH analysis of pump.fun graduations
(Section 4-5).
3. A quantified three-point longitudinal comparison of pump.fun graduation rates across the Q4-2024,
Marino et al. (2026), and RED-PUMP-2026-v1 regimes, and an empirical decomposition showing that
the cross-regime decline is substantially attributable to a compositional shift toward zero-self-buy
launches rather than a fall in success rate among self-buy launches (Section 5).
4. The Graduation Regime Windows (GRW) framework and the Pump.fun Graduation Index (PGI)
metric, intended as standardised anchors for future work (Section 6).
Related work
Pump.fun-specific. Marino et al. (2026) provide the first academic study of the platform: a 655,770-token
sample from September-October 2025, with 4,338 graduations (0.63%). Their methodology is non-
parametric conditional-probability estimation with binning, plus a Shewhart-style control chart for “dump”
detection. They explicitly do not apply Kaplan-Meier, Cox PH, logit, or machine-learning models; they


## Page 3


propose all four as future work. They use only on-chain features (bonding-curve vSol, bot-routed
transaction share, count of distinct trader addresses, identities of historically successful traders, identity of
the creator). They do not release data or code.
Anderson (2025) provides a qualitative discussion of pump.fun tokenisation and market dynamics. Liu et
al. (2026) introduce the MemeTrans dataset for high-risk memecoin-launch detection on Solana and report
the 12.8M-token figure by October 2025; their orientation is classification of malicious launches rather than
graduation modelling.
Broader Solana memecoin literature. Mzoughi et al. (2025) study Solana memecoin trends across Q4
2024 and find < 2% of pump.fun tokens graduate. Niu et al. (2024) introduce the Coin-Meme multimodal
dataset (visual + textual + community + financial) for memecoin analysis. We cite these as the broader
academic context within which the pump.fun-graduation problem is situated.
Survival analysis in microstructure. Kaplan-Meier and Cox PH are standard tools in market-
microstructure research, with extensive prior application to limit-order survival, execution duration, and
asset delisting . We apply them here for the first time in published form to bonding-curve graduation as the
event of interest.
Industry data sources. The Block (The Block, 2026), Dune Analytics (Dune Analytics, 2026), and others
publish daily pump.fun graduation rates as time series. ChainCatcher (2025) report an aggregator value of
0.6% for the September 2025 week corresponding to the Marino et al. (2026) window, which independently
corroborates that paper’s headline number. To our knowledge, no industry source has released a record-
level benchmark for the May-June 2026 window we cover.
Data
Collection
A public-blockchain observer subscribed to the pump.fun program-account update stream on Solana
mainnet between 2026-05-08T17:17:38Z and 2026-06-10T18:11:08Z (34 days). At first detection of a new
launch, the observer records the mint address, Unix-millisecond seenAt timestamp, on-chain
created_timestamp, initial market capitalisation in SOL, and three Boolean indicators for whether the
launch metadata contains a non-empty Twitter (X), website, or Telegram URL. Description-length
(character count) is also recorded. The launch is then tracked for 24 hours or until terminal outcome.
The two terminal outcomes are GRADUATED (bonding curve crossed the graduation threshold and tokens
were migrated to PumpSwap) and TIMEOUT (24 hours elapsed without graduation). TIMEOUT is a right-
censoring event in the survival-analytic sense: tokens remain on the bonding curve indefinitely after
timeout, but follow-up tracking is discontinued. The 24-hour cap is a design constant of the observer and is
documented in the dataset schema.
Headline statistics
The full raw dataset contains 860,213 launch records on 860,194 unique mints (18 mints appear in more
than one launch row, owing to chain reorg events or observer races; we keep the first occurrence per mint).
At the snapshot time, 832,941 unique mints had reached a terminal outcome; the remaining 27,253 were

Regime comparison and decomposition
We compare our 34-day window to two prior measurements: Mzoughi et al. (2025) for Q4 2024 ( < 2%
graduation; we use 2% for the upper bound), and Marino et al. (2026) for September-October 2025 (0.63%
over 655,770 launches). The three-point longitudinal series is shown in Figure 5.


## Page 10


Three-point longitudinal comparison of pump.fun graduation rates across regimes.
The decline from the Marino et al. (2026) window to our window is a factor of 0.63/0.198 = 3.18 × . We
propose a partial decomposition: within our sample, the top market-cap quartile (Q4: launches with mcap 
> 31 SOL, i.e. creator self-buying) graduates at 0.634%-almost exactly matching the Marino et al. (2026)
pooled rate. Q1-Q3 launches (the remaining 75% of our sample, dominated by zero-self-buy launches)
graduate at 0.053% pooled (331 graduations over 624,706 launches). Under the (testable but here untested)
hypothesis that the Marino et al. (2026) sample contains a far higher fraction of self-buy launches than
ours, the cross-regime decline is substantially attributable to a compositional shift toward zero-self-buy
launches rather than a fall in success rate among self-buy launches. We flag this as a hypothesis requiring
confirmation against a record-level dataset for the September-October 2025 window (not currently
available); we present the decomposition as suggestive rather than definitive.
Temporal behaviour within the RED-PUMP-2026-v1 window
Figure 6 shows daily graduation rates within our 34-day window, with the partial last day (2026-06-10)
excluded. The series exhibits two distinct phases. During the first four days (2026-05-08-2026-05-11), rates
run between 0.015% and 0.043%, materially below the full-sample pooled 95% CI of [0.189%, 0.208%].
We attribute this to graduation-tracker startup latency: the launch-detection callbacks appear to have been
wired before the graduation-detection callbacks in the underlying observer infrastructure, so a fraction of
graduation events in the first four days were misclassified as TIMEOUTs. Starting 2026-05-12, daily rates
stabilise inside the pooled CI band and remain there for the remaining 29 days. We define this 29-day sub-
interval as the steady-state window and report a steady-state pooled rate of 0.207% (Wilson 95% CI
[0.198%, 0.218%]) on 763,091 launches (with 1,583 graduations). All structural conclusions in this paper
(social-presence effects, Cox hazard ratios, quartile gradient) replicate with at most decimal-place changes
when restricted to the steady-state sub-interval. The full-sample 0.198% headline is therefore the
conservative lower bound on the true May-June 2026 graduation rate, biased downward by the four-day
warm-up; we present both numbers and let downstream consumers choose between them.

Discussion
Graduation Regime Windows and the PGI
We introduce two related conventions for future work.
Graduation Regime Windows (GRW). A named, time-stamped epoch within which pump.fun graduation
behaviour is approximately stable. Windows we use:
Q4-2024 window (): graduation rate < 2%.
Marino et al. (2026) window (; 2025-09-01 to 2025-10-01): graduation rate 0.63%.
RED-PUMP-2026-v1 window (this paper; 2026-05-08 to 2026-06-10): graduation rate 0.198%.
Pump.fun Graduation Index (PGI). The pooled graduation rate within a stated window, expressed in
basis points. The RED-PUMP-2026-v1 PGI is 19.82 bp. Industry dashboards already publish daily values;
the PGI formalisation gives a single standardised name that is comparable across academic papers.
The proposal is light-weight by design: any monthly time series of pump.fun graduation rates can be
reported as a PGI series indexed by GRW.
Social-presence effects: why so large?
The 5.40 Cox hazard ratio for Telegram presence is the largest social effect, and the social-channel-count
gradient is near-monotone. We do not claim causality. Several non-exclusive interpretations are consistent
with the data:
1. Effort proxy. Setting up a Telegram channel signals creator effort that correlates with other unobserved
graduation-promoting investments (volume promotion, paid promotion, etc.).


## Page 12


2. Discoverability. Tokens with Telegram channels are more findable by buy-side bots and human traders,
increasing early order-book activity and the probability of clearing the bonding-curve threshold.
3. Selection. Tokens that already plan to graduate (e.g.  coordinated launches) advertise more social
channels because their creators have prepared.
Distinguishing among (1)-(3) requires a randomised intervention that is not feasible in this setting. We
report the effect sizes as predictive and useful for filter design, without claiming a particular causal
interpretation.
Compositional decomposition
The observation that the top market-cap quartile of our sample (creator self-buys) graduates at 0.634%-
almost exactly matching the Marino et al.  (2026) pooled rate-is suggestive of a compositional shift. If
launches without creator self-buys have proliferated since September 2025, the pooled rate falls even if
conditional rates within each launch type are stable. A definitive test would require comparing our market-
cap distribution against the Marino et al. (2026) sample, which is not possible until the September-October
2025 record-level data are publicly released or independently replicated.
Limitations
1. The 24-hour observation cap is a design constant. A small fraction of tokens may graduate beyond
24 hours; the literature suggests this fraction is negligible (Marino et al., 2026), but we cannot verify it
directly with the current dataset.
2. Detection lag (median 34.8 s) introduces measurement error in the initial-market-cap covariate for
tokens that move substantially in the first few seconds. We mitigate this by recording both timestamps
but do not correct further.
3. Cox proportional-hazards assumes time-invariant covariate effects. For 24-hour windows in a high-
frequency market this is a strong assumption; we plan to release a time-varying-covariate extension in
a later version of the dataset.
4. We make no real-money trading or filter-deployment claims. The graduation-rate elevations we report
are predictive and useful for filter calibration, not actionable trading signals without further out-of-
sample validation.
Update schedule and replication
We commit to monthly RED-PUMP releases (v2, v3, …) covering successive overlapping windows under
the same v1 schema. Each release will receive a separate Zenodo DOI for citation purposes. We invite
replication; the analysis script in the deposit reproduces all quantitative results in this paper from the raw
files in approximately five minutes on a standard workstation.

Conclusion
We have presented the first Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional-hazards survival analysis of pump.fun
token graduation, on a 832,941-launch sample with a 0.198% pooled graduation rate (Wilson 95% CI
[0.189%, 0.208%])-a 3.18 ×  decline from the September-October 2025 Marino et al. (2026) baseline.
Social-presence indicators are highly predictive: a Telegram-channel indicator alone produces an 8.94 ×
lift, and a full social-channel stack 17.4 × . The top market-cap quartile in our sample graduates at 0.634%,
almost exactly matching the Marino et al. (2026) pooled rate, suggesting that the cross-regime decline is
substantially attributable to a compositional shift toward zero-self-buy launches. We release the underlying
860,213-launch dataset as RED-PUMP-2026-v1 under CC-BY-4.0 with a frozen schema and reproducible
analysis code, and propose the Graduation Regime Windows (GRW) framework and Pump.fun Graduation
Index (PGI) as anchors for future work.
