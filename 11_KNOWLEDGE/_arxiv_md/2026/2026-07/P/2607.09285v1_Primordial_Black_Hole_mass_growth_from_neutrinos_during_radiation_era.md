Primordial Black Hole mass growth from neutrinos during radiation era
Ma¨el Gonin∗
TU Dresden, Institute of Nuclear and Particle Physics (IKTP), 01062 Dresden, Germany and
Deutsches Zentrum f¨ur Astrophysik (DZA), Postplatz 1, 02826 G¨orlitz, Germany
(Dated: July 13, 2026)
We present a new picture of primordial black holes mass evolution through neutrino absorption.
Using semi-classical approach and a closer look at the kinetic of the early plasma we revisit the
thermal absorption of radiation by a population of primordial black holes ranging from 10−3−109M⊙
embedded in a thermal bath. We find significant mass growth of intermediate mass and supermassive
PBHs, the effect shift the predicted e+e−peak from thermal history. Depending on the value of the
collapse fraction γ an additional peak around the intermediate mass range, might become significant.
Moreover, because PBH grow from the thermal bath the fraction of DM in PBH fPBH also change.
These results revise the previous view on mass evolution of PBH and have implications for dark
matter PBH observations.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Black Holes (BH) studies and modern cosmology both
stem from the general relativistic description of gravity.
Primordial Black Holes (PBHs) provide a direct connec-
tion between these two fundamental fields of physics.
PBHs as Dark Matter (DM) candidates have been con-
sidered for a long time in the literature [1]. A plethora
of phenomena could lead to gravitational collapse in the
early universe, the most studied being probably the col-
lapse from inflation-generated overdensities [2]. Gravi-
tational wave detections since 2015 and the subsequent
catalog [3, 4] have revitalized interest in PBHs [5]. Such
populations of BHs have already been suggested to ex-
plain GW observations [6–8]. Many observations could
constrain PBH abundance over a broad mass range [2, 9].
These often rely on the assumption of a monochromatic
PBH mass spectrum. While convenient for estimations,
the monochromatic assumption is hardly justified given
the theoretical uncertainties in inflation scenarios. PBH
clustering could also undermine the validity of constraint
estimates [9].
Moreover, established physical scenarios such as Chop-
tuik’s law [10–12] on the criticality of gravitational col-
lapse naturally break down the monochromatic assump-
tion by broadening the peak. The thermal history of the
Universe impacts the cosmic Equation of State (EoS) [13–
19] and creates peaks in the PBH mass distribution at
specific masses [5, 15, 16, 19, 20]. We can therefore pre-
dict characteristic PBH masses based on ground-based
experiments and simulations of fundamental interactions.
This entanglement between primordial plasma physics
and the mass spectrum makes PBHs probes of the early
Universe [20, 21].
The active debate over the validity
of constraints and what conclusions one can draw from
them requires an accurate description of the PBH history
from its very beginning to today.
The idea of PBHs is about the possibility of forming
BHs in the primordial plasma [22, 23], when the Uni-

verse was radiation dominated. In 1967, Zel’dovich and
Novikov estimated that such objects would accrete too
much matter in the radiation era [22], while in 1974 Carr
and Hawking discarded Zel’dovich’s argument. They in-
voked that the mean free path of radiation λrad
mfp, would
be much smaller than the PBH Schwarzschild radius
rS(MPBH); hence, PBHs would accrete in a hydrody-
namical regime that is not efficient enough for significant
mass growth.
Since this foundational work, the general view on
PBHs is that they are not able to gain mass in the ra-
diation era.
We re-examine this view, arguing that a
closer look at primordial plasma kinetics actually allows
for neutrino radiation absorption.
We do not discard
the Carr and Hawking argument, but rather state that
the condition on the radiation mean free path does not
hold at all times. The mechanism presented in this paper
does not rely on hydrodynamical calculations of a radia-
tion fluid, but rather treats BHs as black body radiation
absorbers. The present work is based on the recent study
Ref. [24].
In Section II, we present the PBH mass spectrum and
its features. In Section III, we review the theory of PBH
absorption and how neutrinos could be absorbed. In Sec-
tion IV, we present the results on the extended PBH mass
spectrum. Throughout this study we use natural units :
ℏ= c = kB = 1.
II.
THE EXTENDED PBH MASS SPECTRUM
FROM THERMAL HISTORY
PBHs can form from a variety of processes; see recent
reviews [2, 9]. In this section, we focus on describing the
PBH mass spectrum from the thermal history [5, 15, 20];
see also Refs. [16, 19] for detailed descriptions of the EoS.
Formation from the collapse of inflationary fluctuations
is often invoked, as it does not require new physical phe-
nomena. Instead, it builds on the theoretical uncertain-
ties regarding inflation scenarios. Many of these scenarios
can leave a tail at small scales [25]. This is our setup: the
Universe is filled with overdense regions of various sizes

V.
SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
In this study, we showed that significant PBH mass
growth is possible in the radiation era for MPBH ∼
103 −107M⊙with γ = 0.55. Using a semi-classical de-
scription of a BH in a thermal bath and a closer look
at the kinetics of the primordial plasma, we find that
neutrinos can be absorbed by PBHs. Depending on the
collapse fraction γ, the resulting mass growth can cre-
ate additional features in the PBH mass spectrum and
increase the portion of dark matter in PBHs. We exam-
ined four models of the transition from non-absorbing to
absorbing PBHs, finding graphical differences but small
variations in f abs
PBH.
In this letter, we coupled leptons and PBHs, but we
did not account for lepton or baryon asymmetries of the
Universe. We defer such work to future studies. These
results have implications for dark matter scenarios in-
volving PBHs. The absorption sweet spot can induce an
additional peak in the PBH mass distribution between
MPBH ∼103 −107M⊙, although the effect is more sig-
nificant for more massive ones.
PBHs are already candidates for the James Webb
Space Telescope’s ”little red dots”; neutrino-absorbing
PBHs could be the culprit.
As light seeds can grow
rapidly, these PBHs evade constraints based on primor-

Thanks to neutrino absorption, the

The PBH mass spectrum is known to be a probe of the

mass in the radiation era.
