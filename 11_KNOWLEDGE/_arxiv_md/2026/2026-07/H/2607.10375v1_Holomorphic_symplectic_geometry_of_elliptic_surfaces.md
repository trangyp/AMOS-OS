ELLIPTIC SURFACES
JUN-MUK HWANG AND GUOLEI ZHONG
ABSTRACT. When a complex surface X admits a nowhere vanishing
holomorphic 2-form, it determines a (holomorphic) symplectic structure
on X. We study the symplectic geometry of such a symplectic structure
when X is an elliptic surface. When the elliptic fibration is nonisotrivial,
we define a factorization of Kodaira’s functional invariant, called the
symplecto-functional invariant and prove that the symplecto-functional
invariant determines the symplectic geometry of a nonisotrivial elliptic
fibration. This leads to a classification of isogenies of nonisotrivial sym-
plectic elliptic fibrations with a fixed source. We also classify isogenies
of symplectic elliptic fibrations with a fixed target by studying symplec-
tic automorphisms of germs of singular fibers. As an application, we
prove that a symplecto-biholomorphic map between germs of fibers of
nonisotrivial elliptic K3 surfaces can be extended to compositions of
isogenies of K3 surfaces.
MSC2020: 14J27, 14J28, 53D05
Key words: Elliptic fibration, K3 surface, Holomorphic Lagrangian fibra-
tion
Conventions
• We work in the complex-analytic category: all varieties are defined
over the complex numbers, and all maps, bundles, and sections are
holomorphic. In particular, a symplectic form means a everywhere-
nondegenerate holomorphic 2-form.
• An open subset of a complex manifold refers to its Euclidean topol-
ogy, whereas a Zariski-open subset means the complement of a
closed analytic subset.
• Let F : C →C be a holomorphic function on a Riemann surface
C. The vanishing order of F at a point t ∈C is denoted by ordtF.
For a holomorphic map G: C →C′ between Riemann surfaces, we
denote by multtG the local degree of G at t ∈C, that is, if z is a
local coordinate on C′ centered at G(t), then multtG = ordt(z ◦G).
We set ordtF = ∞(resp. multtG = ∞) if F ≡0 (resp. G is a
constant map).

1. INTRODUCTION
Let X be a (not necessarily compact) complex surface whose canon-
ical bundle KX admits a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section ω ∈
H0(X, KX). The section ω defines a holomorphic symplectic form on
X. In this paper, we study the symplectic geometry of such surfaces when
there is an elliptic fibration f : X →C over a Riemann surface C, and we
explore how the symplectic structure interacts with the underlying complex
geometry. Naturally, the most compelling case is when X is compact, partic-
ularly when it is an elliptic K3 surface. Nonetheless, the problem remains
interesting on noncompact surfaces as well.
Let us start with some basic terminology.
Definition 1.1. Let f : X →C be a proper surjective holomorphic map
from a two-dimensional complex manifold X onto a Riemann surface C.
(i) We say that f is relatively minimal, if there exists no (−1)-curve in
a fiber of f.
(ii) We say that f has no multiple fiber, if each fiber of f has a nonsingu-
lar point.
(iii) We say that f is a genus 1 fibration if smooth fibers of f have genus
1.
(iv) In (iii), if a section Σ ⊂X of f is given, we call the pair (f : X →
C, Σ) an elliptic fibration. (Here we follow the convention of [SS]:
it is called an elliptic fibration with a section in [FM] and [Mi].)
(v) In (iii) (resp. (iv)), if a symplectic form ω on X is given, we call
the pair (f : X →C, ω) (resp. the triple (f : X →C, Σ, ω)) a
symplectic genus 1 fibration (resp. a symplectic elliptic fibration).
(vi) For an unramified holomorphic map ϕ: U →C, the ϕ-pullback
ϕ∗(f : X →C) is the natural genus 1 fibration over U obtained by
the fiber product of f and ϕ. If Σ ⊂X is given as in (iv) (resp.
ω is given as in (v), resp. Σ ⊂X and ω are given as in (iv) and
(v)), it induces a natural elliptic fibration ϕ∗(f : X →C, Σ) (resp.
a natural symplectic genus 1 fibration ϕ∗(f : X →C, ω), resp. a
natural symplectic elliptic fibration ϕ∗(f : X →C, Σ, ω)).
(vii) In (vi), if ϕ is an inclusion of an open subset O ⊂C, we write
ϕ∗(−) = (−)|O,
where (−) stands for (f : X →C), (f : X →C, Σ), (f : X →
C, ω), or (f : X →C, Σ, ω), respectively.

Throughout the paper, we use the classical theory of elliptic surfaces
originated from Kodaira [Ko] and developed by many authors. We cite
results from standard references [BHPV], [FM] [Hu], [Mi] and [SS]. In
addition, our methodology has two ingredients from symplectic geometry:
action-angle variables and symplectic singularities. A symplectic elliptic
fibration is a Lagrangian fibration, equivalently, a completely integrable
Hamiltonian system. The notion of action-angle variables in the theory of
completely integrable Hamiltonian systems (Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2)
plays a key role in a number of steps in our arguments. On the other hand, in
dealing with the local geometry of a ramification point of an isogeny between
symplectic elliptic fibrations, we use ideas (Definition 4.4 and Proposition
4.5) from the theory of symplectic singularities originated from Beauville
([Be],[Fu]).
Outline. The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we apply a
version of the action-angle variables to prove Theorem 1.4. In Sections 3 and
4, we focus on the symplectic geometry of non-isotrivial elliptic fibrations:
we introduce the symplecto-functional invariant and prove Theorem 1.11
(cf. Theorem 3.6) in Section 3, and we construct the symplecto-elliptic reduc-
tion and prove Theorem 1.12 in Section 4. Then in Section 5, we classify the
symplectic automorphisms of germs of symplectic elliptic fibrations, which
are used to prove Theorem 1.13 and Theorem 1.14 in Section 6. Finally, we
study the symplectic geometry of germs of isotrivial elliptic fibrations and
prove Theorem 1.17 in Section 7.
