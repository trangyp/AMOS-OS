Abstract
Observational data on type IIn supernova SN 2015da are revisited
to clarify some ambiguous issues of this phenomenon. The Hα-emitting
site is found to switch around day 90 from the circumstellar matter
to the unshocked ejecta. This picture makes it possible to recover the
expansion velocity of late time ejecta and to locate the dust formation
in the inner zone of unshocked ejecta.
The bolometric light curve
and expansion velocities combined with modelling the circumstellar
interaction imply that the supernova exploded with an energy of ≳
4×1051 erg inside the circumstellar envelope with the mass of ≈14 M⊙.
The recovered density of the circumstellar matter is consistent with the
Thomson optical depth inferred from Hα modelling on day 46. The
observed circumstellar envelope is produced by an average mass loss
rate of 0.035 M⊙yr−1 during 400 yr before the supernova explosion.
1
Introduction
Supernovae SN IIn is a category of diverse phenomena uniﬁed by the
presence of a dense circumstellar (CS) matter (CSM); "n" stands for the
narrow emission lines in their spectra (Schlegel 1990). These are rare events
and thus are rather distant. The initial stage after the explosion, therefore, is
usually missed that hampers the understading of the overall physical picture.
The supernova IIn SN 2015da is an exception from this rule.
It was
discovered soon after the explosion and was followed spectroscopically and
photometrically for 8 years (Tartaglia et al. 2020: Smith et al. 2024, hereafter
T20 and S24, respectively).
Remarkably, the spectral evolution has been
traced in detail at the early stage, which turns out below a key factor in
clarifying a physical picture of phenomena similar to SN 2015da.
The interpretation of observational data on SN 2015da (T20; S24) sug-
gests that this SN IIn is characterized by a large mass of the CSM estimated
from 5.5 −10.5 M⊙(T20) to 20 M⊙(S24), and a high explosion energy of
(5 −10) × 1051 erg implied by a large radiated energy (S24). Inferred mass
and a time of CS shell formation suggest tremendous average mass loss rate
up to ∼0.1 M⊙yr−1 (S24) that could be provided by either LBV pulsations
(T20) or by a mass loss from a common envelope of a massive binary (S24).
The large volume of observational data, especially at the early stage, mo-
tivates us to revisit the SN 2015da issue in attempt to clarify some uncertain
points, particularly, the CSM mass, explosion energy, and the location the
source of the dust infrared (IR) ﬂux emerged after about day 500 (T20).
Three options for the origin of the IR dust emission are conceivable: (i)
IR echo from the pre-existing dust (T20; Fransson et al. 2014) similar to
SN 1979C and SN 1980K (Dwek 1983); (ii) dust formation in the cold dense
shell (CDS) between the forward and reverse shock (S24) following conjecture
for SN 1998S (Pozzo et al. 2004); (iii) dust formation in the undisturbed SN
envelope likewise in SN 2010jl (Maeda et al. 2013).

Interpretation of Hα evolution
One can identify two physically diﬀerent stages of the SN 2015da spectral
evolution (T20).
At the early stage t < ttr ≈90 days the spectrum of
SN 2015da is a smooth continuum with a prominent Hα emission (narrow
core and broad wings).
The narrow component is emitted by the CSM,
whereas broad wings are due to a Thomson scattering of line photons in
the same CSM with the Thomson optical depth τT ≳1. The opaque CDS
fully blocks the radiation from the unshocked SN ejecra, so we do not see
broad lines from the undisturbed SN, the phenomenon formerly recognized
in SN 1998S (Chugai 2001).
The termination of this stage is manifested
by the weakening CS Hα emission and transition to the late stage t > ttr
that is characterized by the emergence of broad Hα emission with growing

The transition from the early to the late stage is well illustrated by a sharp

1.4 × 1042 erg s−1 is attained on day 210 (T20).
The identiﬁcation of the broad Hα emission with unshocked ejecta at
t > ttr has two important implications. Firstly, it permits us to measure the
maximal velocity of the unshocked ejecta from the blue edge of Hα emission.
Particularly, on day 138 the Hα (T20) shows the maximal ejecta velocity
of ≈6500 km s−1, while on day 2603 (S24) the expansion velocity is of ≈
3000 km s−1. Secondly, it is of high signiﬁcance for the issue of the new dust
location (Section 5).
Noteworthy, the presented picture of the dominant role of the unshocked
SN ejecta in the late Hα luminosity is an alternative option with respect to
the Hα emitted by the CS clouds in the forward shock proposed for SN 2010jl
(Chugai 2018). This model is also plausible, since it is based on the formation
of a broad velocity spectrum of Hα-emitting gas as result of the crushing and
fragments acceleration of CS clouds in the forward shock (Klein et al. 1994).
However, the absence of a broad Hα shock wave component at the early stage
is a strong argument against such origin of the Hα emission at the late stage.

The presented CS interaction model suggests the instant radiation escape,
although at ﬁrst glance tremendous CSM mass might result in the large radi-
ation diﬀusion time comparable to the time of the bolometric light maximum
∼50 −100 d (T20, S24). In reality, at the maximum tmax ≈50 d the CSM

Hα and CSM density
The density distribution of the CSM inferred via the CS interaction model
can be tested using the Hα proﬁle at the early stage, when this line forms
in the CSM. To this end we model the Hα line proﬁle in the Keck/DEIMOS
spectrum on day 46 (T20). The spectrum has been used earlier to demon-
strate that narrow Hα with broad wings forms in the CS shell with the large
Thomson optical depth τT ≫1 (T20).
Here we apply a model that apart from the Thomson scattering takes
into account the resonance scattering in Hα. The resonance scattering brings
about two eﬀects. Firstly, the resonance scattering of the CDS continuum
radiation produces an additional P Cyg component. Secondly, the resonance
scattering can force line photons to experience larger amount of Thomson
scatterings. As result, a moderate Thomson optical depth τT ∼1 turns out
suﬃcient to describe Hα broad wings.
The computational zone of CSM provided by the CS interaction model
lies between the optically thick CDS at rs = 5.2 × 1015 cm on day 46 and
the external radius of 9rs. The electron number density is a fraction (x)
of the model CS hydrogen density (nH), i.e., ne = xnH. The adopted elec-
tron temperature is 104 K that is close to the temperature of the hydrogen
collisional ionization. The recombination Hα emissivity is j = An2

a normalizing factor). The Hα Sobolev optical depth along a direction ⃗s

kinematics u(r) and cosine µ of an angle between the photon wave vector and
radius. The radiation transfer is computed by the Monte Carlo technique.

6
Discussion
The aim of the paper has been to estimate CSM mass and explosion
energy of type IIn SN 2015da and clarify some uncertain points of the physical
picture.
I ﬁnd that the evolution of the Hα luminosity and the line width presented
by Tartaglia et al. (2020) strongly suggests that at t = ttr ≈90 day after
the explosion the Hα-emitting site switches from the CSM to the unshocked
ejecta. This behaviour is consistent with the blocking SN ejecta emission by
the opaque CDS at the early stage (t < ttr) and subsequent clearing the CDS
at the later stage (t > ttr). This picture was recognized in SN 1998S (Chugai
2001) and described in detail for SN 2023ixf (Utrobin and Chugai 2026). The
similar transformation of Hα width, from narrow to broad, is shown by the
spectral evolution of type IIn SN 2024by (Goto et al. 2026).
The conclusion on the origin of the late time Hα emission in the un-
shocked ejecta is of importance in two respects. First, in this picture the
blue edge velocity of Hα emission is a measure of the maximum velocity of
the unshocked SN ejecta; this velocity is used in the model of the CS in-
teraction to recover the mass of the CSM. Second, this picture permit us
to use late time Hα for testing the conjecture on the dust formation in the
unshocked SN ejecta at t ≳500 days.
The association of the late time Hα emission with the unshocked SN
ejecta is an alternative to the model of SN 2010jl (Chugai 2018), where the
dominant Hα emission was attributed to CS clouds crushed, fragmented and
accelerated in the forward shock (Klein et al. 1994).
The CS interaction model combined with the observational light curve
and expansion velocity permits us to determine the CSM mass of ≈14 M⊙
and a low limit of the explosion energy ≳4 × 1051 erg. Remarkably that the
found CSM mass varies in the range of only 7% around the mean value for
explored range of SN ejecta 5 - 20 M⊙. The inferred density of the CSM is
supported by the modelling of the CS Hα on day 46.
The CS Hα modelling reveals an eﬀect of the CSM acceleration ahead
of the CDS up to ≈170 km s−1 that is beyond the acceleration by the SN
radiation (≈40 km s−1). The additional ups = 130 km s−1 could be due to
the CSM acceleration by a cosmic ray (CR) pressure.
The required CR
acceleration eﬃciency (i.e., the ratio of CR pressure at the shock wave to
the dynamical pressure) is η = pc/ρv2
cds ≈2ups/vcds (Chugai 2019), which for
ups = 130 km s−1, and the CS interaction model value vcds = 9200 km s−1 on
day 46 results in η ≈0.03.
The expansion velocity of the CSM u = 80 km s−1 and the outer CSM
radius R = 1017 cm indicate that 14 M⊙has been lost during tloss = R/u ≈
400 yr before the explosion with the average mass loss rate of ≈3.5 ×
10−2 M⊙yr−1. The enormous mass loss rate of pre-SN 2015da could take
place in the common envelope (CE) regime of a massive binary (S24; Chugai
and Danziger 1994).
A conceivable CE scenario for 10 M⊙SN ejecta suggests the preSN of

7
Conclusion
We conclude with a summary of major results.
• The Hα evolution before and after day 90 reﬂects the transition from
the stage of Hα emission by the CSM towards the Hα emission by the
unshocked SN ejecta.
• The bolometric light curve and SN expasion velocity imply the shock
interaction between the SN ejecta with the CS envelope of ≈14 M⊙.
• The SN explosion energy is ≳4 × 1051 erg, beyond the neutrino-driven
mechanism.
• The observed CSM is produced by the mass loss at the average rate of
0.035 M⊙yr−1 during the last 400 years before the SN explosion.
• The Hα emission on day 46 forms in the CSM with the Thomson optical
depth of ≈1.1 that is consistent with the density distribution recovered
by the CS interaction model.
• The Hα blueshift after the day 500 is an outcome of the Hα absorption
by the dust formed in the inner zone of the SN ejecta.
