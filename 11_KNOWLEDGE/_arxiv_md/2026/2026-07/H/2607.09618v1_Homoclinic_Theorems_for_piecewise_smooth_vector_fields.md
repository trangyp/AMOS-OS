Abstract. In this paper we provide extensions of the Birkhoﬀ-Smale Homo-
clinic Theorem and the λ-Lemma for piecewise smooth vector ﬁelds free of
sliding motion. We prove that if the vector ﬁeld is T-periodic in its indepen-
dent variable and has a transverse homoclinic point, then its time-T-map is
conjugated with a Bernoulli Shift and thus the vector ﬁeld is chaotic.
The
Bernoulli Shifts considered here may have any ﬁnite number of symbols, or
countably inﬁnite many.
1. Introduction
When studying the 3-body problem, Poincar´e [23, Chapter 33] observed the
occurrence of transversal intersections between the stable and unstable manifolds
of a hyperbolic point. Such an intersection was named a homocline point [23, p. 384]
and it was proved that the dynamics around it are quite rich, as for example one
homocline point implies the existence of inﬁnitely many [23, p. 387]. Nowadays
such a point is known as homoclinic point and plays a key role for chaos.
The relation between homoclinic points and chaos was explored by Birkhoﬀ[6,
Chapter 4], where it was proved for two-dimensional C1-diﬀeomorphisms, that a
homoclinic point of non-ﬂat contact between the stable and unstable manifolds
is accumulated by periodic points of increasing period [6, p. 178]. Birkhoﬀalso
claimed the existence of richer dynamics, with an “almost inconceivable hierarchy
of solutions” [6, p. 184 and 185], including not only the periodic points, but also
the asymptotic solutions to those periodic points (forward or backward in time),
recurrent trajectories and their respective asymptotics.
This culminates in the seminal contribution of Smale [26, Theorem B], who
proved that in a neighborhood of a transversal homoclinic point of a n-dimensional
C1-diﬀeomorphism there is an invariant Cantor set whose dynamics are topologi-
cally conjugated to a Bernoulli Shift of ﬁnitely many symbols. Smale also proved
that such a dynamics persists under small perturbations [26, Theorem A]. These re-
sults are nowadays know as Birkhoﬀ-Smale Theorem [13, Theorem 5.3.5], or Smale
Homoclinic Theorem [18, Theorem 2.3], and they were the foundation of the ground-
breaking horseshoe map [27, p. 771].
After that, Palis [19, Lemma 1.1] proved that the stable and unstable manifolds of
the transverse homoclinic point accumulate over themselves in a C1-manner. This
result is now known as λ-Lemma and is the foundation of the notion of homoclinic
tangle. Besides that, the λ-Lemma became a keystone in the proof of an extension of

Birkhoﬀ-Smale Theorem regarding the hyperbolicity of the invariant set, meaning
that every point in the invariant set has local stable and unstable manifolds that
are carried with the base-point when iterating the diﬀeomorphism. Nowadays it
is know that invariant set provided by the Birkhoﬀ-Smale Theorem is hyperbolic,
and it is common to include this information in modern versions of the theorem,
see [13, Theorem 5.3.5] or [18, Theorem 2.3].
There is another extension for the Birkhoﬀ-Smale Theorem, provided as far as
we know by Moser [16, Theorem 3.7], although he says having learned it from Con-
ley [16, p. 100] (without mentioning a reference). Moser’s Theorem is two-folded.
First it is proved that there is an invariant set (not compact) whose dynamics are
topologically conjugated to a Bernoulli Shift of countably inﬁnite many symbols.
Then it is proved that this conjugation can be extended to the compactiﬁcation
of the invariant set, in order to include the points that “escape” for positive or
negative time [16, p. 75]. In particular, it is proved that the homoclinic points are
dense [16, Theorem 3.8] in this compactiﬁcation.
From there on the theory ﬂourish and generalizations appeared, such as for non-
transversal homoclinic points [24] (and the references therein), normally hyperbolic
manifolds [8], Lipschitz functions [22], and piecewise smooth vector ﬁelds [4, 5, 7].
Regarding the latter it is known that in the presence of sliding motion, a transversal
homoclinic point does not guarantee chaos [12].
However, as far as we know, only the original version of Birkhoﬀ-Smale Theorem
was provided for piecewise smooth vector ﬁeld without the presence of sliding [5].
In this paper we provide a new proof for this result, but that also includes the
hyperbolicity of the invariant set, and the generalizations of Moser. A proof of
the λ-Lemma is also provided, allowing, for example, the reduction of heteroclinic
intersections to homoclinic ones.
The paper is organized as follows. We begin with a preliminary Section 2 for
piecewise smooth vector ﬁelds, where some technical lemmas are stated. The gene-
ralizations of the λ-Lemma and the Homoclinic Theorems are provided in Section 3.
In Section 4 we obtain a “lift” of these results for the piecewise smooth vector ﬁeld
itself, and apply it on an example. The technical lemmas are proved in Section 5.

3.4. Chaos. We now use Devaney’s notion of chaos [9, Chapter 8] to formulate the
chaotic dynamics provided by the existence of transversal homoclinic points.
Deﬁnition 1. Let (Λ, d) be a metric space and f : Λ →Λ a continuous map. We
say that f is chaotic in the sense of Devaney if the following statements holds.
(a) The set of periodic points of f is dense in Λ;
(b) f is transitive in Λ, i.e. given any two non-empty open sets U1, U2 ⊂Λ,
there is n > 0 such that f n(U1) ∩U2 ̸= ∅.
In his original deﬁnition of chaos, Devaney also included the following condition
in addition with (a) and (b):
(c) f has sensitive dependence in Λ, i.e. there is β > 0 such that for any x ∈Λ
and any neighborhood U ⊂Λ of x, there are r ∈U and n > 0 such that

(b′) f has a dense orbit in Λ.
