HIGH-DEGREE COHOMOLOGY OF CONGRUENCE
SUBGROUPS OF SLn(O) VIA COHOMOLOGY OF
S-ARITHMETIC GROUPS
MATTHEW SCALAMANDRE
Abstract. If p is a prime ideal of a number ring O, then the top-degree
cohomology of the principal congruence subgroup of level p is naturally a rep-
resentation of SLn(O/p). We prove that the multiplicity of the Steinberg rep-
resentation in this cohomology space is one. When O is Euclidean and p is
suitably small – for example a universal side divisor – then we prove that the
multiplicity of the Steinberg representation in the next-highest-degree coho-
mology space is zero. Our proof relies on a computation of the cohomology of
an S-arithmetic group ouside of a linear range of degrees, derived from work
of Blasius–Franke–Grunewald.

1. Introduction
Arithmetic groups such as SLn(Z) are of central importance in number theory,
group theory, topology, and many other areas.
Determining the cohomology of
these groups is therefore a fundamental problem. The cohomology of these groups,
at least with rational coefficients, is often well-understood in cohomological de-
grees which are small relative to n, by stability results such as [6]. However, the
cohomology in high degrees is much more difficult to compute.

If F is a number field and O is its ring of integers, we recall the following family

Definition 1.1. Let I be an ideal of a number ring O, and let n ≥2. Then, the

The outer action of the finite group SLn(O/I) on Γn(I) induces a natural repre-

Indeed, we say that a finite-index subgroup Γ ≤SLn(OF ) is a congruence subgroup

is normal in SLn(O), it is normal in Γ, and so Γ/Γn(I) is a finite group. By the
Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence, we have:
Hq(Γ; C) ∼= Hq(Γn(I); C)Γ/Γn(I).
Our primary goal will be to investigate this structure when I = p is a prime ideal.

2.1. Review of Buildings and BN-pairs. We begin by recalling the definitions
of buildings and Tits systems, following [11]. These definitions will provide a useful
language for later sections.
Definition 2.1. Let G be a group. The data of a Tits system is a pair of subgroups
B, N and a set Σ such that:
(1) B, N together generate G.
(2) N normalizes T = B ∩N.
(3) Σ is a set of generators of W = N/T such that:
(a) For every s ∈Σ and w ∈W, we have
BsBwB ⊆BwB ∪BswB.
(b) For every s ∈Σ, sBs−1 ̸⊆B.
If the group G has a given Tits system, then a subgroup P ≤G is called special
if it is of the form BW ′B, where W ′ is a subgroup of W generated by a subset of
Σ. These special subgroups are self-normalizing.
The poset of left cosets of special subgroups, ordered by reverse inclusion, is the
poset of simplices of a simplicial complex ∆, the building associated to the Tits
system. The homotopy type of this space is known:
Theorem 2.2 (Solomon-Tits Theorem [30]). Let ∆be the building associated to a
Tits system on G. If W is infinite, then ∆is contractible. If W is finite, then ∆
homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres of dimension |Σ| −1.
We will need two examples, which we state here. For verification of the axioms,
see [11].
Example 2.3. Let F be a field, and let G = SLn(F). Let T = Tn(F) be the subgroup
of diagonal matrices of determinant 1. Let B = Bn(F) be the subgroup of upper-
triangular matrices (where the diagonal entries need not be 1, so that T ≤B), and
let N = N(T) be the set of monomial matrices. Then, W = N/T ∼= Sn, and we
choose Σ = {s1, . . . , sn−1} where si is the adjacent transposition (i, i + 1). Then,

parabolic subgroups.

3. Cohomology of S-arithmetic Groups
In this section, we show how to use the automorphic decomposition of coho-
mology to describe the cohomology of S-arithmetic groups. The organization is
as follows. In section 3.1, we collect some results about differentiable cohomology
and relative Lie algebra cohomology. We then provide an expository overview of
