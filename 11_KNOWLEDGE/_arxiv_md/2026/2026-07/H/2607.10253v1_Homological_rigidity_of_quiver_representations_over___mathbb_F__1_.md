HOMOLOGICAL RIGIDITY OF QUIVER REPRESENTATIONS OVER F1
CHANGJIAN FU, LIANG YANG, AND ZHIYUAN ZENG
ABSTRACT. We establish a homological rigidity phenomenon for the category of representa-
tions of quivers over the virtual field F1, which is inherently non-additive and does not admit
classical homological algebra tools. We prove that all higher Yoneda extension groups van-
ish beyond degree two for arbitrary quivers, including infinite ones. Consequently, the global
dimension of the category is universally bounded by 2. Moreover, we obtain a complete classi-
fication of quivers according to their homological dimension, which is determined solely by the
underlying orientation structure.
1. INTRODUCTION
Let F1 be the virtual field, i.e., the “field with one element”. The philosophy of F1 dates back
to Tits [Tit57], who observed that incidence geometries over a finite field Fq have combinatorial
counterparts that can be interpreted as incidence geometries over F1. Later, it also appears in
Manin’s work [Man95] on translating the geometric proof of the Weil conjectures from func-
tion fields to the case of Q with the hope to shed some light on the Riemann Hypothesis. To
this day, the philosophy of F1 continues to attract immense interest and has undergone signif-
icant development across various branches, including algebraic geometry, number theory, and
combinatorics, see [CC10,CC11,Dei05,Dei08,TV09] for instance.
In [Szc12], Szczesny introduced quiver representations over F1 as a degenerate, combinato-
rial analogue of classical quiver representations over fields. Over the past decade, by extending
techniques from classical representation theory, researchers have extensively explored the in-
terplay between the combinatorics of F1-representations and their field-theoretic counterparts
(see, e.g., [JKS23,JS23,JS24,Kle25]). By contrast, the homological properties of quiver repre-
sentations over F1 remain largely unexplored.
Classically, the homological algebra of representation categories is heavily governed by ad-
ditive and abelian structures. In settings such as quiver representations over a field, projective
resolutions and derived categories provide powerful, standard machinery to study homologi-
cal invariants. In the non-additive setting of F1-representations, however, these foundational
tools are no longer directly available. The resulting categories exhibit fundamentally different
homological behaviors, and very little is known about the structures of their higher extension
groups.

To package this exploration rigorously, let Q be a finite quiver and rep(Q, F1) the category
of finite-dimensional F1-representations of Q. Although rep(Q, F1) is not additive, it forms a
proto-exact category in the sense of [DK19]. In particular, one can adapt Yoneda’s original
construction to define the extension spaces Extn(L, N) for any L, N ∈rep(Q, F1) and any
positive integer n. Note that Extn(L, N) is not an abelian group but rather a pointed set, or
equivalently, an F1-vector space.
Definition 1.1. Let Q be a quiver. The global dimension of rep(Q, F1) is defined as
gldim rep(Q, F1) = sup{n | Extn(−, −) ̸= 0}.
Similarly, the subcategory rep(Q, F1)nil consisting of nilpotent F1-representations is
extension-closed. One can thus define the global dimension of rep(Q, F1)nil in an analogous
manner.
Our interest in this global dimension originates from an expectation of Szczesny [Szc12].
Inspired by the fundamental role played by the Euler form in Ringel’s theory of Hall algebras
[Rin90], Szczesny anticipated that the Euler form would play a parallel role in the Hall algebra
of rep(Q, F1), provided it is well-defined. However, as noted above, the breakdown of classical
homological methods poses a formidable challenge to explicitly computing these Ext-spaces
for general quivers. Indeed, it has been recently observed that the homological properties of
rep(Q, F1) differ drastically from those over fields [FRY24]. Specifically, for a linear quiver
of type An with n ≥3, the global dimension is 2, contrasting sharply with the classical global
dimension of 1. This striking phenomenon naturally led to the conjecture that the global di-
mension of a general quiver might grow arbitrarily large (e.g., taking values such as 3, 4, or
5).
The main objective of this paper is to show that this is surprisingly not the case: the global
dimension of an arbitrary quiver (even if infinite) is always bounded by 2.
Theorem 1.2. Let Q be an arbitrary quiver. Then we have
gldim rep(Q, F1) ≤2
and
gldim rep(Q, F1)nil ≤2.
The proof of Theorem 1.2 is based on a non-additive version of a well-known result in the
derived category of a quiver over a field (cf. Section 3). The key ingredient is to replace the
classical octahedral axiom with an explicit combinatorial lifting construction.
According to Theorem 1.2, the global dimension of rep(Q, F1) can only take values in
{0, 1, 2}. This restriction naturally motivates a classification of quivers according to their global
dimensions. By employing the restriction and induction functors for subquivers, we obtain a
complete classification basing on its global dimension.

Let rep(Q, F1) be the category of representations of Q over F1 and rep(Q, F1)nil the full
subcategory of rep(Q, F1) consisting of nilpotent representations. The category rep(Q, F1) is
not additive, but it shares many good properties with the category of representations of Q over
a field. For instance, every morphism has a kernel and a cokernel. It is proto-exact in the
sense of Dyckerhoff and Kapranov [DK19], where all monomorphisms and epimorphisms are
admissible. Since the category is normal, this structure is equivalent to being a proto-abelian
category in the sense of [And09]. It is also combinatorial with exact direct sum in the sense of
[ELY22]. Both the Jordan–H¨older and Krull–Schmidt theorems hold in rep(Q, F1); we refer to
[Szc12, Section 4] for more details.
