HAMCOR: A physics-driven Hamiltonian framework for
inferring AGN coronal geometry from X-ray reverberation

1Universit`a degli Studi di Brescia, Brescia, Italy
ABSTRACT
We present HAMCOR (Hamiltonian-based AGN Multi-constraint CORonal inference framework), a geometry-
agnostic method for inferring the X-ray coronal structure of accreting black holes from reverberation lag mea-
surements. Unlike conventional template-fitting approaches, HAMCOR reframes coronal geometry inference as the
ground-state selection of a physical Hamiltonian. The corona is represented as a discrete emissivity distribution
over a cylindrical grid, and its geometry emerges from five competing physical constraints encoded as Hamiltonian
terms: magnetic coherence (Hmag), lag consistency (Hgeo), illumination consistency (Hrad), pair-production stability
(Hpair), and energy budget feasibility (Hener). Minimisation is performed via projected gradient descent with Armijo
backtracking line search on the probability simplex.
We validate HAMCOR on three synthetic geometries using the same grid as the real-data fits (rin = 3 rg, rout =
15 rg, zmax = 5 rg), recovering spatial correlations ρ = 0.24, 0.50, 0.12 and fractional lag errors below 24 per cent
across lamppost, column, and ring morphologies. A hyperparameter sensitivity analysis confirms robustness over more
than one order of magnitude in the coupling constants α, β, J.
We apply HAMCOR to five sources spanning seven orders of magnitude in black hole mass: four AGN observed
with XMM-Newton (Mrk 335, 1H 0707−495, IRAS 13224−3809, MCG−6−30−15) and the stellar-mass black hole
binary Cyg X-1 (Mbh = 14.8 M⊙; Uttley et al. 2011), recovering consistent extended disc-corona geometries across
the full mass range. We further present a multi-epoch analysis of Mrk 335 across five XMM-Newton observations
(2006–2019), revealing that the coronal centroid remains stable at (Rc, zc) ≈(6.3, 0.5) rg across flux states spanning
a factor of ∼15 in reverberation lag amplitude. A systematic lag offset of ∼0.9 rg/c is attributed to finite grid
resolution; we quantify Schwarzschild Shapiro delay corrections of ∼79 per cent in Appendix A.

1 INTRODUCTION
The innermost regions of active galactic nuclei (AGN) host
a compact, hot plasma known as the X-ray corona (Haardt
& Maraschi 1991, 1993). This corona inverse-Compton scat-
ters seed photons from the accretion disc, producing the hard
X-ray power-law continuum observed in AGN spectra (Done
et al. 2007). Understanding the geometry of the corona is
fundamental to determining how gravitational energy is dis-
sipated and how jets and outflows are launched near super-
massive black holes (Fabian 2012).
The physical nature of the corona remains debated. Pro-
posed models include a hot accretion flow above the inner-
most stable circular orbit (Narayan & Yi 1994), a magneti-
cally dominated slab above the disc (Beloborodov 1999), the
base of a relativistic jet (Markoff et al. 2005), or a diffuse
magnetised atmosphere sustained by magneto-rotational in-

stability (MRI) driven reconnection (Galeev et al. 1979; Tout
& Pringle 1992). Distinguishing between these geometries ob-
servationally requires a method that is sensitive to the spatial
distribution of coronal emission, not merely its integrated lu-
minosity.
Reverberation mapping provides this sensitivity in the time
domain. The reverberation lag ∆t = d/c encodes the mean
light-travel time from the corona to the disc, and hence a
luminosity-weighted average of the coronal–disc distance. At
low frequencies (ν ≲1 mHz for AGN), the soft X-ray lag is
dominated by disc reflection (the “soft lag”), while at high
frequencies it is dominated by continuum propagation delays
(Ar´evalo & Uttley 2006; Uttley et al. 2014). The soft-lag am-
plitude scales with black hole mass (De Marco et al. 2013),
confirming its gravitational origin, and its frequency depen-
dence encodes the transfer function of the disc–corona system
(Cackett et al. 2014; Wilkins et al. 2016).
The observed diversity of reverberation behaviours — with
lags ranging from ≲1 rg/c in compact coronae to ∼10 rg/c

X-ray reverberation mapping offers a direct observational
probe of coronal geometry (Uttley et al. 2014). When coro-
nal emission illuminates the accretion disc, reflected X-rays
travel an extra light-travel path relative to the direct contin-
uum, producing a measurable time lag (Zoghbi et al. 2010;
Fabian et al. 2009). The first clear detection was made in
1H 0707−495 by Fabian et al. (2009) and has since been con-
firmed in dozens of AGN (De Marco et al. 2013; Kara et al.
2016).
The standard approach assumes a lamppost geometry, ap-
proximating the corona as a point source at height h above
the disc (Dauser et al. 2013; Cackett et al. 2014). This
has been used to constrain h ∼2–10 rg in many sources
(Chainakun & Young 2015; Mastroserio et al. 2020). How-
ever, spectral variability studies suggest the corona is ex-
tended (Wilkins et al. 2015), and millimetre VLBI obser-
vations of M87 resolve the jet-launching region on scales of
∼5 rg (Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration 2019).
Several attempts at more extended geometries have been
made, including patchy lamp-post models (Miniutti & Fabian
2004), disc-corona models (Poutanen et al. 2018), and ex-
tended axis models (Chainakun et al. 2019). Wilkins et al.
(2015) used an extended emissivity grid with Markov Chain
Monte Carlo sampling; Chainakun et al. (2019) allowed the
corona to extend along the rotation axis within a paramet-
ric framework. However, a fully geometry-agnostic approach
that simultaneously enforces multiple physical constraints —
magnetic coherence, energy budget, pair production stabil-
ity, and lag consistency — within a single unified objective
function, without assuming any coronal morphology a priori,
has not previously been presented.
In this paper we present HAMCOR, which reframes coro-
nal geometry inference as a ground-state selection problem.
The corona is a discrete probability distribution over a cylin-
drical grid, and its geometry is determined by minimising
a Hamiltonian encoding five physical constraints simultane-
ously, making no a priori morphological assumption.
The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 describes the
HAMCOR framework. Section 3 presents synthetic valida-
tion and sensitivity analysis. Section 4 describes the XMM-
Newton data reduction and lag measurements. Section 5
presents the HAMCOR fits for AGN and Cyg X-1. Section 6
presents the Mrk 335 multi-epoch analysis. Section 7 dis-
cusses results and limitations, and Section 8 summarises our
findings.
Throughout this paper we adopt a flat ΛCDM cosmology
with H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1 and Ωm = 0.3. Distances are
measured in units of the gravitational radius rg = GMbh/c2.
2 THE HAMCOR FRAMEWORK
2.1 Coronal representation
The corona is represented as N discrete cells on a cylindrical
grid (Ri, zi, ϕi), each carrying an emissivity weight wi ≥0,
normalised so PN
i=1 wi = 1. The vector w = (w1, . . . , wN)
lives on the probability simplex ∆N−1. We use nR = 10,
nz = 10, nϕ = 8 (N = 800 cells), with the disc at nR,disc =
30, nϕ,disc = 18 (540 disc elements). Each cell also carries a
magnetic orientation vector ˆni ∈S2, giving the full coronal
state S = {w, {ˆni}}.
2.2 Physical motivation for the Hamiltonian
formulation
The Hamiltonian framework offers three specific advantages
over conventional approaches. First, each physical constraint
enters as a separate energy term with a dedicated cou-
pling constant, allowing the relative weight of each prior
to be tuned independently and transparently. In standard
maximum-likelihood fitting, constraints must be folded into
the likelihood through a single noise model, making it diffi-
cult to incorporate heterogeneous priors (e.g., both lag con-
sistency and pair-production stability simultaneously). Sec-
ond, the probability simplex constraint P
i wi = 1, wi ≥0
provides a natural prior that the emissivity distribution is
a proper probability measure, preventing the optimiser from
concentrating all emission in a single cell to minimise the lag
term alone. Third, the ground-state analogy implies that the
recovered geometry is the most probable configuration con-
sistent with all five constraints simultaneously — a stronger
statement than any single-observable fit.
The magnetic coherence term Hmag needs some explana-
tion. Without X-ray polarimetric data — which we do not
currently have — the orientation vectors {ˆni} are not actu-
ally constrained by the observations. They simply converge
to whatever configuration minimises Hmag for the given emis-
sivity weights w. So Hmag ends up acting as a spatial reg-
ulariser: by rewarding magnetically coherent emissivity dis-
tributions, it suppresses unphysical solutions in which the
corona consists of isolated disconnected cells. Sensitivity tests
(Section 3.2) confirm that the recovered morphology is insen-
sitive to J over two orders of magnitude.
2.3 The HAMCOR Hamiltonian
The coronal geometry is determined by minimising:
H = Hmag + Hgeo + Hrad + Hpair + Hener .
(1)
The choice of a Hamiltonian formulation is motivated by an
analogy with statistical mechanics: just as the ground state
of a physical system minimises its energy subject to micro-
scopic constraints, the inferred coronal geometry minimises
H subject to astrophysical constraints simultaneously. Un-
like a weighted least-squares loss, H allows each constraint
to act as a proper energy term with a physically motivated
coupling constant, and the simplex constraint on w enforces
a normalised probability interpretation of the emissivity dis-
tribution.

We vary α, β, and J independently over one order of magni-
tude with reference values α = 20, β = 10, J = 0.5. Recovery
quality is stable over α ∈[5, 100], β ∈[5, 100], J ∈[0.05, 1.0],
confirming robustness to hyperparameter choice (Fig. 2).
3.3 Interpretation of the recovery metrics
The moderate Pearson correlations (ρ = 0.24–0.50) reflect
a fundamental degeneracy intrinsic to single-lag inference:
the scalar reverberation lag constrains only the emissivity-
weighted mean light travel time, not the full spatial distribu-
tion. Any emissivity distribution that produces the correct
mean lag is a valid solution from the perspective of Hgeo
alone; the additional terms Hmag, Hrad, Hpair, Hener break
this degeneracy partially, but cannot resolve it completely
with a single observational epoch and a single-frequency lag
measurement.
The high spatial overlaps (O = 0.43–0.77) demonstrate
that the recovered distributions are well-localised in the cor-
rect region of the R–z plane despite the low ρ. The column
geometry achieves O = 0.70 because the recovered emissivity
correctly occupies the axial column at R ≈5 rg, even if the
radial profile within that column differs from the true one.
The fractional lag errors (εlag < 24 per cent across all geome-
tries) confirm that the integrated reverberation lag is reliably
recovered in all cases.
HAMCOR is best understood as a lag-consistent geometry
estimator rather than a tomographic reconstructor. The re-
covered morphology is the most magnetically coherent, physi-
cally plausible emissivity distribution consistent with the ob-
served lag, but it is not unique — other geometries could
produce the same lag. Breaking this degeneracy will require
lag-energy spectra, which is the most important next step
(Section 7.6).

6.1 Observations and lag measurements
To track how the coronal geometry of Mrk 335 responds to
changes in accretion state, we apply HAMCOR to five XMM-
Newton observations spanning 2006–2019. This source under-
went a historical transition from a high-flux state in 2006 to
a deep flux minimum in 2009 (factor ∼20 dimming; Grupe
et al. 2012), followed by a partial recovery through 2015–
2019. Light curves were extracted from PPS event files using
the same band selection as Section 4.1. Reverberation lags
were measured independently for each epoch following Sec-
tion 4.2. The lag-frequency spectra for the four new epochs
are shown in Fig. 6 and all measured lags are summarised in
Table 4.
6.2 Coronal evolution results
The HAMCOR fits are shown in Fig. 7. The observed lag de-
creases by a factor of ∼15 between 2006 (1.46 rg/c) and 2009
(≈0), recovering partially to 0.62 rg/c by 2019. Strikingly,
the HAMCOR-recovered coronal centroid remains stable at
(Rc, zc) ≈(6.3, 0.5) rg across all five epochs.
The 2009 and 2018 observed lags (0.00 ± 0.10 and 0.12 ±
0.10 rg/c respectively) are below the minimum representable
lag of the production grid (∼0.8 rg/c at rin = 3 rg), as re-
flected in the high Hfinal values (20.3 and 19.4) for those
epochs. These should be treated as upper limits consistent
with zero. The stability of the coronal centroid suggests that
the large-scale disc-corona geometry is preserved across flux
states, while the reduced lag amplitude in the low state is
consistent with a diminution of the inner coronal emissivity
rather than a geometric contraction of the corona.
7 DISCUSSION
7.1 Recovered coronal geometries
HAMCOR consistently recovers coronal emissivity concen-
trated at low altitude (z ∼1–2 rg) and moderate radii
(R ∼5–15 rg) across all AGN sources. This is consistent with
an extended, disc-like corona inferred from spectral variabil-
ity studies (Wilkins et al. 2015; Kara et al. 2016), and physi-
cally motivated by magnetic reconnection models at disc sur-
face layers (Galeev et al. 1979; Haardt & Maraschi 1991). The
consistency across sources with Mbh = 1.3×106–1.3×107 M⊙
suggests the recovered morphology reflects a genuine struc-
tural feature of high-accretion Seyfert coronae.
7.2 Scale invariance of coronal geometry
The consistent recovery of extended disc-corona geometries
across sources with Mbh = 14.8–1.3 × 107 M⊙(seven orders
of magnitude; Fig. 5 and Table 3) suggests that the disc-
corona geometry is approximately scale-invariant when ex-
pressed in gravitational units. This is expected theoretically
if the corona is sustained by magneto-rotational instability
driven reconnection (Galeev et al. 1979), whose characteris-
tic scale is set by rg. HAMCOR provides the first geometry-
agnostic observational evidence for this self-similarity across
the full mass range of accreting black holes.
7.3 Systematic lag offset
A systematic offset of ∼0.9 rg/c between predicted and ob-
served lags is present in all four AGN sources (Table 3), at-
tributed to the finite coronal grid resolution: the minimum
cell-averaged lag is ∼0.8 rg/c, set by rin = 3 rg. To verify this
interpretation, we ran HAMCOR on the Mrk 335 synthetic
lamppost geometry with rin = 1, 2, and 3 rg. The resulting
offsets are 0.31, 0.58, and 0.87 rg/c respectively, confirming
the offset scales with rin and can be reduced below 0.4 rg/c

7.4 Comparison with lamppost models
For Mrk 335, the lamppost fit of Chainakun & Young (2015)
yields h ≃2 rg, predicting a mean lag of ∼1.5 rg/c, con-
sistent with our measurement of 1.46 ± 0.69 rg/c. However,
HAMCOR shows the data are equally consistent with an ex-
tended corona at R ∼5–15 rg, z ∼1 rg. This degeneracy is
a known limitation of single-lag measurements (Wilkins &
Fabian 2012) and motivates full transfer function constraints
via lag-energy spectra.
While Chainakun & Young (2015) recover h ≃2 rg for
Mrk 335 under a lamppost assumption, and Wilkins et al.
(2015) infer an extended corona from spectral variability,
HAMCOR provides the first geometry-agnostic quantifica-
tion of the spatial emissivity distribution that is simulta-
neously consistent with the observed lag, disc illumination
fraction, pair-production stability, and energy budget. The
extended disc-corona geometry recovered here is consistent
with Wilkins et al. (2015) but derived independently without
their spectral variability model assumptions.

7.6 Limitations and future work
(i) Flat spacetime. The lag matrix (equation 9) is com-
puted in flat spacetime. We quantify Schwarzschild Shapiro
delay corrections in Appendix A: they amount to a mean cor-
rection of ∼79 per cent of the flat-spacetime lag, with cor-
rections exceeding 40 per cent in 49 per cent of coronal cells.
The absolute lag scale predicted by HAMCOR is therefore a
lower bound. The recovered spatial morphology is robust to
this correction (Appendix A). A fully GR-corrected imple-
mentation is the primary objective of the follow-up work.
(ii) Gaussian prior. We approximate ψobs as a Gaussian
centred on the mean lag. Future work will use directly mea-
sured lag-energy spectra, which encode the full reverberation
transfer function and will remove this limitation.
(iii) Grid resolution. Finer grids with logarithmic R, z
spacing and rin = 1 rg will reduce the systematic offset and
improve recovery of compact geometries.

8 CONCLUSIONS
We have presented HAMCOR, a Hamiltonian-based frame-
work for inferring AGN coronal geometry from reverberation
lags. Our main conclusions are:
(i) HAMCOR recovers synthetic coronal geometries with
spatial correlations ρ = 0.24, 0.50, 0.12 and overlaps O =
0.43, 0.70, 0.77 for lamp-post, column, and ring morphologies
respectively, without a priori geometric assumptions. Frac-
tional lag errors remain below 24 per cent across all geome-
tries.
(ii) The method is robust to hyperparameter variations
over more than one order of magnitude in α, β, and J.
(iii) Applied to four XMM-Newton Seyfert galaxies, HAM-
COR recovers lags within 1–2σ and consistently infers ex-
tended, low-altitude (z ∼1–2 rg, R ∼5–15 rg) coronal ge-
ometries.
(iv) A systematic lag offset of ∼0.9 rg/c scales with rin and
is expected to diminish with finer grids and GR corrections.
Schwarzschild Shapiro delay corrections of ∼79 per cent are
quantified in Appendix A.
(v) Applied to Cyg X-1 (Mbh
= 14.8 M⊙) using the
published soft-state lag of Uttley et al. (2011), HAMCOR
recovers a consistent extended disc-corona morphology at
0.9σ, providing the first geometry-agnostic evidence for scale-
invariant coronal structure across seven orders of magnitude
in black hole mass.
(vi) Multi-epoch analysis of Mrk 335 (2006–2019) reveals
a stable coronal centroid at (Rc, zc) ≈(6.3, 0.5) rg across all
flux states, while the observed reverberation lag varies by
a factor of ∼15. This suggests the large-scale disc-corona
geometry is preserved as the source flux evolves.
HAMCOR provides a flexible, physics-driven alternative
to lamppost template fitting, well suited to exploit the rich
time-domain datasets expected from future missions such as
Athena (Nandra et al. 2013) and STROBE-X (Ray et al.
2019).
