HILBERT SCHEMES OF POINTS ON CANONICAL SURFACES
ALASTAIR CRAW AND RYO YAMAGISHI
Abstract. For n ≥1, we investigate the Hilbert scheme of n-points on a surface S with canonical
singularities. We generalise the well-known theorem of Fogarty [17] by showing that the underlying
reduced subscheme of Hilbn(S) is a normal variety of dimension 2n with canonical singularities,
and for n ≤7, we show that Hilbn(S) is reduced. When S has symplectic singularities over C, we
show that the underlying reduced subscheme of Hilbn(S) also has symplectic singularities, thereby
generalising a result of Beauville [1]. Our results build on work of the first author with Gyenge,
Gammelgaard and Szendr˝oi [9] that sought to identify the underlying reduced subscheme of the
Hilbert scheme of n-points on a Kleinian singularity with a Nakajima quiver variety.

1. Introduction
Let n ≥1 and let S be a connected quasi-projective surface over an algebraically closed field k
of characteristic zero. A celebrated result of Fogarty [17] establishes that when S is nonsingular,
then the Hilbert scheme of n-points on S, denoted Hilbn(S), is a nonsingular, connected variety
over k of dimension 2n. When S is singular, an example for which Hilbn(S) is reducible was given
by Mir´o-Roig–Pons-Llopis [33]. However, for a surface S with canonical singularities, also known
as Du Val, Kleinian or ADE singularities, Zheng [39] proved that Hilbn(S) is an irreducible scheme
of dimension 2n, while Jelisiejew [23, Problem XIII] asked whether Hilbn(S) is reduced.
Our main result establishes the appropriate generalisation of Fogarty’s theorem for surfaces with
canonical singularities, at least if we give Hilbn(S) the underlying reduced scheme structure:
Theorem 1.1. Let S be a connected, quasi-projective surface with canonical singularities. For
n ≥1, the underlying reduced subscheme of Hilbn(S) is a normal variety of dimension 2n with
canonical singularities.

In addition, recall [2] that a normal variety X over the field C of complex numbers has symplectic
singularities if the nonsingular locus of X admits an everywhere non-degenerate closed 2-form ω
such that for any resolution of singularities π: Y →X, the pullback π∗(ω) extends to a regular 2-
form on Y . Note that π is a symplectic resolution if π∗(ω) extends to an everywhere non-degenerate
2-form on Y . Our approach to Theorem 1.1 allows us to establish the following result:
Corollary 1.3. Let S be a connected quasi-projective surface over C with symplectic singularities.
For n ≥1, the underlying reduced subscheme of Hilbn(S) has symplectic singularities.
When S is non-singular, then Hilbn(S) is also non-singular, in which case Corollary 1.3 reduces
to the well-known result of Beauville [1] that Hilbn(S) is a holomorphic symplectic variety.
1.1. Nakajima quiver varieties. Recall that the good (or principal) component of Hilbn(S) is
the scheme-theoretic closure of the locus parametrising reduced subschemes of length n in S. We
deduce Theorem 1.1 by establishing the following result when S is a Kleinian singularity:
Theorem 1.4. Let Γ ⊂SL(2, k) be a finite subgroup. For n ≥1, the good component of Hilbn(A2/Γ)
is isomorphic to a Nakajima quiver variety Mθ0 for a special stability parameter θ0. Moreover, when
working over C, it has symplectic singularities and admits a unique projective, symplectic resolution.
Since Hilbn(A2/Γ) is irreducible, the conclusions of Theorem 1.4 actually hold for the underlying
reduced subscheme of Hilbn(A2/Γ). A similar statement was published in work of the first author
with Gammelgaard, Gyenge and Szendr˝oi [9], though Yehao Zhou pointed out a gap in the proof
of [9, Lemma 4.3]. Here, we adopt a more algebraic approach to Theorem 1.4 that enables us to
prove a similar result for many more stability conditions, and we go further by establishing that
Hilbn(A2/Γ) is in fact reduced for n ≤7. In addition, we extend these results to the global situation
considered in Theorems 1.1-1.2 and Corollary 1.3.
