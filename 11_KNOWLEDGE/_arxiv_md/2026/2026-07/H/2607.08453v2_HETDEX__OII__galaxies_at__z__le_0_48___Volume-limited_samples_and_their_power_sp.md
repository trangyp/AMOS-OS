ABSTRACT
The catalog from the Hobby-Eberly Telescope Dark Energy Experiment (HETDEX) Public Data
Release 1 (PDR1) contains half a million emission-line-selected [O II] galaxies spread across 540 deg2 at
z ≤0.48 from HETDEX’s unprecedented untargeted spectroscopic survey. In this paper, we construct
volume-limited samples from PDR1 in three luminosity bins across the two main fields: “Spring”
and “Fall”. The numbers of galaxies in the bins range from 11,354 to 64,794 and number densities,
¯n ≃(2 −5) × 10−3 h3 Mpc−3, are higher than those of typical cosmological spectroscopic surveys of
emission-line galaxies by a factor of five to ten. The monopole and quadrupole power spectra derived
from these samples are in excellent agreement with the mock power spectra from the Uchuu simulation
based on a flat ΛCDM model and the cosmological parameters from the Planck cosmic microwave
background data, at all wavenumbers used for the measurement (0.01 < k < 0.7 h Mpc−1). We find
that the power spectrum amplitudes are consistent with a characteristic dark matter halo mass of
log(M0 [h−1M⊙]) ≃11.9–12.3, with the halo mass showing a weak dependence on [O II] luminosity,
M0 ∝La, increasing with a slope of a = 0.37 ± 0.10. The best-fit mock suggests that approximately
13 percent of the [O II] galaxies in our sample reside in subhalos. The new, high-density tracers of
the underlying matter distribution presented in this paper provide precise measurements of clustering
in a low-redshift regime sensitive to the late-time growth of structures.
These samples will form
the basis for forthcoming analyses of the redshift-space distortion effect, galaxy–halo connection, and
cross-correlations with external low-redshift probes.

1. INTRODUCTION
The Hobby-Eberly Telescope Dark Energy Experi-
ment (HETDEX; K. Gebhardt et al. 2021) has delivered
a catalog of more than two million emission-line-selected
galaxies in a cosmological volume through its unprece-
dented untargeted integral field unit spectroscopic sur-
vey using the Visible Integral-field Replicable Unit Spec-
trograph (VIRUS; G. J. Hill et al. 2021), on the 10m
Hobby-Eberly Telescope (HET; L. W. Ramsey et al.
1998; G. J. Hill et al. 2021).
The catalog from Public Data Release 1 (PDR1; E.
Mentuch Cooper et al. 2026) contains half a million
[O II] emitters at z ≤0.48 and 1.6 million Lyman-α
emitter (LAE) candidates at 1.88 ≤z ≤3.52. In this
paper, we focus on the former catalog.
The low-redshift universe has become an increas-
ingly important regime for cosmological studies. N.-M.
Nguyen et al. (2023) found evidence for a suppressed
growth rate of large-scale structure during the dark-
energy-dominated era compared to the prediction of
general relativity in a flat Λ cold dark matter (ΛCDM)
cosmology. There is also a mild discrepancy in the S8
parameter, which characterizes the present-day ampli-
tude of linear matter density fluctuations (B. Jain & U.
Seljak 1997): some studies report a ≃2σ tension be-
tween the S8 values inferred from the cosmic microwave
background (CMB) and weak gravitational lensing sur-
veys (R. Dalal et al. 2023;
DES Collaboration et al.
2026), while others find them to be in good agree-
ment (A. H. Wright et al. 2025; F. J. Qu et al. 2025).
Furthermore, analyses combining Dark Energy Spectro-
scopic Instrument (DESI) Baryonic Acoustic Oscillation
measurements with Type Ia supernovae data have sug-
gested evidence for dynamical dark energy (M. Abdul
Karim et al. 2025).
To understand better about these tensions and find-
ings, independent probes of matter clustering in the
low-redshift universe are essential. Several cosmologi-
cal spectroscopic surveys have targeted galaxies at z ≳
0.5 (C. Blake et al. 2011; M. Takada et al. 2014; S. Alam
et al. 2017, 2021b; Y. Wang et al. 2022; M. Abdul Karim
et al. 2025;
Euclid Collaboration et al. 2025), includ-
ing the LAE component of the HETDEX survey, leav-
ing the low-redshift regime comparatively less explored.
At lower redshifts, the Galaxy And Mass Assembly sur-
vey (GAMA; S. P. Driver et al. 2011; J. Liske et al.
2015), the DESI Bright Galaxy Survey (C. Hahn et al.
2023), and the DESI Peculiar Velocity Survey (K. Said
et al. 2025) probe the growth of structures at z ≲0.5,
0.6, and 0.1, respectively, using photometrically selected
galaxies.
In this context, the HETDEX [O II] sam-
ple offers a complementary and independent probe of
structure growth using untargeted emission-line-selected
galaxies at z ≤0.48. Combining observations from sur-
veys with different target selection criteria and system-
atics is crucial for obtaining robust cosmological con-
straints.
[O II] galaxies are relatively unbiased tracers of the
large-scale structure since their emission lines provide
accurate spectroscopic redshifts, and their clustering
traces the underlying matter distribution with a rela-
tively modest bias compared to more massive galaxy
populations such as luminous red galaxies (K.-i. Tadaki
et al. 2012; M. Kajisawa et al. 2013; E. Jim´enez et al.
2021). The HETDEX [O II] samples complement those
from other spectroscopic surveys targeting [O II] galax-
ies at z ≳0.5 (M. Takada et al. 2014; S. Alam et al.
2021b; M. Abdul Karim et al. 2025).
However, note
that the surveys listed above are continuum-selected,
meaning that galaxies must exceed a certain apparent
magnitude threshold in order to be targeted for spec-
troscopy.
In contrast, HETDEX [O II] galaxies are
emission-line-selected, without requiring continuum pre-
selection, yielding a galaxy population fundamentally
different in nature. We refer the reader to R. Ciardullo
et al. (2013) for a related discussion of emission-line-
selected galaxy samples in the context of the HETDEX
Pilot Survey.
Since HETDEX use integral field unit (IFU) spec-
troscopy, it is different from other cosmological spectro-
scopic surveys: HETDEX has measured all the galaxy
redshifts above the survey sensitivity with some flags
due to bad data. While HETDEX is primarily designed
to detect LAEs in the distant universe, the experiment
is also capable to produce an unbiased dataset of [O II]
emitters in the low-redshift universe.
This allows us
to construct volume-limited samples from the HETDEX
[O II] emitting galaxy catalog.
In a flux-limited sample, only galaxies brighter than
a given flux threshold are detected: while both faint
and bright galaxies are observed at low redshifts, only
the most luminous galaxies are observable at high red-
shifts, introducing a redshift-dependent selection bias.
The resulting catalog is also sensitive to flux limits,
which depend on complex factors such as observing con-
ditions and sky subtraction residuals. This complicates

the radial selection function and consequently the in-

In contrast, a
volume-limited sample is constructed by selecting galax-

galaxies are detected across the given redshift range.
This approach produces a spatially homogeneous selec-

unbiased representation of the galaxy population with
given luminosity cuts, making the interpretation of the
clustering results more straightforward and reliable.
As the HETDEX survey has been optimized to ob-
tain sufficient number densities of LAEs in the distant
universe, the number densities of our volume-limited
samples of HETDEX [O II] galaxies, ¯n ≃(2 −5) ×
10−3 h3 Mpc−3 depending on luminosity bins, are higher
than those of typical cosmological spectroscopic sur-
veys of emission-line galaxies (ELGs), a few to several
times 10−4 h3 Mpc−3, by a factor of five to ten (C.
Blake et al. 2011; M. Takada et al. 2014; A. de Mat-
tia et al. 2021; Y. Wang et al. 2022; M. Abdul Karim
et al. 2025;
Euclid Collaboration et al. 2025). Here,
the Hubble constant is given by the standard notation,
H0 = 100 h km s−1 Mpc−1.
This makes our sample
useful not only for cosmology but also for astrophysi-
cal studies of galaxy formation and evolution and cross-
correlation studies with other tracers in the low-redshift
universe.
In this work, we present the first robust clustering
measurements of emission-line-selected [O II] galaxies in
the low-redshift universe using the HETDEX PDR1 cat-
alog. By constructing highly complete volume-limited
samples across distinct luminosity bins, we measure the
monopole and quadrupole power spectra down to non-
linear scales. To interpret these measurements and in-
vestigate the [O II] galaxy–halo connection, we model
the clustering signal using mock catalogs based on the
Uchuu N-body simulation (T. Ishiyama et al. 2021; T.
Oogi et al. 2023; H. Aung et al. 2023; F. Prada et al.
2023; C. A. Dong-P´aez et al. 2024) combined with a halo
occupation distribution (HOD; e.g., Z. Zheng et al. 2005;
J. E. Geach et al. 2012) framework. The high-density
tracers and clustering properties established here vali-
date the HETDEX [O II] dataset as a precise probe of
late-time structure growth, providing the critical foun-
dation for forthcoming analyses of the redshift-space dis-
tortions, galaxy–halo connection, and cross-correlations
with independent low-redshift probes.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-
tion 2 describes the HETDEX data, including the pub-
lic catalog of emission-line-selected [O II] galaxies. Sec-
tion 3 explains how we construct volume-limited sam-
ples in three luminosity bins.
Section 4 presents the
power spectrum multipoles of the volume-limited [O II]
galaxies. Section 5 discusses the interpretations of the
monopole power spectra and we conclude in Section 6.
Throughout this paper, we use Ωm = 0.311 when
converting redshift z to comoving distance in units of
h−1 Mpc, assuming a flat ΛCDM model ( Planck Col-
laboration et al. 2020).
2. HETDEX DATA
HETDEX (K. Gebhardt et al. 2021) has completed its
seven-year observing program in 2024. The PDR1 cat-
alog (E. Mentuch Cooper et al. 2026) contains LAEs
at 1.88 ≤z ≤3.52 and [O II] emitting galaxies at
z ≤0.48, as well as stars, low-redshift continuum se-
lected galaxies, and active galactic nuclei, over 540 deg2
of the sky. HETDEX has observed its survey area with
VIRUS (G. J. Hill et al. 2021), a set of 78 IFUs, cover-
ing the wavelength range of 3470 ˚A ≤λ ≤5540 ˚A with
a spectral resolving power of 750 ≲R ≲950.
Each IFU spans 51′′ × 51′′ on the sky and contains
448 fibers of 1′′.5 in diameter, enabling the simultaneous
acquisition of approximately 35,000 spectra. With over
600 million spectra collected, HETDEX is the largest
spectroscopic sky survey ever conducted.
The HETDEX sample of emission-line galaxies is
unique in its combination of a large area, depth, and un-
targeted emission-line search without any requirement
on a continuum magnitude compared to the most spec-
troscopic cosmology surveys. The observed spectra are
searched for emission lines (and the continuum for low-
redshift galaxies) without any photometric preselection.
Using several successful classification approaches, HET-
DEX provides robust spectroscopic redshifts and clas-
sifications for the entire catalog (D. Davis et al. 2023;
E. Mentuch Cooper et al. 2023).
Compared to spec-
troscopic redshifts from external catalogs, 94.1% of the
sources are within |∆z| < 0.02 (E. Mentuch Cooper
et al. 2026).
The IFUs are distributed across a 18′-diameter field of
view on a 100′′-grid. The gaps between adjacent IFUs
are roughly equal to the size of each IFU, resulting in a
fill factor of ≃1/4.6 per observation. The sky is there-
fore sparsely sampled within the footprint of the survey
rather than being uniformly covered with fibers. How-
ever, this sparse sampling has been shown to provide
a constraining power on cosmological models similar to
that of complete imaging of the same area with a lower-
number-density tracer. As discussed by C.-T. Chiang
et al. (2013), complete sky coverage within this area is
not required to achieve the HETDEX’s scientific goals;
a fill factor of 1/4.6 ≃0.22, which optimizes the packing

sufficient.
2.1. HETDEX PDR1
We use the HETDEX Public Source Catalog 2
(HPSC2) from the PDR1 (E. Mentuch Cooper et al.
2026).
HPSC2 consists of all HETDEX survey data
from 2017-Jan-01 until 2024-Jul-31, which is an exten-
sion of the HETDEX Public Source Catalog 1 (HPSC1;
E. Mentuch Cooper et al. 2023) collected from 2017-Jan-
01 to 2020-June-26, and contains four additional years
of data and several updates in masking. Alongside the
catalog, 431 K datacubes are included in PDR1 and can
be accessed through the HETDEX data portal (https:
//hetdex.org/data-results/).
HPSC2 is also publicly
available via Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19581262).
These data consist of two primary observational fields:
an equatorial “Fall” field, whose footprint is centered
at (RAJ2000, DECJ2000) = (22.50◦, +0.00◦) and covers
150 deg2, and a high declination “Spring” field, which
centers at (195.00◦, +51.00◦) and covers 390 deg2. Al-
though HPSC2 also contains other fields, we only use the
Spring and Fall fields in this paper due to their large sky
coverages.
2.2. HETDEX [O II] Galaxies
Although the primary science driver of HETDEX is
the detection of high-redshift LAEs, the sensitivity of
the VIRUS spectrograph is sufficient to ensure high com-
pleteness in the detection of [O II] galaxies at z ≤0.48.
Since HETDEX’s spectral resolution is too low to re-
solve the [O II] doublet, we use a mean rest-frame air
wavelength of λ = 3727.8 ˚A (E. Mentuch Cooper et al.
2026) in our analysis.
In this paper, we use the catalog of [O II] galaxies
from the PDR1 v1.318. For each source observation, the
catalog provides the object’s J2000 equatorial coordi-
nates and redshift. For sources with either Lyα or [O II]
line emission, the catalog also provides the optimal mea-
surement for the dust- and aperture-corrected flux and
luminosity. We apply no selection cuts other than the
choice of fields and object classification.
Figure 1 shows the projected distribution of all HPSC2
[O II] galaxies in the Spring and Fall fields in the plane
of right ascension and comoving distance with corre-
sponding redshifts, collapsed in declination with a width
of 2◦.
There are 286,992 and 140,122 galaxies with
z hetdex > 0 in comoving volumes of 4.7 × 107 and
2.6 × 107 h−3 Mpc3 in the Spring and Fall fields, re-
spectively.
The large-scale structure is clearly visible
across both observational fields. The entire catalog of
the HETDEX [O II] emitting galaxies has a high num-
ber density of about 6 × 10−3 h3 Mpc−3. Note that this
number density is approximately 4.6 times lower than
the underlying density (i.e., the number density derived
from the luminosity function) due to the angular sparse
sampling of the HETDEX survey, as described in Sec-
tion 2.
3. VOLUME-LIMITED SAMPLES
Several previous studies (P. Norberg et al. 2001; I.
Zehavi et al. 2002; M. Tegmark et al. 2004; X.-F.
Deng et al. 2007; E. Tempel et al. 2014; D. J. Far-
row et al. 2015; G. Favole et al. 2017) have utilized
volume-limited samples to investigate the luminosity de-
pendence of galaxy bias. These studies compared flux-
limited samples with volume-limited samples or galaxy
groups extracted from the volume-limited samples. M.
Tegmark et al. (2004) reported that the power spectra
of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) from different
volume-limited samples are consistent in shape at large
scales but differ in amplitude and the power spectrum
of each sample is free of the overall red-tilting effect
that affects the power spectrum of flux-limited sam-
ples. P. Norberg et al. (2001) found that the projected
two-point correlation functions of volume-limited sam-
ples from the Two-degree-Field Galaxy Redshift Survey
(2dFGRS) reveal a slowly increasing clustering ampli-
tude for galaxies brighter than the bJ-band magnitude
of −19.7, with a more pronounced rise for the bright-
est galaxies. D. J. Farrow et al. (2015) reported that
the projected two-point correlation functions of volume-
limited samples from the GAMA survey exhibit stronger
clustering of r-band luminous, more massive and red-
der galaxies.
G. Favole et al. (2017) studied the de-
pendence of galaxy clustering on the [O II] emission line
luminosity using volume-limited samples from the SDSS
DR7 Main galaxy sample for five different minimum lu-
minosities, Lmin
[O II] = (0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10) × 1040 erg s−1,
which correspond to maximum redshifts of zmax =
(0.05, 0.09, 0.14, 0.17, 0.20), respectively.
Our analysis is based on the methodology developed
in these previous studies. To satisfy the volume-limited
criterion and to utilize as much data as possible, we di-
vide the galaxy catalog into five luminosity bins. Then,
we apply additional redshift cuts to each bin to extract
volume-limited samples from the HETDEX [O II] cata-
log in HPSC2, as described in Section 3.1.

p-value that represents the probability that the two dis-
tributions are drawn from the same underlying distribu-
tion. This procedure is repeated for all possible combi-
nations of redshift bin pairs within the range of interest.
For each luminosity bin, we identify the (zmin, zmax) pair
that yields a sufficiently high p-value (p > 0.05), ensur-
ing the volume-limited feature, while also providing an
adequate galaxy number density and volume coverage
for meaningful cosmological analyses.

4.2. Mock: Simulation-based Model
Interpreting the clustering of galaxies requires a model
for their connection to the underlying dark matter halo
population.
A wide variety of approaches have been
proposed to describe this connection, ranging from per-
turbative methods to empirical models and numerical
simulations, each with varying levels of physical assump-
tions and computational complexity. Examples include
perturbation theory with bias expansion (e.g., V. Des-
jacques et al. 2018; O. H. E. Philcox & M. M. Ivanov
2022), HOD (e.g., Z. Zheng et al. 2005), subhalo abun-
dance matching (e.g., A. V. Kravtsov et al. 2004; P.
Behroozi et al. 2019), and hydrodynamical simulations
(e.g., V. Springel et al. 2005; R. Pakmor et al. 2023).
Traditionally, the galaxy–halo connection has been de-
scribed using the HOD framework, in which the proba-
bility of a halo hosting central and satellite galaxies is
parameterized as a function of halo mass (e.g., Z. Zheng
et al. 2005). However, galaxies selected through their
[O II] emission trace ongoing star formation and are
known to exhibit occupation statistics that differ from
those of mass-selected samples.
Semi-analytic models
and direct clustering measurements from Subaru Hyper
Suprime-Cam (HSC), eBOSS, and DESI surveys indi-
cate that [O II] emitters preferentially reside in halos
of characteristic mass Mh ≃1012 h−1M⊙and exhibit
relatively low satellite fractions (e.g., G. Favole et al.
2016; V. Gonzalez-Perez et al. 2018; S. Alam et al. 2020;
S. Avila et al. 2020; T. Okumura et al. 2021; H. Gao
et al. 2023; K. Osato & T. Okumura 2023; S. Ortega-
Martinez et al. 2025; S. Yuan et al. 2025; G. Favole et al.
2026; S. Ortega-Martinez et al. 2026). The HETDEX
[O II] sample offers a complementary perspective: unlike
higher-redshift emission-line galaxy samples targeted by
other surveys, HETDEX selects [O II] galaxies through a
well-defined flux threshold at z ≤0.48, probing a lower-
redshift, star-forming galaxy population.
A key advantage of the HETDEX data is that, unlike
surveys that rely on color or stellar mass cuts in photo-
metric data as a pre-selection strategy, HETDEX per-
forms an untargeted spectroscopic survey and obtains
redshifts for all galaxies with emission lines brighter
than its sensitivity limit, without any prior target se-
lection. This approach eliminates an additional layer of
complexity in modeling the galaxy–halo connection that
would otherwise arise from such selection effects. In this
paper, we employ HOD modeling to interpret the power
spectra of the HETDEX [O II] volume-limited samples.
We find that both the monopole and quadrupole power
spectra can be successfully described by a simple log-
normal HOD model with only three free parameters.
We follow Equation (9) of J. E. Geach et al. (2012)
for the HOD, but exclude the error function term. J. E.
Geach et al. (2012) constructed a HOD model specifi-
cally tailored for emission-line galaxies, motivated by the
agreement between observations of Hα emitters (HAEs)
at z = 2.23 and GALFORM semi-analytic model pre-
dictions. A key feature of their model is that the cen-
tral galaxy occupation distribution follows an approxi-
mately lognormal distribution at low halo masses, with
a characteristic host mass M0 and width σlog M, rather
than the standard step function used in mass-limited
HODs. This lognormal component reflects the fact that
the [O II] emitting galaxies, like HAEs, are star-forming
galaxies whose occupation number depends on the star
formation efficiency as a function of halo mass, and
therefore is not a simple monotonically increasing func-
tion of halo mass. We therefore adopt this form as the
basis of our HOD model, which is well-suited for mod-
elling emission-line galaxy populations such as [O II]
emitters. Since our mock dataset resolves subhalos, we
populate galaxies to all halos including subhalos. We
demonstrate in Appendix B that excluding galaxies in
subhalos in the mock leads to a poorer agreement with
the data, especially the quadrupole power spectra, jus-
tifying their inclusion.

6. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we have presented the first measure-
ment of the power spectrum multipoles of emission-line-
selected [O II] galaxies from the HETDEX Public Data
Release 1 (PDR1; E. Mentuch Cooper et al. 2026) in
the low-redshift universe, z ≤0.48.
The untargeted
nature of the HETDEX survey, combined with its un-
precedented spectroscopic depth, has enabled the con-
struction of well-characterized volume-limited samples
of [O II] emitting galaxies.
These samples provide a
unique, independent probe of the growth of large-scale
structures complementary to other contemporary cos-
mological surveys (C. Blake et al. 2011; M. Takada et al.
2014; S. Alam et al. 2017, 2021b; M. Abdul Karim et al.
2025).
We constructed volume-limited samples in three lumi-
nosity bins from the Spring and Fall fields of the HET-
DEX PDR1. Using two-sample KS tests to validate the
volume-limited nature of each sample, we confirmed that
the distributions of galaxy number densities are consis-
tent with being roughly constant across their respective
redshift ranges.
We measured the monopole and quadrupole power
spectra of these volume-limited [O II] samples using an
FFT-based Yamamoto estimator (K. Yamamoto et al.
2006; N. Hand et al. 2017), and modeled them using
mock catalogs constructed from the Uchuu N-body sim-
ulation (T. Ishiyama et al. 2021) with a simple log-
normal HOD model.
By fixing σlog M = 0.6 and the
galaxy fraction, Fg, to match the observed number den-
sity of each sample, we reduced the HOD to have a single
free parameter: the characteristic halo mass M0. The
best-fit mocks provide an excellent description of both
the monopole and quadrupole power spectra across all
wavenumbers up to kmax = 0.7 h Mpc−1, demonstrat-
ing that this simple HOD model is sufficient to describe
the clustering properties of the HETDEX [O II] volume-
limited samples without additional complexity. We find
that approximately 13 percent of the [O II] galaxies in
the best-fit mock reside in subhalos.
The main results of this paper are summarized as fol-
lows:
• The best-fit characteristic halo mass, log(M0),
slightly increases with [O II] luminosity following
a power-law relation, M0 ∝La, with a slope of
a = 0.37 ± 0.10, consistent between the Spring
and Fall fields. This trend is expected, as [O II]
luminosity is a proxy for the star formation rate,
which correlates with stellar mass, and stellar mass
in turn correlates with dark matter halo mass (A.
Vale & J. P. Ostriker 2004; P. S. Behroozi et al.
2010; S. Alam et al. 2021a).
• The best-fit log(M0) values also show a slight in-
crease with redshift, consistent with a scenario in
which higher-luminosity, higher-redshift galaxies
tend to reside in more massive dark matter ha-
los (H. J. Mo & S. D. M. White 1996; J. L. Tinker
et al. 2010). However, since luminosity and red-
shift are correlated in volume-limited samples by
definition, the two effects — luminosity and time
evolution — cannot be disentangled from the cur-
rent analysis alone.
• The inferred linear galaxy bias parameters in-
crease from b1 ≃0.8 to 0.9 from Bin 2 to Bin 4 in
both the Spring and Fall fields. These values are
consistent with the well-established picture that
galaxy bias increases with both halo mass and red-
shift (H. J. Mo & S. D. M. White 1996; J. L. Tinker
et al. 2010), and are in line with expectations for
low-mass, star-forming galaxies at z ≤0.48.
The HETDEX [O II] volume-limited samples offer sev-
eral unique advantages over other spectroscopic galaxy
surveys using [O II] emitting galaxies at higher redshifts
(M. Takada et al. 2014; S. Alam et al. 2021b; M. Ab-
dul Karim et al. 2025). In particular, the emission-line-
selected and untargeted nature of the HETDEX survey
eliminates the need to model complex photometric pre-
selection effects, resulting in a cleaner and more straight-
forward interpretation of the galaxy–halo connection.
The high number densities, ¯n ≃(2–5)×10−3 h3 Mpc−3,
which are five to ten times higher than those of typi-
cal cosmological spectroscopic surveys of emission-line
galaxies (C. Blake et al. 2011; M. Takada et al. 2014;
A. de Mattia et al. 2021; Y. Wang et al. 2022; M. Ab-
dul Karim et al. 2025; Euclid Collaboration et al. 2025),
make these samples not only well-suited for cosmological
applications, but also valuable for astrophysical stud-
ies of galaxy formation and evolution, as well as for
cross-correlation analyses with other large-scale struc-
ture tracers in the low-redshift universe.
These results represent the first step in a broader
program of cosmological analyses using the HETDEX
[O II] galaxy catalog. In future work, we will extend
this analysis to constrain the growth of structure using
the quadrupole and higher-order multipoles of the power
spectrum, as well as to study the galaxy–halo connec-
tion.

These data will also be useful for void cosmology anal-
yses (S. Contarini et al. 2026). The combination of these
measurements with complementary probes — such as
weak gravitational lensing (R. Dalal et al. 2023; A. H.
Wright et al. 2025; DES Collaboration et al. 2026), the
CMB ( Planck Collaboration et al. 2020; F. J. Qu et al.

APPENDIX
A. EFFECT OF NOVERLAP WEIGHT
In this appendix section, we check the effect of the weight, w(r) = N −1
overlap, on the power spectrum measurements
described in Section 4.1.
Figure 15 shows the sky distribution of Noverlap for Spring Bin 4 and Fall Bin 4, with the size of the circles reflecting
the Noverlap value. We adopt Bin 4 from both fields as representative of all bins for this check, since these bins span
the largest volume and contain the most galaxies. We find that the distribution of Noverlap is fairly random across the
sky.
Figure 16 shows the monopole and quadrupole power spectra for Spring Bin 4 and Fall Bin 4 with and without the
weight. This weight is either applied to both the data and random catalogs (solid lines), or omitted from both (dashed
lines) for the comparison. Applying the weight lowers the monopole power spectrum amplitude at high k, since it
prevents galaxies with Noverlap > 1 from being counted multiple times. The effect on the quadrupole power spectra is
minimal.
B. EFFECT OF SATELLITE GALAXIES IN POWER SPECTRUM MEASUREMENTS
This appendix describes how satellite galaxies, i.e., galaxies populated in dark matter subhalos, affect the power
spectrum measurements, following the mock modeling described in Section 4.2.
Figure 17 compares the monopole and quadrupole power spectra from the best-fit HOD mocks of Spring Bin 4 to
those from the data, with satellite galaxies excluded from the mocks. The mock measurements show poor agreement
with the data: the mock monopole amplitude is suppressed overall, while the mock quadrupole amplitude is higher
at high k than the data. The former effect can be compensated for by increasing log(M0) (Figure 6), but the latter
cannot (Figure 7). This indicates that populating satellite galaxies in subhalos is essential for accurately modeling the
HETDEX [O II] volume-limited samples.
