THE REAL K-THEORY OF THE SPHERE WITH AN ARBITRARY
INVOLUTION
JEFFREY L. BOERSEMA
Abstract. We complete the investigation begun in a previous paper to find unitary rep-
resentations of the non-trivial real K-theory elements for the sphere Sd with an involution.
Here we consider all involutions except the antipodal involutions. We write down explicit
unitaries representing the generators in all cases for d ≤3, and for d > 0 we describe a
recipe for generating such unitaries.

This paper is concerned with identifying explicit descriptions of the non-trivial K-
theory elements, using the unitary picture of real K-theory developed in [6] and [4]. Some
low-dimensional cases (for d = 1, 2) of this was accomplished in Section 4 of [4]; but in [5]
and in this paper, we take a systematic approach to finding unitary elements to represent
all non-trivial K-theory classes in all cases. We find explicit formulas for this, for all spheres
(Sd, τ a,b) with d ≤3 and a > 0, and with all possible involutions. More generally, for each
a, b with a > 0, we will describe a process to find a formula for a unitary u that represents
the key generator ga,b. The cases where a = 0 was fully addressed in [5], so together these
two papers address all possible spheres with involution.
This paper is also closely connected with the work of Schulz-Baldes and Tom Stoiber in [16],
which addresses the case of complex K-theory for the sphere Sd. There, in Proposition 1,
the authors display unitaries that represent the non-trivial elements g in eKi(C(Sd)). In
particular,

The formulas that we find for ga,b will be derived from the formulas of [16] in the complex
case, but extra care must be taken throughout to make sure that they satisfy the required
symmetries for the real K-theory elements and taking into account the given involution on
Sd, as dictated by the unitary picture of real K-theory.
In the unitary picture of real K-theory, elements of real K-theory are represented by
unitary matrices over the complexification of the real C∗-algebra in question, that satisfy
certain symmetry relations. This picture is summarized in Section 2 below.
In the low dimensional cases d ≤3, we present these formulas for generators of all the
non-zero K-theory groups, presenting these formulas as concretely as possible, so that they
can be used off-the-shelf. This is accomplished in Section 3. We treat the general case, for
arbitrary d, in Section 4. We produce formulas of unitaries to represent the key generators
ga,b. All other non-trivial elements of g

a dedicated reader can combine the formulas for unitaries representing ga,b in this paper with
the concrete description of these operations in [4], to find a unitary representative for any

Formulas such as the ones we are developing in this paper are topological markers in
physical models such as in [12], [14], and [15]. These physical models tend to involve a
space with involution (X, τ) where the involution might represent time-reversal symmetry or
particle-hole symmetry (or a combination of both) and the space X might be homeomorphic
to Rn, a sphere Sd, a torus (a product of spheres), or another space.
These methods have been used to study topological insulators and more recently these
methods have been proposed to classify topology in photonic systems as in [8], [9], [10], [11].
We wish to be able to address physical models in a variety of possible dimensions, with a
variety of symmetry structures, which may or may not contain time-reversal symmetry, and
which may or may not contain particle-hole symmetry. Together with the previous paper
[5], this article extends the range of such models that can be addressed to include all spheres
with involution. The formulas that we develop in this paper should be seen as K-theory
generalizations of the index formulas described in [13], particularly Section 5.
