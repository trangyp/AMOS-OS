Abstract
We investigate modular Laplacian automata on triangular lattices with evolution governed
by binary and ternary moduli. Extending previous studies on square lattices, we examine
how lattice geometry influences long-term growth, density, fragmentation, and the emergence
of self-similar structures. We further investigate whether sparse ternary interventions can
stabilize predominantly binary dynamics.
The experiments reveal that mask geometry is the primary determinant of large-scale mor-
phology. Full hexagonal masks generate recurrent density crises and fragmentation, whereas
triangular masks support persistent growth and reveal a threshold phenomenon governed by
growth-capable nuclei. Although seed symmetry influences transient behaviour, the asymp-
totic morphology is inherited mainly from the mask.
To control binary fragmentation, we investigate sparse developmental ternary perturba-
tions in which a small number of carefully timed occurrences of modulus 3 are inserted into
an otherwise binary sequence. A Monte Carlo optimization demonstrates that as few as three
interventions are sufficient to redirect the subsequent binary evolution toward substantially
denser carpet-like configurations. The effectiveness of this strategy depends primarily on the
timing of the interventions rather than on their number.
Analysis of the post-intervention dynamics shows that ternary shaping does not replace
binary evolution. Instead, it produces denser self-similar structures, substantially reduces cri-
sis depth, and resets the phase of the binary crisis clock. The results suggest that geometry
determines the family of admissible morphologies, whereas sparse developmental perturba-
tions select favourable long-term trajectories within that family.

Introduction
Previous studies of modular Laplacian dynamics on square lattices revealed several recur-
ring phenomena, including Frobenius-type revivals, periodic fragmentation, density crises,
and the emergence of persistent carpet-like structures under suitable modulus sequences
Nowak-Kępczyk (2025a,b). In the purely binary case, recurrent replication events repeat-
edly reorganize the configuration into spatially separated copies of the initial seed, leading to


## Page 3


characteristic cycles of growth, collapse, and recovery Nowak-Kępczyk (2025a). More general
binary–ternary sequences may substantially alter this behaviour, producing long-lived high-
density structures and suppressing large-scale fragmentation Nowak-Kępczyk (2025b). These
observations raise two questions. First, how does binary modular Laplacian dynamics depend
on lattice geometry beyond the square lattice? Second, can long-term binary evolution be
redirected by a small number of ternary interventions?
To address these questions, we study modular Laplacian automata on regular hexagonal
tilings whose cell centres form a triangular lattice. The resulting neighbourhood masks exhibit
different symmetries and growth properties, allowing us to investigate the role of geometry
in large-scale morphology, density evolution, and fragmentation.
While square lattices display a relatively uniform crisis–fragmentation scenario, triangular
lattices support several distinct regimes, including extinction, frozen configurations, persis-
tent growth, and recurrent crisis-driven evolution. We focus on the latter, since recurrent
density crises provide a natural setting for testing developmental control.
Motivated by the exceptional role of modulus 3 observed previously, we investigate whether
a small number of early ternary interventions can redirect subsequent binary evolution.
Ternary insertions are treated as developmental perturbations rather than persistent forcing.
Specifically, we ask:
1. Which binary regimes occur on triangular lattices?
2. Which regimes exhibit recurrent density crises?
3. Can sparse ternary interventions redirect such trajectories?
4. How many interventions are required and when should they occur?
5. What dynamical changes remain after the ternary insertion phase has ended? In par-
ticular, how are density crises and long-term stability affected?

1.4. Monte Carlo optimization of ternary sequences
To identify efficient ternary intervention strategies, we perform a Monte Carlo search over
predominantly binary construction sequences.
Starting from the binary sequence [2, 2, 2, . . .], a prescribed number of binary steps is
replaced by ternary steps. Each candidate sequence is simulated and evaluated using the
statistics introduced above.
For a fixed number of ternary interventions n3, candidate sequences are ranked according
to their long-term density performance, primarily using the mean density on the evaluation
window 33 ≤t ≤80. Secondary ranking criteria include density fluctuations measured by
the standard deviation of the density.
For diagnostic purposes, the implementation additionally records crisis-related observ-
ables, including crisis depth, recovery characteristics, and derived crisis scores. These quanti-
ties are used to identify and characterize density crises but do not enter the final optimization
score.
The ultimate objective is to identify sparse ternary weavings that maximize long-term
density while using as few occurrences of (3) as possible.
2. Results
To address the questions posed above, we perform three experiments. We first classify
binary dynamical regimes on triangular lattices, then investigate sparse ternary interventions
as a mechanism for trajectory shaping, and finally analyse the timing and severity of density
crises after the intervention phase.
2.1. Experiment 1. Binary dynamics for different masks and seeds
We investigate purely binary evolution on triangular lattices for a range of neighbourhood
masks and seed configurations.
Binary evolution on triangular lattices falls into three broad regimes:
1. non-growing trajectories (extinction or freezing),
2. recurrent crisis-driven collapse,
3. persistent growth.
Morphology versus dynamics. The neighbourhood mask is the primary determinant of large-
scale morphology, whereas the seed mainly affects transient behaviour and symmetry. Sym-
metric masks (hex6, tristar, tristar_rev) generate families of geometrically similar struc-
tures, while asymmetric masks (penta, quadra) produce a wider variety of morphologies.
Hexagonal mask. The full hexagonal mask hex6 reproduces the crisis–fragmentation scenario
previously observed on square lattices. The evolution alternates between densification and
density crises, producing expanding hexagonal shells and repeated fragmentation. No per-
sistent high-density binary structures were observed within the investigated time interval.

Triangular masks. The masks tristar and tristar_rev support persistent growth for sev-
eral seed configurations. Adjacent edge pairs generate expanding carpet-like structures, while
the triangular seed produces patterns with pronounced threefold rotational symmetry.
Threshold phenomenon. The triangular masks exhibit a clear growth threshold. The one-
point seed and the tristar seed remain frozen, whereas slightly larger seeds already initiate
unbounded growth. This suggests the existence of growth-capable nuclei.
Most frozen configurations remain unaffected by sparse ternary interventions. The only
observed exception is the complementary tristar/tristar_rev mask–seed combination,
which can be activated by short developmental sequences such as [3, 3, 3, 2] and [3, 3, 3, 2, 2, 2].
Symmetry mismatch. The rotational structures generated by the triangular seed appear to

2.2.1. Does timing matter?
We compared ternary insertions applied at t = 8k −1, 8k, and 8k + 1. The resulting evo-
lutions proved highly sensitive to timing. Interventions synchronized with the characteristic
binary crisis rhythm (t = 8k) generated substantially denser carpet-like structures, whereas
shifts by a single iteration produced trajectories resembling the binary reference evolution
(Figure 4).
2.2.2. What does a ternary insertion change?
The timing experiment demonstrates that ternary insertions can strongly influence sub-
sequent binary evolution. We now ask a more fundamental question: what aspect of the
trajectory is actually modified?
For the quadra mask, binary evolution follows a recurrent eight-cycle family of self-similar
motifs. Figure 5 shows the effect of inserting a single ternary update at t = 25. Although
all subsequent updates are again binary, the trajectory does not return to the original mo-
tif family. Instead, it develops a different sequence of recurring structures. This suggests
that ternary insertions act as developmental switches capable of redirecting binary evolution
between distinct self-similar trajectory families.

2.2.4. Experiment 2 results
Three observations emerge from the optimization experiments.
Timing dominates intervention effectiveness. Figure 4 already suggests that ternary shap-
ing is highly sensitive to timing. Interventions synchronized with the characteristic binary
crisis rhythm (t = 8k) generate substantially denser carpet-like structures than interven-
tions shifted by a single iteration. The latter produce trajectories that remain close to the
oscillatory binary reference evolution.
A similar picture is visible in Figure 6. Even with the same number of ternary insertions,
different schedules produce markedly different outcomes, ranging from trajectories nearly
indistinguishable from the binary baseline to dense, persistent carpet-like configurations.
These results indicate that intervention timing is at least as important as the number of
interventions.
Only a few interventions are sufficient. The Monte Carlo optimization confirms this obser-
vation quantitatively. Figure 7 shows that the mean density increases rapidly with the first
few ternary insertions and then approaches saturation. Most of the improvement is already
achieved with two to four interventions, while additional insertions provide only marginal
gains.

3. Conclusions
The present work demonstrates that modular Laplacian dynamics on triangular lattices
is governed by the interplay between geometry and developmental perturbations.
At the geometric level, the neighbourhood mask determines the family of admissible
large-scale morphologies. Full hexagonal masks reproduce the familiar crisis–fragmentation
scenario observed previously on square lattices, whereas triangular masks support a richer
spectrum of behaviours, including extinction, persistent growth, and threshold phenomena
associated with growth-capable nuclei. Seed geometry influences symmetry and transient
behaviour, but the asymptotic morphology is determined primarily by the mask.
Interestingly, the most persistent carpet-like structures do not arise from the most sym-
metric neighbourhoods. Several high-density configurations emerge for masks with partially
broken rotational symmetry, suggesting that symmetry breaking may play a constructive role
in long-term self-organization.
At the dynamical level, sparse ternary perturbations act as developmental interventions.
Monte Carlo optimization shows that only a few carefully timed ternary updates are sufficient
to redirect crisis-prone binary trajectories toward persistent carpet-like regimes. Most of the
attainable density gain is achieved with two to four interventions, while additional insertions

The most striking dynamical result is that ternary shaping does not eliminate the binary
crisis clock. Instead, it resets its phase. Subsequent crises remain organized around the
characteristic binary timescales, but are measured relative to the final ternary intervention.
In this sense, ternary shaping resets the binary crisis clock rather than removing it. At
the same time, subsequent density crises become substantially shallower and recover more
rapidly, leading to significantly improved long-term density.
These observations indicate that developmental perturbations do not replace binary dy-
namics. Instead, they select among alternative long-term trajectories already permitted by
the underlying binary system.
More broadly, the results suggest a separation between two complementary mechanisms
in modular Laplacian evolution. Geometry determines the space of admissible morpholo-
gies, whereas sparse developmental perturbations select among the corresponding long-term
trajectories Haken (2006); Turing (1952); Whitesides and Grzybowski (2002).
Understanding how these trajectories are organized, and how they relate to the hierarchy
of density crises observed in binary dynamics, remains an open problem. Future work may
benefit from spectral and multiscale analyses of the density signal, as well as from extending
developmental control strategies to other modular rules and lattice geometries.

schedule, or derived directly from the underlying Frobenius-type replication process?
More generally, it remains unknown whether sparse developmental control is a specific
feature of modular Laplacian dynamics or a broader principle of self-organizing systems.
Exploring this possibility may help connect modular automata with questions arising in
morphogenesis, programmable matter, and controlled self-assembly.
