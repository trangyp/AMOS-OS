The Knop-Luna-Vust theory of spherical embeddings,
extended to non-reductive groups
Guido Pezzini
Abstract. We extend the theory of spherical embeddings to actions of connected non-
reductive groups. This generalization is formally very similar to the usual reductive
case: equivariant embeddings are described essentially by collections of convex cones in
a rational vector space.
We also show some new relationship between the combinatorics emerging in this case
and the properties of the unipotent radical of the acting group. Finally, we apply our
techniques to prove a characterization of log homogeneous varieties.
1. Introduction
In 1983 Luna and Vust developed a general theory of equivariant embeddings of varieties
that are homogeneous under the action of a connected reductive algebraic group G, in the
paper [LV83]. This theory has been reformulated and simplified by F. Knop in 1991 in
the paper [Kn91] in the special case of spherical varieties, which are a generalization of
flag varieties, symmetric varieties, and also of toric varieties.
Formally, there is an evident similarity with the classification of toric varieties by means
of their fans, which are finite collections of polyhedral convex cones in a rational vector
space and where each cone corresponds to an orbit. For a spherical homogeneous variety
Y , any G-equivariant open embedding X of Y is described by a similar finite set F(X),
called colored fan.
Its elements are still polyhedral convex cones, but they are called colored cones because
they are equipped (in slightly loose terms) with marked points, choosen among a finite
list of special points which depend only on Y . In addition, the relative interior of each
colored cone must intersect a given polyhedral cone, called the valuation cone, which again
depends only on Y . The points of this cone correspond to discrete valuations of the field
C(Y ) that are G-invariant, for example the valuation given by a G-stable prime divisor in
some embedding.
In this paper we extend this theory to the case where Y is homogeneous under the action
of a connected linear algebraic group P, not necessarily reductive. The basic assumption
is still that Y be spherical, under the action of a Levi subgroup G of P. So the standard
theory of spherical embeddings is available, and one must take into account the action of
the bigger group P.
The end result is that the theory looks quite similar to the classical reductive case. Given a
P-equivariant embedding X of Y , the first task is to study its P-orbits. This is equivalent
to studying the subfamily F(X)↑of the colored cones that correspond to G-orbits that
have P-stable closure (see Proposition 4.12 below). We introduce the notion of colored
P-fan based on the properties of F(X)↑.

Then, several statements of [Kn91] generalize smoothly with everything now reformulated
in terms of F(X)↑instead of F(X). The main result of the paper, Theorem 4.15, states
that P-equivariant embeddings up to isomorphisms are classified by colored P-fans. The
condition for the identity Y →Y to extend to a P-equivariant map X1 →X2 between
two P-equivariant embeddings is the same as in the reductive case, but expressed only in
terms of F(X1)↑and F(X2)↑.
The valuation cone VG(Y ) is also replaced by the subset VP(Y ) corresponding to P-
invariant valuations. This is again a convex polyhedral cone, and it is used also to describe
P-equivariant maps between different P-homogeneous varieties, in the same way as in the
reductive case.
Some parts of this theory had been already developed in our previous paper [Pe17],
including the construction of the cone VP(Y ), so here we build on that work to complete
the picture with the full embedding theory. On the other hand, it is remarkable that
several proofs in this paper can simply follow the lines of corresponding proofs in Knop’s
paper [Kn91], which essentially turn out to work also with non-reductive groups.
Beyond these many similarities, a novelty is discussed in Section 5: we show that the
shape of the P-invariant valuation cone VP(Y ) is related to some properties of the action
of the unipotent radical P u on Y .
This relationship has applications even in the reductive case. Indeed, suppose that a
G-homogeneous variety Y admits a G-equivariant map φ: Y →G/Q to a partial flag
variety G/Q, so Q is a parabolic subgroup of G. Then the equality VG(Y ) = VQ(φ−1(eQ))
was proved in [Pe17], and in this paper we use it to relate VG(Y ) to the action of the
unipotent radical Qu on Y .
This provides new informations on the cone VG(Y ) from the knowledge of a generic
stabilizer of Y . In general, computing VG(Y ) from the stabilizer is reputed to be a rather
difficult problem, solved only under additional assumptions (see Avdeev’s paper [Av24])
or in special cases with laborious case-by-case considerations (see e.g. our paper [BP15]
with P. Bravi).
A last analogy with the reductive case is discussed in Section 7. For an affine spherical
G-variety X, the valuation cone has an important relationship with the multiplication in
the ring of regular functions C[X]. We prove in Theorem 7.1 that if the G-action on X
extends to P then a similar relationship holds with the cone of P-invariant valuations,
but where the action of P on C[X] is considered together with the multiplication.
Using this result we are able to check in examples that VP(X) is not always cosimplicial:
its maximal faces can have linearly dependent equations, see Example 7.4. This is another
relevant novelty, because on the contrary VG(X) is always a Weyl chamber of a Weyl
group (thanks to an important theorem of Brion’s), generalizing the little Weyl group of
symmetric varieties to arbitrary spherical G-varieties. So, under this point of view there
is no analogue of the little Weyl group for the action of P.
In Section 8 we apply our theory to log homogeneous varieties, introduced by Brion
in [Br07]. We first recall how they are related to the so-called regular embeddings (see
the paper [BDP90] by Bifet, De Concini, Procesi), thanks to results of Brion’s. Then we
prove a characterization of varieties that are log homogeneous under the action of P in
Theorem 8.3.

2. Notations and basic assumptions
In this paper all algebraic groups and varieties are defined over the field C of complex
numbers and varieties are always assumed to be irreducible. Subgroups of algebraic groups
are always assumed to be closed.
We fix a connected linear algebraic group P, a Levi (i.e. maximal connected reductive)
subgroup G ⊆P, a Borel subgroup B of G, a maximal torus T of B, and we denote by B−
the opposite Borel subgroup with respect to T. The set of simple roots of G corresponding
to these choices will be denoted by S.
In this paper we will consider varieties X that are spherical under the action of G, i.e.,
such that X is normal and has an open B-orbit. For details about the definitions and
results mentioned in the rest of this section we refer to [Kn91].
We will denote by D(X)B the finite set of B-stable prime divisors of X; the sets D(X)P
and D(X)G are defined analogously. We define the set
∆(X) = D(X)B ∖D(X)G
and call its elements the colors of X.
We denote by C(X)(B) the set of all B-eigenvectors in C(X), and we denote by Ξ(X) the
group of their B-eigenvalues χ: B →C∗. It is a free abelian group of finite rank, which is
also called the rank of X. We define the vector space
N(X) = HomZ(Ξ(X), Q).
The usual pairing between v ∈N(X) and ξ ∈Ξ(X) will be denoted by ⟨ξ, v⟩.
The value of any discrete valuation1 v of C(X) on a B-eigenvector f ∈C(X)(B) depends
only on the B-eigenvalue of f, thus v yields a well-defined element ρ(v) of N(X). This
applies in particular to the valuation given by any B-stable prime divisor D of X, and in
this case we denote simply by ρ(D) the corresponding element of N(X).
There are only finitely many G-orbits Z in X, each is itself spherical, and one associates
with any such Z the polyhedral (i.e. finitely generated) convex cone CZ ⊆N(X) generated
by ρ(D) for all D ∈D(X)B such that D ⊇Z. The colored cone associated with Z is the
couple (CZ, FZ) where FZ is the set of the colors of X that contain Z. It turns out that
for each D ∈D(X)G containing Z, the element ρ(D) generates an extremal ray of the
cone CZ not containing the image of any color.
The set of the colored cones of all G-orbits of X is called the colored fan of X and is
denoted here by F(X). Two G-orbits Z, W satisfy W ⊇Z if and only if CW is a face
of CZ and FW = FZ ∩ρ−1(CW). In this case we say that (CW, FW) is a colored face of
(CZ, FZ).
The variety X is called G-toroidal if any element of D(X)B containing a G-orbit is
G-stable.
The set of G-invariant discrete valuations on C(X) is identified via the map ρ with its
image VG(X) in N(X), and VG(X) is a polyhedral convex cone of maximal dimension

Finally we recall that there are combinatorial notions of “abstract” colored cones and

The definition is as follows. A couple (C, F) is a colored cone in N(X) if F is a subset of
∆(X), the set C is a convex cone in N(X) generated by ρ(F) and finitely many elements

strictly convex and ρ(F) does not contain 0.
A colored subspace is a colored cone (C, F) such that C is a vector subspace. A colored
face of a colored cone (C, F) is a colored cone (C′, F′) such that C′ is a face of C and
F′ = F ∩ρ−1(C′).
A colored fan in N(X) is a non-empty finite set F of colored cones in N(X) such that F
contains every colored face of every element of F, and for every v ∈VG(X) there is at
most one (C, F) ∈F such that v ∈C◦. A colored fan is strictly convex if all its elements
are strictly convex.

4. The classification of P-embeddings
4.1. Lemma. Let X be a P-embedding of P/H. Then the following assertions hold.
(1) Each P-orbit Z of X contains a unique G-orbit Z↑such that Z↑is open in Z, and
a unique G-orbit Z↓such that Z↓is closed in Z.
(2) For all P-orbits Z of X, all G-stable prime divisors of Z contain Z↓.
Proof. We prove the first assertion. Let Z ⊆X be a P-orbit. It is a locally closed
G-stable irreducible subvariety of X, whence Z is G-spherical. Being a P-orbit, it can
be identified with a homogeneous space Z = P/K, let Q ⊆P be a parabolic subgroup
minimal containing K. The homogeneous variety P/Q is complete and Q contains the
unipotent radical of P, hence P/Q is a single G-orbit.
Let W ⊆Z be a G-orbit closed in Z, and consider the natural projection φ: Z →P/Q.
Since P/Q is G-homogeneous, we have that W is mapped surjectively to P/Q via φ and
W intersects the fiber Z′ = φ−1(eQ).
The subgroup K is regularly embedded in Q, i.e., the unipotent radical Ku is contained in
Qu, see [Ti11, Definition 3.6]. This holds by the minimality in the definition of Q, because
this minimality implies that the image of K in Q/Qu is reductive. We conclude that
Z′ ∼= Q/K is affine, by [Ti11, Theorem 3.7]. Moreover Z′ is spherical under the action of

and P, being the union of all P-stable prime divisors (see Remark 2.1.1 in [Br07]),
hence we will also simply say that X is log P-homogeneous.
(2) We say that X is P-regular if the following conditions holds:
(a) the group P has on X an open orbit, whose complement is a union of
irreducible divisors with normal crossings, called boundary divisors,
(b) any P-orbit closure in X is the transversal intersection of some of the boundary
divisors,
(c) for all x ∈X the normal space TxX/Tx(P · x) contains an open orbit of the
stabilizer Px.
We recall some results by Brion from [Br07] on these two notions.
8.2. Theorem. Let P be a connected linear algebraic group with Levi subgroup G and let
X be a smooth irreducible P-variety.
(1) (Corollary 2.1.4 in [Br07].) If X is P-regular then it is log P-homogeneous.
(2) (Part of Theorem 3.2.1 in [Br07].) If X is complete and log P-homogeneous, then
(a) the variety X is G-spherical, and
(b) there exists an open G-stable and G-regular subset XG ⊆X such that every
P-orbit of X intersects XG along a unique G-orbit.
We complete the above picture in the following theorem.
8.3. Theorem. Let P be a connected linear algebraic group with Levi subgroup G, and
let X be a smooth complete irreducible P-variety. Then X is log P-homogeneous if and
only if X is G-spherical and P-toroidal.
Proof. Suppose X is log P-homogeneous. Then X is G-spherical by Theorem 8.2, part (2a).
Let XG be as in Theorem 8.2, part (2b). We claim that XG is G-toroidal, and this follows
from the proof of Lemma 3.1.2 in [Br07] and Proposition 2.2.1 in the paper [BB96] by
Bien and Brion.
More precisely, the local structure of XG is explained in details in [Br07]: for a point x
of any closed G-orbit, one takes the stabilizer Q ⊆G of x. It is a parabolic subgroup
of G, and we can assume it contains B. A neighborhood of x in XG is Q-equivariantly

As in [BB96], one notices then that the coordinate hyperplanes of this affine space An, i.e.
the M-stable prime divisors, are the intersections of An with the G-stable prime divisors
of XG containing x, and there are no other B-stable prime divisors in this open chart.
This shows that any B-stable prime divisor containing x is G-stable, therefore XG is
G-toroidal. Finally, the embedding X is P-toroidal thanks to [Pe17, Corollary 6.4].
Viceversa, suppose X is G-spherical and P-toroidal. Then there exists an open G-stable
subset XG such that XG is a G-toroidal embedding of the open G-orbit of X, and XG
intersects any P-orbit of X in a single G-orbit, thanks to [Pe17, Corollary 6.4]. The local
structure of G-toroidal varieties, such as XG here, is well-known to be as above again. It
follows easily, as in [BB96], that XG is G-regular.
By the second part of Theorem 3.2.1 in [Br07] we conclude that X is log P-homogeneous.
