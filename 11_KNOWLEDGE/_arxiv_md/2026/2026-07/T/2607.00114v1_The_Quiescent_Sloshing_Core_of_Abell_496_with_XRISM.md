ABSTRACT
Context. Gas motions provide insight into the dynamical history and physical processes within galaxy clusters. The nearby, X-
ray bright, strong cool-core cluster Abell 496 (A496) is a unique system. Strong gas sloshing features have been observed, and
detailed simulations have predicted that these were induced by a previous north-south minor merger. Consistent with this scenario,
SRG/eROSITA All-Sky Survey data confirm an emission enhancement in the northwest direction.
Aims. We investigate the kinematics of the intracluster medium (ICM) in the core of A496 using high-resolution data from the Resolve
micro-calorimeter on board XRISM.
Methods. The Resolve A496 observation enables us to directly probe the ICM motions in the cluster core, measuring line-of-sight
(LOS) bulk and turbulent velocities via line shifts and broadening of characteristic lines such as Fe xxv Heα and Fe xxvi Lyα. We
compared our measurement with other Resolve cluster core measurements and further compared our results with simulations and
multiwavelength observations.
Results. From an optical redshift analysis, we found that the BCG is at rest with respect to the systemic velocity of the cluster.
Despite multiple previously detected cold fronts and harboring a weak central radio source, Resolve observation shows that the core
of A496 is dynamically quiescent. The ICM is moving with respect to the brightest cluster galaxy with a LOS bulk velocity of
vbulk = −69+25
−20 km s−1. We measured a turbulent velocity of σv = 78+18
−16 km s−1, the lowest value reported by the instrument on a cluster
core to date. This value is also in good agreement with the velocity dispersion of the Hα filament in the core, which may indicate
condensation of ICM in the wake of the radio bubble. Assuming isotropic turbulence, the ICM turbulent velocity corresponds to a
subsonic 3D Mach number of 0.15+0.04
−0.03 and a non-thermal pressure fraction of 1.2+0.6
−0.5 %. The mechanical AGN feedback from the
recent activity of the central radio source is estimated to contribute about 7–9 % to the ICM heating. The 1D LOS bulk velocity from
the Simulating the LOcal Web (SLOW) constrained Universe simulation is consistent with the measured value, suggesting that AGN
feedback has a negligible contribution. The A496 SLOW turbulent velocity, as in other reported Resolve−simulation comparisons, is
higher, but remains within 1.5σ uncertainty.
Conclusions. A496 may represent one of the most quiescent sloshing cores observed so far.

1. Introduction
Galaxy clusters form and grow both through major mergers and
continuous accretion of lower-mass systems along cosmic web
filaments (e.g., Kravtsov & Borgani 2012). These events leave
imprints at various scales in the hot plasma (107 K to 108 K), the
intracluster medium (ICM), that permeates between the mem-
ber galaxies. Major mergers produce distinct large-scale distur-
bances in the ICM, such as shocks, bulk flows, and turbulence
(Markevitch & Vikhlinin 2007). On smaller scales, gas mo-
tions are driven by localized activities, such as sloshing, AGN
feedback, and galaxy motion. In the centers of clusters, active
galactic nuclei (AGNs) harbored by the brightest cluster galax-
ies (BCGs) redistribute their energy through low-density, ris-
ing bubbles inflated by relativistic AGN jets (for reviews see,
e.g., Fabian 2012; Hlavacek-Larrondo et al. 2022). The so-called
AGN feedback has been known to contribute to the heating of the
ICM in cool-core clusters (e.g., Mittal et al. 2009; Pasini et al.
2022; Veronica et al. 2026), which is also expected to cause bulk
and turbulent motions.
Studying gas motions is non-trivial and has previously been
challenging due to a modest spectral resolution of the CCD-
based X-ray instruments, which is of the order of ∼150 eV. The
Hitomi satellite was launched in 2016 (Takahashi et al. 2016)
and carried a non-dispersive X-ray microcalorimeter with an un-
precedented spectral resolution of 4.9 eV. Despite its short mis-
sion life, the instrument successfully returned precise measure-
ments of gas motions in the center of the Perseus cluster (Hitomi
Collaboration et al. 2016, 2018). Hitomi is then succeeded by the
X-ray Imaging and Spectroscopy Mission (XRISM), which was
launched in September 2023 (Tashiro et al. 2025). XRISM car-
ries two science instruments: the Resolve soft X-ray spectrome-
ter (Ishisaki et al. 2022) and the Xtend soft X-ray imager (Noda
et al. 2025). Resolve is an X-ray microcalorimeter that consists
of a 6 × 6 pixel array. It has a field-of-view (FoV) of 3.1′ × 3.1′
and an energy resolution of 4.5 eV FWHM at 6.0 keV. The Xtend

Over decades, A496 has been the subject of targeted and
sampled studies in various wavelengths. In X-ray, the cluster
appears relaxed, with the X-ray peak coinciding well with the
position of the central dominant galaxy (cD), which is also its
BCG (Dupke et al. 2007). In general, the X-ray morphology
of the cluster is elongated in the northwest direction (see the
yellow contour in Fig. 1 and Xtend image in the left panel of
Fig. 2). However, detailed investigations of the core using XMM-
Newton and Chandra observations reveal a spiral pattern (La-
ganá et al. 2010) and several cold fronts associated with sloshing
(e.g., Dupke et al. 2007; Ghizzardi et al. 2014). Dedicated hy-
drodynamical simulations show that these observed features are
well reproduced by an off-axis minor merger, which induced a
sloshing motion of the core (Roediger et al. 2012). A496 hosts a
central radio galaxy detected across multiple frequencies (Hogan
et al. 2015b,a; Ubertosi et al. 2024). To investigate the inter-
play between the central radio galaxy of A496 and its immediate
atmosphere, Ubertosi et al. (2024) utilized multiple deep radio
data including data from the GMRT at 150, 330, and 617 MHz,
the VLA at 1.4 and 4.8 GHz, and the VLA Low Band Iono-
sphere and Transient Experiment (VLITE) at 340 MHz, as well
as Chandra X-ray and Very Large Telescope (VLT) MUSE ob-
servations. From multi-frequency radio images, they identified

2. XRISM A496 observation
The core of the A496 cluster was observed by XRISM on 24
March 2025 as a part of the AO1 (OBSID: 201122010, P.I.:
T. H. Reiprich). The pointed observation was carried out us-
ing both XRISM instruments: the Resolve high-resolution mi-
crocalorimeter and the Xtend soft X-ray imager. The informa-
tion on the XRISM A496 observation is listed in Table 2. In this
work, we utilized the Resolve data to estimate the ICM proper-
ties in the core of A496, while we assessed the sky background
component using the Xtend data. The data screening and reduc-
tion steps were performed following the prescription outlined in
The XRISM Data Reduction Guide and Ota et al. (2026) for the
analyses.
2.1. Data reduction
Data reduction and analysis of Resolve and Xtend data were per-
formed using the XRISM tasks implemented in HEASoft v6.35
and using the calibration database (CALDB) version 20250915.
They are summarized below.
For the Resolve data, firstly, the event file was reprocessed
using the latest CALDB. Frame events were then screened by ap-
plying PI and rise time cuts, as well as STATUS[4]. The clean ex-
posure time is 23.1 ks. A lightcurve in the 2.0 −10.0 keV energy
band was inspected and showed no temporal variation through-
out the observation. For the spectral analysis, we only included
High-primary events (Hp; ITYPE==0) that achieve the highest
Resolve energy resolution. We excluded pixel 12 (the calibra-
tion pixel) and pixel 27, which shows different gain variation
characteristics from the other pixels.
For the Xtend data, after reprocessing, we removed the
anomalous pixels in the Xtend field-of-view (FoV) using the
xtdpixclip task. The task was run in two steps: in the HISTO
mode to build a histogram of the supplied region file, then in
the APPLY mode, when the threshold cut for the provided region
was applied. A clean event file was generated by the task when
mkclean=yes parameter was given. The fully cleaned Xtend
photon image in the 0.3−10.0 keV band and the Resolve photon
image in the 2.0 −10.0 keV band are shown in Fig. 2. In the Re-
solve photon image the counts are randomized in location within
the detector pixels.

statistical error in the temperature increases by almost a factor
of three. Alternatively, other line complexes in the broad energy

and narrow energy bands are consistent within their 1σ signifi-
cances, with a relative difference of much less than 0.1% for the
redshift and about 10% for the turbulent velocity.
The Fe xxv Heα complex consists of several prominent emis-
sion lines, including z, y, x, and w lines. Resonance scattering is
expected to influence the flux of the strongest line, i.e., the w
(resonance) line. As shown in Fig. 3 (right), the model (blue/red
line) appears to underpredict the various emission lines in the
Fe xxv Heα complex, except for the w line, which may result
from accommodating this brightest line. To test the impact of
the effect on our velocity measurements, we performed spec-
tral fittings where this line is excluded, in broad and narrow
energy bands. The derived bulk velocities are consistent within
the 1.3(1.6)σ statistical uncertainties for the full (narrow) band
fit, and the turbulent velocities are within 0.9(1.4)σ within the
statistical uncertainties to the upper limits estimated from the
w line-excluded fits (see Fig. A.1). This suggests that resonant
scattering has only a minor impact on our results.
To test the influence of a multitemperature structure, we
added an additional bapec component (2T). While the Bayesian
information criterion test (BIC; Schwarz 1978) shows that there
is no significant improvement in the fit when adding the sec-
ond component, we report that the LOS bulk velocity resulting
from various 2T-fits (e.g., all source parameters from both com-
ponents are untied and thawed, σv linked, σv and Z linked, and
σv, Z, and z linked) are consistent with the default 1T-fit listed
in Table 3.
We assessed the systematic uncertainties originating from
the Resolve energy scale (gain) and line spread function. The
current Resolve energy-scale accuracy in the 5.4 −9.0 keV en-
ergy band is ±0.3 eV. From the Resolve Energy Scale report5 of
our observation, the worst line shift inferred from the calibration
pixel is reported to be 0.11 eV. Adding these two terms to the
quadrature results in a total energy scale uncertainty of 0.32 eV,
which translates to a systematic uncertainty in the LOS bulk ve-
locity of ∼16 km s−1 at 6 keV. Furthermore, the instrumental line
spread uncertainty is ∼0.13 eV FWHM at 6.0 keV. This corre-
sponds to a Gaussian width of σE ≈0.055 eV and translates into
a systematic uncertainty in the one-dimensional turbulent veloc-
ity of ∼3 km s−1. Both systematic uncertainties are smaller than
the statistical uncertainties of the respective velocity measure-
ments, and therefore do not affect our conclusions.

3. Results
3.1. Galaxy redshift analysis
To compare the gas motion measured by Resolve with the galaxy
component of the A496 cluster, an optical galaxy redshift anal-
ysis was carried out. Spectroscopic and heliocentric galaxy red-
shifts within R500 were compiled from the NASA/IPAC Extra-
galactic Database (NED). The distribution of the 96 galaxies
within the boundary is presented in Fig. 4. The redshift of the
BCG, zBCG = 0.03281 ± 0.00004 (Wegner et al. 1999), is con-
sistent with the mean of the distribution, ⟨z⟩= 0.0329 ± 0.0003,
suggesting that the BCG is at rest with respect to the cluster sys-
temic velocity. The standard deviation is estimated to be 0.0028,
corresponding to a LOS velocity dispersion of σz ≈813 km s−1
with respect to the BCG redshift.

4. Discussion
4.1. Bulk Velocity
Resolve reveals a dynamically quiescent state of the ICM in
the A496 core. Despite some sloshing-induced features, such as
multiple cold fronts and an enhanced spiral pattern identified by
previous studies using XMM-Newton and Chandra observations
(e.g., Tanaka et al. 2006; Dupke et al. 2007; Laganá et al. 2010;
Ghizzardi et al. 2014), which are indicative of coherent gas mo-
tion (Markevitch & Vikhlinin 2007), the line-of-sight bulk veloc-
ity of ICM relative to the motion of the BCG is moderate with
vbulk = −69+25
−20 km s−1. In dedicated A496 simulations, these fea-
tures were predicted to be triggered by an off-axis minor merger
(Roediger et al. 2012). The Simulating the LOcal Web (SLOW)
constrained Universe simulation (Sorce 2018; Dolag et al. 2023;
Hernández-Martínez et al. 2024; Seidel et al. 2025; Groth et al.
2026) further supports a merger configuration consistent with
that proposed by Roediger et al. (2012).
We further compare the observations to the non-radiative
LowerDecks zoom-in simulation (Seidel et al. 2026) based on
SLOW with the same setup as described by Groth et al. (2026),
using emissivity-weighted projected velocity maps similar to
Vazza & Brunetti (2026). As uncertainties can arise due to tim-
ing differences of the simulation compared to the observed clus-
ter, we analyze the redshift evolution close to z = 0, where
we have simulation output available. The gas bulk velocity in
the center relative to the BCG is estimated to be ≈0 km s−1
at z = 0.001, and reaches O(−100) km s−1 at z = 0, fully con-
sistent with XRISM measurements. This indicates that no AGN
feedback is required, consistent with the cool-core nature of the
cluster.
Compared to other bulk velocity measurements of CC clus-
ters by XRISM, our value lies between the most relaxed clus-
ter known, A2029 (XRISM Collaboration et al. 2025b; Sarkar
et al. 2025) and the Centaurus cluster (XRISM Collaboration
et al. 2025e). While there appears to be no trend among the
CC clusters, this may indicate that the clusters are at differ-
ent stages of sloshing, as also shown by the bulk velocity evo-
lution in the SLOW simulations. For example, it is suggested
that the observed low bulk velocity of the Ophiuchus cluster,
|vbulk| = 8 ± 7 km s−1, might be because its sloshing core is ap-
proaching a turning point (Fujita et al. 2025).

From the formation history of A496 alone (i.e., without feed-
back), the SLOW simulation predicts a higher 1D emission-
weighted velocity dispersion of 125 ± 25 km s−1 specifically
in this Resolve FoV (Fig. 6). This estimate was calculated at
z = 0.001. The value does not change from z = 0.001 to z = 0,
as there has been no recent major merger event, indicating that
this result is unaffected by timing uncertainties. While the veloc-
ity dispersion is slightly above the observed value, it is a better
match than previous statistical comparisons. Selection effects are
thus important to explain these differences (Groth et al. 2026). In
addition, SLOW predicts an increasing velocity dispersion gra-
dient to the outskirts, consistent with predictions from sloshing
motions (Bellomi et al. 2025). Nevertheless, the overestimation
in simulations is also presented in XRISM Collaboration et al.
(2025d). The authors compare the available Resolve measure-
ments with three cosmological simulation suites of galaxy clus-
ters that incorporate different baryonic physics models and all in-
clude key physical processes relevant to the formation and evo-
lution of galaxies. While the predicted velocity dispersions in
the central pointings of the CC clusters are in agreement among
the different simulation suites, the observed values from Resolve
are systematically below the simulation medians by a factor of
1.5 −1.7 (XRISM Collaboration et al. 2025d). They suggest that
the discrepancy between the predicted and observed velocity dis-
persion in the CC clusters might be due to incomplete modeling
of ICM physics in simulations; for instance, AGN feedback may
be too ejective.
The idealized numerical simulations aimed at disentangling
the roles of merger-induced sloshing and AGN feedback in
Perseus-like clusters show that these two processes, separately
and together, influence velocity dispersion profiles in clusters,
especially in the innermost regions (Bellomi et al. 2025). At r ≲
50 kpc, the velocity dispersion values vary between ∼50 km s−1
for the sloshing-only model and ∼200 km s−1 for AGN-only
model (Pjet ∼1 × 1045 erg s−1). Our value lies between the
sloshing-only and sloshing+AGN feedback of 3 × 1044 erg s−1
models. To compare with their predictions, we estimated the me-
chanical feedback from the most recent activity of the A496 cen-
tral radio source. We note that a visual inspection of the Chan-
dra image reveals no X-ray AGN corresponding to the central
radio source. The total radio flux density of the core and the
jets from the VLA at 5 GHz is S 5 GHz = 57 ± 6 mJy (Uber-
tosi et al. 2024). This innermost radio component has a largest
linear size (LLS) of about 10 kpc. Using the flux to luminos-
ity conversions (Eq. 2 and 3 in Veronica et al. 2026), adopting
α = 0.7 from Ubertosi et al. (2024), and the mechanical AGN
power equation from Heckman & Best (2014), we estimated a
value of (1.27±0.12)×1043 erg s−1. This outcome seems to align
with the prediction from Bellomi et al. (2025). Additionally, we
estimated the bolometric luminosity from Resolve FoV, which
is (1.57 ± 0.02) × 1044 erg s−1. The half-width of Resolve FoV
corresponds to 0.07R500 (Table 1), which is of the same order
of the cooling radius determined by Hudson et al. (2010) us-
ing the CC clusters in the HIghest X-ray FLUx Galaxy Clus-
ter Sample (HIFLUGCS; Reiprich & Böhringer 2002), which is
Rcool = 0.08R500. Comparing the radio mechanical power and
X-ray cooling luminosity, the contribution of the central AGN to
the ICM heating through mechanical feedback is 7−9%, which is
consistent with the efficiency derived from the relation between
AGN feedback and cooling luminosity in the eRASS1/ASKAP
CC cluster sample in Veronica et al. (2026).
Furthermore, we conducted a first-order two-region spectral
analysis (no PSF-mixing modeling), namely, the inner 4 × 4 pix-
els and outer 18 pixels, to investigate for any hint of spatial de-
pendence in the velocity measurements. We found that the tur-
bulent velocities of the inner and outer regions are consistent
with each other within their 1σ statistical uncertainties, and their
bulk velocities agree well within their 1.5σ uncertainties (see
Fig A.1). These results suggest little to no velocity gradient or
enhanced turbulence near the central AGN. Since the LLS of
the central component is smaller than leff (assuming it lies in the
plane of the sky), in addition to it being a relatively weak cen-
tral AGN, turbulence driven by the current AGN activity may be
diluted in the observed LOS emissivity-weighted Resolve spec-
trum, hence, might further explain the relatively low turbulent
velocity.
4.3. Multiphase structures in the core
In the scenario of a top-down multiphase condensation cascade,
warm and cold filaments and clouds form as the hot plasma con-
denses. A fraction of this condensed gas is accreted onto the cen-
tral supermassive black hole (SMBH), triggering self-regulating
AGN feedback (cold chaotic accretion (CCA); Gaspari et al.
2013). The condensed structures may retain some properties of
the hot plasma (Gaspari et al. 2018), for example, according to
their model, there is a tight correlation in the LOS velocity dis-
persion between hot and warm gas (see Fig. 1 of Gaspari et al.
2018, their best-fit correlation is also shown as the solid black
line with the gray shaded area in Fig. 7), as well as the positive
linear correlation in their metallicity (see Fig. 3 of Olivares et al.
2026). A strong correlation between the morphology of warm
and hot gases has also been reported by McDonald et al. (2010)
in the sample study of Hα filaments in CC clusters. Olivares et al.
(2025) further found a tight positive correlation between the X-
ray and the Hα surface brightness of the filaments in the cores of
seven CC clusters.
To investigate the multiphase structures in the core of A496,
we compare our results with the warm gas structure studied
in Ubertosi et al. (2024). These authors utilized optical ob-
servations of A496 with the MUSE integral-field spectrograph
mounted on the Very Large Telescope to derive the Hα-line in-
tensity and kinematics in the cluster core. The map reveals an
extended warm (104 K) gas nebula surrounding the BCG. The
structure is composed of a bright central region, a ∼10 kpc fil-
ament extending toward the east, and a slightly longer, fainter
filament toward the northeast. The bright region of the warm

5. Summary and conclusions
Gas motions provide insight into the dynamical history and
physical processes within galaxy clusters. Large-scale motions
are typically driven by structure formation, such as mergers and
accretion, while small-scale motions are governed by processes,
such as AGN feedback, gas sloshing, and instabilities. In this
work, we probed the dynamics and kinematics of the ICM in
the core of A496 using XRISM/Resolve data. We discussed the
ICM kinematics and compared it with other Resolve cluster core
measurements. We also compared our results with simulations
and multiwavelength observations. Our findings are,
– Optical redshift analysis confirmed that the BCG (zBCG =
0.03281±0.00004; Wegner et al. 1999) is at rest with respect
to the cluster systemic velocity (⟨z⟩= 0.0329 ± 0.0003).
– Despite multiple previously detected sloshing-induced fea-
tures, such as cold fronts and an enhanced spiral pattern, and
the presence of a (weak) central radio source, Resolve ob-
servation reveals that the core of A496 is dynamically qui-
escent with a LOS bulk velocity of vbulk = −69+25
−20 km s−1
with respect to the BCG and a turbulent velocity of σv =
78+18
−16 km s−1. The σv value is one of the lowest measured in
the core of any cluster by the instrument considered in this
work. We note that a more quantitative comparison should
account for the physical scale sampled by Resolve. Assum-
ing isotropic turbulence, this value corresponds to a subsonic
3D Mach number of 0.15+0.04
−0.03 and a non-thermal pressure
fraction of 1.2+0.6
−0.5%.
– Previous studies showed that A496’s Hα filament in the core
is spatially correlated with an ICM surface brightness en-
hancement. In this work, we show that the ICM 1D velocity
dispersion is in good agreement with the velocity dispersion
of the Hα filament in the core. Moreover, by adding literature
values of five clusters, we establish a correlation between
the central warm and hot gas velocity dispersions, consis-
tent with
σv,hot
σv,warm = 1. This may support the notion that at least
some of this warm gas originates from the condensation of
the ICM in the wake of radio bubbles.
– We estimated the mechanical AGN feedback from the re-
cent activity of the central radio source and found a level
of contribution of 7 −9% to ICM heating. Since the largest
linear size of the central radio component is smaller than
the approximated effective length within the FoV, turbulence
driven by the current AGN activity may be diluted in the ob-
served LOS emissivity-weighted spectrum, thereby explain-
ing the relatively low turbulent velocity.
– We compared our velocity measurements with the Simulat-
ing the LOcal Web (SLOW) constrained Universe simula-
tion. The 1D LOS bulk velocity from SLOW is consistent
with the measured value, suggesting that AGN feedback has
a negligible contribution. The A496 SLOW turbulent veloc-
ity, as in other reported Resolve−simulation comparisons, is
higher, but remains within 1.5σ uncertainty.
