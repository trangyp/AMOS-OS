THE ISOMORPHIC SECTION-PROJECTION PROBLEM FOR CONVEX BODIES
JOHANNES HOSLE
07/12/2026
Abstract. Let K, L be convex bodies in Rn with K centered. Assume that |K ∩θ⊥| ≤|L|θ⊥| for all
θ ∈Sn−1. We prove that |K| ≤c√n|L|, which is sharp up to the choice of the absolute constant. The
result gives the sharp isomorphic order in a mixed section-projection comparison problem, complementing
the isomorphic Busemann-Petty and Shephard problems. It also removes the John’s position assumption
from an earlier result of the author, up to an absolute constant factor.
1. Introduction
The subject of comparing volumes of convex sets via comparisons of lower dimensional measurements
is a central theme in convex geometry. The Busemann-Petty problem, posed in [BP56], is the following
question:
If K and L are origin-symmetric convex bodies in Rn such that
|K ∩θ⊥| ≤|L ∩θ⊥|
for all θ ∈Sn−1, does it follow that |K| ≤|L|?
The answer is affirmative in dimensions n ≤4 and negative in dimensions 5 and higher. The disproof
for n ≥5 was carried out by Papadimitrakis [Pap92], Gardner [Gar94a], and Zhang [Zha94], while the
affirmative answers for n = 3, 4 were given by Gardner [Gar94b] and Zhang [Zha99] respectively. Note that
the case n = 2 is immediate, since the assumptions imply K ⊂L. A uniform solution for all dimensions
using the method of Fourier transforms on the sphere was accomplished by Gardner, Koldobsky, and
Schlumprecht [GKS99].
The Shephard problem, posed in [She64], asks the analogous question about projections onto hyper-
planes, rather than hyperplane sections:
If K and L are origin-symmetric convex bodies in Rn such that
|K|θ⊥| ≤|L|θ⊥|
for all θ ∈Sn−1, does it follow that |K| ≤|L|?

As in the case of the Busemann-Petty problem, the answer to this question is negative in general. The
claim only holds in dimensions n = 2, as was demonstrated by Schneider [Sch67] and Petty [Pet67].
A variant of the original Busemann-Petty and Shephard problems asks if, under the same hypotheses,
one can conclude |K| ≤C(n)|L| for a dimensional constant C(n), and it asks for the optimal order
of magnitude of C(n).
These problems are known as the isomorphic Busemann-Petty problem and
isomorphic Shephard problem respectively. For the first question, Milman and Pajor [MP89] gave the
estimate |K| ≤cLK|L|, where LK is the isotropic constant of K and c > 0 is an absolute constant.
The question of whether LK is uniformly bounded for convex bodies K in any dimension was known as
the Bourgain slicing problem, which has now been answered affirmatively by Klartag and Lehec [KL25].
As an immediate corollary, it follows that |K| ≤c|L| under the assumptions of the Busemann-Petty
problem. For the isomorphic Shephard problem, Ball [Bal91a] gave the bound |K| ≤c√n|L|. Moreover,
by considering random polytopes, Ball showed that his bound was sharp, that is, for all n, there exist
K, L origin-symmetric convex bodies in Rn with |K|θ⊥| ≤|L|θ⊥| for all θ ∈Sn−1 such that |K| ≥c√n|L|,
for some absolute constant c > 0.
We will now discuss two natural variants of the (isomorphic) Busemann-Petty and Shephard problems.
Both use mixed section and projection hypotheses. Giannopoulos and Koldobsky [GK17] proved that
if K, L are origin-symmetric convex bodies in Rn where now |K|θ⊥| ≤|L ∩θ⊥| for all θ ∈Sn−1, then
|K| ≤|L|. Hosle [Hos21] considered the reverse question. He showed that if K, L are origin-symmetric
convex bodies in Rn with |K ∩θ⊥| ≤|L|θ⊥| for all θ ∈Sn−1, and K has outradius R and L has inradius
r, then |K| ≤R
r |L|. In particular, if K, L are in John’s position, this yields the bound |K| ≤√n|L|. In
this note, we prove that, even without the assumption of being in John’s position, we obtain a bound
with this same √n order of magnitude.
Theorem 1. Let K, L be convex bodies in Rn, with K centered, such that
|K ∩θ⊥| ≤|L|θ⊥|
for all θ ∈Sn−1. Then,
|K| ≤c√n|L|
for an absolute constant c > 0.
