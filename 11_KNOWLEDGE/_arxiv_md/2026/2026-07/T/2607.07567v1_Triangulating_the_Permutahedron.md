TRIANGULATING THE PERMUTAHEDRON
THOMAS BRADY, EMANUELE DELUCCHI, AND COLUM WATT
Abstract. For a finite irreducible real reflection group, W, we triangu-
late its permutahedron and use this to give an explicit homotopy equiv-
alence between two known classifying spaces for the associated Artin
group, B(W). In the process, we characterise the Bruhat intervals from
w1 to w2 in W, where w−1
1 w2 is a Coxeter element.
1. Introduction
For π an Artin group of finite type with reflection group W, there are two
well-studied K(π, 1)’s. The Salvetti K(π, 1), S(W), is obtained by making
identifications on the corresponding W-permutahedron ([16]) and derives
from a general construction ([15]) of a model for the homotopy type of the
complement of a general complexified real hyperplane arrangement. On the
other hand, the non-crossing partition K(π, 1), N(W), is obtained by mak-
ing identifications on the order complex of the corresponding non-crossing
partition lattice for W ([1], [5]) and is a special case of a classifying space
construction ([2]) for the braid group of a general finite complex reflection
group. The interplay between analogues of these two K(π, 1)’s has led to
recent progress in the K(π, 1) conjecture for Artin groups of non-finite type
[14, 10].
Homotopy theory ensures the existence of a homotopy equivalence from the
Salvetti K(π, 1), S(W), to the non-crossing partition K(π, 1), N(W). In
fact, the proof of Proposition 1B.9 of [11] shows that such an equivalence
can be constructed recursively on skeleta. This construction begins with
an inclusion of the 1-skeleton of S(W) into the 1-skeleton of N(W), since
the standard generators of the Artin group of finite type are a subset of the
non-crossing partition generators, and concludes with an expression for the
image of the single top-dimensional cell of S(W) as a combination of top-
dimensional cells of N(W). In the universal covers, this gives a triangulation
of the permutahedron. We describe such a triangulation in the case of an
irreducible group, W, and show that it induces a homotopy equivalence
from S(W) to N(W). Our triangulation uses a new characterisation of the
Bruhat intervals from w1 to w2, where w−1
1 w2 is a Coxeter element.

2.3. Geometry of polytopes. A convex polytope P is the convex hull of
a finite set of points in Rn. An (affine) hyperplane H is bounding for P if H
meets P and P is contained in one of the closed halfspaces determined by H.
A face of P is any intersection of P with a bounding hyperplane. A vertex of
P is any face of dimension 0 and a facet is any face whose dimension is one
less than the dimension of P. Each facet of an n-dimensional polytope, P,
determines a unique hyperplane which contains it and is called the support
of the facet. See [18] for background on the theory of polytopes.

Recall that a geometric p-simplex in Rn is the convex hull of an affinely

geometric simplices which contains every face of each of its elements and
such that the intersection of any two of its elements is a face of both. The
vertices of a geometric simplicial complex are its 0-simplices.
Definition 2.5. A triangulation of a convex polytope, P, is a geometric
simplicial complex whose vertex set is contained in the vertex set of P and
whose union of simplices is equal to P.

7. Triangulation criteria
In this section we prove that the set T consisting of all special n-simplices
and their faces is a triangulation of the permutahedron, P(W), and we
prove that this triangulation induces a homotopy equivalence from S(W) to
N(W). The proof of the former result consists of checking that T satisfies
two conditions (from [8]) which, in our context, are defined as follows.
Definition 7.1. The set T has the intersection cocircuit property if
and only if (i) whenever F is an (n−1)-simplex in T which is not contained
in a facet of P(W), then there are exactly two n-simplices in T which contain
F and (ii) the two additional vertices from these n-simplices lie on opposite
sides of the supporting hyperplane of F.
Definition 7.2. The set T has the general facet property if and only if
(i) P(W) has a facet Q which is triangulated by those simplices of T which
are contained in Q and (ii) no (n−1)-simplex of this triangulation is a facet
of two distinct n-simplices of T .
Remark 7.3. If the set T satisfies the intersection cocircuit property and
the general facet property, then it is a triangulation of P(W), by Corollaries
4.5.19 and 4.5.20 of [8].
