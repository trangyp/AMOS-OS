structure

Different diagnostics of black hole thermodynamic branch structure do not always identify the
same local event. We examine this issue in charged AdS black hole examples where a thermodynamic
topological phase transition is known to occur. In these examples, previously reported in [J. High
Energy Phys. 06 (2024) 213], the inverse temperature τa at which the total topological number
changes is separated from the inverse temperature τb at which the fixed charge parameter branch
response considered here becomes singular.
Reanalyzing the same zero point branch geometry,
we find that the two diagnostics track different local structures. The change at τa is a boundary
membership and index flow event of the positive radius physical branch set: a zero point with
nonzero winding enters or leaves the counted domain. The singularity at τb is instead caused by a
turning point at finite radius, where a pair of branches annihilates. Since the two branches carry
opposite windings, this event changes the branch count but preserves the total topological number.
Thus, in the analyzed examples, the thermodynamic topological phase transition and the branch
response singularity are distinct diagnostics of the same zero point branch geometry, with different
local origins.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Black
hole
phase
structure
is
usually
diagnosed
through ordinary thermodynamic quantities. Free ener-
gies, heat capacities, response functions, spinodal curves,
and coexistence lines describe how branches appear, dis-
appear, exchange stability, or dominate an ensemble.
These tools underlie the standard analysis of Hawking-
Page phase transitions, Van der Waals-like criticality,
reentrant phase transitions, and related AdS black hole
phase phenomena [1–17].
A practical question is how
much of the same branch structure is seen by other diag-
nostics, and what those diagnostics mean when they do
not identify the same local event.
Thermodynamic topology gives one such diagnostic.
In this approach, black hole branches are represented by
zero points of a thermodynamic vector field. Each zero
point carries a winding number, and the total topolog-
ical number W summarizes the topology of the phys-
ical branch structure [18, 19].
This construction has
been used to classify a wide range of black hole ther-
modynamic systems, including vortex/anti-vortex pair
creation, Gauss-Bonnet and Born-Infeld systems, critical
points, Hawking-Page phase transitions, rotating black
holes, AdS black holes, accelerating black holes, NUT-
charged spacetimes, BTZ black holes, ultraspinning black
holes, and charged black holes in gauged supergravities
[20–40].
These studies show that topological data can
encode branch number, winding ordering, stability, lim-
iting behavior, and refined thermodynamic topological
subclasses [41–44].

The interpretation becomes sharper when the topolog-
ical number itself changes with temperature. Then the
issue is not only how to classify a fixed branch structure,
but what physical rearrangement the change of W is de-
tecting. If the black hole charge parameters, pressure,
and other external parameters are held fixed while W
changes, one should ask whether the topological change
is the same local event as an ordinary thermodynamic re-
sponse singularity, or whether it probes a different part
of the branch geometry.
The examples reported in Ref. [45] provide a concrete
setting for this question.
In the four-dimensional
Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton-Axion
(EMDA),
four-
dimensional Horowitz-Sen (HS), and five-dimensional
Kaluza-Klein (K-K) examples, the charge parameters,
pressure, and other external parameters are fixed, while
the auxiliary inverse temperature τ is scanned.
In
each case the total topological number takes the values
W = 0 and W = 1 in different inverse temperature
intervals. Along increasing τ, the phase transition at τa
is W : +1 →0; along increasing temperature T = 1/τ,
the direction is reversed. The same zero point curve also
contains another special inverse temperature τb, where
a turning point at finite radius appears and the fixed
charge parameter branch response used below develops
a turning point singularity.
This separation raises the question addressed in this
paper: why does W change at τa without a divergence
in the branch response at fixed charge parameters, while
the same response diverges at τb without changing W?
This is a diagnostic question about what the two signals
measure in the local branch geometry.
The existence
of the temperature dependent thermodynamic topolog-
ical phase transition, the relevant black hole families,
and the representative transition points were identified in

Ref. [45]. Here we reexamine the same zero point curves

We show that the answer is encoded in two local in-

physical branch set Bphys(τ), whose elements have pos-

the analyzed examples, τa is a physical branch member-

event at finite radius that also induces a net zero opposite
winding pair annihilation.
This gives a mechanism level interpretation of what
the topological diagnostic is measuring when it separates
from an ordinary response singularity. In the examples
studied here, the separation is controlled by the local
branch geometry: the change of W is governed by phys-
ical branch membership and index flow, while the re-
sponse singularity is governed by a finite radius turning
point of τ(rh). The discussion below keeps the focus on
this diagnostic separation and its local branch geometry
origin.

III.
WHAT CHANGES AT τa?
We now address the first half of the puzzle: what local
object changes at the W-changing point τa? A change of
W could be mistaken for a local singularity of an exist-
ing thermodynamic branch. The zero point curves show
a different possibility in the analyzed examples. Crossing
τa changes which zero points with positive radius carry-
ing winding are counted in the physical branch set. Since
the level set τ(rh) = τ can meet the boundary rh = 0,
while only roots with rh > 0 are counted as physical
black hole branches, the relevant object for topological
accounting is the intersection of the zero point curve with
the positive radius physical domain. For a nondegenerate
value of τ, we define
Bphys(τ) = { (Bα, rα(τ), wα) | rα(τ) > 0, τ(rα) = τ }.
(20)
Bphys(τ) is the set of labeled zero points, or local branch
germs, with positive radius together with their wind-
ing numbers. The label Bα records the local zero point
branch germ to which the root belongs; in the examples
below these labels are Bin and Bout. At τa and τb them-
selves, where the level set touches the boundary or be-
comes degenerate, Bphys is understood through one sided
limits in τ. Here “physical” means that the zero point
has positive horizon radius and is counted in the thermo-
dynamic topological construction. It does not mean local
thermodynamic stability, global free energy dominance,
or membership in a particular thermodynamic ensemble.

We use the following local criterion.
If, as τ passes
through an isolated value τ∗, a single branch germ with

leaves the open domain, the change is −w. By contrast,
an interior pair creation or annihilation at finite positive
radius preserves W whenever the pair carries opposite
windings and no additional zero point is involved. This is
a local statement about the counted physical domain, not
a global degree theorem on a compactified state space.

VII.
CONCLUSIONS
We have clarified why two diagnostics of black hole
thermodynamic branch structure, the total topological
number W and the branch response Cpar, identify dif-
ferent inverse temperatures in the examples of Ref. [45].
The separation follows from the local geometry of the
same zero point curve. The point τa, where W changes,
is a physical branch membership and index flow event:
along increasing τ, the branch Bin carrying winding num-
ber −1 enters Bphys(τ), so W : +1 →0. The correspond-
ing positive radius point is regular, with τ ′(ra) ̸= 0, and
the branch response Cpar remains finite. The response
singularity instead occurs at τb, where the zero point
curve has a turning point at finite radius, τ ′(rb) = 0.
The annihilating pair carries opposite windings, so this
event produces the divergence of Cpar while preserving
W.
The result provides a local mechanism for distinguish-
ing topological changes of the counted physical branch
structure from spinodal type branch response singulari-
ties. In this mechanism, changes of W are governed by
physical branch membership and index flow, whereas the
response singularity is governed by a finite radius differ-
ential degeneracy of τ(rh). For the branch response Cpar
studied here, with qi, P, and r0 held fixed, the change
in thermodynamic topological number is therefore not a
relabeling of the finite radius response singularity, but a
distinct local event of the same branch geometry.
