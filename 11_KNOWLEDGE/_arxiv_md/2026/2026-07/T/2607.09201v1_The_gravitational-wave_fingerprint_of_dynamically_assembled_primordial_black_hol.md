The gravitational-wave fingerprint of dynamically assembled
primordial black hole cluster seeds in JWST’s Little Red Dots
Juan Garc´ıa-Bellido1
1Instituto de F´ısica Te´orica UAM-CSIC, Universidad Aut´onoma de Madrid, Cantoblanco, 28049 Madrid, Spain
(Dated: July 13, 2026)
The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has revealed compact, red, overmassive accreting black
holes—the so-called “Little Red Dots” (LRDs)—in chemically near-pristine hosts at z ≃5 −9,
straining standard heavy-seed models. We show that a population of strongly clustered primordial
black holes (PBH) with a broad mass function predicted by a QCD-epoch thermal history naturally
realizes the configuration that assembles LRD-scale seeds: an intermediate-mass PBH nucleus M• ∼
103–105 M⊙surrounded, within a few parsecs, by a swarm of light (m ∼30 M⊙) PBHs embedded
in dense baryonic gas. Gas dynamical friction keeps the loss cone full and lets the core contract, so
the swarm sinks and is swallowed on tseed ∼10–50 Myr, well inside the cosmic time at z ∼10–15.
Because a heavy nucleus is present ab initio, the captures occur at extreme mass ratio q ∼10−4–10−2:
the remnant is retained against gravitational recoil, and each capture reaches the innermost stable
orbit under gravitational-wave (GW) emission, radiating ≃0.06 mc2 so that the assembly efficiency
is ζ ≃0.06 independent of M•.
The superposed swarm inspirals form a stochastic background
ΩGWh2 ∼10−13–10−11 with a ΩGW ∝f 2/3 shape truncated below the gas-decoupling frequency
and topped by a ringdown “comb” at fring(M•) ≃13 mHz for 105 M⊙and ≃1.3 Hz for 103 M⊙
nuclei at zf ≃12. The few comparable-mass nucleus–nucleus coalescences are instead individually
resolvable LISA/deci-Hz sources. Detection, and discrimination of these signatures from a directly
formed PBH seed of the same mass, would identify the LRDs as PBH-nucleus seeded black holes.
Introduction.—JWST has uncovered an abundant pop-
ulation of faint, broad-line active galactic nuclei at the
redshift frontier whose compact morphology and V-
shaped continuum define the LRDs [1–4].
They host
black holes (BHs) of MBH
∼106–108 M⊙that are
strongly overmassive relative to their stellar hosts [5, 6],
though the broad-line masses are actively debated and
some may be overestimated [3]. The cleanest case is the
strongly lensed A2744-QSO1 at z = 7.04: a dynamical
measurement reveals Keplerian rotation about a point
mass MBH ≃5 × 107 M⊙with MBH/M⋆> 2 [7], in gas
that is extremely metal poor, Z ≃4.7 × 10−3 Z⊙[8]. We
take A2744-QSO1 as the motivating example rather than
as representative of the class.
These two facts are jointly awkward for the leading
heavy-seed channel. Direct-collapse black holes (DCBHs)
form in atomic-cooling halos only where a strong Lyman–
Werner flux (J ≳Jcrit) suppresses H2 cooling [6, 9, 10],
which requires a star-forming neighbor and is rare; the
early growth is capped by the halo baryon fraction,
MBH/Mdyn ≲0.1 [11], and the accompanying star forma-
tion enriches the gas. A near-pristine, isolated, strongly
overmassive BH instead points to a seed assembled grav-
itationally [8].
Primordial black holes [12–14] are a natural candi-
date: they are expected to be strongly clustered at for-
mation [15, 16], and a broad mass function can consti-
tute all of the dark matter (DM) [17].
Crucially, the
mechanisms that make PBHs also make them broadly
distributed [18]: the QCD thermal history imprints a
peak near the solar mass with a high-mass tail [19], and
quantum diffusion during inflation generates exponen-
tially heavy, non-Gaussian tails and enhanced cluster-
ing [20–22]. A dense PBH cluster therefore generically
contains a few heavy members: an intermediate-mass
PBH (IMBH) nucleus (M• ∼103–105 M⊙), bathed in
a swarm of light (m ∼30 M⊙) PBHs. Earlier work con-
sidered PBHs as SMBH seeds either statistically [23–25]
or by direct formation of ∼105 M⊙PBHs with a narrow
mass function [26]; here the seed is dynamically assem-
bled, and we show that this assembly is what makes it
loud in GWs.
Embedded in a halo of dense gas, we compute how
fast does the swarm is able to build the nucleus into
an LRD-scale seed, and what GW signal (background
plus resolvable events) does the assembly leave. We find
prompt seeding and a distinctive LISA-band signature
(see Fig. 1), and that the nucleus-plus-swarm configura-
tion is free of the recoil, core-collapse-stall and energy-
budget difficulties of a runaway of equal-mass PBHs.

Retention against recoil.—Because a heavy nucleus
feeds on light PBHs, growth proceeds at extreme mass
ratio q = m/M• ≲10−2. The GW recoil of the rem-
nant scales as vkick ≃A η2 at small symmetric mass
ratio η ≃q, with A ≃1.2 × 104 km/s [34].
For the
M• = 105 M⊙nucleus (q = 3 × 10−4) this is a negligi-
ble vkick ∼10−3 km/s, far below vesc = (2GM•/R)1/2 ≃
29 km/s at R = 1 pc, so retention is automatic. For the
M• = 103 M⊙nucleus (q = 3 × 10−2) the kick is larger,
∼10 km/s, comparable to the bare-core vesc ≃3 km/s
but below the escape speed of the host atomic-cooling
halo (∼30–50 km/s); such sub-escape kicks are damped
and the nucleus re-sinks by gas dynamical friction within
a crossing time, so it is retained provided the core sits
in a dense halo.
This is in sharp contrast to equal-
mass assembly, where kicks of 50–175 km/s (and up to
∼103 km/s for spinning superkicks) eject the products
and stall the runaway—the classic obstruction to hier-
archical IMBH growth [35–37].
The only events that
kick significantly are the rare comparable-mass nucleus–
nucleus coalescences; these are precisely the individu-
ally resolvable sources discussed below, so their ejection
would remove a resolvable event, not the background.

Crucially, the loudest events are not stochastic. The
comparable-mass nucleus–nucleus (103–105 M⊙) coales-
cences at z ≃12 are gold-plated LISA/deci-Hz sources
with SNR ∼102–103 [44, 45], individually resolvable and
removable; heavy IMRIs from the high-mass tail of the
swarm are likewise resolvable. With nseedN ∼O(1) ring-
downs per Mpc3 over ∼108 yr, the observed burst rate
is ∼104–105 yr−1 with ∼103 s redshifted durations—a
duty cycle ∼0.1–1, confirming that the correct detec-
tion statistic is a burst/non-Gaussian search rather than
the Gaussian cross-correlation behind the SNR=1 power-
law-integrated (PI) curve. We therefore present the re-
solvable events as a sharper, more falsifiable prediction
than the stochastic slope: a few to tens per year of re-
solvable, eccentric, light-seed coalescences at z ∼10–15.

Conclusions.—A clustered, broad-mass-function PBH
population, as predicted by the QCD thermal history and
by quantum diffusion during inflation, naturally forms
IMBH nuclei surrounded by light-PBH swarms in very
dense halos very early on (z ≫10). Gas keeps the loss
cone full and contracts the core, so the swarm assem-
bles a 103–105 M⊙seed on a time scale t ∼1–50 Myr,
Eq. (1), fast enough to explain the supermassive, chem-
ically pristine LRDs without a Lyman–Werner neighbor
or super-Eddington growth.
The extreme mass ratio
guarantees both retention against recoil and GW-driven
captures with ζ ≃0.06. The assembly can be detected
as a non-Gaussian, f 2/3-truncated stochastic background
with a ringdown comb at fring(M•) ∼13 mHz and ∼
1.3 Hz for 105 and 103 M⊙respectively, with an ampli-
tude ΩGWh2 ∼10−13–10−11, plus resolvable, eccentric
nucleus and IMRI coalescences that carry the falsifiable
weight and discriminate assembled from directly-formed
PBH seeds. Fixing κ, ζ and the retention budget calls
for dedicated N-body-plus-hydrodynamics simulations of
the gas-embedded, loss-cone-fed nucleus.
