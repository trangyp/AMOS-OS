Toric decomposition in algebraic groups
D´avid R. Szab´o∗
7th July 2026
Abstract
Over an arbitrary field F, we construct n+1 maximal tori T1, . . . , Tn+1 in PGLn(F) so
that the product T1 . . . Tn+1 is almost the whole PGLn(F) and every g ∈T1 . . . Tn+1 can
be expressed uniquely as g = t1 . . . tn+1 where ti ∈Ti. The construction is optimal, as the
number of tori with this property attains a general upper bound for connected reductive
groups over an algebraically closed field, as well as over finite fields.
We also show that n+2 suitably chosen maximal tori T1, . . . , Tn+2 are enough to cover
the whole group, i.e. PGLn(F) = T1 . . . Tn+2, provided |F| > n2. This is optimal over a
finite field and is conjecturally optimal over algebraically closed fields, i.e. the number of
such tori is as small as possible.

Definition 1.1. Let G be a group, A1, . . . , Ar be subsets of G, and consider the multiplication

translates of the torus Tr along r −1 different directions. On the other hand, if T1, . . . , Tr
induce a toric covering, then G = T1 . . . , Tr, explaining the terminology.

1.2
Motivation
Product decomposition in algebraic groups is intensively studied. We list some of them without
aiming to be comprehensive.
For a connected, reductive algebraic group G, the Bruhat decomposition is G = BWB
where B is a Borel subgroup and W the corresponding Weyl group. The big Bruhat cell BwB
is a decomposition of an open subset of G in the form of a double coset, but it gives a dense
decomposition of the form BwB, the product of two Borel subgroups. In this fashion, Brundan
investigated dense decomposition of the form XgX for proper reductive subgroups X is an
irreducible reductive group G [Bru95]. Liebeck, Saxl and Seitz determined all factorisations of
a simple algebraic group G of the form G = XY of a product of two maximal closed subgroups
X and Y [LSS96]. Guralnick, Malle and Tiep investigated the product of conjugacy classes,
and the product of centralisers in finite and simple algebraic groups [GMT13].
There are various variants of the covering number. The covering number cn(G) of a group G
is the minimal r such that Cr = G for every nontrivial conjugacy class C of G. Lev showed that
cn(PSLn(F)) = n for finite and infnite fields F [Lev96]. The covering number cn(G, C) of a non-
central conjugacy class C of a simple algebraic group G (over an algebraically closed field) is the
minimal r such that G = Cr. Liebeck and Simion showed that cn(G, C) ≤120dim(G)
dim(C) Liebeck
and Simion. Gordeev and Saxl defined the extended covering number ecn(G) (to be the smallest
integer r such that the product C1C2 . . . Cr = G whenever C1, . . . , Cr are conjugacy classes of
G not contained in any proper normal subgroup of G) and showed that ecn(G) ≤C rank(G)
for Chevallley groups G [GS02].
For finite groups, decomposition into various subgroups was studied. Guralnick and Malle
showed that every finite simple group is a product of 3 conjugacy classes [GM12]. Liebeck and
Pyber showed that many finite subgroups of GL(n, Fpr) generated by p-elements are actually
the product of 25 of its Sylow p-subgroups [LP01]. Garonzi, Levy, Mar´oti and Simion improved
this to 4 Sylow p-subgroups [GLMS17]. They also studied conjugate product factorisation of a
group G using a subgroup A (i.e. G = Ag1 . . . Agk for some gi ∈G) when a is solvable/nilpotent
and gave upper bounds to its length k [GLMS17]. Vavilov, Smolensky and Sury considered this
problem using conjugates of unitriangular subgroups of Chevalley groups over commutative
rings of stable rank 1 [VSS12]. Smolensky extended this to some twisted Chevalley groups
over finite fields or the field of complex numbers [Smo13]. Note the toric covering number
(Definition 1.2) is a special case of this conjugate product factorisarion problem as maximal
tori are known to be conjugate. Nikolov considered the decomposition of finite quasisimple
group of classical type into a product of boundedly many conjugates of central quotients of
SL [Nik07]. If the multiplication map is bijective, then the product is called a tiling. The
(non)existence of tiling in finite groups was studied by many authors e.g.
[RT66], [FX26],
[KMMS24].
1.3
Outline
The paper is organised as follows. In §2, we fix some notations and recall useful tools. In
§3, we discuss general bounds for irreducible reductive groups and prove Theorem 1.4 with
dimensional reasoning.
The main part is §4 where we study PGL(V ) via its regular action on the projective frames
in P(V ). In §4.1, considering carefully chosen points, we realise optimal-size toric packings that
actually cover an open subset in PGL(V ). Then in §4.2, we use the Combinatorial Nullstel-
lensatz together with various computations to show that the remaining closed set can actually
be reached with the addition of a single maximal torus to complete the proof of Theorem 1.6.
We prove Corollary 1.7 about generic maximal tori in §4.3 using a standard argument.
We conclude the paper with some questions, open problems and conjectures in §5.

Linear algebraic groups
Let F be an algebraically closed field. Let Gm(F) be the multi-
plicative algebraic group that is isomorphic to F× as groups. A torus in G is a closed subgroup
isomorphic to Gm(F)s for some s ∈N. A maximal torus is a torus maximal with respect to
containment. An algebraic group G is reductive, if the largest connected, normal, unipotent
subgroup is trivial.
We will use the following facts from [MT11, §6, §8, §14]. In an irreducible algebraic group
G, every maximal tori are conjugate, and rank(G) is defined to be their common dimension.

4
Decompositions in PGL(V )
In this section, we prove Theorem 1.6, the main result of this paper. We do so in two
steps. First, in §4.1, we consider an explicit construction for toric packings to cover an
open subset of PGL(V ). Then in §4.2, we modify this construction to cover the full group
using a single maximal torus. Finally, in §4.3, we prove Corollary 1.7.
The main idea is to study PGL(V ) via its regular action on the projective frames in
P(V ), using which maximal tori can be characterised as pointwise stabilisers of dim(V )
projectively independent points. Most constructions work over arbitrary fields.
In this section, the field F is not assumed to be algebraically closed unless it is explicitly
stated. We start by fixing the framework of this section.

4.2
Toric coverings in PGL(V )
We show that the packing construction of §4.1 actually defined an open subset of PGL(V ).
While using twice as many maximal tori, it is easy to reach the remaining closed set, we
can do much better. The idea is to move an arbitrary projective frame Q into a fixed one
P from §4.1 using the action of some maximal tori.
We prove that for a single carefully chosen maximal torus T, no orbit in P(V ) can be
fully contained in the closed set from above. This means that even if we start from the
bad closed set, we can ‘kick Q off’ to the good open set by an appropriate element of T.
For the technical details, we us the Cauchy–Binet formula for determinants to show that
none of the dim(V )2 polynomials defining the bad closed sets are contant 0 provided T is
chosen suitably. Finally, we use the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz to show that provided
the field is large enough, we can avoid all such bad sets simultaneously.
We prove Theorem 1.6 alongside some concrete estimates in the case of finite fields.
