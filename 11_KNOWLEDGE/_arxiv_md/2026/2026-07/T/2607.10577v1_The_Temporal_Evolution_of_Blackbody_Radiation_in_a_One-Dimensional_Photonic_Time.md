The Temporal Evolution of Blackbody Radiation in
a One-Dimensional Photonic Time-Crystal
Luis Cortes-Herrera, Naren Ganesh, Jack Hasty, and Yuzhe Xiao
Abstract—Perhaps one of the most intriguing phenomena
in time-varying-media photonics is amplification of light in a
photonic time-crystal (PTC). However, studies have focused only
in PTC-based amplification of coherent light so far. In this work,
we examine theoretically the PTC-based amplification of thermal
radiation, specifically blackbody radiation. Such amplification
of thermal radiation is fundamentally intriguing because of
its inherently stochastic nature, and technologically relevant
because of its ubiquity. For simplicity, and for the experimental
relevance of transmission lines, we consider the case of a one-
dimensional medium. To analyze the PTC-based amplification
of blackbody radiation, we examine the electromagnetic fields’
spatial correlations and spatial spectra. We show that the initially
blackbody radiation converges periodically to Gaussian spatial
correlations and spectra, with gradually increasing amplitudes,
coherence lengths, and both spatial- and wavenumber-domain
purities. We demonstrate that these asymptotics are governed by
the PTC’s momentum band-structure and can be understood
via a rotating-wave approximation for the pseudo-Hermitian
dynamics of an electromagnetic field in a PTC.
Index Terms—thermal radiation, blackbody radiation, coher-
ence theory, time-varying media, photonic time-crystal
I. INTRODUCTION
Driven by advances in ultra-fast modulation of photonic
media and the growing demand to engineer optical sig-
nals, time-varying photonics [1], [2] has emerged as a
paradigm for surpassing the limits of static photonic systems.
Under this paradigm, researchers have demonstrated novel
electromagnetic-wave phenomena such as frequency-shifting
[3]–[6], optical nonreciprocity [7], optical buffering [5], [8]–
[10], negative refraction [11], synthetic light drag [12], and
amplification via photon-pair generation in photonic time crys-
tals (PTCs) and via cascaded photon up-conversion [13]–[16].
Time-varying-media photonics have also been investigated as
an approach to manipulate thermal radiation beyond its usual
limits in static media [17], [18], such as breaking Lorentz
reciprocity to bypass Kirchhoff’s law [18]–[20], manipulating
radiative heat flow [21], [22], overcoming the blackbody
limit and amplifying quantum vacuum fluctuations [23], and
shaping the radiation’s spatial coherence [24].
One of the most fascinating photonic phenomena in time-
varying media is the amplified emission and lasing in PTCs
[13], [14]. This is the phenomenon that occurs when a co-
herent radiation source is embedded in a PTC, and conse-
quently, its radiation within the PTC’s momentum bandgap

is exponentially amplified in time. While this phenomenon
is fundamentally intriguing, it has practical limitations. First,
the amplified wavelength must fall within the bandwidth of
the emitter, making the PTC source inherently non-tunable.
Second, the analysis in Ref. [13] assumes lossless media and
no background thermal radiation, conditions which are hardly
met in practice. For instance, transparent conducting oxides
in their epsilon-near-zero regime are promising candidates to
implement PTCs [1], [13], but they possess non-negligible
material loss. Such lossy PTCs must emit non-vanishing
thermal radiation. Moreover, even for PTCs with negligible
material losses, the environment’s background thermal radia-
tion is ubiquitous. Thus, thermal radiation from these sources
within the PTC’s bandgap is generally present and ampli-
fied concurrently. Besides its aforementioned technological
relevance, PTC-based amplification of thermal radiation is
fundamentally intriguing because it is intrinsically stochastic
and conventionally broadband [25]. Hence, the analysis of
amplification of thermal radiation in PTCs requires specialized
analysis, beyond that of coherent optical radiation.
Thus, in this paper, we examine PTC-driven amplification
of thermal radiation, specifically, blackbody radiation [25].
For simplicity and for its technological relevance [14], we
consider the simplest non-trivial case of a one-dimensional,
spatially homogeneous system. We study the time-evolution
of the field’s spatial spectra or, equivalently, its spatial cross-
and auto-correlations [25]–[27] As expected, we show that the
initially thermal radiation progressively increases its energy
and spatial coherence. Furthermore, we show that the spatial
spectrum converges periodically to a Gaussian shape. We
show that the electromagnetic field’s variances grow quasi-
exponentially and oscillate with the period of modulation
after several modulation periods. We show that the purity of
the electromagnetic field increases as a result of parametric
amplification. In the spatial-frequency domain, this purity
increase occurs exclusively inside the PTC’s bandgaps. In
the spatial domain, the purity increase occurs over the field’s
coherence length. We explain this behavior of the fields’ spatial
spectra in terms of the PTC’s band-structure and based on an
adaptation of the rotating-wave approximation to the pseudo-
Hermitian system of an EM field in time-varying media.

II. SYSTEM UNDER CONSIDERATION
In this section, we introduce and discuss the PTC medium
and electromagnetic radiation we analyze in the subsequent
sections of the paper. We first discuss the dimensionality of
the PTC medium. In this manuscript, we examine the simplest
non-trivial case of a system with one spatial coordinate (longi-
tudinal propagation coordinate z). The study of such a medium
is tractable via approximate analytical techniques (Subsection
IV-B) and via numerical analysis; yet it still exhibits intriguing
physics of a spatially homogeneous, time-periodic PTC. Fur-
thermore, one-dimensional PTCs are currently technologically
relevant, as they they are more experimentally accessible, and
have already enabled the realization of time-reflections [28],
[29] and PTCs [30], [31].
Technically, dielectric waveguide could represent a spatially
one-dimensional PTC. Of course, there is a greater technolog-
ical challenge of engineering the permittivity modulation of a
dielectric medium [32] compared to capacitance modulation of
a transmission line [28]–[31]. Furthermore modal dispersion
is intrinsic to waveguide’s guided modes for sufficiently large
bandwidth. Thus modal dispersion of a dielectric waveguide
generally complicates the system analysis, obscures the fun-
damental physics, and complicates extracting implementation-
independent insight.
Next, we briefly discuss the nature of the thermal radiation
examined in this paper. In this work, we consider blackbody
radiation present from weak interaction with a thermal en-
vironment (a thermodynamic heat bath). For simplicity, we
assume interaction of the medium’s electromagnetic radiation
with this environment is weak enough so that it is negligible
over the modulation times considered in this work. Of course,
a more complete description would consider coupling the field
inside the PTC to this thermal, unmodulated environment.
However, considering this coupling to such a thermal envi-
ronment further complicates the system. So investigation of
this effect is left for subsequent analysis beyond the scope of
this paper.
Next, we discuss the particular type of time-dependence
governing the PTC for our subsequent analysis. In this work,
we consider a non-dispersive PTC with a sinusoidally varying
electric impermittivity, η(t) = ε−1(t), varying with time t
where ε(t) is the PTC’s time-varying electric permittivity.
Specifically, we take η(t) to be of the form
η(t) = ηs + H(t)∆η0 sin Ωt.
(1)
In Eq. (1), H(t) is the Heaviside step function; ηs, the static
value of the impermittivity before modulation, and its time-
averaged value; ∆η0, the amplitude of the impermittivity
modulation; and Ω, the impermittivity modulation frequency.
The sinusoidal impermittivity modulation of Eq. (1) is
convenient for analysis for two reasons. The first reason is that
it describes a smoothly modulated PTC with finite rise time
and finite decay time. This is useful because then the resulting
PTC model can describe modulation of field components
oscillating both rapidly and slowly with respect to these rise
and decay times. This is in contrast to PTC models with,
for instance, stepwise varying permittivity [14]. The second
reason is that the sinusoidal impermittivity modulation of
Eq. (1), while continuous and smooth, still suitable for approx-
imate analytical treatment (see Subsection IV-B). This can be
seen from the fact that the wavenumber-domain Maxwell’s
equations are then analogous to the Hamiltonian equations
for a harmonic oscillator with sinusoidally varying resonance
frequency. In particular, the choice of modulation in Eq. (1)
enables us to develop an accurate rotating-wave approximation
(see Subsection IV-B) for the pseudo-Hermitian dynamics of
the wavenumber-domain Maxwell’s equations.
In this work, we specifically analyze the electromagnetic
fields’ spatial correlations and spatial spectra (Section III) as
they evolve over time. Such spatial-domain analysis of the
fluctuations is useful because it allows us to decompose field
variances and correlations over the wavenumber domain via
the Wiener-Khinchine theorem, and thus relate their evolution
to the underlying PTC band-structure and dynamics.

IV. ANALYTICAL TOOLS
In Section III, we defined all the elements necessary to
model the evolution of the spatial correlations and spectra
of initially blackbody radiation in a one-dimensional PTC.
Nonetheless, to aid the interpretation of the results and their
relations to the underlying physics in a PTC, it is useful to
introduce auxiliary analytical tools. In this section, we intro-
duce two such tools. These are the PTC’s wavenumber band-
structure (Subsection IV-A) and the rotating-wave approxima-
tion for optical fields in time-varying media (Subsection IV-B).

V. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
In this section, we examine the time-evolution of the figures
of merit characterizing the spatial auto- and cross-correlations
introduced in Section III. We analyze their behavior by lever-
aging the analytical tools of Section IV. We separate this
section into two subsections. The first subsection (Subsection
V-A) analyzes the evolution of the fields’ spatial correlations
and spectra, alongside that of the figures of merit defined
to describe them (i.e., their mean wavenumber, coherence
length, and spatial bandwidth). The objective of this sub-
section is to understand how the field variances and cross-
correlations and spatial correlations evolve over time, how the
field variances are resolved over wavenumber, and how this
evolution is governed by the PTC’s band-structure and can be
understood via the RWA. The second subsection (Subsection
V-B) examines the evolution of the spatial correlation matrix
and the cross-spectral density matrix. The objective of this
subsection is to understand the underlying linear combinations
of electromagnetic fields which give rise to the observed field
variances over wavenumber and over space, and how these
linear combinations are governed by the PTC modulation.
A. Spatial correlations and spectral-densities
This subsection analyzes the evolution of the fields’ spatial
correlations Γj(∆z, t) and spectra Wj(k, t). Our objective is to
understand how the field variances and cross-correlations and
spatial correlations evolve over time, how the field variances
are resolved over wavenumber, and how this evolution is
governed by the PTC’s band-structure and can be understood
via the RWA. We do this analysis in two parts. First, we
examine qualitatively the evolution of the spatial auto- and
cross-correlations over time (Figure 5). In this manner, we
identify the main effects of PTC modulation on the spatial
correlations, and we distinguish between two qualitatively
distinct time regimes for their evolution: a transient regime,
and an asymptotic regime. Second, we dissect quantitatively
the evolution of the spatial correlations and spatial spectra
through their (spectrally integrated) field variances (Figure 7)
and through the figures of merit introduced in Section III, i.e.,
their central wavenumbers, coherence lengths, and bandwidths
(Figure 6). In this way, we show that, in the asymptotic (long-
time) regime, the spatial correlations converge to a Gaussian
shape with coherence length growing as square-root of the
modulation time. We further identify periodic bursts when
the spatial spectra diverge from this Gaussian shape, and we
explain them in terms of the PTC band structure and the RWA.

interpret them to provide a qualitative description of the
PTC-based amplification of blackbody radiation. Initially, both
normalized displacement and magnetic variances (equivalently
electric and magnetic energies) are equal and the field cross-
correlation vanishes because the field is initially fully thermal.
Then, the PTC modulation creates an imbalance between these
variances. This imbalance causes the field to split into standing
waves (satisfying conservation of the initial Minkowski mo-
mentum of zero), leading to non-vanishing cross-field correla-
tion. The PTC modulation drives the system again, repeating
this process. This process of parametric driving and standing-
wave formation interferes constructively for waves inside the
PTC’s bandgaps. The increase in spatial coherence can be
understood as an increase in correlation between spatially
separated fields due to this coherent process of standing-wave
formation. The increase in spatial purity is also a consequence
of the coherent nature of PTC amplification and of the PTC’s
nature to amplify one temporal mode, while deamplifying
another.
VI. CONCLUSION
We examined the evolution of initially thermal radiation,
specifically blackbody radiation, in a one-dimensional pho-
tonic time-crystal (PTC). To analyze the dynamics of such
broadband, stochastic field in a PTC, we examine the fields’
spatial correlations and spatial power spectral densities. We
derive the evolution of these spectral densities from Maxwell’s
equations in spatially homogeneous, time-dependent media.
To interpret the dynamics of the fields’ spatial spectra, we


## Page 15


15
examine how their figures of merit evolve over time. We
then describe their evolution based on the PTC’s band struc-
ture, and a rotating-wave approximation for pseudo-Hermitian
dynamics of nearly resonant electromagnetic radiation in a
PTC. In general, we show that the fields’ spatial spectra
exhibit a clear asymptotic behavior, governed by the PTC’s
main bandgap peak. Specifically, the initially blackbody radi-
ation converges periodically to Gaussian spatial correlations
and spectra, with gradually increasing amplitudes, coherence
lengths, and both spatial- and wavenumber-domain purities.
Such a one-dimensional system is interesting as the simplest
non-trivial system for PTC-based amplification of thermal
radiation, and as a technologically relevant case realizable as a
time-modulated transmission line. The insights from this paper
shall be useful to understand the amplification of background
thermal radiation concurrent to the PTC-based amplification of
coherent light, and possibly to engineer PTCs which directly
amplify thermal radiation in the absence of a coherent light
source.
