2. Melonic graphs
A dipole δ of colour c is a graph with two vertices joined by D ´ 1 edges differently
coloured by the set t1, 2, . . . , c ´ 1, c ` 1, . . . , Du, and one colour-c half-edge at each
of the two vertices. The insertion of a dipole δ of colour c at a c-coloured edge e of a
D-coloured graph B, is the only replacement (out of two) of e by δ inside B that yields
a D-coloured graph.
By definition, a melon (or a melonic graph) is a coloured graph B that is constructed
from a regular D-ary edge-D-coloured rooted tree T in the following way. Let p be the
number of vertices that are not leaves (p is the number of parents).

the following restriction: any numbered vertex must have a larger label than its
parents.

6. Conclusions
6.1. Summary of the results. We have proven a new type of universality of melonic
random tensors that manifests in a twofold way in the limit N Ñ 8:
‚ Tensor rank universality or the independence of the number of indices of a tensor
while performing an integral (more precisely, Thm. 4.2) and the independence
of melonic tensor models of the number of indices (Thm. 5.1).
‚ Universality of the invariants (‘traces’), as far as these are melonic and share
the same number of vertices (Thm. 5.1).
None of these follows from the famous Gaussian Universality [Gur14]. Gaussian uni-
versality is a deep result that relates melonic 2p-order correlators with the p-th power
of the two-point function, but it does not yield the cumulants of one model in terms
of other model, and Theorem 5.1 does (whence we claim that our universality is new).
We also introduced melonic polynomials in Eq. (4.6), which solve melonic integrals at
large-N and agree with explicit computations with feyntensor [Pér26b].
6.2. Physics outlook. The present result stresses the importance in the future of:
‚ The study of non-melonic tensor models (examples are [BL17, LT19]).
‚ Non-melonic cumulants in melonic tensor models. (In [PPSS26] we proposed
positivity bootstraps that include non-melonic observables.)
‚ Finite-N methods, which surely detect D (feyntensor [Pér26b] confirms this,
but also positivity bootstraps [LT26] at finite-N do so for the 1
1-model).
‚ It is pertinent to comment on a family of 2-matrix models [Pér26a] with a
potential that includes the terms 1
4 TrrhpA4`B4q`2qgABAB`2p1´qqgABBAs
(with 0 ď q ď 1, g, h P R). This is so constructed, that the sum of the couplings
of the two latter words is always g. Monte Carlo simulations by the author
show that, despite this restriction, the phase portraits in the pg, hq-plane are
different for different q. From that view point, the implication of Theorem 5.1
for the tensor case that, at large-N, there exist one quartic, unitary invariant
melonic tensor model—and that the effective coupling in the universal measure
is the sum of the couplings of all quartic operators—seems strong, specially
contrasting with the previous matrix case, in which criticality is not the same
for all convex combinations (q “ 1{2 seems critical for fixed h, g).
