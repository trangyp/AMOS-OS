THE ALGEBRAIC MONTGOMERY–YANG PROBLEM
WOOHYEOK JO, JONGIL PARK, AND KYUNGBAE PARK
Abstract. We completely resolve the algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem, a conjecture of
Koll´ar stating that every rational homology projective plane with quotient singularities and a
simply-connected smooth locus has at most three singular points. The crux of our proof is a new
lattice-theoretic constraint, obtained by combining Donaldson’s diagonalization theorem with the
distinguished spinc structure on the smooth locus whose determinant line bundle is the canonical
bundle. Together with the orbifold Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality, this constraint rules out
all remaining cases in the problem and completes the proof.
1. Introduction
A normal projective complex surface whose Betti numbers agree with those of the complex
projective plane CP2 is called a rational homology projective plane, or simply a Q-homology CP2.
Such surfaces have the smallest possible Betti numbers among algebraic surfaces and thus occupy a
prominent position in the theory of algebraic surfaces; see [Keu18] for a survey. At the same time,
as observed by Koll´ar [Kol08], these surfaces are deeply connected to questions in low-dimensional
topology. For instance, they arise in the study of small fillings of spherical 3-manifolds and in
problems concerning embeddings of rational homology balls into CP2. For related recent works,
see [JPP24a, GO].
A non-singular Q-homology CP2 that is not biholomorphic to CP2 is called a fake projective
plane. These surfaces have been completely classified [PY07, PY10, CS10]. In the singular setting,
one usually restricts attention to surfaces with only quotient singularities. One of the fundamental
questions concerning such surfaces is the classification problem in terms of singularity type, namely,
determining which collections of quotient singularities can occur on a Q-homology CP2 [Bre77,
BDP81a, BDP81b, BB84, Fur86, MZ88, Zha89, Koj99, Ye02, Koj03, AN06, HKO15, Keu15, Sch23,
Lac24, JPP24b, BH25, PP25]. Closely related to this is the question of determining the maximal
number of quotient singularities that a Q-homology CP2 can support [Miy84, KM99, Bel08, Bel09,
HK11b]. At the heart of both questions lies the so-called algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem,
introduced by Koll´ar [Kol08] as an algebraic analog of the Montgomery–Yang problem for pseudo-
free smooth circle actions on the 5-sphere. The latter problem, originally asked by Montgomery and
Yang [MY72] and later formulated by Fintushel and Stern [FS87], remains widely open; see also
[BKR26, Problem 5.7]. Hence the algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem belongs to a broader circle
of questions at the interface of algebraic geometry and low-dimensional topology. In particular,
it is closely related to Koll´ar’s proposed smooth Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau (BMY) inequality for
differentiable orbifolds [Kol08], which connects the Montgomery–Yang problem with H-cobordism
questions for Seifert fibered 3-manifolds and with the study of algebraic surfaces with quotient
singularities. A concrete instance of this connection is the three fibers conjecture, first indicated
by Fintushel and Stern and explicitly stated by Lawson [Law88]; see also [Kol08, Conjecture 20].
It asserts that a Seifert fibered homology 3-sphere with more than three exceptional fibers cannot
bound a homology 4-ball.
Conjecture 1.1 (Algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem, [Kol08, Conjecture 30]). Let S be a
rational homology projective plane with quotient singularities. If its smooth locus S0 := S \Sing(S)
is simply-connected, then S has at most three singular points.

Yang problem. It is already known, essentially by the weak version eorb ≥0 of the orbifold BMY
inequality, that a Q-homology CP2 with quotient singularities and a simply-connected smooth lo-
cus can have at most four singular points. Moreover, Conjecture 1.1 has been verified when S has

With the algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem now resolved, the next natural task is to classify
the possible singularity types of rational homology projective planes with quotient singularities
and a simply-connected smooth locus. For recent progress in this direction, we refer the reader to
[JPP24b]. Since Theorem 1.3 provides an effective tool for obstructing the existence of surfaces with
prescribed singularity types, we expect it to play an important role in advancing this classification
problem.
Organization of the paper. In Section 2, we review basic facts about spinc structures, establish
our conventions regarding intersection lattices, and explain how the gluing of spinc structures can
be interpreted in terms of lattice embeddings. In Section 3, we provide a detailed analysis of spinc
structures on lens spaces and their behavior under certain orientation-reversing diffeomorphisms.
The results established in this section will play a crucial role in the proof of Theorem 1.3. Section
4 summarizes the topological properties of rational homology projective planes and derives key
properties that will be utilized in the proof of Theorem 1.3. We also recall the orbifold BMY
inequality (Theorem 4.4), which will be applied in the proof of Theorem 1.4. In Section 5, we
prove the main lattice-theoretic constraint, Theorem 1.3, using the results of Sections 2, 3, and
4. Finally, in Section 6, we apply this constraint to prove Theorem 1.4, thereby completing the
resolution of the algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem.
