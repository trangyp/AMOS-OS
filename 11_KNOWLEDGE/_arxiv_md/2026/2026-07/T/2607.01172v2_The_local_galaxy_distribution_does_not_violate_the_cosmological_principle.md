The local galaxy distribution does not
violate the cosmological principle
Till Sawala
University of Helsinki, Department of Physics, Helsinki, Finland
This version: July 9, 2026
Version 1: July 1, 2026
The cosmological principle, which states that the Universe is statistically homogeneous
and isotropic on sufficiently large scales, is a foundational assumption of the standard
cosmological model. A recent analysis of DESI DR1 galaxy samples reported coherent
anisotropic features in the local galaxy distribution extending to gigaparsec scales. If
correct, this result would directly contradict the cosmological principle and motivate
inhomogeneous cosmologies. Here I analyze the same data and compare them with
galaxy distributions predicted by the FLAMINGO cosmological hydrodynamic simulation,
performed in the standard ΛCDM paradigm. I show that the apparent anomaly disappears
when the correct comoving distance scale is used and when compared to mock catalogs
that account for bias and redshift-space distortions. Rather than violating the cosmological
principle, the observed structures are consistent with those expected in a ΛCDM Universe.

1.
Introduction
The standard paradigm of hierarchical structure formation predicts a Universe that is
strongly inhomogeneous on the scales of galaxies and galaxy clusters, but statistically
homogeneous and isotropic on sufficiently large scales (Peebles 1980; Davis et al. 1985).
Individual, extended structures have been observed since the first redshift surveys (Huchra
and Geller 1982; de Lapparent, Geller, and Huchra 1986; Geller and Huchra 1989; Gott
et al. 2005), and modern observations reveal a complex cosmic web of voids, filaments,
clusters and superclusters (e.g. Tully et al. 2014; Valade et al. 2024). Such features are also
a natural outcome of cold-dark-matter simulations (White et al. 1987; Springel et al. 2005;
Park et al. 2012; Sawala et al. 2024; Schaller 2024), and indeed required to explain the
observed distribution of structures in the Local Universe.
There have been several claims about discoveries of stronger, or even larger-scale clus-
tering than predicted by the standard model. These include local underdensities (Keenan,
Barger, and Cowie 2013), source-count dipoles (Secrest et al. 2021; Land-Strykowski, Lewis,
and Murphy 2025), anisotropies in galaxy-cluster scaling relations (Migkas et al. 2020),
supernovae (Sorrenti, Durrer, and Kunz 2023), peculiar velocities (Watkins et al. 2023;
Courtois et al. 2025), or gigaparsec-scale patterns (Lopez, Clowes, and Williger 2022).
Individually and collectively, these claimed discoveries are often interpreted as evidence
for a breakdown of the cosmological principle, or a motivation for non-standard physics
(e.g. Buchert and Räsänen 2012; Kumar Aluri et al. 2023; Lopez, Clowes, and Williger 2024;
Lapi et al. 2023; Constantin et al. 2023; Mazurenko, Banik, and Kroupa 2025; Meissner and
Penrose 2025). However, sparse sampling, survey geometry, a posteriori feature selection
and look-elsewhere effects can also produce individual apparently significant patterns.
Several claimed structures were later shown to be statistically insignificant, consistent
with random fluctuations, or compatible with standard model predictions (Nadathur 2013;
Park et al. 2015; Marinello et al. 2016; Balázs, Rejtő, and Tusnády 2018; Fujii 2024; Sawala
et al. 2025).
A recent work by Sylos Labini and Galoppo (2026) makes a broader, and apparently
stronger claim: that the DESI (Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument) DR1 (Data Release 1)
galaxy distribution contains statistically significant anisotropic structure on gigaparsec
scales. Using a volume-limited sample from the bright galaxy catalog of DESI DR1 (Hahn
et al. 2023; Abdul Karim et al. 2026), they report galaxy clustering far in excess of geometry-
matched mocks based on ΛCDM simulations.
By including a large fraction of the public DESI DR1 survey data, this analysis avoids
any suspicion of cherry-picking that could give rise to a look-elsewhere effect. The com-
parison to a large sample of simulation mocks also appears to provide a robust and even
conservative demonstration of statistical significance. A detection of clustering at this
magnitude and statistical significance would thus directly challenge and possibly falsify

the cosmological principle, that is, the statistical homogeneity and isotropy inherent not
only in ΛCDM but in the broader Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker framework.
The present work examines the same DESI DR1 data, but arrives at the opposite conclusion.
2.
The DESI DR1 data
Following Sylos Labini and Galoppo (2026), the observational data used here consist of the
public DESI DR1 spectroscopic galaxy catalog (Abdul Karim et al. 2026), and in particular
the Bright Galaxy Sample (BGS; Hahn et al. 2023). The BGS is a low-redshift, high-density
DESI sample selected from Legacy Surveys photometry. In their work, Sylos Labini and
Galoppo (2026) restricted the analysis to a contiguous, nearly uniform subregion. Their
main BGS sample, labeled S2, contains N = 36,290 galaxies with Mr < −20 within a cylinder
of reported radius R = 300h−1 Mpc and thickness 40h−1 Mpc.
The present work is based on the same public BGS catalog, which is complete to
Mr < −21.5 to the maximum distance considered. For the full R = 290h−1 Mpc compar-
ison, Mr < −21.55 is used to match the surface number density of the S2 sample in its
fiducial coordinates, while galaxies up to Mr < −20 are included in smaller subvolumes.
Importantly, comoving distances are computed directly from the published, spectroscopic
redshifts. Throughout this work, the D3A cosmology is adopted, with h = 0.681, Ωm = 0.306,
Ωb = 0.0486, ΩΛ = 0.694, σ8 = 0.807, ns = 0.967, and ∑mν = 0.06eV. Details are given in
Appendix A.
The left panel of Figure 1 shows the distribution of galaxies within the DESI DR1
footprint. The position of the observer is evident from the geometry of the cone and the
concentric redshift-space distortions. The DESI data extend to 680h−1 Mpc and enclose a
cylinder of R = 290h−1 Mpc with a center at a distance of 390h−1 Mpc, corresponding to
redshift z = 0.13, and spanning a redshift range of z ≃0.03 −0.24.
The central panel of Figure 1 shows the overlap between DESI DR1 and the Sloan
Digital Sky Survey Data Release 18, in a region defined by Einasto et al. (2011). The region
is centered on the “Sloan Great Wall", a prominent structure in the Local Universe (Gott
et al. 2005) at a comoving distance of ∼235h−1 Mpc. The right panel showing the S2 data
is discussed in Section 5.

4.
Clustering in DESI DR1 compared to ΛCDM predictions
Figure 2 provides a visual comparison of the projected galaxy distribution found in DESI to
those in five non-overlapping simulation regions (stacked along the simulation z-axis) of
radius R = 290h−1 Mpc at z = 0.15. The amount of structure in DESI DR1 visually resembles
that found in the mock galaxy catalogs of the FLAMINGO simulation, once redshift-space
distortions are included. The absence of redshift-space distortions reduces the clustering
strength relative to realistic galaxy mocks, and the direct sampling of simulation particles
further reduces the amount of clustering. Compared at its fiducial scale, the clustering in
the S2 data is far stronger than in the simulation.

5.
Explaining the Discrepancy
The discrepancy between the results presented here and those of Sylos Labini and Galoppo
(2026) is plausibly explained by a distance error inherited from the construction of the S2
coordinates in Sylos Labini and Antal (2026). The published S2 coordinates appear to treat
luminosity distances expressed in Mpc as if they were comoving distances in h−1 Mpc,
resulting in a spurious enlargement by a factor 1+z
h . This has substantial implications for
the inferred inhomogeneity and anisotropy. Details are provided in Appendix D.
Figure 5 demonstrates the consequence of the distance error. The left panel shows the
S2 data in fiducial coordinates. The middle panel shows the DESI data, but in coordinates
where the luminosity distance in Mpc is deliberately equated to comoving coordinates in
h−1Mpc. It is worth noting that I do not compute independent luminosity distances, but
simply apply the 1+z
h factor to the redshift-derived comoving distances. Nevertheless, this
erroneous projection closely aligns with the S2 data in fiducial coordinates.
The right panel shows the same DESI galaxies selected from the same region that

in actual comoving coordinates. Comparison to circles placed at constant intervals in

An immediate consequence is also visible in Figure 1, which compares the DESI and
SDSS data to the S2 data, shown in the published coordinates, as presented in Figure 1 of
Sylos Labini and Galoppo (2026). The prominent structure close to the center of S2 can
be identified as the Sloan Great Wall, albeit slightly distorted and significantly enlarged.
Due to the scale-dependent nature of clustering in the standard model evident in
Figure 3, comparing structures at different scales can result in apparent discrepancies.
In addition to boosting the apparent large-scale power, the distortion also introduces
spurious anisotropy.
Furthermore, the comparison in Sylos Labini and Galoppo (2026) was made to simple
dark matter particle mocks, equivalent to those shown in the bottom row of Figure 2,
whose power spectra are shown as gray lines in Figure 3. These only sample the matter
distribution and significantly underestimate the amount of inhomogeneity and anisotropy
in the galaxy distribution, already predicted by the standard model.
6.
Discussion
In the hierarchical paradigm, clustering strength is strongly scale-dependent, making
comparisons between observations and predictions highly sensitive to systematic dis-
tance errors. In addition, the inhomogeneity and anisotropy of the galaxy distribution
that is predicted by the standard model require mock galaxy catalogs that account for
halo bias, galaxy bias, and redshift-space distortions. Comparing DESI DR1 data to real-
istic mock galaxy catalogs based on equivalent regions in the FLAMINGO simulation, I
have shown that the observed local large-scale structure is in excellent agreement with
theoretical predictions in terms of both inhomogeneity and anisotropy. The earlier claim
of a gigaparsec-scale departure from homogeneity and isotropy instead originates from
an incorrect distance scale, as well as inadequate mock catalogs.
It is worth emphasizing that the absence of large-scale inhomogeneities is no proof
that the standard model is correct: indeed, analyses by the DESI team, combining DESI
BAO measurements with CMB and supernova data, show a small preference for a time-
dependent dark-energy equation of state in the w0waCDM model over ΛCDM (DESI Col-
laboration et al. 2025b,a). However, based on the available data, there is no evidence for a
violation of the cosmological principle.

Code
The numerical analysis was performed in Python. Data processing and cosmological
distances were computed using astropy (Astropy Collaboration et al. 2013, 2022), and
the calculations including the Fourier transforms used numpy (Harris et al. 2020). All
figures were produced with matplotlib (Hunter 2007). The code used in the anistropy
analysis is based on code by Sylos Labini and Galoppo. An LLM (ChatGPT 5.5) was used in
the creation of the analysis code. Code to produce the results and all figures presented here
directly from public data is provided at: https://github.com/TillSawala/DESI-
FLAMINGO.

Appendix A.
Selection and processing of the DESI Data
The DESI DR1 data consist of the clustering catalogs of the Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS).
From these, the sky positions, redshift, and dereddened Legacy Survey fluxes are used,
namely the columns RA, DEC, Z, flux_g_dered, and flux_r_dered. Only galaxies
with finite sky coordinates, positive redshifts, and positive finite g- and r-band dereddened
fluxes are retained.
All DESI redshifts are converted directly to comoving distances using the same D3A cos-
mology as the FLAMINGO simulation used for the mocks, with h = 0.681, Ωm = 0.306, Ωb =
0.0486. The redshifts naturally include peculiar velocities, making the distances subject to
redshift-space distortion. The selection is restricted to 127○≤RA ≤225○, −7○≤Dec ≤3○
and to a comoving distance of ≤680h−1 Mpc.
To compute completeness and select sets of the N brightest galaxies, an approximate

Appendix E.
Windowed power spectra
The clustering strength within the cylinders is quantified using windowed two-dimensional
power spectra in circular apertures. The same estimator is applied to DESI, S2, and all
FLAMINGO mocks. This is important because the finite circular aperture has a non-trivial
window function. No attempt is made to deconvolve the windowed spectra, instead they
are directly compared to each other.
After projection into the xy plane (where necessary, the S2 data are already 2D), the
data fall within a circular aperture of radius R, which is embedded within a square of
side length Lbox = 2R. The square is divided into Ngrid = 512 cells per dimension. The
