Abstract. This is the first paper of a project on new integral models X(N)
of the modular curve X(N). The final results for a general level N will be
obtained in the second paper, while this paper is devoted to giving all necessary
background and definitions applicable to any N and then working out the case
of X(p) with all possible details. We define X(N) as the closure of Y (N) in
the space M1,N2 = M1,Γ, where Γ = (Z/NZ)2, and show that for N = p it
is the blowup of the Katz-Mazur model e
X(p) at all supersingular points, and
hence (X(p), Y (p)) is the minimal toroidal resolution of ( e
X(p), Y (p)). In fact,
it is even log smooth over (Z, Z[1/p]), but this is special for the case when
p = N. One can tautologically view X(p) as the moduli space of Γ-stabilized
genus-1 curves (E, Γ) which can be smoothed to an elliptic curve labelled by its
N-torsion, but our main results provide explicit criteria of the smoothability:
X(p) parameterizes Γ-equivariant stable genus-1 curves (E, Γ) such that the
action satisfies two explicit conditions formulated in the paper.

We refer to [AO01] for details and only mention here that the embedding into
the moduli space of stable maps with four marked points forces one to keep the
”ramification points” disjoint even modulo 2. Their proof provides a natural stable
map E →P between prestable H-curves of genus 1 and 0, respectively, and E also
comes equipped with four disjoint sections P0, P1, P∞, Pλ. To a large extent the
construction reduces to first extending the cover E →P over Z[1/2] to a finite cover
of prestable curves (simultaneous semistable reduction) and then blowing up the
locus over F2 where the closure of the sections collide. Sometimes such a blowup
also forces one to modify the base so that the curves stay flat over the base. The
collision pattern is different in the ordinary and supersingular cases and this is
reflected in the space H.
Remark 1.1.2. In my view the decisive and most non-standard step in this ap-
proach is to ignore the fact that ramification patterns are different for tame and
wild ramification, and to prevent the four points from colliding in characteristic 2
by using moduli spaces of stable marked curves. In a sense, one forces the wild
ramification pattern to deform to a tame one already in characteristic 2, which is
an easier task than studying an immediate deformation to mixed characteristic. As
a result, a valuable information is added to the functor when p is not invertible,
and I view it as a new version of the wild ramification/level structures. This in-
formation possesses moduli of its own, so this approach naturally produces moduli
spaces with reducible fibers over wild primes, which is usually crucial in order to
obtain nice enough, e.g. log regular, models.

1.1.5. Modular curves. The example of Abramovich-Oort has also an interpretation
from a different world: H2,4 is just the modular curve X(2) because giving such a

principle – do not allow marked points in characteristic zero to collide modulo p,

The integral models X(N) of the modular curves are not directly related to
Hurwitz spaces of maps to P1, though they are components of the Hurwitz spaces
that parameterize isogenies of order N 2 between elliptic curves. We think that
they are fascinating moduli spaces which can be viewed as moduli spaces of ellip-
tic curves with wild level structure and (to the best of our knowledge) were not
described earlier. We hope that the methods and ideas developed in this project
will be also useful to study wild level structures on curves of higher genus and on
abelian varieties, but of course one should first study the simplest cases of elliptic
curves and their moduli spaces. In particular, in this case many additional tools
(such as the well studied regular model of Katz-Mazur) are available. Even in this
setting, we still decided to split this project to two papers. This paper is devoted
to defining X(N) and the objects it parameterizes – prestable (but not semistable)
Γ-equivariant elliptic curves stabilized by Γ and various properties of the action
needed to isolate X(N) among the other components. We test these properties on
the case of N = p, which is worked out with all details in this paper. In particular,
we study what other components are, and explain why we need to impose various
restrictions on the action to get rid of the non-smoothable components. This study
is not needed to prove our main results about X(p) but illustrates our choices and
the wide context we are working with. In the sequel paper we will use the founda-
tions developed here to study the case of an arbitrary N. Finally, I hope that quite

1.2. Overview of the paper. We start with introducing the stacks X(N) in §2
as the closure of Y (N) in M1,Γ and observing that over Z[1/N] one just recov-
ers the Deligne-Rapoport compactification. Then we provide in Lemma 2.1.7 the
tautological interpretation of X(N) as the moduli space of smoothable Γ-stabilized
genus-1 curves (E, Γ) and start to study the main question of the paper – what is
a constructible/explicit definition of such a functor? The general principle is very
simple – consider a smoothing, and study how all properties of the generic fiber
(Eη, Γ = Eη[N]) restrict to the closed fiber, what are the restrictions they impose.
We provide a whole list of such properties in §2.1.8
(1) The Γ-action by translations is compatible with Pic0 and a global deriva-
tion.
(2) The isogeny of multiplication by p.
(3) A group structure.
It turns out that versions of (1) and (2) suffice to cut out X(p) (and also X(N) as
we will prove in the sequel paper), while the group structure is not needed and in
fact can be reconstructed from (1).
Of course, the choice of properties that characterise smoothability can be non-
unique and to some extent is a matter of taste or convenience, but at least we will
show on examples that our list is minimal and no property can be removed, see
Remarks 4.1.5 and 4.2.12(ii). Note that Γ acts on M1,Γ by translating the marked
points, and this fixes the points of Y (N) because the action on Γ = E[N] comes
from the action on E itself. It follows that X(N) ⊆(M1,Γ)Γ and hence X(N)
parameterizes Γ-equivariant Γ-labelled curves (E, Γ), that are called Γ-marked in
§2.2. We view this condition as the most important rigidifying property, which
provides a first approximation of smoothability.
One of the advantages of Γ-equivariance is its amenability for study by quite
standard tools, and in §3 we apply them to the space (M1,Γ)Γ of Γ-marked curves
when N = p. The situation over Z[1/p] is quite simple, so we mainly concentrate
on studying the Fp-fiber. First, we classify the geometric points and show that on
the set-theoretic level the smoothability imposes just one more restriction of su-
persingular nature beyond Γ-equivariance. Then we study deformations and show
that there do exist non-smoothable deformations of smoothable Γ-marked curves.
This provides concrete examples justifying two restrictions on the action we im-
pose later to reduce (M1,Γ)Γ further to X(p). In particular, we interpret some
non-smoothable deformations along the ordinary locus as those that point in the
direction of non-semistable components of (M1,Γ)Γ over Z[1/p]. Such components
did not appear in [DR73], but in our setting they must be ruled out by an addi-
tional condition. We finish this section with an explicit description in §3.3 of a few
simpler fixed locus spaces, such as (M0,Γ0)Γ0 = Spec(Z[ξp]), where Γ0 = Fp acts
on Γ0 = Fp ∪{∞} by translations. This geometrically explains some phenomena
with non-liftable deformations in terms of ramification over Z.

2.1. The model X(N).
2.1.1. Γ-stable curves. Fix an integer N > 1 and the group Γ = (Z/NZ)2 with
generators γ1 = (1, 0) and γ2 = (0, 1). By a Γ-pointed stable S-curve or just a
Γ-stable S-curve we mean a stable S-curve (C, D) with a trivialization D = ΓS =
Γ×S. The moduli space Mg,Γ of Γ-stable curves is a smooth and proper DM stack
over Z, which is canonically isomorphic to Mg,N 2, once a bijection Γf
→{1, . . . ,N 2}

2.1.2. The modular curves Y (N) and X(N). The moduli space Y (N) classifies
elliptic curves with Γ-trivialized (or rigidified) N-torsion or just Γ-leveled elliptic
curves. In other words, if S is a scheme, then Y (N)(S) is the groupoid of pairs
(ES, ΓSf

only when N is invertible on S, so Y (N) is a Z[1/N]-scheme, and even a Z[1/N, µN]-
scheme, as follows from the existence of the Weil pairing.
Deligne-Rapoport constructed in [DR73] a modular Z[1/N]-compactification X(N)
which classifies Γ-leveled generalized elliptic curves. Its cusps correspond to N-gons
of P1’s.
2.1.3. The model X(N). We can interpret an element (ES, ΓS) ∈Y (N)(S) as a Γ-
stable elliptic S-curve, so a natural closed immersion Y (N) ,→M1,Γ arises. Then
we define X(N) to be the closure of Y (N) in M1,Γ. In particular, X = X(N) is
equipped with the tautological family of Γ-stable genus-1 curves (EX , ΓX ).

Y (N) over Spec(Z[1/N]).

2.2. Γ-action.
2.2.1. The action. Note that M1,Γ is a scheme for N > 2, but M1,Γ is a stack. For
example, there exists a Γ-stable curve with a genus-1 component having one node
and no marked points, and such a component possesses non-trivial automorphisms
fixing the node. The group Γ acts on the whole stack M1,Γ just by the action on
the set of marked points. Intuitively, it is of a “set theoretic nature”, so one does not
have to use the general (and more subtle) theory of group actions on stacks. The
way to formalize this is to observe that the morphism M1,Γ →M1 is projective and
the action is fiberwise (the action is Γ-equivariant once the target is provided with
the trivial action). So, one can describe this action in terms of the usual descent
and admissible Γ-actions on schemes.
2.2.2. The fixed point locus. We can thus use descent to define the fixed locus
(M1,Γ)Γ, and in our case it is a closed substack, as one should expect in the case
of actions of “set theoretic origin”. In particular, an S-point (ES, ΓS) of M1,Γ is Γ-
fixed if and only if there exists an S-action of Γ on ES which extends the translation
action of Γ on ΓS.
2.2.3. Γ-marked curves. A Γ-equivariant Γ-stable S-curve (ES, ΓS) will be called
Γ-marked. As we saw, (M1,Γ)Γ is the moduli space of such objects. In the case of
an integral S, we say that (ES, ΓS) is generically smooth if (Eη, Γη) is a Γ-leveled
elliptic curve. Thus, X(N) is the substack of (M1,Γ)Γ, which classifies Γ-smoothable
Γ-marked curves. The substack (M1,Γ)Γ provides an initial approximation of X(N),
though it is still quite larger. Let us first see what it gives in the classical setting.

3.2.8. Conclusions. To cut out X(p) from (M1,Γ)Γ one has to solve two issues:
(1) remove the supersingular configurations with an ordinary core, (2) eliminate
the non-smoothable components and non-reduced structure due to existence of
parasitic equivariant deformations of rational components. The first task will be
easily solved by use of the retraction onto the core (or ϕp). The second task is
more delicate and will be solved by imposing a compatibility condition with certain
vector fields of a global nature. This seems to be unavoidable because the non-
smoothable deformations which are “parasitic” for our goals do have a natural local
interpretation as we will see in the next subsection.
