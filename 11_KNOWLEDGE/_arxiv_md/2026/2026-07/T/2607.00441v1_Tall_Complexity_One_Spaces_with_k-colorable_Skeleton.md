TALL COMPLEXITY ONE SPACES WITH K-COLORABLE SKELETON
YICHEN LIU
Abstract. Tall complexity one T-spaces are Hamiltonian T-spaces (M, ω, Φ) such that
1
2 dim M −dim T = 1 and the symplectic quotient at each moment value is a surface. The
skeleton of a complexity one T-space is an important invariant in the classification and
encodes the information about non-generic orbits. In this paper, we study properties of the
skeleton of a compact, connected tall complexity one T-spaces. We prove that when the
skeleton is k-colorable, i.e., when it can be partitioned into k closed and open subsets such
that the orbital moment map is injective on each of them, its information can be recovered
by the one-skeleton (the set of non-generic orbits whose dimension is at most one). We
also prove that for any cloesd and open subset of the skeleton on which the orbital moment
map is injective, one can construct a symplectic toric (T × S1)-manifold whose underlying
complexity one T-space has the skeleton isomorphic to this subset.
1. Introduction
Let T be a torus with Lie algebra t and dual space t∗. Recall that we say T acts on
a symplectic manifold (M, ω) in a Hamiltonian fashion if there exists an T-invariant map
Φ : M →t∗such that d⟨Φ, X⟩= ω(X#, ·) for all X ∈t, where X# is the fundamental vector
field on M corresponding to X. We call the tuple (M, ω, Φ) a Hamiltonian T-space. If not
otherwise stated, we always assume the action is effective1 in this paper. The complexity
of (M, ω, Φ) is 1
2 dim M −dim T; it is half the dimension of the reduced space Φ−1(α)/T at a
regular value α ∈Φ(M). Hamiltonian T-spaces of complexity zero are known as symplectic
toric manifolds. Such spaces are classified by their moment images (see Theorem 4.1).
Let (M, ω, Φ) be a 2n–dimensional Hamiltonian T–manifold. Recall that the Liouville
measure on M is given by integrating the volume form
ωn
n! with respect to the symplec-
tic orientation and that the Duistermaat–Heckman measure is the pushforward of the
Liouville measure by the moment map. Recall that the symplectic slice at p ∈M is the
symplectic vector space (TpO)ω/(TpO ∩(TpO)ω), where O is the T-orbit of p. Let H ⊂T be
the stabilizer group of p. The isotropy representation of H on TpM induces a representation
on the quotient space, called the slice representation. Since the symplectic slice inherits
the complex structure from TpM, the slice representation decomposes into one-dimensional
complex irreducible representations each of which is determined by a weight vector ηi ∈h∗.
Under the identification (TpO)ω/(TpO∩(TpO)ω) ∼= Ck, we can express the slice representation
as a group homomorphism ρ : H →(S1)k such that h.(z1, . . . , zk) = (ρ1(h)z1, . . . , ρk(h)zk),
where the differential of ρi is ηi.
Fix a complexity one T-space (M, ω, Φ), we say a point p ∈M is tall if every open
neighborhood of the orbit through p in the reduced space Φ−1(Φ(p))/T contains more than
one orbit.
A complexity one T-space (M, ω, Φ) is tall if every point in M is tall.

Correspondingly, a point in an exceptional orbit is called an
exceptional point. The skeleton of (M, ω, Φ) is the set of tall exceptional orbits, considered
as a subspace of M/T. The one-skeleton is the set of tall exceptional orbits that are at
most one dimensional. Given k ∈N, we say that the (one-)skeleton of M is k-colorable
if the (one-)skeleton is the disjoint union of k (possibly empty) clopen2 subsets so that the
orbital moment map3 Φ: M/T →t∗restricts to an injection on each subset. Two complexity
one T-spaces have isomorphic (one-)skeletons if there exists a homeomorphism from the
(one-)skeleton of one space to that of another which intertwines the moment maps and takes
each exceptional orbit to an exceptional orbit with the same stabilizer and isomorphic slice
representations. The k-colorability is preserved by isomorphisms between (one-)skeletons.
Finally, the T-action on M extends to a toric action if there exists a torus eT with
dim( eT) = 1
2 dim(M) that acts effectively on M in a Hamiltonian fashion and an inclusion
T ,→eT so that the eT-action restricts to the given T-action.
Complexity one spaces were first studied in dimension four by Karshon [Kar99]. Later, in
a series of papers [KT01, KT03, KT14], Karshon and Tolman classified tall complexity one
spaces in any dimensions. These spaces also demonstrate new phenomena: Tolman [Tol98]
constructed a complexity one space that admits no invariant K¨ahler structure, while all
symplectic toric manifolds are K¨ahler [Del88].
In [LPT], the author, Palmer and Tolman prove that a complexity one T-space has the
same combinatorial invariants (the skeleton, the Duistermaat Heckman measure) as a com-
plexity one T-space obtained by restricting the toric action on a symplectic toric manifold
if and only if the skeleton is 2-colorable. In this paper, we will focus on properties of the
skeleton of tall complexity one T-spaces. Our theorems should still hold true if we assume
the moment map to be proper as a map to a convex open subset of t∗instead of assuming
the manifold to be compact. For simplicity, we will just state and prove our main results in
the compact case, but prove some auxiliary lemmas in the proper case. We first notice that
the definition of k-colorability can be reduced to the k-colorability on the one-skeleton.
Theorem 1.1. Let (M, ω, Φ) be a compact connected tall complexity one T-space.
The
skeleton of (M, ω, Φ) is k-colorable if and only if the one-skeleton of (M, ω, Φ) is k-colorable.
Moreover, we apply similar techniques as in [LPT] to prove that as long as the orbital

one T-space has the skeleton isomorphic to E. Charton-Kessler-Tolman [CKT26, Question

4. Complexity one properties of a symplectic toric manifold
In this section, we focus on a special family of complexity one spaces, the ones that are
obtained from restricting a toric action on a symplectic toric manifold to a codimension one
sub-torus action. We start with a few definitions.
Let t be the Lie algebra of a compact torus T and ℓ⊂t be its integral lattice. A subset P of
t∗is polyhedral if it is the intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces. A polyhedral set
is rational if each half-space admits an outwards pointing normal vector which is primitive
in ℓ. Additionally, a rational polyhedral set P is unimodular if for each point α ∈P
the primitive outward normal vectors of the half-spaces that α lies on the boundary of can
be extended to a Z-basis of ℓ. P is locally unimodular if for any point α ∈P there
is a neighborhood Uα of α in t∗and a unimodular rational polyhedral set Dα such that
Uα ∩P = Uα ∩Dα. In particular, every open set is locally unimodular. Finally, a Delzant
polyhedral set is a unimodular, rational polyhedral set and a Delzant polytope is a
compact Delzant polyhedral set. Delzant [Del88] classified compact connected symplectic
toric manifolds by their moment images.
