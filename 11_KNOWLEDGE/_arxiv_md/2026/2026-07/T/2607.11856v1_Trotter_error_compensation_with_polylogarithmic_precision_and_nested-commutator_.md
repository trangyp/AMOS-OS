Product formulas are among the most practical approaches to Hamiltonian simulation, requiring
no ancillary qubits and exhibiting error bounds governed by nested commutators rather than only by
Hamiltonian norms. Their circuit size, however, scales polynomially with the inverse precision. We
develop a high-order nested-commutator compensation (HNCC) algorithm that preserves the main
advantages of product formulas while achieving polylogarithmic precision dependence in the circuit

expansion to represent high-order Trotter errors by products of nested commutators and compen-

I.
INTRODUCTION
Hamiltonian simulation, the task of simulating the dy-
namics generated by a Hamiltonian, is a fundamental
problem in quantum computing [1, 2].
Beyond its di-
rect application to studying the dynamics of physical sys-
tems [3, 4], it serves as an algorithmic primitive for var-
ious computational tasks, including ground-state prepa-
ration [5, 6], solving linear systems of equations [7, 8]
and differential equations [9], and combinatorial opti-
mization [10].
Lloyd [2] introduced the first explicit quantum algo-
rithm for this task based on product formulas [11, 12],
also known as the Trotter method.
For a Hamilto-
nian H = PΓ
γ=1 Hγ, a K-th order product formula
SK(x), composed of evolutions under the individual
terms Hγ, approximates U(x) = e−iHx to K-th order
in x.
Although various “post-Trotter methods” were
subsequently developed [13–17], product formulas re-
main practically appealing because of their simple cir-
cuit structure, low ancilla requirements, and low gate
count [12]. Furthermore, their error bounds can exploit
nested commutators among the terms {Hγ}, rather than

Despite these advantages, product formulas exhibit a

more favorable in practice. In this regime, the polyno-
mial dependence on both t and 1/ε becomes a bottleneck
for long-time or high-precision simulation.

for Hadamard tests or ancillary qubits.
The main technical challenge is to construct such an
LCQC approximation to arbitrarily high order while pre-
serving the nested-commutator structure of the Trotter
error.
We use the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH)
expansion to express the generator of VK(x) in terms
of nested commutators.
Using BCH truncation-error
bounds based on doubly right-nested commutators [26],
we retain only finitely many BCH terms and therefore
do not rely on convergence of the full BCH series, which
cannot generally be assumed for local many-body sys-
tems [25].
After expanding the Hamiltonian terms in
the Pauli basis, we rewrite the truncated generator as a
linear combination of adjoint-Pauli superoperators adiP
and truncate the Taylor expansion of its exponential. A
parameter-shift identity converts each adjoint-Pauli fac-
tor in this expansion into a difference of Pauli-rotation
channels, thereby yielding an LCQC approximation to
the Trotter remainder VK(x).
For the leading-order
adjoint-Pauli terms, we instead pair them with the iden-
tity channel and represent each paired term using two
Pauli-rotation channels. This reduces the coefficient 1-
norm of the LCQC and hence the sampling overhead,
leading to the effective 2K + 1-order time scaling while
preserving commutator scaling.
We also provide finite-size resource estimates for the
periodic Heisenberg chain, comparing HNCC with other
product-formula-based methods.
When applied to the
second-order product formula S2, HNCC achieves up to
a 17.5× reduction in the CNOT count per circuit and a
19.8× reduction in the estimated T-gate count per circuit
relative to the uncompensated S2 formula.

VII.
DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
We developed a high-order nested-commutator com-
pensation (HNCC) algorithm for estimating observables
in product-formula Hamiltonian simulation.
By com-
pensating Trotter errors at the level of quantum chan-
nels, HNCC avoids the Hadamard tests required by
unitary-level compensation methods and remains ancilla-
free. Truncating the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff expan-
sion expresses the compensation map in terms of prod-
ucts of nested commutators.
This preserves the com-
mutator scaling of product formulas while improving the
simulation-time and precision dependence.
In particu-
lar, for fixed product-formula order, HNCC achieves the
time scaling of a product formula of order 2K + 1 and
polylogarithmic precision dependence in the circuit size.
In the numerical experiments, we reduce the LCQC
1-norm by merging identical Pauli terms in the trun-
cated BCH generator using connected-cluster preprocess-
ing. For fixed locality and bounded overlap degree, this
procedure has polynomial classical cost and is linear in
the number of Pauli terms for fixed q0.
Whether the
LCQC 1-norm can be reduced efficiently for general k-
local Hamiltonians remains open.
The same channel-level compensation may also apply
beyond Hamiltonian simulation.
Trotter error mitiga-
tion has recently been used in several related settings,
including eigenstate problems [6], Lindbladian simula-
tion [26, 33], and quantum singular value transforma-
tion [34]. Whether channel-level compensation extends
to the product-formula approximations in these algo-
rithms remains open.
