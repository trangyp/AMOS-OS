Quantum Gromov–Hausdorff Convergence for
Extensions of C∗-Algebras
Vibhor Bhatt, Satyajit Guin, Bipul Saurabh
July 8, 2026
Abstract
We study Toeplitz type C∗-algebraic extensions of unital C∗-algebras by stable
ideals, from the perspective of noncommutative metric geometry. Using the spec-
tral metric space construction of Hawkins and Zacharias (Comm. Math. Phys. 350
(2017), 475–506), we analyze the interaction of these extensions with the quantum
Gromov–Hausdorff distance. We show that complete sub-operator systems of the
quotient, or of the unital algebra underlying the stable ideal, canonically determine
complete sub-operator systems of the extension. We introduce the notions of unital
2-contractive approximation and its Toeplitz type refinement as our key approxi-
mation tools. Our main results show that if a sequence of complete sub-operator
systems of the unital algebra underlying the stable ideal converges in the quantum
Gromov–Hausdorff distance under the unital 2-contractive approximation condition
and a compatibility condition on the quotient, then the corresponding sequence in
the extension also converges. An analogous statement holds from the quotient to
the extension under the 2-contractive Toeplitz type refinement condition.
AMS Subject Classification No.: 58B34, 46L87, 81R15, 81R60
Keywords. Compact quantum metric space, spectral triple, spectral metric space,
operator system, Toeplitz type extension, quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

Introduction
The central ingredient in Connes’ noncommutative geometry [15] is the notion of a
spectral triple.
From the point of view of Kasparov theory, spectral triples can be
regarded as abstract Dirac-type elliptic operators associated to a C∗-algebra A: they
are Baaj–Julg (A, C)-cycles [6], the unbounded Kasparov modules in KK-theory [25, 9],
and determine K-homology classes via the corresponding bounded Kasparov cycles [22].
When equipped with additional regularity conditions, spectral triples provide a powerful
framework for encoding geometric information in a noncommutative setting [16]. In this
paper, we focus on the metric structures induced by spectral triples. Given a spectral
triple (A, H, D) over a unital C∗-algebra A that is nondegenerate (i.e. [D, a] = 0 only
for a ∈C .1A), Connes [14, 15] showed the following formula
distD(ϕ, ψ) := sup{|ϕ(a) −ψ(a)| : a ∈A, ∥cl([D, a])∥≤1}
defines an extended metric (possibly ∞-valued) on the state space S(A) of the C∗-algebra
A. For the canonical Dirac triple on a closed spin manifold, the restriction of the metric
to the point evaluation measures coincides with the geodesic metric.
This metric aspect of spectral triples inspired Rieffel to systematically develop the
theory of compact quantum metric spaces (CQMS), a generalization of compact metric

spaces, or Lipschitz functions, to order-unit spaces with the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff
distance as a central tool for quantifying the proximity between such spaces [38, 39, 41,
42]. We note the existence of several other distances due to Kerr [26, 27], Li [34], and
Latrémolière [30, 31], but we do not consider them here. Although Rieffel’s initial CQMS
framework is formulated in the setting of order-unit spaces, it can be recast within the
operator system framework of Connes and van Suijlekom [17], and so can the notion of
spectral triples (called operator system spectral triples), as has become a recent trend
in noncommutative geometry [2, 3, 18, 43, 44, 4, 23, 5]. Every operator system has an
underlying order-unit space given by its self-adjoint part. We work in this generalized
framework for convenience.
To work in this setting precisely, we now fix the relevant objects. Throughout the
article, X denotes a (concrete) complete operator system that is a closed subspace in
a unital C∗-algebra A stable under adjoint operation and containing the unit 1A. A
complete sub-operator system of a complete operator system X is a closed linear subspace
stable under adjoint operation and containing the unit. Every unital C∗-algebra A is
in particular a complete operator system, so the definitions below specialize to the case
of X = A.
A Lipschitz seminorm on a complete operator system X is a seminorm
L : X →R+ defined on a dense unital involutive subspace X of X such that L(a∗) = L(a)
for each a ∈X and L(1) = 0. A Lipschitz seminorm L is called nondegenerate if the set
{a ∈X : L(a) = 0} contains only multiples of identity. A Lipschitz seminorm L on X
determines an extended metric on the state space S(X):
dX,L(ϕ, ψ) := sup{|ϕ(a) −ψ(a)| : a ∈X, L(a) ≤1}.
This construction is, in fact, reversible, which is what makes the seminorm and metric
pictures interchangeable. A metric d on S(X) induces a nondegenerate seminorm on X:

spectral metric spaces [7]. Examples of spectral metric spaces include group C∗-algebras

Spectral metric spaces are but one source of CQMS; approximation of a CQMS by
other, often finite-dimensional, CQMS is an important aspect of the theory, made pre-
cise by the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance introduced above, and it applies just
as much to spectral metric spaces as to CQMS in general. Rieffel himself showed con-
vergence of matrix algebras to the sphere [42], and, under suitable conditions, that of
Fourier truncations of compact quantum groups [44]. Other examples include approxi-
mations of quantum tori by fuzzy tori [29], and the Podleś sphere S2
q by q-analogue of
fuzzy spheres [2]. The need for approximations arises naturally from both mathematical
and physical considerations [42, 43]. A different, though related, notion of approximation
motivated Connes and van Suijlekom to introduce spectral truncations in the operator
system framework [17]; this line of investigation is developing rapidly [45, 18, 32, 19], and
new convergence results in the Gromov–Hausdorff distance have recently been obtained
in this setting as well.
The central object of this paper is certain extensions of unital C∗-algebras by stable
ideals, together with their associated spectral metric structures.
We work with the
so called Toeplitz type C∗-extensions investigated by Hawkins and Zacharias [21], which
generalizes earlier work of Christensen and Ivan [12], and with the construction of spectral
metric structures on these extensions. The central concern of this paper is:
“Given a Toeplitz type C∗-extension equipped with spectral metric structures on the
quotient and on the unital algebra underlying the stable ideal, together with a family of
complete sub-operator systems converging in the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance
at either level, can the convergence be lifted to the extension?”
We make this precise and describe our work in detail in the following.

a crucial role in C∗-algebra theory. Given separable unital C∗-algebras A and B and
faithful unital representations πA : A →B(HA) and πB : B →B(HB), the C∗-extension

Main Results.
The work of Hawkins and Zacharias [21] establishes that Toeplitz
type C∗-extensions inherit CQMS structures from their constituents. This article takes
the next step: we investigate the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff convergence for these
extensions.
We discuss our findings in this article. Our goal here is not to be overly meticulous
in the exact formulations of the theorems; instead, we seek to convey the overall picture.
This allows us to avoid delving into the notational details and technicalities they entail.

the distinction between separate continuity and joint continuity for functions of several
variables; in our case, an additional layer of interaction needs to be controlled for the
convergence and left to future research.

are used to a greater extent than C∗-algebraic methods to handle the Lipschitz algebra.
We expect that the convergence results established here should hold in that setting as
well, although a complete proof is not yet available to us.

2
Preliminaries
In this section, we formally recall the relevant material on compact quantum metric
spaces, generalized in the operator system framework.
2.1
Operator systems and compact quantum metric spaces
We begin with the definition of (concrete) complete operator systems.
Definition 2.1. A (concrete) operator system X is a subspace of a unital C∗-algebra A
such that X is stable under the adjoint operation and contains the unit 1A. We call it a
complete operator system if additionally it is closed.
Definition 2.2. A linear subspace Y of an operator system X is called a sub-operator
system if 1X ∈Y and y∗∈Y for all y ∈Y.
A sub-operator system Y of a complete operator system X is called a complete sub-
operator system if Y is closed in X.
Throughout the article, notation X, Y etc. will denote complete operator systems,
whereas calligraphic letters X, Y etc. stand for operator systems. We shall only work
with concrete operator systems and hence drop the word ‘concrete’ for brevity.
A complete operator system X has an associated state space S(X) consisting of all
positive linear functionals that preserve the unit. A state on X automatically has norm
1, and the state space S(X) therefore becomes a compact Hausdoff space for the weak ∗-
topology. It is known that any positive map Φ : X →Y into another operator system
Y need not be a contraction, but it is bounded with ∥Φ∥≤2∥Φ(1X)∥[37, Propn. 2.1].
If Φ is completely positive, then Φ is completely bounded and ∥Φ∥= ∥Φ∥cb = ∥Φ(1X)∥
[37, Propn. 3.6]. If Φ is assumed to be unital and contractive, then it is automatically
positive.
The self-adjoint part of a complete operator system X
Xsa := {x ∈X : x = x∗}
forms a real vector space, which is an order-unit space equipped with the order-unit 1X
and the partial order inherited from the ambient C∗-algebra A. In particular, positivity
in Xsa is determined by the positivity relation in A.
8


## Page 9


Any state on Xsa extends uniquely to a complex-valued state on X. Indeed, given a
state φ : Xsa →R, the extension eφ : X →C is defined by evaluating φ separately on
the real and imaginary parts of an element x ∈X:
eφ(x) := φ(ℜ(x)) + i φ(ℑ(x)) .
Definition 2.3 ([24]). A compact quantum metric space is a complete operator system
X together with a seminorm L : X →[0, ∞] satisfying the following:
(i) L(x) = 0 if and only if x ∈C.
(ii) The set Dom(L) := {x ∈X : L(x) < ∞} is dense in X and L satisfies that
L(x∗) = L(x) for all x ∈X.
(iii) The function dL(µ, ν) := sup{|µ(x) −ν(x)| : L(x) ≤1} defines a metric on the
state space S(X) that metrises the weak ∗-topology on it.
A seminorm L on X satisfying conditions (i) and (ii) above is referred to as a non-
degenerate Lipschitz seminorm, and if (X, L) defines a compact quantum metric space,
then L is called a Lip-norm on X. The metric dL, sometimes written as mkL, is referred
to as the Monge-Kantorovich metric.
The following result of Rieffel is an indispensable tool in verifying compact quantum
metric space structure.
Theorem 2.4 ([38]). Let X be a complete operator system and L : X →[0, ∞] be a
seminorm satisfying (i) and (ii) from Definition 2.3. Then (X, L) is a compact quantum
metric space if and only if (X, L) has finite diameter and the image of the set {x ∈X :
L(x) ≤1 and ∥x∥≤1} under the quotient map X →X/C is totally bounded for the
quotient norm.
Now, let us recall the definition of spectral triple, the fundamental ingredient in
noncommutative geometry, generalized in the operator system framework.
Definition 2.5 ([17]). An operator system spectral triple (X, H, D) consists of a space
X of bounded operators on a separable Hilbert space H such that its norm closure is
∗-invariant, D is an unbounded self-adjoint operator on H with compact resolvent such
that for all T ∈X we have that T (dom(D)) ⊆dom(D) and [D, T] extends to a bounded
operator.

Any spectral triple (X, H, D) over X induces a Lipschitz seminorm LD : X →[0, ∞]
given by LD(x) := ∥cl([D, x])∥for x ∈X. If LD metrises the weak ∗-topology on the
state space S(X), one often says that the spectral triple (X, H, D) is a spectral metric

It is always an interesting and nontrivial question whether a spectral triple inherits
a spectral metric structure, but the verification is not trivial and, in fact, often quite
involved.
2.2
Quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance
