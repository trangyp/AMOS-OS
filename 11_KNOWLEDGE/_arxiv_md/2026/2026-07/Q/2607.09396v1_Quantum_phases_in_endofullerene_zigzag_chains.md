Quantum phases in endofullerene zigzag chains
Tobias Serwatka,1 Muhammad Shaeer Moeed,2, 3, 4 Roger G. Melko,2, 4 and Pierre-Nicholas Roy1, 3, 4
1Department of Chemistry, University of Waterloo, Ontario, N2L 3G1, Canada
2Department of Physics, University of Waterloo, Waterloo, Ontario, N2L 3G1, Canada
3Institute For Quantum Computing, University of Waterloo, Waterloo, Ontario, N2L 3G1, Canada
4Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics, Waterloo, Ontario N2L 2Y5, Canada
(Dated: July 13, 2026)
We employ large-scale density matrix renormalization group calculations to study the quantum phases of
dipolar molecules confined in bent (zigzag) endofullerene chains, as a function of the chain angle γ. For LiF,
ferroelectric order persists across the full range 60◦< γ < 180◦, with the critical effective dipole moment in-
creasing as the chain bends and parallel alignment becomes less favorable. Near the equilateral configuration
(γ = 60◦), geometric frustration drives a transition to an antiferroelectric Néel-ordered phase in which neighbor-
ing dipoles anti-align along the chain axis. We show that capturing this reorientation requires including dipolar
couplings beyond the nearest-neighbor approximation, since next-nearest-neighbor interactions become equally
strong at γ = 60◦. For confined water, o-D2O reproduces both ordered phases, whereas p-H2O—owing to its
large rotational constants—develops no order at any chain angle despite the enhanced coordination of the bent
geometry. Because a zigzag chain is the narrowest stripe of a two-dimensional lattice, these results suggest
that engineered endofullerene layers could host a rich variety of dipole-ordered quantum phases beyond the
ferroelectric ordering observed in previous work.
Nano-confined molecules are a versatile platform for re-
alizing many-body quantum effects [1, 2], from the anoma-
lous transport of nano-confined water [3–5] to dipole-ordered
(ferroelectric) and disordered (paraelectric) phases of polar
molecules in endofullerene chains [6]. Indeed, dielectric spec-
troscopy of water confined in beryl and cordierite nanochan-
nels reveals incipient ferroelectricity, quantum paraelectricity,
and associated phase transitions between these ordered states
[7–13], establishing such assemblies as a tunable platform for
studying quantum criticality [14–17]. The cages inhibit hy-
drogen bonding so the guests interact through their dipoles
[18], and ordering arises from spontaneous breaking of the
dipole reflection symmetry at zero temperature once the lat-
tice spacing is small enough [6, 19, 20]. Beyond linear chains,
bent (quasi-1D) carbon nanostructures can also be realized ex-
perimentally [21–24]; Fig. 1 shows such a configuration with
chain angle γ and neighbor spacing R0. Dipoles in this geom-
etry are frustrated, as no local orientation satisfies all interac-
tions on a triangular sub-lattice. Such frustration is a central
theme in correlated quantum matter, where competing inter-
actions on triangular geometries stabilize unconventional or-
dered and disordered ground states [25, 26].

This reorientation marks a quantum phase transition dis-
tinct from the paraelectric-ferroelectric (order-disorder) tran-
sition studied previously [1]: here, at sufficiently large dipole
moment, the chain angle γ drives a transition between two or-
dered phases (ferroelectric and antiferroelectric). To exhibit
this we plot the NN correlation function versus µeff and γ in
Fig. 3. The NN correlation changes sign between ferroelec-
tric and antiferroelectric order, making it a good phase marker.
Fig. 3 shows that the critical dipole moment grows as γ moves
away from the optimal alignment angles—180◦(parallel) and
60◦(antiparallel)—in either regime. The ferroelectric region
is markedly larger than the antiferroelectric one, plausibly be-
cause the NNN distance ∝sin(γ/2) varies faster near γ = 60◦
than near 180◦.

Using DMRG, we studied LiF and o-D2O endofullerene
chains over a range of bend angles γ. For large enough µeff we
find ferroelectric phases for 60◦< γ < 180◦, with the transi-
tion shifting to larger µeff as γ decreases. Since linear o-D2O
is near criticality [1], bending drives it from ferroelectric to
paraelectric, whereas LiF stays ordered for γ > 60◦. Ground-
state stability decreases with γ down to 60◦, where the equi-
lateral chain restabilizes into an antiferroelectric Néel phase
with dipoles anti-aligned along the axis—showing that cage
arrangement controls guest alignment. A bent chain is the
narrowest stripe of a two-dimensional material; future work
on true 2D endofullerene lattices, fabricable via molecular
surgery and layer synthesis [47–51], should host a far richer
variety of quantum phases.
Comparing p-H2O and o-D2O, the entropy rises from linear
to equilateral chains in both, yet p-H2O remains disordered:
even the equilateral stripe cannot overcome its large rotational
constants. A 2D or 3D lattice may be needed to order water,
as hypothesized for fullerene crystals [52, 53]. This parallels
the long-debated question of ferroelectric ordering in confined
and low-dimensional water and ice [54–59], where confine-
ment geometry, hydrogen bonding, and large rotational con-
stants continue to challenge experiment and theory.
